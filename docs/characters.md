# Characters: pose, skeleton and attachments

Players and npcs share one character: `character_movement` consumes a `usercmd` from a controller (`player_controller`, `ai_controller`; `character_movement.RegisterController` adds more, `character_movement:GetCommand()` is the current command). Looks follow the same split.

```
usercmd -> character_movement (body yaw, velocity, grounded, crouch)
        -> character_pose     (replicated aim, crouch, holding; derived pitch, crouch_alpha, speeds)
        -> a driver writes the biped armature
        -> attachment_points  (named sockets on the armature)
        -> weapons attach to a socket, never to a raw bone
```

- Animations are definitions (`lua/animations.lua` registry, one file per group in `lua/animations/`, loaded by `animations/definitions.lua`). `Kind = "action"` is a timed one-shot (`Exit` fades its weight out over the last seconds so layers and the crouch do not snap off; `Cutoff(strength)` returns the fraction of `Duration` that a triggered instance plays, so a weak `land` plays only the start of its clip) (`pose:PlayAction(name, duration)`, replicated through `Action`/`Actions`), `"state"` is held with `Enter`/`Exit` (`pose:SetState(name, arg)`, replicated through the `States` string `name=arg;...`; `Period` + `Advance` make a cycling state finish its current cycle before it fades), `"impulse"` is set by a counter (`Trigger = "Shots"`/`"Hits"`) and decays (`Decay`). Any number run at once; `character_pose` keeps the instances in `pose.anims` (`weight`, `phase`, `cycle`, `time`, `arg`) and aggregates `crouch_alpha` (`Crouch`), `locomotion` (`Locomotion = false` zeroes the speeds the source gait sees), `legs` (`Legs = false` skips foot ik) and `viewing`. `Sequence(inst, suffix, clips)` (clips is the skeleton's `ClipsByName`; return nil to not override; `Loop = false` plays once, `Speed` scales the playback) makes any animation the Source base whenever it returns a name (it checks its own weight), the newest one winning (`source_pose` picks it each frame): `air` plays `jump_holding_jump` then `jump_holding_glide` (the HL2 citizen clips `source_pose` adds to player models) or the native `jump_<weapon>`, and `land` (an action with `Duration`, strength 0 at 2 m/s of fall speed to 1 at 11.5 m/s) plays the first `strength` of `jump_holding_land` (a continuous spectrum, no hard/soft split). States and impulses that the pose derives itself are started with `pose:EnableState(name, arg)` every frame (`block`, `air` while `pose.airborne`) and `pose:Trigger(name, weight)` (`land`, weight from the fall speed); neither replicates because every client sees the same grounded flag and velocity. A definition supplies optional layers: `AimKeys` (`{phase, yaw, pitch}` keys) or `Aim(inst, aim)` (read by `aim_pose.GetAim`), `Torso(inst)` (extra spine pitch), `BoneKeys` (data, `lua/bone_keys.lua`: `Time = "phase"|"cycle"` picks what keys are sampled by, each entry is `{Pivot, Chain, Pitch/Yaw/Roll = radians, {{t, value}, ...} or a function(inst, pose) returning radians (the `air` tilt follows the ground speeds), Wobble, Frequency, Offset}` rotating the chain about the pivot bone, scaled by the weight; wobble adds a per-entry sine of the cycle so limbs drift out of sync) and `Bones(bones, inst, pose)` (the same in code), both model space and run by `source_pose` after the aim, `Body(inst, pose, position, rotation)` (the transform override), `View(inst, rotation)` (the first person camera and the aim rotation). The weapon arm pose eases with the `hold` state (`source_pose` sets `pose.hold_wanted` while a posed weapon is wanted, not sprinting or airborne unless `AlwaysPose`): while its weight is under 1 `MirrorBones` runs `aim_pose.Apply` twice (with and without the weapon) and blends the eight arm bones and the hand grip matrices with `aim_pose.BlendMatrix`, and the block arm blends the same way with the `block` weight. A weapon with `Holster` (the pistol) is held lowered until it is fired: each shot (`pose.Shots`) sets `pose.ready_time` to 2-3 s (a hash of the pose seed and the shot count, so every client agrees) and the `ready` state is on while it runs, other weapons switch `ready` on at once; `aim_pose.GetHoldAim(pose, aim)` blends the direction the weapon arms hold from `aim_pose.LoweredPitch` (down) to the view aim by the `ready` weight, so the arm solve, the grip rotation and the eye body hand targets all follow it (the eye body also drops the hold point by `LOWER_DROP`). Selecting another posed weapon keeps the previous one for `SWITCH_TIME` (0.2 s) and blends its solve into the new one (a third `aim_pose.Apply`). The eye body (`eye_pose.Compute`) sets `pose.hold_wanted` itself and blends its hand targets, grips, swing and hand rotation with the same `hold` and `block` weights and the same weapon switch blend. Query with `pose:IsPlaying`, `pose:GetWeight`, `pose:GetActiveAction`. Layer functions live at module level, never create closures in them.
- Body yaw: `character_movement:SetBodyYaw(view)` snaps the body to a yaw with no physics interpolation (it also resets the body's `StepStartRotation` and the transform's cached interpolated rotation, which otherwise lag the yaw by up to a tick and quantize it to physics ticks, so everything attached to the body, the weapon, the muzzle and the physgun beam start, jittered against the camera while turning). `player_input` applies it every frame in `OnPreUpdate` from the current look rotation (first person only, `movement.frame_body_yaw`; the tick then skips its own call), third person and camera modes keep the yaw from the tick commands.
- `character_pose` is state only. `ViewRotation`, `Crouching`, `Holding`, `Blocking`, the counters `Shots` and `Hits` and the one-shot action (`PlayAction(name, duration)`: `Action`, `ActionDuration`, the `Actions` counter; read `action`/`action_fraction`) replicate; `pitch` (view relative to the body, frozen when dead), `crouch_alpha`, `forward_speed`, `right_speed`, `vertical_speed` and `grounded` are computed every frame. Where there is a movement it reads the command, the local player reads `player_input` for zero latency, remote players use relayed commands. It exists on every character, so drivers and `weapon_holder` never look at a controller. `weapon:Think` calls `pose:RegisterShot()` after a primary fire and `health` damage reaches `pose:OnTakeDamage`; every client notices the counters change and starts `recoil` or `flinch` (both decay to 0), so fire and hit reactions need no extra packets. A driver sets `pose.driver` so only one writes the armature.
- The biped (`lua/biped.lua`) is the in-engine skeleton (`armature` skeleton `biped`, ~20 bones). It only exists where something drives it (client side, rendering) and humanoids only; creatures just animate their model.
- Drivers: `procedural_pose` (the eye body of the default outfit: `eye_pose.Compute` fills every biped bone each update, headless, from the pose, the velocity and a ray down to the ground, with feet stepping, arm swing and weapon grips; it writes them to the armature when there is one, and `GetBones()` hands them to the outfit and the ragdoll) and `source_pose` (plays Source sequences on the entity's `animator` from the pose, and with `Mirror` mirrors the animated Source bones into the biped through `biped.SourceBones`). `source_pose` has two profiles for sequence choice: `creature` (idle/walk/run sequence names, ground speed driven rate, `move_yaw`) and `gmod_player` (gmod player sequence naming by weapon, `move_x/move_y`). `OwnsArmature` makes the component add the armature to its own entity (npcs), otherwise the armature belongs to an ancestor (the player). It also sets the socket profile of its `attachment_points` (`SocketProfile`).
- Aim (`lua/aim_pose.lua`, pure functions over a table of bone matrices, tested without a rig): the pose pitch (plus recoil) is shared over waist, spine, chest, back (Source `Spine`, `Spine1`, `Spine2`, `Spine4`), neck and head (`aim_pose.Shares`, each joint rotates itself and everything above it about its own position, so the arms ride along); flinch leans spine and chest. With no posed weapon the shoulders and arms are turned back by the torso share of the pitch (about the clavicles), so they keep the animation's orientation while the head and torso look around. While a weapon is held before the arm ik the clavicle swings `ClavicleShare` of the way towards the target (shoulder protraction and lift), the elbow cannot fold past `aim_pose.ElbowFlexion` (the ragdoll hinge limit, so a target nearer than that makes the hand stop short; for a held weapon the whole weapon is shifted instead (`compute_shift`, a few iterations pulling both wrists into their arm's reach window), so both hands stay on their handles), the right arm gets a two bone ik to a point `ArmReach` along the aim in front of the shoulder (plus the weapon's `HoldOffset`, a body space position, not rotated by the aim), and the left arm too when the weapon is `TwoHanded` (a one handed weapon pins the other hand at `aim_pose.NeutralHand`, a body space point beside the hip, so it does not swing with the animation; the eye body stops swinging that arm too) (physgun, camera): to `SupportGrip`, a weapon space offset from the right grip, with `SupportRoll` about the aim (the camera's left palm is up under the lens). A weapon with `HoldFromHead` (the hands) puts the right hand at its `HoldOffset` from the head bone (in the aim frame, so it follows the pitch as well as the yaw) instead of from the shoulder along the aim, and `HoldOffset` and `SupportGrip` replicate, so a script can move both hands at runtime (the hands weapon sweeps from both sides at the object's actual position every tick, so the palms rest on its surface). A weapon with `PoseArms` false (the empty hands) does not pose the arms at all: `weapon_holder:GetPosedWeapon()` is what the pose code reads, so the animation or idle plays. The weapon's origin is the centre of the gripping hand, so a weapon model is a handle at the origin, a body and a muzzle. The ik places the palm centre (`aim_pose.PalmOffset`, hand local) on the target and turns the hand bone to the aim (the Source hand has fingers along -z, palm normal +x, the thumb on -y for the right and +y for the left hand), so the mesh hand wraps the handle; the armature hand takes the aim rotation at the palm (the grip socket and so the weapon point where the character aims). `source_pose` writes the aimed bones back into the animator rig as model space overrides so the mesh follows; it clears its own overrides before reading the bones each frame, otherwise the rotations would accumulate. `Quat:Lerp` mutates its receiver, use `GetLerped`.
- Sockets (`armature:SetSocket(name, bone, offset, rotation)`, listed by `GetSocketNames`) work like bones for `transform.Attachment`. `attachment_points` applies a named profile from `biped.SocketProfiles` (`grip_r`, `grip_l`, `eye`, `head`, `center`, `feet`) when its entity's armature is built (`OnArmatureRebuild`, `OnEntityConstructed` for prefab built entities, whose events are suppressed). A weapon model attaches to `grip_r`; per-model offsets or rotations go into a profile.
- First person: the outfit stays shown and only its head is `ViewHidden` (not drawn, still in reflections and shadows). The eye outfit's script flags its eye parts, a Source outfit has `animator:SetViewHiddenBone(head bone)` hide the primitives whose vertices are mostly weighted to that bone's subtree (`player_avatar:ApplyFirstPerson`).
- The eye body (`eye_pose`): the eye centre is fixed `EyeRadius` behind and `BodyDrop` under the camera's eye point and the pupil sits on the sphere around it, so looking around rotates the eye about its centre (the head is hidden in first person, so the pupil need not be at the camera). Hips hang under the centre, shoulders at its sides follow `TORSO_PITCH` of the view pitch only, and held weapons sit at a fixed offset to the lower right of the eye in the aim frame (`WEAPON_OFFSET`, right handed, plus the weapon's `HoldOffset`), so in first person they stay at the edge of the view at any pitch; the hands go to the grip origin and `SupportGrip`. The outfit's limb capsules are sized from `ArmLength`/`LegLength` when the prefab is generated, delete a saved `player_outfit` after changing them.
- Head camera (`cl_outfit_head_camera`, with `cl_outfit_head_vertical` and `cl_outfit_head_smoothing`, set in the Outfit window): with a Source outfit `player_avatar` measures the head bone against the capsule eye point, smooths it and writes it into the `head` of every `usercmd` (a `CreateMove` listener), so the server sees the same offset. `character_movement:GetViewOffset()` is `GetEyeOffset()` (crouch eye height) plus `GetHeadOffset()` (the command's head, clamped to two capsule radii on every side, `ClampHeadOffset`); the camera component does not use the command (a tick old and quantized to ticks) but runs `player_avatar:UpdateHeadOffset` itself in `OnLateUpdate`, after every update has posed the armature, and adds that to the eye offset, so the view sits exactly on the head bone of the current frame; weapon origins, use, teleport and npc sight all read `GetViewOffset()`, while placing the capsule from an eye position (`SetEyePosition`, the ragdoll `FollowHead`) uses `GetEyeOffset()`. The camera does not sit at the head bone (the middle of the head, inside the skull and, when looking down, inside the coat): `cl_outfit_head_forward` moves it that far along the view towards where the eyes are, and `cl_outfit_head_near` is the near plane while it is on (it replaces the normal 0.1 one, so it can be much closer; the depth is reversed) (`camera` reads `player_avatar:IsHeadCameraActive()`). The Source `eyes` attachment would be the exact point, the mdl decoder does not read attachments yet. The aim direction stays the command's view rotation.
- Variation (`lua/pose_variation.lua`, computed in `character_pose` for every character): a per character `seed` gives a fixed animation rate scale (±`RateSpread`) and a slow drift (`rate_variation`, multiplied into the animator speed and the eye pose time), and smooth sum-of-sines noise sways `pose.pitch` (pitch and yaw, smaller while moving), so the aim, head and held weapon wander a little. `lean_side` and `lean_forward` follow the horizontal acceleration of the body (smoothed, `atan(a / g)`, clamped to `MaxLean`, zero in the air and when dead): the eye body tilts about the ground, and `aim_pose.Apply` tilts the upper body of a Source rig about the pelvis. Keep noise frequencies incommensurate so nothing visibly loops; tests set `SwayPitch`/`SwayYaw` to 0 when they compare exact aim angles.
- Pose state that has to match between the server and the clients follows one rule: it is either a pure function of state every machine has, or the server replicates the one input that is not. The seed is hashed from the network owner (players) or network id (npcs) and so is the same everywhere without a packet; noise and rate drift are functions of the seed and `system.GetGameTime()` (the server clock clients sync to, a fraction of a second apart); the lean target is computed where the character is simulated (the server and the owning client, from velocity) and the server replicates it as `LeanSide`/`LeanForward` (sequenced, interpolated) to everyone else, who smooth it with the same filter. Locomotion and idle phase work the same way: `character_movement.distance` is an odometer integrated per physics tick while grounded (the server's value reaches the owning client in `player_ack` and `Reconcile` pulls the client's odometer to it), `character_pose.Distance` is that value on simulating machines and the replicated, interpolated one elsewhere, and `source_pose` calls `animator:SyncCycle(cycle, dt)` every update with `pose_variation.GetStrideCycle` (`distance / stride`, the stride being ground speed times clip duration, or `reference * duration` for the gmod player) while moving and `GetClockCycle` (`GetGameTime()`) while idling. `SyncCycle` snaps when the animator is more than a quarter cycle away and follows smoothly otherwise, so a joining client lands on the right phase at once; the eye body's arm swing phase is `Distance` too. Not synced: clips that are not periodic (jump, attack, actions: start times follow the replicated counters) and the individual foot steps of the eye body (they follow synced positions, not a clock). New pose inputs should be added by the same rule, never from `math.random` or local frame time.
- Gait: `GroundSpeed` is the run speed. `BUTTON.WALK` (left alt) multiplies it by `WalkMultiplier`, `BUTTON.SPRINT` (left shift) by `SprintMultiplier`, `BUTTON.CROUCH` by `CrouchMultiplier`; crouch beats walk, and walk beats sprint. The shield of jrpg blocks while `WALK` is held. `source_pose` picks the gmod player sequence from the measured ground speed, so remote clients agree without extra state: below 1.4x walk speed `walk_*`, then `run_*`, and above 1.5x run speed `run_all_02` with `sprinting` set, which skips the aim arm solve of `MirrorBones` so the animation keeps both arms (unless the weapon has `AlwaysPose`, the physgun and the carrying hands, which keep posing while sprinting and in the air) (a held weapon follows the animated hand). The playback rate of a gait is the movement speed over the ground speed the clip itself reports at full forward `move_x` (`clip:GetGroundSpeed`, read from the sequence's movement, so no per clip tuning). The gmod strafe and backwards clips cover less ground than the forward ones (run: forward 4.0, backwards 3.2, sideways 2.85 m/s), so every rate and the odometer scale the distance by `pose_variation.GetStrideScale(forward, right, crouching)` (an ellipse through `BackRatio` and `SideRatio`, 1 when crouching); `character_movement.distance` and so `Distance` is therefore a stride distance, not meters; only the sprint is capped at `SPRINT_MAX_RATE` times natural, a visual compromise because the sprint speed is twice run speed. A clip without ground speed falls back to the constants of `GAIT_SPEED`. Jumping: the gmod player is airborne from the first frame it is ungrounded with a vertical speed over `AIR_SPEED` until it is grounded again (no re-entering at the apex, where the vertical speed crosses zero, which restarted the clip), plays `jump_<weapon>` (`jump_passive` unarmed, there is no `jump_all`) once from the start, and does not solve the aim arms while airborne, like sprinting, so the clip keeps both arms. The eye outfit does all of this procedurally.
- Leg ik (`lua/leg_pose.lua`, Source outfits only; the eye body grounds its own feet): every update `source_pose:UpdateLegs` casts a ray down at each animated foot, smooths the ground height under it in world space (not relative to the capsule, which still steps up ledges and stairs, so a smoothing relative to it would show every step), turns that into a shift from the ground under the origin (clamped, zero in the air) and takes the ground normal, and `leg_pose.Apply` moves the pelvis and everything above to the mean of the two feet's ground (smoothed with a rise estimate so a constant slope does not lag), then solves each leg with a two bone ik (the knee keeps the animation's bend direction but is turned back to within about 37 degrees of forward (`KneeForward`, and `KneeTrust` blends the pole to forward when the animated leg is almost straight, so a nearly straight animated leg cannot flip it backwards for a frame) and cannot fold past `KneeFlexion`) so the foot lands on the ground at the animated clearance, tilted to the normal up to `MaxTilt`. The animation's feet still step as before, only their height and tilt follow the surface; horizontal foot planting is not locked.
- Slopes versus steps: `character_movement:TryStepUp` only treats a hit as a step when the surface it hits is steeper than `MinGroundNormalY`; a walkable slope is followed through the ground velocity along its normal, so the capsule slides up it instead of teleporting up in step sized lifts.
- Ground gap: a grounded capsule rests up to `GroundReach` above the surface, and animated Source feet sit at the entity origin, so `source_pose:UpdateGroundSink` casts a ray down from the render position (rigid bodies included, ignoring the character) and sinks every overridden bone, and with it the armature and the weapon sockets, by that gap (clamped to `MAX_SINK`, smoothed, zero in the air). The eye body grounds its feet with its own rays.
- Source models do not all have every biped bone (`zombie_classic` has no neck or head bone). Anything that reads or writes the rig by `biped.SourceBones` goes through `biped.HasBone(rig, name)` and `biped.ReadBones(rig, bones)`, which gives a missing bone the matrix of its biped parent, so the pose, the armature and the ragdoll always see all of them; a missing bone just is not overridden in the rig. The first person head hiding takes the first present bone of head, neck, chest.
- A weapon with `HidesPlayer` (the camera) hides the whole outfit, shadows and reflections included, while it is active in first person (`player_avatar:UpdateWeaponHidden`, a `visibility` hider on the outfit) and the head camera is off meanwhile, since a hidden outfit is not animated and the camera is held at the capsule eye anyway. The eye outfit's arms no longer have a hide of their own.
- `weapon_holder` updates the model visibility of its weapons for players and npcs alike (hidden in first person through `pose.first_person`).
- Body parts and ragdoll (`ragdoll`, `ragdoll_part`, `biped.Profiles`): every character with a `ragdoll` component has one capsule body per part of its `Profile` (`humanoid`, or `eye`: a sphere head with four limbs and no torso), always. While alive they are kinematic, driven every physics tick from `biped_pose` (the bones of the visible animated rig when the character has one, `pose.driver.Owner.animator:GetRig()`, so the parts follow walking, running and aiming exactly; otherwise, on a server without models, the biped rest pose scaled to `character_movement:GetHeight()` with crouch and the aim pose, which has no walk cycle; the `eye` profile reads `procedural_pose:GetBones()` instead and has the walk cycle), with `CollisionMask = 0` so they never push anything. They exist for traces: `damage.Sweep(origin, movement, attacker)` skips the movement capsule of any character that has parts. A body's `TraceOwner` (the character, for parts) makes a sweep or ray that ignores that entity ignore the body too, so a character never hits its own parts with the physgun beam, teleport or use traces. and `damage.GetVictim(entity)` returns the character and the part's damage multiplier (head 2.5). Parts become dynamic through limpness (below); on death every part does, with a little jitter (a perfectly balanced stack of capsules would stand still), and the movement capsule becomes a disabled kinematic body that follows the head so the dead view stays on the corpse. `character_movement:Reset` fires `OnCharacterReset` and the parts go back to kinematic at the pose. `CorpseTime` removes the character after that many seconds (npcs).
- `biped.Rest` is measured from the idle pose of the ValveBiped models, not guessed: parts that are shorter than the mesh limbs leave the mesh poking through the floor and looking mangled once it follows the parts. A part is fixed to its bone: `BuildFrames` stores where the segment sits in the bone's own frame (centre and a rotation taking the capsule axis onto the segment), taken once from the rig, so it holds in every pose. Alive, the part is `frame * bone`; dead, `DriveRig` writes the bone back as `part * frame^-1`. Nothing is sampled at the moment of death (a sampled offset was wrong whenever the parts had already moved, and limbs drifted from the mesh), and a client computes the same frames from its own rig. Each part's frame hangs on its `Bone` (the head part is the head bone, not the neck-to-head segment). `DriveRig` clears the overrides of the other aimed bones once so they follow their parents, and recomputes the rig at the end.
- Drive kinematic bodies through their entity's `transform`, not `body.Position` (bodies are synced from the transform every step), and pass copies: `transform:SetPosition` keeps the reference.
- Limpness: every part has a limpness from 0 to 1 and is dynamic, jointed to its parent, while it has any; at 0 it is kinematic and follows the pose. A dynamic part is only turned towards the pose through its angular velocity, relative to its parent as the parent is now, with a gain that falls with limpness (`POSE_RATE`); translation is left to the physics engine, so the pose can never drag a body into a position physics would not reach. A child is never less limp than its parent. A part goes back to kinematic once its limpness is gone and it tracks the pose (or after `EXIT_FORCE_TIME`). The limpness of a part is the largest of its sources: a hit (`ragdoll:OnTakeDamage` with `info.Part`, looked up in `Hits` of the profile: amount, time held, time to recover, impulse; a hit on the root part goes to its children, the root is only limp for the whole body), the physgun hold, and death. Limp arms and legs spoil what the character does with them: the pistol spreads its aim by the limpness of the right arm (`GetLimp`). The parts of a limp part's chain are made dynamic in place, placed where their parent now has them, so joint limits (measured from the pose a joint is built in) keep their meaning.
- The whole body goes limp when the root part reaches `ROOT_LIMP` (a physgun hold, or death): movement and the weapon holder ignore the command (`Limp`, replicated), the capsule is a disabled kinematic body, and the entity frame follows the root with its yaw kept. `ragdoll:Grab(body)` / `Ungrab(body)` are the physgun's (its sweep accepts kinematic parts and skips the movement capsule of characters with parts): a hold ramps to `HOLD_LIMP`, the held part gets the mass of the whole body so the light limb does not give way, and is left to the physgun (its rotation is not locked). Letting go moves the hold to `SETTLE_LIMP`; the body stays a ragdoll for at least `SETTLE_MIN_TIME` and until nothing moves faster than `SETTLE_SPEED`/`SETTLE_SPIN`, then the capsule is put on the ground under it and the parts blend from where they lie to the pose over `BLEND_TIME` (no stand-up animation yet). Fall damage applies while the whole body is limp: the speed the parts lost (not counting held ones) goes to `character_movement:Impact`. Dying during a hold or a blend ends as an ordinary ragdoll.
- A corpse puts all its parts to sleep once every part has stayed under `REST_SPEED`/`REST_SPIN` for `REST_TIME` (`UpdateRest`): jointed limbs on world geometry keep a slow residual motion below no practical sleep threshold, so the engine's own sleep rule alone leaves most ragdolls awake for tens of seconds.
- Joint limits are measured from the pose the joint was built in (the engine takes the relative rotation at creation as zero), so hinge limits are shifted by the bend the pose already has; hinge angles are positive when the child swings forward (elbows `{0, 2.4}`, knees `{-2.4, 0}`). Parts collide with each other except pairs the joints connect and pairs that already overlap when a part goes dynamic (restored when it goes kinematic). Parts are server only until they are dynamic. A dynamic part gets a `network` component (kept `NETWORK_LINGER` after it recovers), and `ragdoll_part` replicates `Group`, `CharacterId` (the character's `network.NetworkId`) and `Limp`; a client's `ragdoll:HasLimp()` and `IsPartLimp(name)` come from those. `source_pose` plays the animation and aim every update and then calls `ragdoll:DriveRig(rig, armature)`: each bone of a limp part gets `part * frame^-1` as a model space override on top of that, the aim overrides of what hangs on it are freed so it follows, and the armature (the weapon sockets) is updated for those bones; the animation bones `source_pose` read are what the parts follow, so they must not carry the overrides of the last frame (`ClearRig`). Without a rig, `procedural_pose` keeps the pure pose (`GetPoseBones`, what the parts follow) apart from the shown bones (`GetBones`) and calls `ragdoll:ReadBones(bones)` on the shown ones: the bones of limp parts are taken from the parts and every other biped bone below them stays rigid to its parent (`Attach` in the profile can name another one), so the outfit follows a limp limb or a corpse. A part bone of the eye profile has to point along its segment, which `eye_pose` does, or the frame would not follow the limb.
- The physics debug (`N`, `addons/debug/lua/autorun/physics/physics_debug.lua`) shows the characters: every part as a wire capsule (head red, torso and pelvis orange, other parts blue while following the pose, green once released, grey while collision is off), the movement capsules faint, ragdoll joints in yellow, the target line of an npc, and the trace a weapon fired from the player would take with the part, multiplier and victim health at the hit. The hovered body is picked the way weapons pick (`damage.Sweep`), so it lists the part, its group and multiplier.
- Not done: Source `.phy` ragdolls (bone names, parents and constraints are in the phy text and are not parsed), hitboxes for players that wear the eye outfit (the server does not know the outfit, so a player uses the humanoid profile and only npcs set `Profile`), lag compensation, an own-collision rule for pushing props with the alive body.
- Also not done: per-model profiles beyond `source_humanoid`, sockets for non-humanoid models (nothing needs one yet, they have no biped).
