# Material baking

`goluwa/render3d/material_bake.lua` bakes what a material's flags derive (roughness, metallic, normals, tints, channel selection) into three compressed textures once, so the shader samples them instead of evaluating the flags.

How: `material_bake.Render(material, width, height)` runs the engine's own surface shader code (`BuildPBRSurfaceGlsl`: `get_albedo_world`, `get_normal_map`, `get_roughness`, `get_metallic`, `init_surface_features` including derived roughness) over uv space, one texel per uv texel, through the same material uniform blocks the gbuffer uses (`render3d.SetMaterial`, a quad whose vertices are its uvs, an identity matrix) into three render targets. Evaluating the real functions means the bake cannot disagree with the flag path, there is no second copy of the logic. `Bake` hands the targets to `texture_compress.Recompress` (see [texture-compression.md](texture-compression.md)) and returns `Albedo` (bc7 srgb, alpha is the material's alpha), `Normal` (bc5, the xy the shader would use) and `MetallicRoughness` (bc7 linear, r metallic, g roughness, b the specular scale divided by 2 with the derived cavity damping in it, read back through `PackedSpecularScale` so `SpecularMultiplier` stays a runtime value), plus `Albedo2`, `Normal2` (only when the material has a second normal) and `MetallicRoughness2` for a vertex blend (see below), plus `Emissive` (bc7 srgb, the color before `EmissiveMultiplier`) when the material emits (`Emits`: an emissive texture, albedo alpha or metallic alpha as emissive). The render neutralises `EmissiveMultiplier` for the draw and the multiplier stays on the material, so a tint or intensity set at runtime still works. `Apply` sets `EmissiveTextureIsColor` and clears the two alpha flags. `Apply` swaps the material to them and resets every property that existed only to derive them (channel flags, remaps, multipliers, derived roughness, ssbump, tints).

`material_bake.Request(material)` is called by the vmt loader once a material is set up. It is a no-op unless the pvar `r_bake_materials` is on (off by default, not stored). With the pvar on, the vmt loader creates the material's textures deferred (`Texture.New{defer = true}`: the fallback texture until `Texture:Load()`), so `Request` can decide before any original is read: `CanBake`, then the cache key (`GetKey`, the material's vmt path plus a sha1 of every property and the path of every texture). On a hit the three baked dds files load through the normal async texture path and replace the material's textures, the originals are never loaded. On a miss, or when the material cannot be baked, `LoadDeferred` loads everything, and a miss is queued once its textures are ready and baked at most two per frame from the update loop, after a few frames so views and descriptors have settled. Results are stored in `storage/cache/baked_materials/v<Version>/<name>_<hash>/{albedo,normal,metallicroughness[,emissive][,albedo2,normal2,metallicroughness2]}.dds`, where `name` is the vmt path without the `materials/` prefix and without the `maps/<map>/` and `_x_y_z` of a map patch, so patched variants of one material share an entry, and folders of other versions are deleted on first use (see `GetVersion`). A miss re-checks the cache when it is dequeued, another material may have written the same key meanwhile. A material re-applied by a vmt reload is requested again and hits the cache.

Rules that were learned the hard way:
- Every deferred texture must end up loaded, the ones a hit replaces excepted. `LoadDeferred` walks all texture slots of the material (micro detail included), a texture that is never loaded stays the pink and black fallback.
- A bake must never run on a fallback or unloaded texture: `process` errors on a texture that is not ready and skips a material whose texture `IsFallback` (a missing file), so nothing wrong reaches the cache.
- `Render` clears the micro detail texture for the draw and restores it, so the grain is not baked in and applied twice.
- Callbacks on a texture becoming ready (albedo gain reads `texture.reflectivity`) run inside `MakeReady`, anything they read must be set before it.
- The key must not depend on load order. The vmt loader's albedo ready adjustments (albedo gain, metal gate, derived roughness) are held back in `material.deferred_adjustments` while baking is on: `Request` takes the key first, then a hit drops them (the baked textures contain their effect) and anything else runs them when the albedo is ready.

Estimators: the metal gate is not one of them, it is the runtime feature `DerivedMetalAlbedo` (see animation-and-source.md) and the bake just renders it. Because the bake runs once per material over the whole texture, `Render` can apply estimates that would be too expensive or too wide for the per pixel runtime path. They live in the bake shader only (`material_bake.estimators`, each can be switched off), so the flag path is unchanged and a material that is not baked does not get them. The cache version is `material_bake.GetVersion()`, a hash of `material_bake.lua`, `texture_compress.lua` and the generated bake shader (which includes the surface feature code), so editing any of them re-bakes without anyone remembering to bump a number. `estimate_albedo` (strengths in `material_bake.estimator_params`: `flatten`, `local_contrast`, `saturation`, tuned against a Marigold v2 albedo from ComfyUI as a reference, which only judges structure and colour: it cannot judge absolute brightness because Source lightmaps modulate world textures by up to 2x) pulls tileable world textures (not `materials/models/`, whose atlases cannot be blurred across islands) towards their own mean luminance at a large scale and compresses local contrast, moves saturation towards what a decomposition gives (low saturation textures gain the most), then lifts regions darker than any real albedo by a smooth multiplicative gain from the blurred luminance (capped at 6, so the ratios inside a shadow survive; an additive floor flattens them into one grey and shows the source texture's few near black levels and compression blocks), blurs near black texels half way towards their neighbourhood to hide those blocks, adds a little dither grain scaled by the gain so the lift does not band, and caps the brightest at 0.9. `estimate_normal` runs only for a material without a normal texture or height map: the local darkness of the albedo against its neighbourhood (the shading the albedo estimator removes, band passed between 2 and 16 texels, clamped so cavities count more than ridges, faded near black so island borders do not get outlines) is treated as a height and turned into a gentle normal (`estimator_params.normal`). `Bake` renders that normal on its own at twice the albedo size (at most 2048, the albedo and packed textures stay at the albedo size) with the sampling point warped by smooth noise under one source texel and a faint height jitter (`estimator_params.normal_noise`), so the relief does not follow low resolution texels pixel for pixel; radii and slope scale are measured in source texels so they do not depend on the render size. It is shape from shading from one texture, so it also embosses paint and stains and is kept subtle. `estimate_roughness` runs only where the material has no explicit roughness source (a mask texture is authoritative): it adds the slope variance of the normal map around the texel (a Toksvig style term, rough where the normals vary) and a little of the local luminance contrast (mottled surfaces are rough), so grout lines, crevices and ridges come out rougher than the faces next to them while the family mean stays within a few hundredths. The albedo estimate is deliberately mild; the metal mask is the visible one. To compare estimators against a reference, render each material raw, send it through the `pbr_assets` ComfyUI client (`comfy:Run` with `passes = {"albedo"}`, `restyle = false`) and score low frequency error, local contrast and saturation against the result, never absolute brightness. Judge changes with a contact sheet (raw albedo, before, after, metal, roughness per material) over a spread of families, not with one scene.

Blends (`GetLayerKind`): the blend weight is vertex data, so it can never be baked, but each layer can be, once per material. `"multiply"` (the second albedo only modulates the first) bakes as a whole and drops the second albedo. `"blend"` bakes layer one as usual and layer two from a copy of the material with `Albedo2`/`Normal2` swapped into the primary slots, into `Albedo2`, `Normal2` and `MetallicRoughness2`; the shader still mixes the layers by the vertex weight and `MetallicRoughness2Texture` is mixed into roughness and metallic like the layer one texture. Without a second normal the layer one normal keeps applying across the blend, as in the flag path. The deviation from the flag path: roughness and metallic derived from the mixed albedo or normal become the mix of the two layers' values. A blend with `BlendTintByBaseAlpha`, `AlbedoTextureAlphaIsRoughness` (layer one only in the shader), `MultiplyAlbedo2` together with a second normal or blend texture, a `Texture2` transform, or an emissive layer is skipped.

Not baked yet (`CanBake` says why, and `GetStats().skipped` counts them): blends that cannot be baked per layer, terrain and ground colour, additive materials (the additive path of `get_emissive` ignores the emissive texture), the specular masks (`RoughnessMaskOnlyScalesSpecular`, `SpecularFromRoughnessMask`), texture transforms other than identity (the baked textures are the untransformed content, one packed texture cannot follow three transforms), vmt proxies, no-draw materials, and materials with a texture that has no path to key it by. Detail and micro detail stay runtime features, so do the render state flags (alpha test, translucent, double sided), height and parallax, and the lighting feature params. What also stays runtime because it is not a function of the material: `get_antialiased_roughness` (screen space derivatives), `r_normal_map_strength`, weather, conditions and overlays.

Verification: `test/tests/render3d/material_bake.lua` bakes a material that uses normal alpha as roughness, a roughness remap, a metallic multiplier, a colour tint and a normal multiplier, applies the bake to a copy and renders both through the same pass: normals agree within 2, metallic exactly, roughness within 5 and albedo with a mean error under 3 (the rest is bc7). On a gm_flatgrass load, 20 materials were baked in a few seconds and the next load served all 27 from the cache.

Not done: the cry material loader does not call `Request` yet, the vmt loader still sets the properties the bake then drops, height is not baked (it is an input texture, not a derived value), and the baked set has not been compared against the gbuffer output for a real mesh, only through the bake pass itself.

Height from normals: `goluwa/render/height_from_normals.lua` solves for the height whose slopes a normal texture describes (the Frankot Chellappa Poisson solve) in compute shaders: a gradient pass, a Stockham radix 2 fft (one complex fft carries both slope fields as `p + iq`, the frequency space pass separates them from the symmetry of a real signal), the solve, the inverse fft and a normalise pass, all on the gpu with no readback until the caller maps the returned float buffer. It needs a square power of two texture and assumes the surface tiles; `cutoff` (cycles over the texture) drops the slowest frequencies, the drift a tiling solve cannot know. The height is in texels for slopes in texels, so a strong normal map gives a deep one. `height_cut` (cycles) fades out the fine frequencies. The bake does not use it yet; `vmt_material` uses it to generate parallax heights for the bump maps in its `NORMAL_PARALLAX` table.
