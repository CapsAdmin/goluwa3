# Material baking

`goluwa/render3d/material_bake.lua` bakes what a material's flags derive (roughness, metallic, ambient occlusion, normals, tints, channel selection) into three compressed textures once, so the shader samples them instead of evaluating the flags.

How: `material_bake.Render(material, width, height)` runs the engine's own surface shader code (`BuildPBRSurfaceGlsl`: `get_albedo_world`, `get_normal_map`, `get_roughness`, `get_metallic`, `get_ao`, `init_surface_features` including derived roughness) over uv space, one texel per uv texel, through the same material uniform blocks the gbuffer uses (`render3d.SetMaterial`, a quad whose vertices are its uvs, an identity matrix) into three render targets. Evaluating the real functions means the bake cannot disagree with the flag path, there is no second copy of the logic. `Bake` hands the targets to `texture_compress.Recompress` (see [texture-compression.md](texture-compression.md)) and returns `Albedo` (bc7 srgb, alpha is the material's alpha), `Normal` (bc5, the xy the shader would use) and `MetallicRoughness` (bc7 linear, r metallic, g roughness, b ambient occlusion, the layout of `MetallicRoughnessIsMRAO`), plus `Albedo2`, `Normal2` (only when the material has a second normal) and `MetallicRoughness2` for a vertex blend (see below), plus `Emissive` (bc7 srgb, the color before `EmissiveMultiplier`) when the material emits (`Emits`: an emissive texture, albedo alpha or metallic alpha as emissive). The render neutralises `EmissiveMultiplier` for the draw and the multiplier stays on the material, so a tint or intensity set at runtime still works. `Apply` sets `EmissiveTextureIsColor` and clears the two alpha flags. `Apply` swaps the material to them and resets every property that existed only to derive them (channel flags, remaps, multipliers, derived roughness, ssbump, tints).

`material_bake.Request(material)` is called by the vmt loader once a material is set up. It is a no-op unless the pvar `r_bake_materials` is on (off by default, not stored). With the pvar on, the vmt loader creates the material's textures deferred (`Texture.New{defer = true}`: the fallback texture until `Texture:Load()`), so `Request` can decide before any original is read: `CanBake`, then the cache key (`GetKey`, the material's vmt path plus a sha1 of every property and the path of every texture). On a hit the three baked dds files load through the normal async texture path and replace the material's textures, the originals are never loaded. On a miss, or when the material cannot be baked, `LoadDeferred` loads everything, and a miss is queued once its textures are ready and baked at most two per frame from the update loop, after a few frames so views and descriptors have settled. Results are stored in `storage/cache/baked_materials/v<Version>/<name>_<hash>/{albedo,normal,metallicroughness[,emissive][,albedo2,normal2,metallicroughness2]}.dds`, where `name` is the vmt path without the `materials/` prefix and without the `maps/<map>/` and `_x_y_z` of a map patch, so patched variants of one material share an entry, and folders of other versions are deleted on first use. A miss re-checks the cache when it is dequeued, another material may have written the same key meanwhile. A material re-applied by a vmt reload is requested again and hits the cache.

Rules that were learned the hard way:
- Every deferred texture must end up loaded, the ones a hit replaces excepted. `LoadDeferred` walks all texture slots of the material (micro detail included), a texture that is never loaded stays the pink and black fallback.
- A bake must never run on a fallback or unloaded texture: `process` errors on a texture that is not ready and skips a material whose texture `IsFallback` (a missing file), so nothing wrong reaches the cache.
- `Render` clears the micro detail texture for the draw and restores it, so the grain is not baked in and applied twice.
- Callbacks on a texture becoming ready (albedo gain reads `texture.reflectivity`) run inside `MakeReady`, anything they read must be set before it.
- The key must not depend on load order. The vmt loader's albedo ready adjustments (albedo gain, metal gate, derived roughness) are held back in `material.deferred_adjustments` while baking is on: `Request` takes the key first, then a hit drops them (the baked textures contain their effect) and anything else runs them when the albedo is ready.

Blends (`GetLayerKind`): the blend weight is vertex data, so it can never be baked, but each layer can be, once per material. `"multiply"` (the second albedo only modulates the first) bakes as a whole and drops the second albedo. `"blend"` bakes layer one as usual and layer two from a copy of the material with `Albedo2`/`Normal2` swapped into the primary slots, into `Albedo2`, `Normal2` and `MetallicRoughness2`; the shader still mixes the layers by the vertex weight and `MetallicRoughness2Texture` is mixed into roughness, metallic and ao like the layer one texture. Without a second normal the layer one normal keeps applying across the blend, as in the flag path. The deviation from the flag path: roughness and metallic derived from the mixed albedo or normal become the mix of the two layers' values. A blend with `BlendTintByBaseAlpha`, `AlbedoTextureAlphaIsRoughness` (layer one only in the shader), `MultiplyAlbedo2` together with a second normal or blend texture, a `Texture2` transform, or an emissive layer is skipped.

Not baked yet (`CanBake` says why, and `GetStats().skipped` counts them): blends that cannot be baked per layer, terrain and ground colour, additive materials (the additive path of `get_emissive` ignores the emissive texture), the specular masks (`RoughnessMaskOnlyScalesSpecular`, `SpecularFromRoughnessMask`), texture transforms other than identity (the baked textures are the untransformed content, one packed texture cannot follow three transforms), vmt proxies, no-draw materials, and materials with a texture that has no path to key it by. Detail and micro detail stay runtime features, so do the render state flags (alpha test, translucent, double sided), height and parallax, and the lighting feature params. What also stays runtime because it is not a function of the material: `get_antialiased_roughness` (screen space derivatives), `r_normal_map_strength`, weather, conditions and overlays.

Verification: `test/tests/render3d/material_bake.lua` bakes a material that uses normal alpha as roughness, a roughness remap, a metallic multiplier, a colour tint and a normal multiplier, applies the bake to a copy and renders both through the same pass: normals agree within 2, metallic and ao exactly, roughness within 5 and albedo with a mean error under 3 (the rest is bc7). On a gm_flatgrass load, 20 materials were baked in a few seconds and the next load served all 27 from the cache.

Not done: the cry material loader does not call `Request` yet, the vmt loader still sets the properties the bake then drops, height is not baked (it is an input texture, not a derived value), and the baked set has not been compared against the gbuffer output for a real mesh, only through the bake pass itself.
