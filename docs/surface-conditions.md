# Surface conditions

Fire, frost and water (and game kinds such as poison) that cover patches of the static world: a sparse cell field simulated on the CPU, uploaded to the GPU and read by the gbuffer (char, embers, frost, wet film), a particle system (smoke, embers, mist, steam), procedural flame sheets and pooled point lights. Start from `goluwa/render3d/surface_conditions.lua` (field, simulation, upload, gbuffer GLSL, lights), `surface_conditions/*.lua` (the engine kinds), `surface_condition_particles.lua` and `surface_condition_sheets.lua`. The example is `addons/examples/lua/examples/render3d/surface_conditions_demo.lua`. Things that move are not part of the field.

## The field

- Cells are `CELL_SIZE` (0.5 m) voxels created only where a ray found a surface; a cell keeps that surface point and normal, the material's `Flammability` (the `effect_response` feature; Source materials get it from their surfaceprop family) and whether it is thin fuel (alpha tested or bending vegetation). Two surfaces facing away from each other in one cell make it omnidirectional.
- Every cell has every kind's channels (named floats, at most 6 in total, uploaded as unorm8) plus CPU-only state the kinds add in `Init`. Kinds read and write each other's fields: interactions (fire melts frost into water, water quenches fire, heat burns the game's poison off) live in the kind that reacts.
- `surface_conditions.Apply(kind, position, radius, amount, normal)` finds surfaces with a grid of rays along `-normal` plus a small fan, and fills the tangent plane between hits. `Query(position, radius, channel)` is the gameplay read (max of a channel around a point). `Clear()` drops everything.
- The simulation ticks at `TICK` over the active cells only. `Reach(cell, direction)` returns the neighbouring cell along the surface, discovering it with up to three rays (along the tangent, down onto a continuing surface, back around a convex edge) and remembering misses. Rays are limited by time per tick (`RAY_TIME`), not by count, because CPU raycast cost varies by two orders of magnitude between scenes; a cell that runs out of budget simply tries again next tick.
- The field only exists on static geometry. Rays skip anything with a non static rigid body, and a visual the engine treats as moving (`Visual.Library.IsVisualDynamic`: kinematic or dynamic bodies, armature children, attached transforms) is flagged in its instance rows (`instances.SetDynamic`, rescanned every `DYNAMIC_SCAN_TICKS` ticks by `Step` or on demand with `MarkDynamicVisuals()`): the gbuffer skips the field for flagged instances, so a crate sliding over burning ground is not painted.
- Weather: `surface_weather.GetWetness()` (rain rate or set wetness, also what the gbuffer's rain look uses) and `GetSnowCover()` are the weather's numbers; cells take the larger of the two. `GetWeatherWetness(cell)` returns it for cells open to the sky (`IsExposed`, one cached ray along `surface_weather.precipitation_direction`, the same direction the GPU shelter map looks along, recast when that direction turns). Rain and snow wet burning cells (water kind) and damp ignition (fire kind), so a fire under a roof keeps burning in the rain.

## GPU side

- Cells are packed into 4x4x4 bricks (`uvec3` per cell: two words of channels, one of flags and octahedral normal) in staged `scene/store.lua` tables, found through an open addressing hash of brick coordinates. The Lua and GLSL hash must stay identical. Uploads happen in a `PreRenderPass` listener that runs before render3d's.
- The field lookup warps the position along the surface with noise (`WARP_DISTANCE`) and then blends the 8 surrounding cells, renormalised over the cells that exist (so a surface lying on a cell boundary is not halved) and weighted by normal agreement (so a thin wall burnt on one side stays clean on the other). The warp, plus jittered `Apply` rays and filled cells, is what keeps the cell grid from showing; keep both if the cell size changes.
- The dynamic flag travels in the instance row (`dynamic`, written into `world[2][3]` by the main view cull and read as `in_instance_dynamic` by the multi draw vertex stage, with the object local effect position and normal that overlays use; the `effect_space` and `instance_dynamic` options of the vertex stage). Visuals the store does not draw (cpu drawn entries) never get the flag.
- In the gbuffer: `sample_surface_conditions` (the field), then kinds' `glsl.weather` changes `SurfaceWeather` before snow and rain are applied (heat melts snow and dries the rain film, frost removes the film), then `apply_surface_conditions(surface, weather, conditions)`. It returns how much of an alpha tested surface is consumed, which the gbuffer discards with a dither (burnt leaves and grass disappear; their shadows do not). Wet looks go through `apply_surface_wetness(s, w, wet)` (darkening by porosity plus the rain film, the same function rain uses, so a water patch looks like rain) and other films through `add_surface_film(w, film, film_alpha)`; both keep the strongest of what is already there. Snippets must take noise from `e.P`/`e.N` (object local when the visual carries an overlay), not from `s.world_pos`.
- Surface looks come from a procedural tileable noise texture (`GetNoiseTexture`, triplanar `effect_noise`: two fbm channels, voronoi edge distance and cell id), never from per pixel hashing. Thresholding a channel against noise gives the ragged edges; keep effect boundaries soft or the cell grid shows.
- Sites: each tick the active cells a kind wants visible are written as sites (`position`, `normal`, four kind-defined values) grouped by kind, to a ring of host visible buffers. The particle system spawns from them (scheduled spawning, sites picked by rejection on their value, spawn rate per emitter is `Rate * sum of values` capped at `MaxRate`) and the sheet renderer draws `Count` flame sheets per site.
- Flame sheets are camera facing quads whose axis follows world up (shortened when seen from above), with a soft depth fade against the scene. They are partly opaque OIT layers (moments and accumulate, `OPACITY`) plus a smaller additive share (`GLOW`): purely additive sheets pile up where many overlap and wash out to flat salmon, as layers they settle at the flame's own colour. The flame shape is an envelope times scrolling noise; anything noise adds must still go to zero inside the quad or the quad edges show.

## Adding a kind

A kind is a table: engine kinds are in `goluwa/render3d/surface_conditions/` listed in `surface_conditions.kinds`, game kinds are files in any addon's `lua/surface_conditions/` (poison is `addons/jrpg/lua/surface_conditions/poison.lua`), found when `surface_conditions.lua` loads, which is before addon autorun because the passes are built from the kind list. Fields: `name`, `channels`, `Init(cell)`, `Apply(sim, cell, amount)`, `Step(sim, cell, dt)` (returns whether the cell stays active; call `sim.Touch(cell)` after changing channels and `sim.Activate(neighbor)` for cells it wakes), `Site(cell)` (four values), `particles` (emitter tables, see the existing kinds), `sheet`, `light` + `LightPower(cell)`, and `glsl.common` / `glsl.weather` / `glsl.surface` snippets (`apply_surface_wetness` / `add_surface_film` for wet looks, `consumed` to burn away alpha tested surfaces). Kinds cannot be added after the passes are built.

## Gameplay

Nothing in the game applies the field by itself: `Query` is the gameplay read, and the console has `surface_condition <kind> [radius] [amount]` (applies at the crosshair) and `surface_condition_clear`. The jrpg spells do not touch it; their looks are surface overlays on the victim.

## Verifying

`test/tests/render3d/surface_conditions.lua` covers discovery, spreading against non flammable material, frost/water/fire interactions, rain against a roofed fire, that the GPU field changes the gbuffer albedo and that dynamic visuals are left alone by it. Judge looks with `Shot` at a locked EV in daylight and at night with auto exposure: flames are emissive in absolute units, so they read very differently at the two.
