# Procedural foliage

Trees, grass, rocks, flowers, bushes, ferns, mushrooms, logs, stumps, leaf litter and fallen branches, made in lua from a seed and a config, with textures drawn on the gpu. Nothing needs to be installed. The addon is `addons/foliage/`, its modules are `lua/foliage/*.lua` and `lua/autorun/render_3d/foliage.lua` registers everything as assets at startup (nothing is built until it is loaded).

## How it fits together

- `foliage.lua` is the registry. A generator (`foliage.AddGenerator(name, {category, defaults, build})`) makes one kind of thing from a config, `foliage.Build(name, config)` returns its parts, `{mesh = polygon3d, material = material}`, and builds a config once (the key is the serialized config with the defaults filled in). A config only has the keys that differ from the defaults, an unknown key is an error. A preset (`foliage.AddPreset(category, preset, generator, config)`) is registered as the model asset `models/foliage/<category>/<preset>.lua` (`trees`, `grass`, `rocks`, `plants`), `foliage.RegisterModel(path, generator, config)` adds more.
- **Reproducible**: everything a generator draws comes from `random.lua`, a xorshift stream seeded from `config.seed` with exact 32 bit hashing (a double product of two 32 bit numbers is not exact, `mul32` splits it). A part of an asset takes a named fork (`rng:Fork("leaves")`), so drawing one more number for the trunk does not move the leaves. Rocks use `goluwa/math/noise.lua` (deterministic). The textures use integer hashes in glsl (`hash_u`), not `fract(sin())`, so every gpu draws the same picture. `test/tests/render3d/foliage.lua` checks the same seed and config give the same vertices and pixels and another seed does not.
- **Model assets**: a model asset is a descriptor `{kind = "procedural_model", create_primitives(options)}`, the options are keys over the preset's config (the model component's `ModelOptions`, e.g. `{seed = 7}`). `model_loader.LoadModel` loads any `.lua` model asset through `assets.GetModel` (the default options), so the paths work everywhere a model path does: `Visual:SetModelPath`, the `model` component, a scatter prototype's `model` and its baked billboard (`billboard.Request` loads by path). The model component builds `.lua` paths itself with its options.
- **Materials and textures**: `materials.lua` lists every material with how its textures are drawn (`foliage.AddMaterial(name, {textures = {albedo = {kind, config}, normal = ..., roughness = ..., transmission = ...}, material = {Material.New keys}})`). They are the assets `materials/foliage/<name>.lua` and `textures/foliage/<name>_<channel>.lua`. Every material has an albedo, a normal and a roughness map (`AddMaterial` refuses one without), all drawn from the same `surface_at`: the normal map from its height, the roughness map (`RoughnessTexture`, `r8_unorm`) the perceptual roughness, which the engine scales by `RoughnessMultiplier` and squares. The roughness varies within a texture: bark crevices, lichen and moss are rougher than the plates, leaf blades are waxy and their veins and twigs rough, berries glossy, a fly agaric's cap smooth and its warts rough, the discs of flowers matte, grass tips drier.
- **Textures** (`textures.lua`): a kind is glsl that defines `Surface surface_at(vec2 uv)` (albedo, alpha, height, roughness, transmission), and `Texture:Shade` draws a channel of it: `albedo`, `normal` (from the height), `roughness` or `transmission`. The clear texels of a cutout's roughness map get the kind's `roughness`, so the mips don't take the edges of leaves to 0. Every key of the config is a constant in the shader (`color_*` are srgb and become linear, `style` is a `#define`), so a config is a shader, compiled once and then from the disk cache. Kinds: `bark` (furrowed, flaking plates, birch, palm rings, smooth), `leaves` (twigs of leaves in an atlas, or single leaves, lobes, teeth, veins, berries), `needles`, `frond` (palm and fern), `blade` (grass), `seed_head`, `flower` (heads from above, spikes), `rock`, `mushroom`, `end_grain`. A request's config is merged over the registered one: `assets.GetTexture("textures/foliage/oak_bark_albedo.lua", {config = {seed = 3, width = 256}})`.
- **Cutouts** (leaves, needles, fronds, heads, flowers) are drawn into a canvas premultiplied (`Canvas`, `paint` with a color, coverage, height, roughness and transmission), downloaded and made into a texture by `goluwa/render/cutout_texture.lua`: the colors are dilated into the clear texels and every mip keeps the share of texels over the cutoff of the first, so cards do not thin out in the distance. The billboards bake through the same code.
- **Meshes** (`mesh_builder.lua`) are written into the packed vertex layout and uploaded with `Polygon3D:UploadVertexArray` (kept packed, so the bvh and scatter's proxies can read them), with tangents from `vertex_math.BuildTangents`. A triangle is given counter clockwise from the side its normal faces, the builder turns it to the engine's winding (`cross(b - a, c - a)` points into the surface).
- **Wind**: the parts of a model share `BendHeight` (its height) and the materials have `Bending` and `DetailBending` (`leaves` for trees and bushes, `grass` for grass, flowers and heads). The vertex color is the bending convention of `model_pipeline.lua`: r flutter, g phase, b stiffness. The main bending sways the whole about its root by its height. Its motion (`get_vertex_animation_offset_at_time` in `model_pipeline.lua`) is a lean along the wind plus gusts (sines of unrelated periods) that roll over the land with the wind at 6 m/s, and a swing at the instance's own frequency (1.2 / sqrt(height) hz) along and a little across the wind, so the tip goes in loops. Each instance has a phase from a hash of its position. The clock of all of it is `atmosphere.GetWindTime()` (the elapsed time at `weather.SetWindTimeScale(scale)`, 0 stops the plants where they are), which the material block, scatter's multi draw block and the shadow maps read. Far plants stand still (2026-10-19, `r_wind_still_pixels`, 2): the material block holds `Motion`, how far the wind moves the plant at most in object units (`FillVertexAnimationData`: the sway and the gust of the main bending, twice the amplitude of the detail's flutter, from the formulas of the shader) over the pixels it must span to move at all, the vertex stage multiplies it by the instance's scale and the pixels a meter spans at the instance's distance and blends from the animation to the pose at time 0 (the lean without the sway and the flutter) between 2 and 1 times the threshold. It is the same function in the gbuffer, the translucent passes and the shadow maps (their push constants carry the camera and the pixel scale), so a plant and its shadow stop together, and what is by the camera or tall and strongly bent keeps moving to a larger distance (a 15 m tree in a breeze to about 100 m, grass to 5-10 m). Scatter used to skip the animation past `animation_distance` (60 m), which jumped to the rest pose, that is gone. 0 pixels moves every plant at every distance. `test/tests/render3d/vertex_animation.lua` draws a plate near and far. `addons/examples/lua/examples/render3d/wind_debug.lua` is every part of the bending on flat colored debug trees and grass: turned, scaled, short and tall instances, a scatter patch (conform) and, while q is held, the wind, multipliers on the bending params and the time scale. It used to be smoothed triangle waves whose phase was the distance to the world origin, so every tree moved together and in straight lines. The detail bending (see "Crysis vegetation bending" in `docs/crysis.md` for how it maps onto cryengine's) moves a vertex up and down and downwind by `1 - b` (times `BendDetailBranchAmplitude`, meters) and flutters it along its normal by r (times `BendDetailLeafAmplitude`), the part of the normal that faces the wind pushed downwind as well. Its amplitude is the wind's detail strength (0.28 in a gentle breeze, 1.07 in a hurricane) times `BendDetailFrequency`, doubled up to 0.5 and the square root of twice it above, and its rate is fixed (the detail clock runs faster in a strong wind). The foliage's trees and leaves are at 1, small plants 3 (in the grass mode the strength is multiplied by the height of the vertex), reeds 0.8: a leaf's edge goes about half its amplitude in a gentle breeze and the whole of it in a fresh one. A part whose b is the same everywhere moves rigidly, one whose b falls along it turns about where b is 1, so b is where the pivots are. The trunk (and its limbs, a bush's stems, a palm's trunk) is rigid (b 1), it only sways. A branch is held where its limb leaves the trunk and loses `flex` of its stiffness a meter of horizontal reach out from there (`trees.Grow` measures the reach, `trees.Stiffness`), so a branch going out turns about its attachment while one going up or a willow's strand hanging down moves with its attachment instead of being stretched along itself. A child starts with its parent's stiffness where it grows, a leaf card with its twig's at the bottom edge (no flutter there) and less at the top by its own horizontal extent (`trees.StiffnessAlong`), a frond at its base in the crown, a blade, stem or flower at its root (`grass.FLEX` a meter out), a head with its stem's top. Each big limb has its own phase, which its branches and leaves share so they move together. Small plants have their own amplitudes (`SMALL` in `materials.lua`): more sway (`Bending` 3, the sway is a share of the height), a bob and flutter of centimeters and `BendDetailPhase` 1. The main bending's lean is a curve of the wind's speed in m/s (`MAX_BENDING` 7, `BENDING_SPEED` 22 in `model_pipeline.lua`): the top of a plant moves about 2% of its height in a gentle breeze (4.4 m/s), 4% in a moderate one, 9% in a strong one, 14% in a gale and 21% in a hurricane. The default wind is a gentle breeze, 4 m/s (`addons/game/lua/autorun/render_3d/weather.lua`, it was 0.5, which left everything still), and the debug scene's readout names the beaufort force. Bending each vertex about the root keeping its distance (crysis') was compared with moving it with the axis at its height (a shear) and with turning its offset from the axis with the axis (`MAIN_SCHEME` in `wind_check.lua`): turning halves the stretch of a pine or palm but doubles it for a spreading crown and makes a patch of flowers (one mesh around one origin) stretch 2-3 times as much, the shear is worse everywhere, so it stays crysis'.
- **Light through leaves**: leaf, needle, frond, blade and petal materials have `DiffuseTransmission`, a `TransmissionColor` and `TransmissionScattering`, the tree and bush leaves also a `TransmissionTexture` (the veins and twigs let less through).

## Generators

- `trees.lua`: a weber and penn style branching engine (`trees.BuildTree`): a trunk (or `stems`), an optional `split` into limbs, `levels` of branches spread along their parent by a golden angle or in whorls, the crown shape setting how long the branches off the trunk are at each height, branches as curves that curve, wander, droop and reach up. Leaf cards (`leaves`) are placed on the outer levels, `out`, `hang` (willow, birch) or `flat` with `cross` (a second card at 90 degrees, pine sprays are invisible edge on otherwise), and their normals lean away from the trunk by `bend` (0 to 1): towards the direction from the nearest point of the center lines of the trunk, its limbs or a bush's stems (`state.trunk`, the level 0 branches), so the crown is shaded as the shape of the tree around its trunk (a cone for a pine, a curtain for a willow) instead of card by card. It replaced an ellipsoid around all the cards, whose middle at half the height had the lower branches of a pine face down. A palm's fronds lean away from the top of its trunk (`frond_bend`), a fern's from its root (`bend`). A card's front faces out of the crown (`BuildCards` turns it), and the leaf materials set `BentNormals`: the back of a double sided face mirrors the normal through the face instead of reversing it. Reversed, the back of every card seen from outside pointed its bend at the trunk, about half the visible leaves, which cancelled the bend on average and depended on where the camera was (the shading normals of the cards seen from four sides, averaged along the direction out from the trunk: 0 before, 0.2-0.4 after). Species: `oak`, `pine`, `birch`, `willow`, `dead_tree`, `palm` (its own builder: ringed trunk, folded frond strips, dead fronds, coconuts), and the bushes `bush`, `berry_bush`, `juniper`. `trees.AddFrond` is shared with the ferns.
- `grass.lua`: patches of blades in tufts (`grass_lawn`, `grass_meadow`, `grass_wild`, `grass_dry`, `grass_tussock`, `reeds`), quadratic curves that lean out of their tuft and arch over, stems with two crossed cards of seed heads. A blade picks a tile of the blade atlas for its shade.
- `rocks.lua`: a cube sphere (six grids) pushed by noise, cut by planes (`facets`, a soft minimum) and flattened underneath, `boulder`, `slab`, `crag`, `mossy_boulder`, `pebbles`, `scree` (`count` stones). Its uv is the place of the vertex's direction in the 3x2 atlas of the `rock` texture, whose texels are drawn from their direction on the same faces (`FACES` in both, keep them the same), so there is no seam. The normals are taken from the shape in direction space, the same on both sides of a face edge. A second level of detail at half the grid (`LODLevel` 1) is what scatter puts in the scene bvh.
- `plants.lua`: flowers (`daisies`, `poppies`, `cornflowers`, `buttercups` as cupped heads the texture is laid over from above, so they read from the side; `lupines` as crossed spike cards), `fern`, `mushrooms` (lathe caps, gills, stems), `log`, `stump`, `leaf_litter`, `branches`.
- `scatter_sets.lua`: `foliage_meadow`, `foliage_forest`, `foliage_tropical`, `foliage_rocky`, `foliage_wetland`, the classes and layers of the Crysis presets (`addons/game/lua/autorun/render_3d/scatter_presets.lua`). `scatter_showcase.lua` falls back to `foliage_meadow` without Crysis, `SET=foliage_forest test/harnesses/scatter/run.sh` renders one over the hills.

## Looking at it

- `addons/examples/lua/examples/render3d/foliage_world.lua`: a landscape with all of it, `luajit glw --3d lua "import('addons/examples/lua/examples/render3d/foliage_world.lua')"`. A meadow valley, forest hills to the east, a rocky ridge to the west and a lake with a palm beach and a reedy shore, grown by one scatter set made of the five foliage sets (each on its own terrain layer: grass meadow, dirt forest, rock, sand beach, the wetland's by altitude near the water). You spawn on a mesa whose top is above `SCATTER_CEILING`, so nothing is scattered on it, with every preset in rows (`lua/foliage/gallery.lua`) labelled within 10 m. `foliage_world_view N` jumps to a view (0 lists them and hands the camera back), `foliage_world_labels` toggles the labels. `world.GetHeight(x, z)` is the terrain's height in lua without the glsl's small noise, for placing things. `test/harnesses/foliage/world_seq.lua` takes a png of each view (`ONLY=1,3`, `SETTLE`); right after a jump the terrain can show holes while its tiles swap, settle for longer.
- `addons/examples/lua/examples/render3d/foliage_showcase.lua`: every preset on a flat ground (the same gallery), nothing else.
- `test/harnesses/foliage/shot.sh out.png "path;path" x,y,z pitch,yaw,roll` takes a shot of models on a ground (`SPACING`, `COLUMNS`), `textures.lua` draws the textures of the materials into `tmp/` (`ONLY=bark`), cutouts over a checker and normal maps lit, `bench.lua` times each generator, `wind_check.lua` and `wind_clip.lua` are for the wind (above).
- Building is quick (`bench.lua`): trees 10-20 ms (oak 21k triangles, pine 18k, willow 20k), rocks 15-40 ms (the normals sample the shape three times), the rest a few ms. A texture is a draw and, for a cutout, a download and the mips on the cpu.

## Things that went wrong

- `goluwa/math/noise.lua` clamped the falloff of a simplex corner with `max(0, t^4)`, which is never below 0, so corners with `t < 0` added huge values (Simplex3D gave +-200 instead of +-1): spikes out of the rocks. A corner past its falloff adds nothing now.
- Cutouts painted with `mix(color, leaf, coverage)` over a twig colored background kept that color at the edges, and the dilation spread it into a brown halo. Paint premultiplied and divide by the coverage at the end.
- Pine sprays as flat horizontal cards vanished edge on from the side, the tree looked bare. A second card across each.
- **The wind moved trunks up and down and branches as a whole** (2026-10-16): the stiffness fell with the distance along the path from the root, trunk included, so the top of a 17 m pine's trunk was at 0.2 and slid up and down along itself (it looked like it was scaled), and a branch started at the stiffness of where it was on the trunk, so a high branch moved up and down as a whole, base included. Grass, stems and flowers had the trees' amplitudes (a 0.2 m bob of a 10 cm blade) and the default `BendDetailPhase` of 100, which has the two edges of a blade a centimeter apart flutter apart: they stretched to 3-14 times their length. `test/harnesses/foliage/wind_check.lua` runs a lua copy of the vertex animation over every model in a strong wind and prints how much the wood's edges stretch and how far what hangs on it moves from it: trees 5-7% / 15-25 cm before, the stretch now is the sway's own shear (4-6%, the same with the detail bending off) and 3-5 cm; small plants 250-1300% before, 10-35% now, most of which is the detail bending's vertical bob shearing a leaning blade, the engine's model, where a rotation would move it sideways too. `NO_MAIN`, `NO_DETAIL` and `NO_FLUTTER` turn the parts off. `wind_clip.lua` records frames of a scene in a strong wind for a gif, the per pixel range over the frames shows what moves.
- The normal map of a cutout took the height falling to 0 at the edge of a leaf as a slope, a bevel around every leaf. A clear neighbour counts as the texel's own height now (`surface_normal`), and the height is unpremultiplied.
- The rock's normal map had the large noise of its albedo in it, bumps that the mesh (displaced by another noise) does not have, which flattened the shape under a low sun. Its height is only the detail, the cracks, lichen and moss now.
- The orientation of the normal maps is checked with a dome on a flat quad lit from the side (x is +u, y is +v, the bitangent of `vertex_math.BuildTangents`, no green flip): the flank towards the sun is the bright one on both axes. `test/harnesses/foliage/maps_ab.sh` shoots a close up with the normal maps, without them and without the roughness maps.
- Virtual model assets: `assets.Load` used a virtual asset's `load` instead of the category's, and a model's load has to fill the entry and run the callbacks. The models category now has `loads_virtual`: a virtual model's load returns what a lua model file would (the model or a function of the config), and the category finishes the entry. A model load that returned nothing left the entry loading in the cache forever, it is an error now.
- `Vec3:GetRotated` swapped sine and cosine and crossed the axis with its own projection (always zero), nothing used it. It is a rodrigues rotation now and the generators use it.
