# JRPG mode

A port of the gmod jrpg addon, in the `jrpg` addon (`addons/jrpg/lua/jrpg/` plus its components in `addons/jrpg/lua/components/`), which depends on `game`. `!rpg` toggles it on the local player (`!rpg cheat` pins mana and stamina), `!level <attribute>` spends attribute points (no argument lists them), `!status <name> <amount>` applies a status to yourself.

- Being in rpg mode is having the `rpg` component (mana, stamina, xp, level, attributes; stats persist per player in `data/jrpg/`). `jrpg.SetEnabled(entity, on)` adds it, gives the loadout (sword in the right hand on right mouse, the greatsword, rapier and reaper to switch to, shield in the left raised with left mouse, one `weapon_magic_<type>` per damage type, potions) and puts the old max health, speeds and weapons back when removed. `RPGEnabled`/`RPGDisabled` events turn the battlecam on and off for the local player.
- Everything that is only presentation (jrpg HUD, damage numbers, npc bars, item highlights, damage flashes) checks `actors.IsLocalRPG()`; the plain HUD hides itself while it is on. Gameplay (statuses, projectiles, npc fights) runs regardless.

## Damage

`damage.Deal(victim, info)` -> `ModifyDamage` (jrpg: attack/defense attributes, heal sign, roll dodge, shield block, the type's `OnDamage`) -> `health:TakeDamage` -> `HealthDamaged` (effects, xp bookkeeping, knockback). `info.Type` is a damage type name (`jrpg/damage_types/*.lua`, listed in `damage_types.Names`), `info.Cancel = true` from a listener drops the hit. After the listeners, positive damage to a victim in god mode is dropped too (`damage.IsGod`: the replicated `health.GodMode`, toggled per player with the `god` command, or the stored `sv_godmode` pvar for every player); heals still land and `health:TakeDamage` (the `kill` command) bypasses it. Damage dealt by a status sets `info.Status` so its type's `OnDamage` does not apply the status again.

- A damage type is a table: `Color`, `EndColor`, `Particles` (gpu emitter properties for bursts and projectiles), `Glow`, `Light`, `Flicker`, `Knockback`, `Heals`, `HitSounds`, `Adjectives`/`Names`, `OnDamage(victim, info)` (authority).
- Physical types (`damage_types.Physical`, `slash`, `Physical = true`) are registered like the others but are not in `damage_types.Names`, so they have no spell, and `IsMagic` is false for them (physical attack and shield rules). A sword hit (`slash.Strike` in `lua/jrpg/slash.lua`, shared by the sword and by `weapon_debug`, a model-less, animation-less weapon in the rpg loadout whose clicks cycle down, up, left, right and stab at exact angles, print the cut and normal and draw the cut as a flat arrow in the cut plane for five seconds) deals damage at the cut of the weapon's type (`weapon:GetDamageType("slash")`: `slash` unless the weapon has an `Attribute`, see [weapons.md](weapons.md); an attributed hit is one hit of that type, not an extra one): every hit of a move has a `Cut` direction (right, up, forward of the character, the way the blade travels at the hit), the cut plane is the one that holds that direction and the character's forward, `Normal` is that plane's normal turned to the attacker's side (a thrust uses minus forward) and `Position` is where that plane meets the victim (the victim centre projected onto it, then a sweep from the attacker finds the surface and the hit part). That assisted pick almost always lands on the torso, so a strike takes an optional ninth argument `target` that only refines the part: `{Origin, Direction}` sweeps along an aim ray, `{Part = name}` sweeps from the attacker to that ragdoll part of each victim. When the sweep reaches a part of that victim its point and part replace the assisted ones, otherwise the assisted result stays, so a target can never turn a hit into a miss. Each entry of the returned hits carries its `part`. The cut sparks run along the cut (both ways, see below) tilted toward the surface normal the sweep hit on that part by `SPRAY_TILT` and jittered a few degrees (`SPRAY_JITTER`) per hit, so they follow the blade and still take the shape of the part that was struck; only a strike whose sweep found no surface falls back to the cut plane normal. The sword passes none; `weapon_debug` passes its aim (F toggles aimed and assisted). `effects.Impact(position, normal, type, strength, cut, direction)` takes that cut direction (a Vec3, or nil for spells): with a cut the burst is a tight stream (2 degrees, `SLASH_SPREAD`) along the normal instead of the 55 degree cone and no ring. The sword also fires `effects.HitOverlay(victim, kind)` (a replicated event, `ShowHitOverlay` on the clients) for every victim `slash.Strike` returns: a self expiring surface overlay (`HIT_OVERLAY` in `effects.lua`: 0.35 s hold, 0.5 s fade) on each visual of the victim, one per damage type of the weapon (`effects.HitOverlay(victim, type_name)`: the type's `Overlay` kind, `status_fire`, `status_frozen`, `status_poison`, `status_lightning`, `status_decay` or `magic_generic`, tinted with the type's `Color`; the physical `slash` stays untinted generic). The sword trail's overlay follows its first attribute the same way. The cut sparks (`effects.BurstType` with `cut`) have no gravity, high drag so they stop a short way out, four times the damage type's lifetime and the emitter's `Fizzle`, which scatters them in noise directions in the last part of their life. The cut bursts go both ways along the spray direction from the hit point (each side gets half the particles), fall with `CUT_GRAVITY` and follow a curl noise flow field (`CUT_FLOW`, `CUT_FLOW_SCALE`, the gpu emitter's `Flow` and `FlowScale`). The hit's damage scales the stream (`damage / SLASH_DAMAGE`, 40 is the baseline, clamped to 0.025..2): speed, lifetime and flow scale by the nearest power of two of it (at least 1/8), because every scale is its own burst definition. `effects.Impact` takes the damage as its last argument.
- Status effects (`jrpg/status_effects/*.lua`) live in the `status_effects` component: amounts 0..`Max` decaying by `Decay` per second, `Think` every `Rate` seconds on the authority, `Speed(amount)`, `CanAttack(amount)`, `AnimationSpeed(amount)`, `Particles` (one emitter that hops to a random ragdoll part every frame, so the particles come out of random limbs, or sits at the centre without a ragdoll), `Overlay` (a `surface_overlays` kind from `lua/surface_overlays/status_*.lua`), `Orbit`. They replicate as one string (`fire=45 frozen=10`) that clients smooth. Change them only through `status_effects.Set/Add/Remove` on the authority.
- `PlayerMove` listeners may change `move.move`, `move.wish_speed` and `move.jump_pressed`; jrpg uses that for lunges, frozen, confused, blocking and stamina.
- A weapon or component returning false from `OnCanAttack` on the character blocks attacks (frozen, stunned, rolling, blocking).

## Effects and animation

- `jrpg/effects.lua`: functions called on the authority (`Impact`, `Damage`, `Text`, `Block`, `LevelUp`) are `replicated_event`s (`goluwa/network/replicated_event.lua`): broadcast on a server, run directly in a standalone game. Visuals exist only when the renderer is up (`effects.Visual`); headless tests get the routing alone. The look is compositions of the engine layer in [vfx.md](vfx.md) (`effects.vfx` is the module, only set when visual); weapons guard their visual calls with `effects.Visual`.
- Additive glows go through sprite batches, so a pixel's brightness is tint x texture and alpha is passed squared where the old per object materials squared it (`b.flare:Add(..., alpha * alpha)`). The refractive lens is the exception: its batch is keyed by colour and cannot fade, so its radius scales with alpha.
- Materials are registered for good in the scene material list (`render3d/scene/materials.lua` is append only and DDGI reads every entry), so never create or remove materials per hit: effects share tinted materials and pool the rest. A gpu emitter that is `Visible` holds one of `MAX_EMITTERS` slots even when not `Playing`, so hide emitters that are not needed.
- The dropped weapon highlight (`components/item_highlight.lua`) shades the item itself with the `item_highlight` surface overlay kind (`lua/surface_overlays/item_highlight.lua`, one layer per child visual, added while the weapon is dropped and removed when held), plus ribbons, glow sprites and motes. See [surface-effects.md](surface-effects.md). `lua/examples/overlays_showcase.lua` (run it with `lua`, nothing imports it) shows every status kind and the item highlight on spheres, and drops a few real weapons.
- Status particles are one gpu emitter per (entity, status) under a small budget; status looks are surface overlay layers (one per visual of the entity, intensity set from the status amount with `handle:SetIntensity`, rebuilt when the children list changes); the damage flash and low health pulse still use the forward `effects.AddOverlay` rim/fill material.
- One-shot character animations are replicated pose actions (`character_pose:PlayAction(name, duration)` on the authority, definitions in `lua/animations/actions.lua`, or keyframed with `keyframes.Register`, see [characters.md](characters.md)); the base `character_movement` roll (hold crouch while moving on the ground; the `roll` state replicates its kind) rotates the rendered body and the view (`character_pose`), creatures play `source_pose.AttackSequence` during `attack`.
- Npcs fight with a gun (`weapon_holder`), with moves (`Attacks`, a `strike` or `cast` event, see [navigation-and-npcs.md](navigation-and-npcs.md)).

## Melee and movesets

Every melee weapon (`sword`, the two handed `greatsword`, the fast `rapier`, the `reaper` hook) runs one shared script, `lua/weapons/melee.lua` (`weapon_melee`), parameterized by its script values (`Moveset`, `Damage`, `Range`, `SwingPitch`, `TrailColor`). The weapon files only describe the model (`lua/weapons/blade.lua` builds the tilted blade nodes, markers and the placeholder pole; the gmod model replaces the pole) and the sheath. A moveset (`lua/jrpg/movesets/<name>.lua`, registered with `movesets.Register`) is data:

- `Combo` (the light chain on primary), `Heavy` (secondary, or holding primary for 0.3 s queues it), `Air` (attacking while airborne), `Dash` (attacking while sprinting), `ComboWindow`, `Poses` (named poses local to the set, falling back to `keyframes.Poses`; the shared sword poses are in `movesets/poses.lua`) and `Moves`.
- Context slots (`movesets.Slots`, picked by `weapon_melee:PickMove`): `Crouch` / `CrouchHeavy` (attacking on the ground while holding crouch: the launchers and the grab lifts), `Plunge` (attacking in the air while holding crouch), `AirHeavy` (heavy in the air), `Evade` (back + jump on the ground) and `Vault` (forward + jump with a target within `VAULT_REACH` in front: flip over it and land behind). Jump moves are started from the weapon think and can be queued during a move like light and heavy. A queued input chains to the move's `Next`/`Branch`, otherwise it is picked again for the current context (so a launcher's follow-up in the air picks `Air`).
- `Guard` names a pose: the set registers a keyframed state `<set>_guard` from it. Holding back (S) and pressing secondary raises the guard (it stays while secondary is held, back + jump drops it for an evade); only a weapon alone in the hands has a secondary, so with the shield in the left hand the shield blocks instead. The weapon sets `character_pose` `Blocking` (gameplay: speed, no attacks, stamina) plus its own state, and the shield sets the `block` state (the raised left arm) itself, so the pose never derives an arm from `Blocking`. Both hand `rpg:SetGuard(guard)` a table `{Physical, Magic, Angle, Cost, Parry, Raised}` that `ModifyDamage` reads: the shield blocks everything physical and half the magic, a blade blocks its `Guard` script value of physical damage (half of that for magic) in a narrower cone at `Cost` 1.5 stamina per point. A melee hit within `Parry` seconds (script value) of raising a blade guard, from within `PARRY_RANGE`, is a parry: no damage, `PARRY` and a stagger on the attacker.
- A move: `Duration`, `Stamina`, `Reach`, `Approach`, `Retreat`, `Cancel` (when a queued input chains, never before the last event), `Next` and `Branch` (the light and heavy follow-ups instead of the combo order), `Dodge = {from, to}` (phases during which physical hits are dodged, like a roll), `Hover` (drive the height even without height keys, so an air move holds the attacker where it started instead of falling), `Relative` (motion distances are added to the starting distance, for back steps), `GripHands`, `Keys` and `Events`. A move is one timeline on the clock 0..1 (fractions of `Duration`); everything is plain data (numbers, strings, lists; no `Vec3`, no code), so a file can be written and read back by tools. `movesets.Register` validates it (`movesets.Validate`: unknown fields, unknown poses or event types, a key out of order, a missing required field, each named by its path) and turns the lists into `Vec3` for the game.
- `Keys` are keyframes (see [characters.md](characters.md) and `keyframes.lua`): `{t, "pose", ease = ..., hold = ..., <track> = values}` with the tracks `aim`, `hand`, `torso`, `head`, `pelvis`, `body` (radians), `motion`, `carry` and `grip`; `ease` is a name or `{track = name}` per track. `motion = {orbit_degrees, distance, blend, height}` places the attacker every tick through `character_movement:Drive`; a move with a height key (or `Hover`) is vertical and drives the body centre to its starting height plus `height` (leaps, flips over the target, dives that the ground stops), around an anchor fixed when the move starts: the best target within `Reach` in front (else a point ahead) as it stood then, orbit 0 is where the attacker started and positive goes to its right, blend 0 keeps the starting distance and 1 goes to `distance`, and the last key holds to the end. `Approach`/`Retreat` cap how far closer or further it may go. Moves face the live target (the replicated `character_pose` focus turns the rendered body), so circling and dashing past it stay pointed at it, and hits strike toward it (`slash.Strike` takes the forward).
- `Events` is the list of things that happen at a time, in order: `{At, Type = "hit", Cut, Damage, Range, Swing, Knock, Hang}` (`Swing` is the trail and sound window, `Knock` a velocity added to the victim in the attacker's right/up/forward frame through `character_movement:Push`, or to a pending knockdown with `ragdoll:AddKnock`, negative forward pulls, never closer than `PULL_GAP`; `Hang` is the short apex hold of a launched victim, the window for the follow-up, refreshed by every hit that has one; held victims get neither), and `latch` and `release` for grabs (see [weapons.md](weapons.md)). Only the authority runs events. Put a `hit` at the time of the move's contact pose key: a stagger freezes the attacker where the hit lands (`stagger.FreezeFraction`), so a hit earlier than the key freezes the blade short of the target.
- Each move is registered as the pose action `<moveset>_<move>`; `movesets.GetMove(action)` maps it back for the trail.

## Move editor

The `Move editor` desktop program (`lua/jrpg/move_editor.lua`, `autorun/render_3d/move_editor_program.lua`) is an interactive program like the entity editor: it leaves the desktop and takes the left of the screen (a window with a `FILE` menu bar over a `PropertyEditor`) and the bottom (a `TIMELINE` window with the playback controls over `ui.Timeline`); escape or the close button ends it. It needs the local player: it turns rpg mode on, spawns a citizen in front, puppets the player through `move_preview` (see [weapons.md](weapons.md)) and uses its own `View` aimed so the characters sit in the free part of the screen. Space plays and pauses (unless a text field has focus). The view is the editor's fly camera (`lua/camera.lua` of the `editor` addon): drag with the left mouse button over the free part of the screen to look, `w a s d` move, `e` and `q` go up and down, shift is faster and control slower; the camera dropdown resets it to a preset.

- The property panel shows the move's numbers and, for the selected key or event, its time, hold, ease, pose and the values of each track in it (degrees for angles) as nodes whose `OnChange` edits the data. Its items are rebuilt only when the selection, move or structure changes (`session.SelectId`, bump it with `move_editor.Select`), never per value edit, otherwise a drag in a number field would be cut off; a change made from the timeline updates the row with `UpdateValueForKey`.
- The editor works on a copy of `movesets.Source[name]` (the plain data a moveset was registered with) and every change goes through `movesets.Update(name, data)`, so the game plays the edit at once; a failing validation is shown in the status line and leaves the registered moveset as it was. `FILE > save` writes `movesets.Source` back to `lua/jrpg/movesets/<name>.lua` with `table.to_source` (the formatter reflows it afterwards), `load` picks a moveset and move, `revert to file` runs `movesets.Reload`.
- `lua/jrpg/move_edit.lua` is the editing logic without any ui (`Rows`, `MoveFrame`, `Split`, `AddTrackKey`, `Sample` which gives a track's current values in file units so a new key starts where the animation already is, event helpers); moving a key moves the frame it belongs to with everything authored in it, `Split` gives a track its own frame.

## Stagger, knockdown and hitstop

`lua/jrpg/stagger.lua` grades a single hit (not `Secondary` or `Status`) on a living character by the share of the health the victim had before it, so the same blow reacts harder as the victim weakens. A hit below the stagger threshold only plays the `flinch` of `character_pose` (the same animation at a strength from the damage, no stun and no lunge, see [characters.md](characters.md)); a staggered hit sets `info.Staggered` so it does not flinch as well. At least `max(MinDamage, before * Fraction)` freezes the attacker and the victim for `FreezeFraction` (0.3) of the stagger `Duration` (`character_pose:Freeze`, a render hold: the character's `transform` keeps returning its world matrix from the freeze start (`transform:SetFrozen`, children inherit it, the previous matrix equals it so there is no motion blur, and the render history is reset on release) and its rigs and armatures ignore writes and keep their last computed and skinned bones (`Rig:SetHeld`), so nothing that writes the pose, the transform or a socket can move what is drawn, while movement and physics keep running underneath (an npc's behavior and knockback still wait; `Freeze(duration, true)` also pauses the pose clock, which only the victim asks for so its reaction starts when the freeze ends, while the attacker's clock keeps running so the move and its drawing stay in sync with the `move_runner`, whose own clock never pauses); the drawing snaps to the current state when the freeze ends; a knockdown keeps the 0.08 to 0.16 s hitstop growing with the overkill) and plays `stagger` on the victim (`lua/animations/stagger.lua`: the push direction rides along as `character_pose.ActionDirection` and `Init` blends the four `stagger_back/forward/left/right` direction components by its body-local x and z, so any angle works; keyframed, `Stun`, `Group = "melee"` so it cancels the victim's own swing, `CreatureSequences` for models without a biped) with a knockback lunge after the freeze (the hit's `info.Part` rides along as `character_pose.ActionPart`; a head or arm part swings from its joint along the push with a damped spring and the rest of the body follows at a part-specific share via `inst.body_scale`, other parts lean the whole body), plus the `STAGGER` text and `effects.Stagger`. `effects.ShowStagger` gets the attack direction (`info.Direction`, else the push): the local player's screen shakes randomly when it is the victim, and when it is the attacker the shake runs along that direction in screen space (`screen_shake.Add` with an axis, the direction projected on the camera right and up, the same amplitude, frequency and length as the random one). It also adds a screen wide zoom punch (a `screen_fields` field, see [vfx.md](vfx.md); its zoom, blur and aberration scale with the hitstop) to the local player's screen when it is the victim or the attacker, centred on the victim, or a point ahead of the camera when the victim is the player. At least `max(KnockdownMinDamage, before * KnockdownFraction)` on a victim with a `ragdoll` instead throws it: `ragdoll:Knockdown(velocity)` (see [characters.md](characters.md)) with a speed that grows with the overkill, the same hitstop and `KNOCKDOWN` text. Victims without a ragdoll stagger instead. A hit that kills, dealt by a melee weapon (`info.Inflictor.weapon_melee`, a move's hit or throw), also throws the corpse with the knockdown velocity (`stagger.GetKnockdownVelocity`) on top of the death freeze, and the hit's `Knock` is added to it (a killing slam drives the body down); a held victim is left to the release. A victim held by a grab only gets the hitstop (`stagger.Hold`), never a stagger lunge or a knockdown out of the hands. `weapon_debug` (the old slash debug) also picks the damage (right click: 1, 5, 10, 20, 40) and the damage type (reload: `bullet`, a pistol like hitscan shot of `generic` damage with the hit part, then `slash` and every magic type as strikes), shown as floating text, to test the flinch and stagger thresholds. The npcs `debug_stagger` and `debug_flinch` stand in the `reference` sequence to test hit reactions: the first staggers on every hit (`AlwaysStagger`), the second has so much health that no hit reaches the stagger threshold, so it only flinches. The npc `breen` (`Dummy`) has `RefillDelay`: it dies like anyone else and `health:Revive()` stands it up again after that many seconds, so one npc shows the whole ladder (flinch at full health, stagger, knockdown, death, get up) as it loses health.

## Testing

`luajit glw test entities/jrpg.lua`. In-game harnesses need gm_construct for the source models: load it with `engines.Load("gm_construct")`, wait for `static_world.GetActive()`, then `commands.RunLine("rpg")`. Simulate keys with `input.CallOnTable(input, "Key", key, press)` plus `event.Call("KeyInput", key, press)` and `player_input:SetKeyboardMovement(true)`.

- Weapon categories (label, color) are registered in `lua/weapon_categories.lua` (`Register`, `Get`); a weapon only names its `Category`, and an unregistered name falls back to `unknown`. The game addon registers its own, jrpg adds `melee`, `magic` and `defense` from an autorun. The weapon ring colors its wedges from there and separates categories with a glow line.
- A melee hit deals its damage with `Direction = <the cut direction>` and the `Part` it struck, which the victim's flinch or stagger follows (see [characters.md](characters.md)).

## Weapon rarity and statuses

Ported from the gmod jrpg `wepstats`. `lua/jrpg/weapon_stats.lua` has seven rarities (`common` to `godly`: damage multiplier, bonus range, color, how many statuses it may have) and a status registry: every damage type except generic is a positive elemental status (the weapon's `Attributes`), plus `leech`, `fast`, `decay`, `deflect` (in `Statuses`); weapons never roll penalties. A status has `Chance`, `Names`/`Adjectives` and an optional `Roll(weapon, rarity)` (applied once, like `fast`'s rate), and runtime hooks `OnDamage(weapon, attacker, victim, info)` (the inflictor weapon of a hit) and `OnReceiveDamage(weapon, victim, attacker, info)` (equipped weapons of the victim), called from `ModifyDamage` and skipped for `info.Status`, `info.Secondary` and `info.WeaponStatus` (set on damage the statuses deal themselves, so they do not trigger each other).

`Roll(weapon, rarity, bonus, names)` (nil arguments are random) picks the rarity with `random()^4`, a 25% chance of a bonus step count (each 2% strength, shown as `(+N)`), and statuses: positives with their chance up to the rarity's positive limit (three where the original was unlimited), negatives with `random() / tier < chance` up to the negative limit. The title follows the original: `<rarity> <negative adjectives and the first positive's adjective> <name> of <the other positives' names> (+N)`, e.g. `Epic Faded and Cold Sword of Embers and Quickness (+5)`. Rollable weapons (sword, pistol) are rolled when constructed, including the rpg loadout sword. The item highlight (beams, glow, motes, overlay and HUD label) uses the rarity color and rebuilds when the rarity changes.
