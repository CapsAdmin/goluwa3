# Surface effects

Fire, frost and water (and game kinds such as poison) that cover patches of the world: a sparse cell field simulated on the CPU, uploaded to the GPU and read by the gbuffer (char, embers, frost, wet film), a particle system (smoke, embers, mist, steam), procedural flame sheets and pooled point lights. Start from `goluwa/render3d/surface_effects.lua` (field, simulation, upload, gbuffer GLSL, lights), `surface_effects/*.lua` (the engine kinds), `surface_effect_particles.lua` and `surface_effect_sheets.lua`. The example is `addons/examples/lua/examples/render3d/surface_effects_demo.lua`.

## The field

- Cells are `CELL_SIZE` (0.5 m) voxels created only where a ray found a surface; a cell keeps that surface point and normal, the material's `Flammability` (the `effect_response` feature; Source materials get it from their surfaceprop family) and whether it is thin fuel (alpha tested or bending vegetation). Two surfaces facing away from each other in one cell make it omnidirectional.
- Every cell has every kind's channels (named floats, at most 6 in total, uploaded as unorm8) plus CPU-only state the kinds add in `Init`. Kinds read and write each other's fields: interactions (fire melts frost into water, water quenches fire, heat burns the game's poison off) live in the kind that reacts.
- `surface_effects.Apply(kind, position, radius, amount, normal)` finds surfaces with a grid of rays along `-normal` plus a small fan, and fills the tangent plane between hits. `Query(position, radius, channel)` is the gameplay read (max of a channel around a point). `Clear()` drops everything.
- The simulation ticks at `TICK` over the active cells only. `Reach(cell, direction)` returns the neighbouring cell along the surface, discovering it with up to three rays (along the tangent, down onto a continuing surface, back around a convex edge) and remembering misses. Rays are limited by time per tick (`RAY_TIME`), not by count, because CPU raycast cost varies by two orders of magnitude between scenes; a cell that runs out of budget simply tries again next tick.
- Rays skip anything with a non static rigid body, so moving things never get world cells. A visual the engine treats as moving (`Visual.Library.IsVisualDynamic`: kinematic or dynamic bodies, armature children, attached transforms) is an object instead: one cell for the whole visual, created when an `Apply` sphere reaches its box or when touching world cells or other objects make a kind's `Exchange(sim, cell, around, dt)` react (`around` holds the max of each channel nearby). The kinds step it like any cell; its position is the bottom of its box and `Reach` starts from one of its surface points (random points on its local box faces, the top and the sides), so a burning crate sets the floor alight.
- Objects are tracked once per frame in `PreRenderPass` (after every Update listener, so scripted motion is never a frame behind): position, velocity and a short history of positions. Their sites are the surface points plus `OBJECT_TRAIL` sites along that history, faded by age and speed, which is what draws a flame trail behind a fast object. Sites carry the velocity: flame sheets lean back and stretch with it, particles inherit `Inherit` of it and spawn spread along the last frame's path. A light whose cluster is an object follows it every frame.
- Weather: `surface_weather.GetWetness()` (rain rate or set wetness, also what the gbuffer's rain look uses) and `GetSnowCover()` are the weather's numbers; cells take the larger of the two. `GetWeatherWetness(cell)` returns it for cells open to the sky (`IsExposed`, one cached ray along `surface_weather.precipitation_direction`, the same direction the GPU shelter map looks along, recast when that direction turns). Rain and snow wet burning cells (water kind) and damp ignition (fire kind), so a fire under a roof keeps burning in the rain.

## GPU side

- Cells are packed into 4x4x4 bricks (`uvec3` per cell: two words of channels, one of flags and octahedral normal) in staged `scene/store.lua` tables, found through an open addressing hash of brick coordinates. The Lua and GLSL hash must stay identical. Uploads happen in a `PreRenderPass` listener that runs before render3d's.
- The field lookup warps the position along the surface with noise (`WARP_DISTANCE`) and then blends the 8 surrounding cells, renormalised over the cells that exist (so a surface lying on a cell boundary is not halved) and weighted by normal agreement (so a thin wall burnt on one side stays clean on the other). The warp, plus jittered `Apply` rays and filled cells, is what keeps the cell grid from showing; keep both if the cell size changes.
- Object values travel without a lookup: up to 6 channels at 7 bits, three per float with the exponent of 1.0 as a marker, in the instance row's `effects` (`instances.SetEffects`), which the main view cull writes into `world[2][3]` and `prev_world[2][3]` (only `.xyz` of those products is used). The multi draw vertex stage (`instance_effects` option) decodes them and also passes object local position and normal, so object noise sticks to the object while it moves. Visuals the store does not draw (cpu drawn entries) show no object effects.
- In the gbuffer: `sample_surface_effects` (field and object values), then kinds' `glsl.weather` changes `SurfaceWeather` before snow and rain are applied (heat melts snow and dries the rain film, frost removes the film), then `apply_surface_effects(surface, weather, effects)`. It returns how much of an alpha tested surface is consumed, which the gbuffer discards with a dither (burnt leaves and grass disappear; their shadows do not). Wet looks go through `apply_surface_wetness(s, w, wet)` (darkening by porosity plus the rain film, the same function rain uses, so a water patch looks like rain) and other films through `add_surface_film(w, film, film_alpha)`; both keep the strongest of what is already there. Snippets must take noise from `e.P`/`e.N` (object local for objects), not from `s.world_pos`.
- Surface looks come from a procedural tileable noise texture (`GetNoiseTexture`, triplanar `effect_noise`: two fbm channels, voronoi edge distance and cell id), never from per pixel hashing. Thresholding a channel against noise gives the ragged edges; keep effect boundaries soft or the cell grid shows.
- Sites: each tick the active cells a kind wants visible are written as sites (`position`, `normal`, four kind-defined values) grouped by kind, to a ring of host visible buffers. The particle system spawns from them (scheduled spawning, sites picked by rejection on their value, spawn rate per emitter is `Rate * sum of values` capped at `MaxRate`) and the sheet renderer draws `Count` flame sheets per site.
- Flame sheets are camera facing quads whose axis follows world up (shortened when seen from above), with a soft depth fade against the scene. They are partly opaque OIT layers (moments and accumulate, `OPACITY`) plus a smaller additive share (`GLOW`): purely additive sheets pile up where many overlap and wash out to flat salmon, as layers they settle at the flame's own colour. The flame shape is an envelope times scrolling noise; anything noise adds must still go to zero inside the quad or the quad edges show.

## Adding a kind

A kind is a table: engine kinds are in `goluwa/render3d/surface_effects/` listed in `surface_effects.kinds`, game kinds are files in any addon's `lua/surface_effects/` (poison is `addons/jrpg/lua/surface_effects/poison.lua`), found when `surface_effects.lua` loads, which is before addon autorun because the passes are built from the kind list. Fields: `name`, `channels`, `Init(cell)`, `Apply(sim, cell, amount)`, `Step(sim, cell, dt)` (returns whether the cell stays active; call `sim.Touch(cell)` after changing channels and `sim.Activate(neighbor)` for cells it wakes; `cell.object` is set for object cells), optional `Exchange(sim, cell, around, dt)`, `Site(cell)` (four values), `particles` (emitter tables, see the existing kinds), `sheet`, `light` + `LightPower(cell)`, and `glsl.common` / `glsl.weather` / `glsl.surface` snippets (`apply_surface_wetness` / `add_surface_film` for wet looks, `consumed` to burn away alpha tested surfaces). Kinds cannot be added after the passes are built.

## Gameplay

`addons/jrpg/lua/jrpg/surface_hazards.lua`: damage types with a `Surface` entry apply that kind on impact through a replicated event, and on the authority actors standing on heat, frost or poison get the matching status (burning actors ignite the ground). Hazards read the world field only, objects burning do not hurt actors directly (they ignite the floor). The field only exists where the renderer runs, so on a dedicated server there are no hazards. Console: `surface_effect <kind> [radius] [amount]` applies at the crosshair, `surface_effect_clear`.

## Verifying

`test/tests/render3d/surface_effects.lua` covers discovery, spreading against non flammable material, frost/water/fire interactions, rain against a roofed fire, that the GPU field changes the gbuffer albedo and that a dynamic object burns and darkens through its instance rows. Judge looks with `Shot` at a locked EV in daylight and at night with auto exposure: flames are emissive in absolute units, so they read very differently at the two.

## Surface overlays

Temporary, animated looks on one visual (magic: crystal, void, gilding, shields, runes, storms), with no simulation. `goluwa/render3d/surface_overlays.lua`, the engine has no kinds of its own: they are files in any addon's `lua/surface_overlays/` (found when the module loads, like game surface effect kinds; the nine example kinds are in `addons/examples/lua/surface_overlays/`). The example is `addons/examples/lua/examples/render3d/surface_overlays_demo.lua`.

- `surface_overlays.Add(visual, kind, {Duration, FadeIn, FadeOut, Intensity, Scale, Speed, ColorA, ColorB, Params, Delay})` returns a handle; `handle:Remove(fade_out)` fades it out, `handle:SetIntensity(v)` changes a live layer (a kind can read `l.intensity` directly, for example as a progress), `Duration` 0 lasts until removed, `surface_overlays.Clear(visual)` drops all. A visual holds `MAX_LAYERS` layers (the oldest is replaced) and at most `MAX_SLOTS` visuals have overlays at once.
- Animation is evaluated on the GPU from the layer's start time and the block's `overlay_time`, so an overlay costs nothing per frame on the CPU; add, remove and expiry only write one slot row. The CPU refreshes a slot's world bounds when the visual moves (sweeps use them).
- The carrier is the instance row's `overlay` float (`instances.SetOverlay`, the `world[1][3]` slot of the draw matrices, `in_instance_overlay` in the gbuffer); it indexes a staged table of slots. Visuals the store does not draw show no overlays.
- `apply_surface_overlays` runs in the gbuffer after the surface effects and before the features' `weather` stage, so overlays may change albedo, roughness, metallic, normal, ao and emissive and ask for a clearcoat film with `add_surface_film`. Noise is object local (`c.P`), so it sticks to moving visuals. The translucent pass applies them too (`in_instance_overlay` plus the effect position and normal come from the multi draw vertex stage): they change the pane's albedo, metallic, roughness, normal and emissive, and a kind's coverage (`overlay_cover(s, amount)`, written to `s.alpha`) raises the pane's alpha and lowers its refraction amount, so a frosted or darkened glass stops showing what is behind it. The moments pass does not run overlays, so a covered pane still counts as its plain alpha when other panes are accumulated. Reflection rays and DDGI hits do not see overlays.
- A kind is `name`, `defaults` (every config key) and `glsl`, the body of a function with `s`, `w`, `l` (the layer), `c` (context: `P`, `N`, `V`, `height` 0-1 over the bounds, `ndv`), `age`, `t` (age times speed), `grow` (0-1 over `FadeIn`), `outro` (1-0 over `FadeOut`) and `fade` (both envelopes times intensity). Reveals (sweeps, spreading) use `grow` and `outro`, pulses use `t`. Helpers: `overlay_noise(c, scale)` (r, g fbm, b voronoi edge distance, a cell id), `overlay_cover`, `overlay_hash`, `overlay_hue`, `overlay_reveal`, `overlay_set_roughness`, `overlay_tilt`. Emissive added by a kind is scaled by `surface_overlays.EMISSION` (emissive is in absolute units, so a glow of 1 is invisible).
- Tests that need the overlay to age override `surface_overlays.GetTime`: the test frame clock does not advance (`test/tests/render3d/surface_overlays.lua`).
