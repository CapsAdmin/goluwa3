# Render3D lighting, GI, fog, sky, water and exposure

Physically based, absolute-unit lighting: scene radiance is in cd/m2 (daylight is thousands, the sun disc around 1e9), exposure is applied at the end. When something looks off, isolate which term is wrong (one light, locked exposure, other terms off) instead of tuning by eye.

Map:

- Passes are in `goluwa/render3d/passes/`, each with an `r_pass_<name>` pvar. Shared lighting GLSL lives in `surface_lighting.lua`, `ibl.lua`, `atmosphere.lua`, `gi.lua`, `radiance_cache.lua`, `radiance.lua`, `scene_hits.lua`.
- `r_quality` (`goluwa/render3d/quality.lua`) gates passes and scales grids, step counts and shadow map sizes; consumers read `quality.Get()`.
- Ray traced consumers read the scene store ([gpu-scene.md](gpu-scene.md)). On hardware without ray queries they compile against stubs; test that path with `GOLUWA_NO_RAY_TRACING=1` (no GI passes, GI falls back to sky irradiance, no traced shadows or reflections, screen space paths remain).
- Pvars that shaders are built from must be set through `GOLUWA_PVARS` in scratch scripts. Scratch scripts go in `./tmp/`.

## Exposure and units

- **Scene color is pre-exposed.** The fp16 scene targets hold absolute radiance times `post_source.get_pre_exposure()` (last frame's exposure over a headroom), because fp16 cannot hold both a starlit night and the sun. Lighting math stays absolute: multiply when writing scene color, divide when reading it, and rescale histories by the ratio of current to previous pre-exposure. Probe captures are absolute. New passes that touch scene color use `post_source.pre_exposure_block` and `GetPreExposureGLSL`. Firefly clamps belong in exposed units (`post_source.GetExposureTexture(true)`), not absolute ones.
- Any pass that keeps history in pre-exposed units must rescale it when the pre-exposure changes, or the first frames of an unadapted exposure leave huge values behind.
- **Exposure** is one EV100 with two front ends, both in `goluwa/render3d/exposure.lua` (plain state, `exposure.Get/Set`, no pvars; the Look desktop program edits it):
  - Camera (`camera.*`): a centre weighted meter normalised to a middle grey lightness, clamped to an EV range, with a program line (`camera.mode`) that derives ISO, aperture and shutter. Those only drive effects (motion blur length, grain `r_grain`, depth of field `r_dof`); shaders read them through `exposure.GetCameraSettingsGLSL`.
  - Eye (`eye.*`): no normalisation; exposure follows the wide-field log-average adapting luminance along a cone/rod threshold model with mesopic mixing and rod desaturation, plus a viewer adaptation term that dims the display in dim scenes. The eye has no artistic layer.
  - A view's `ExposureLock` (EV100, `glw shot --ev`) fixes exposure in either mode; `ExposureHold` freezes auto exposure while the view is active.
- **Glare angles use `eye.display_fov`** (the angle the screen takes up at the viewer's eye, `exposure.GetPixelsPerDegree`), never the game camera's field of view: the viewer's eye does not zoom with the picture.
- **Bloom and glare are optics**, built from the scene before depth of field and tone mapping (`passes/bloom.lua`, the CIE glare function in `eye_glare.lua`). Under TAA bloom reads its own linear resolve (`post_source.GetBloomSceneTexture`), never the displayed TAA output: that one blends in compressed space, so anything far above white decompresses to a meaningless brightness.
- Eye-only effects, each behind a pvar so it can be isolated: `r_eye_starburst` (`passes/starburst.lua`, pupil diffraction from `eye_psf.lua`), `r_eye_afterimage` (`passes/afterimage.lua`), `r_eye_chromatic_halo`, and in `passes/blit.lua` `r_eye_chromatic_defocus` and `r_eye_noise`.
- **Motion blur** (`passes/motion_blur.lua`) runs after TAA from the velocity buffer, with tile max / neighbour max tiles. `r_motion_blur_trailing` gathers only behind the motion; test velocity sign changes with it on, since a symmetric kernel hides a wrong sign.

## Display, grading and HDR

- Tone mapping is `r_tonemapper` in `passes/blit.lua`. `neutral` leaves everything below about 0.76 of exposed white unchanged; SDR and HDR are the same curve with a different peak. `agx`, `aces`, `gt7` are looks.
- HDR (`--hdr`, `r_hdr_peak`, `r_hdr_paper_white`, `r_hdr_ui_white`): the window target is an fp16 layer of sRGB encoded values where 1.0 is UI white, so UI blends exactly as in SDR; `goluwa/render/hdr_output.lua` decodes to the swapchain at the end. Never blend UI in linear space: alpha and text antialiasing come out thinner and lighter.
- `goluwa/render3d/grade.lua`: presets are whole tables of `r_grade_*` pvars (`grade.SetPreset(name, seconds)` blends). The tone curve (`r_grade_curve` and per channel) runs before the other controls and covers the HDR range; the other controls only grade up to paper white, so HDR shaping belongs in the curve. At defaults the grade is exactly the tonemapper. The blit's data block goes through push constants (256 byte limit), so new grade fields must stay small.
- Local adaptation (`local_adaptation.lua`, `passes/local_adaptation.lua`, `r_local_*`, off at the defaults) runs on the exposed scene before the tonemapper in either exposure mode: a bilateral grid over log luminance (binned by brightness, so regions separate at edges without halos) holds each region's mean level and cone chroma; shadows and highlights pull a region's mean toward middle grey, detail scales the rest, and white balance divides out part of the region's cast in a white normalised Bradford space, keeping luminance.
- LUTs (`r_grade_lut`, read by `goluwa/codecs/lut.lua`; `grade.FindLuts()` lists `luts/` folders of any addon) are applied to the tonemapped image, so they must be display referred. Camera log LUTs need `r_tonemapper slog3`, `logc` or `cineon`, which are log encoders whose output is the LUT's input. `test/tests/render3d/grade.lua` checks the encoders and gamut matrices against published primaries.
- `lut_bake.Bake` bakes a display transform into a `.cube` that reads like a camera table: its input is the scene in a log encoding (`slog3`, `logc`, `cineon`), its output sRGB encoded display. The `bake_transform` glsl maps the exposed scene (18% grey is 0.18) to display linear and may call the compute color helpers, which are part of the cache key.
- `r_test_chart` replaces the scene with exact scene linear reference patches (`test_chart.lua`, `test_chart.GetPatches()` for patch positions and values). Judge tonemappers, encoders and LUTs on it before on a scene.
- Scopes: `grade.GetHistogram()` and `scopes.lua` (waveform of the displayed image) only run while someone asks for them.

## Camera lens

`goluwa/render3d/lens.lua` (model, settings, presets) and `passes/lens_flare.lua` put a real lens in front of the camera mode; the eye never uses it. Settings are plain state (`lens.Get/Set`, `lens.fields`), presets are tables of them (`lens.SetPreset`, console `lens <name>`, the Look program's Lens tab). `lens.design` none is an ideal lens and costs nothing.

- Designs are prescriptions (radius, thickness, index, Abbe number, clear diameter per surface, a stop). Everything is derived from the glass: paraxial ray transfer matrices give ghosts, real chief rays give distortion, lateral colour and falloff, a traced grid gives optical vignetting, coatings give ghost colour. Check a new design with `test/tests/render3d/lens.lua` (its paraxial focal length must match the nominal one); a new design must be achromatic, check `focus_shift`.
- The game renders rectilinear; everything before the final warp (sources, ghosts, sunstar, bloom, depth of field) works in that frame and the warp bends it into the lens's real mapping. The camera's field of view maps to a focal length through `lens.GetFocalLengthForFOV`.
- Ghosts come from the brightest lights on screen; the largest are traced through the real glass, the rest are paraxial aperture images. The sensor counts as a reflecting surface.
- With a lens, bloom weights come from the lens's own point spread (aperture diffraction, dirt, smear), energy conserving, and the bloom threshold and floor are off.
- Point spread tables are built on the GPU and must match the CPU `eye_psf` path; compare against it after changing them. `lens.GetBuilt` keeps optics and aperture apart so aperture edits are cheap.

## Choosing the GI

There is one screen GI, the `radiance` pass (per pixel ReSTIR final gather over a world cache), and one world space cache, the `radiance_cache` pass (DDGI probe cascades). `r_pass_radiance` turns the screen GI off (lighting falls back to sky irradiance); `r_radiance_cache` (`probes`, `none`) picks whether probes are built and rebuilds the pipeline bundles. Bundles with an explicit pass list (tests, previews) get whatever they list, so ask `gi.GetMode()` and `gi.IsPerPixel()`, not the pvar.

- Consumers go through `goluwa/render3d/gi.lua`, never a system directly: screen textures (`gi.GetScreenTexture`, `gi.GetScreenSpecularTexture`), the `radiance_cache_data` uniform block (`gi.GetProbeBlockLayout`, `gi.WriteProbeBlock`; it also carries sun, sky, emitter and cloud shadow data so every ray hit shader can read them), and the world lookups from `gi.GetCommonGLSL` / `gi.GetMediumGLSL` (`gi_sample_irradiance`, `gi_sample_medium`, `gi_covers`, ...). They read bindless textures only.
- `gi_covers` decides whether a lookup that found nothing means darkness or "unknown, use the sky fallback".
- **Irradiance is stored and returned as E/pi** in every system; shading multiplies it by a reflectance (`EON_directional_albedo`), never by albedo/pi. A new integrator that returns E is pi times too bright and makes multi-bounce feedback run away.

### Emitters

`goluwa/render3d/scene/emitters.lua` is shared by the probes, ReSTIR and the fog. Emissive surfaces reach GI only through emitter samples (rays alone give fireflies on small emitters). The emissive triangles form a light tree rebuilt on the GPU (`emitters.Build`: leaves and levels refit every frame, order rebuilt when the list changes); consumers draw candidates from it and `emitter_light` turns a pick into radiance over its pdf.

- `emitter_random` is a low discrepancy sequence over frames: pass a stable index (slot, cell) and the frame, never a hash that includes the frame.
- While emitters are sampled, rays must not also add the emission of what they hit, or it counts twice (`SCENE_HIT_EMISSION`). Reflections keep it.
- Emitter power and the pick's light use the per triangle emission, not the material's average, so masked emitters (a dark panel with a few lit strips) are weighed correctly.
- The tree cannot see occlusion; where most nearby emitters are hidden the noise is visibility noise.
- Any sampler change is tested on brightness, not only noise: compare long means against a plain power-only pick, which is unbiased. Check total emitter power before tuning samplers when emitter noise appears. `gi_emitters` turns sampling off; additive materials only emit with `gi_additive_emitters`.

## Radiance cache (DDGI probes)

`goluwa/render3d/radiance_cache.lua` (GLSL and lookups) and `passes/radiance_cache.lua` (trace, shade, relocation, integrate, guide). It is ReSTIR's world cache and the source of the fog, particle, water and glass ambient; it does not light the screen itself.

- **Cascades**: `radiance_cache.CASCADES` grids of the same size, spacing doubling from `radiance_cache_probe_spacing`, fitted to the scene. Each cascade updates on its own interval (`radiance_cache_update_interval_*`) and early when the sun moves. Only the sun is tracked; other changes wait for the interval or `radiance_cache.ResetHistory()`. Cost is probes updated per frame times rays plus emitter samples.
- **Rays** are partly guided by each probe's own octahedral radiance guide; every pass recomputes ray directions from the same guide, which is only swapped at the end of the frame, so trace, shade and integrate agree (any guide is unbiased if they agree). `radiance_cache_ray_clamp` caps a ray's weighted contribution, not its raw radiance, or it undoes the guiding.
- **Moving instances stay out of the probes**: probe rays, their shadow rays and every visibility ray use `RADIANCE_CACHE_SCENE_MASK` / `RADIANCE_CACHE_VISIBILITY_MASK` (`instances.MASK_GI`, `MASK_GI_SOLID` in `scene/instances.lua`), never `0xFF` or `MASK_SOLID`; a new trace against the probe scene must too. `scene/bridge.lua` clears the GI bits of character gear and of dynamic visuals while they move, so moving things are lit by probes but do not write into them and leave no trail.
- **Lookups** (`radiance_cache_sample_cascade`) use a Chebyshev test plus visibility ray queries to probes that would contribute much (by weight or by share of light). A pass must bind the TLAS to get them; distant consumers like fog need them or sealed buildings leak outdoor probes. If a leak persists while visibility rays seem to do nothing, check that they are issued at all.
- **Relocation**: probes in gaps narrower than twice the relocation distance or inside thin sheets stay disabled; probes resting on a surface move to its open side. Inspect with `radiance_cache_debug_probes`.
- A coarse cascade only keeps the shadow of occluders taller than its spacing. Planes without thickness cannot be traced reliably from a surface.
- Octahedral atlas borders: edge texels mirror the adjacent interior row/column, corners take the diagonally opposite interior corner. A wrong border shows as seams wherever a normal's x or y crosses zero.
- `radiance_cache_strength` scales what consumers read, not the probes' own bounces.
- **Art controls** (`indirect_look.lua`, the `gi_*` pvars, neutral at 1): `gi_bounce_*` change the albedo that GI rays see (probe and screen hits, not reflections), so they compound per bounce and leave sky light alone; `gi_sky_gain` scales what missing rays bring back; `gi_gain`/`gi_contrast`/`gi_saturation` curve the diffuse irradiance on opaque surfaces in the lighting pass, with the pivot in exposed units. Compare against the path tracer only at the neutral values.
- Verify with `test/tests/render3d/radiance_cache.lua`. For noise, score the rms of many frames against their mean and print the mean luminance too (flicker that comes with a darker result is bias); compare runs at the same warm up, since multi-bounce settles slowly.

### Screen space AO

`passes/ambient_occlusion.lua`: half resolution, bit mask occlusion over cosine distributed directions giving occlusion, bent normal and a short bounce, then temporal and a bilateral blur. Tunables are `ambient_occlusion_*`; `ambient_occlusion_debug` shows a single output. The temporal pass gates history by distance from the raw neighbourhood mean in units of its deviation, to keep moving occluders from trailing. Measure noise against a long converged still render, not the previous frame, and trailing with an object moving past a wall.

The AO weighs below-horizon directions down because the probes already include the ground; the bent normal of an unoccluded surface must equal its normal. Under ReSTIR the lighting skips the AO and its bounce for diffuse (the rays hold near field occlusion); specular occlusion still uses it. Its bounce gathers last frame's lit occluders, so it is already coloured multi-bounce; a fitted multi-bounce AO (Jimenez) on top would count the interreflection twice.

## Global illumination (ReSTIR)

`goluwa/render3d/radiance.lua` (pvars) and `passes/radiance.lua` (passes), after ReSTIR GI (Ouyang et al. 2021) with the generalized RIS weights of Lin et al. 2022. It produces the screen GI and the reflections; world lookups still go to the cache picked by `r_radiance_cache`. Everything runs at `radiance_resolution` (`auto` follows `r_quality`).

- **Passes**: `radiance_pick` chooses the screen pixel each GI pixel stands for; `radiance_trace` traces one diffuse ray (cosine or probe guided) and the specular ray for pixels whose turn it is (`radiance_rays`), keeps each ray as a record (origin, direction and distance, flags with the instance, primitive, barycentrics, the pdf or lobe roughness; the specular origin is a half precision offset from the diffuse one) and appends its hits to a list; `radiance_hit_shade` shades that list (an indirect dispatch, so misses and pixels without a surface take no lanes and the shading kernel stays small) and writes the light into the candidates' outputs, which read it at their own pixel before overwriting it; `radiance_candidates` reads the records and the shaded light, adds the emitter sample and writes the reservoirs; `radiance_temporal` resamples against last frame's reservoir; `radiance_spatial` combines with neighbours, writes irradiance and resamples next frame's history; `radiance_accumulate` and the a-trous `radiance_filter*` passes denoise, stopping taps by plane, normal and luminance deviation.
- **Samples are points**: a reservoir holds a hit point, its normal and outgoing radiance (or a sky direction), its contribution weight `W` and confidence `M`; reuse reconnects with the solid angle Jacobian. Emitter samples hold emission only and ray samples reflected light only, so anything that retraces a sample must keep them apart. Emitter samples are stored as triangle and barycentrics so moving emitters stay correct.
- **Unbiased reuse rules**:
  - Every reuse step weighs samples against the same target function; mixed targets darken creases.
  - Use balance heuristic / pairwise MIS weights with `M` as confidence, not weighting by `M` alone.
  - Never clamp bright samples against history and never drop or rewrite reservoirs by a test that correlates with their brightness (re-shading, dropping outliers, retracing from a shifted origin): the estimator is heavy tailed and most light in indirectly lit rooms arrives in rare bright samples, so these darken the result.
  - A pixel's history is capped by how far it moved, or one sample is dragged into a streak. Confidence is capped at `radiance_max_history` when read, or chained samples snowball.
  - Visibility for spatial neighbours is tested both ways; samples found through gaps otherwise leak weight.
- **Spatiotemporal chaining**: the spatial pass's resampled reservoir is next frame's history, which spreads rare finds (light through a doorway) across a region. Check against a converged reference, not frame to frame change.
- **Hits read the screen**: a hit on something the camera saw last frame takes its further bounces from last frame's raw spatial output, off screen hits read the cache. Never feed back the denoised output: every bounce then waits on its history. Very near hits (crease walls) read the accumulated estimate.
- **Resolution**: below full resolution each GI pixel stands for the exact centre of its block in the unjittered image, its history is reprojected without jitter and the lighting upsamples with geometric weights (`radiance_cache.GetScreenSampleGLSL`): a reduced resolution buffer that followed the camera jitter would show jitter at its own resolution, which TAA cannot resolve. `radiance_rays` below every pixel needs temporal reuse.
- **Light changes**: a sun or moon change caps history for a while (`radiance_light_change`). Local changes are found at the source (`radiance.GetChangedLights`) and only shorten history for pixels that see that light; per pixel change detectors fire on heavy tailed noise and must never read a reservoir's own noisy estimate.
- Ray origins are lifted off the planes of neighbouring pixels; GI rays skip decals (`instances.MASK_SURFACES`) since decal meshes float above what they cover.
- The world cache lookup at off screen hits is a large part of the hit shading cost; `radiance_hit_gi_share` trades it per thread group. Never thin the probe visibility rays themselves.
- **Verifying**: `radiance_debug` shows irradiance. A reference is `radiance_temporal`, `radiance_spatial` and `radiance_filter` off with a huge `radiance_accumulation` and a still camera. Compare means of the accumulated texture over hundreds of frames, never one frame. Count fireflies with `FrameRecorder` (`Clip:Stats`) with TAA off; measure noise both settled and right after a camera cut, and `r_aa fxaa` to see GI without jitter. Look at creases with a dark exposure and stretched contrast. The raw estimate is fp32 and unclamped: do not put a clamp in the path when comparing emitter samplers. Tests: `test/tests/render3d/radiance.lua`.

## Shadows

- **No fade of direct light by N·L near grazing**: flat ground at sunset has N·L below 0.1 everywhere. The terminator on smooth shaded low poly meshes is handled at its source: where the shading normal faces the light but the facet does not, shadow lookups and rays start from the inner side and skip the body's own far side (`leave_body`). Check with a sphere at a low sun and `r_ray_shadows` on and off.
- Direct diffuse takes the light the specular lobe leaves (`GGXDiffuseCoupling`, roughness aware), the same term as the view side.
- **Sun shadow maps**: `weather.BuildSunShadows`, cascades following the frustum, the first one an `exact` cascade redrawn every frame (vegetation only animates there). Change settings with `weather.SetSunShadow(category, {...})` (`SUN_SHADOW_DEFAULTS` in `weather.lua`); the Sun dial window's Shadows tab shows reach and texel size. Cost over foliage is fill (texel count). A cascade that updates every N frames shows its cost as a spike in `gpu_timing`.
- Shadow map blind spots that no bias fixes (occluders within a few texels, single sided walls, the soft filter averaging near and far blockers) are covered by a contact ray toward the sun from lit points. `single_sided_room` in `addons/examples/lua/examples/render3d/gi_reflections.lua` reproduces those cases without game assets. Prefer physically derived filtering (PCSS with the sun's real angular size) over fixed filters.
- `r_ray_shadows` shadows every light in the lighting and translucent passes with rays (sun disc, light `SourceRadius` spheres) instead of maps; the maps are still built for fog, GI and particles. The test is `ray_shadow_visible` (`ray_materials.GetShadowRayGLSL`), shared with the path tracer, so a difference between them is sampling, not geometry. It is compiled in, so the pvar reloads passes (`render3d.ReloadPasses`). With `r_ray_shadow_denoise` the `ray_shadows` passes trace one visibility per pixel per group (directional, local), accumulate and filter it. A pixel without a group's light stores placeholder visibility 1, so every average must weigh visibility by the group's presence. Use blue noise with a per frame R2 step: IGN with a frame offset leaves a drifting moire.
- Point and spot lights without a shadow map use their light occlusion map (`light_occlusion.lua`, octahedral distance moments, PCSS style for lights with a `SourceRadius`). Measure changes against the path tracer in an isolated scene, splitting error into lit, penumbra, umbra and a contact band.
- Everything that lights a surface with the sun multiplies by the cloud shadow and the ground fog's sun transmittance.
- Ray origins off a gbuffer surface move by a fraction of a millimetre scaled by view distance, never centimetres: props and floors meet within millimetres in real maps, and a larger offset starts rays on the far side of a wall.
- The terrain heightfield shadow (`terrain_shadow.lua`, `ShadowExtent` on the terrain) applies at every distance: a receiver's distance says nothing about its caster's.

### Ray tracing layers

Lowest first: `scene/hit.lua` (geometry only, `scene_trace`, `scene_occluded`; audio, particles, light occlusion), `scene/ray_materials.lua` (material buffer, optical volume records, alpha test, `r_rt_hit_*` pvars), `optical_rays.lua` (straight rays through transparent bodies: `get_optical_tint`, `get_light_transmittance`, `get_body_exit`, media inscatter), `scene_hits.lua` (trace and shade a hit with lights, GI and fog: `trace_scene_hit`, `shade_scene_hit`, `trace_scene_radiance`). The acceleration structure is `scene_tlas` in every shader. Instances past `r_rt_distance` are not in the ray traced scene.

- Glass tints light with a ray, not a shadow map (`r_light_transmittance`): glass casts no shadow of its own (`Material.GlassCastsShadow()`), and `get_light_transmittance` traces only `instances.MASK_GLASS`. `radiance_cache.HasGlass()` gates it so scenes without glass pay nothing. Without ray queries glass falls back to dithered shadows (`Material:GetShadowOpacity`).
- A glass layer passes `mix(1, albedo * texel.rgb, texel.a)`: Source 2 glass stores dirt as colour with coverage in alpha.

## Sky, atmosphere, fog, clouds

- **Atmosphere** (`atmosphere.lua`, `atmosphere_scattering.lua`): Bruneton style transmittance, a precomputed multiple scattering table (`sc_lookup`) and marched single scattering (`integrate_scattering`). One physical `SUN_COLOR` (luminance 1) is used for direct light, sky, fog, clouds and the disc. Visibility is two distances in metres: `atmosphere.SetAerosolVisibility` and `SetGroundFogVisibility`; climates and `atmosphere_controller` take the same. Tables are for a unit sun.
- The rgb scattering bands are sRGB weighted folds of the spectrum (`RAYLEIGH_BAND`, `OZONE_BAND`, ...), never hand picked single wavelengths. Check sky changes against a spectral reference at fixed directions, not by eye.
- Code that integrates the atmosphere must clip at the planet (density clamps altitude at 0, so underground air integrates at ground density). Sky LUT caches must be baked at their quantised key.
- **The sun and the moon both always light the air**; the primary light only decides who gets the shadowed direct light. Every radiometric sky term is a sum over both bodies at their own directions, never a function of which one is primary. `test/tests/render3d/fog_primary_light.lua` checks this.
- Scattering tables are interpolated in log space along the sun axis (`sc_ambient`), since twilight radiance changes by orders of magnitude between nodes; `fog_twilight_smooth.lua` checks the slope.
- **View directions** for sky, stars and discs are built by rotating the view space ray (`screen_reconstruct.GetWorldRayGLSL`), never `world_pos - camera_position`: subtracting large world coordinates quantises the direction by more than the sun's radius.
- **Environment probe** (`envprobe.lua`): it is fp16 but the sun disc is about 1e9, so it is stored divided by a power of two scale kept in alpha (`get_probe_scale`); every reader multiplies `.rgb` by `.a`. Only mip 0 contains the discs (analytic, supersampled); higher mips, irradiance and the cache's sky are disc free or the sun would light things twice next to direct light. Stochastic lobe rays read lod 1 or higher. `weather.SetEnvironmentMap(path)` uses an HDRI and disables sun and atmosphere. `envprobe.UpdateEnvironmentAltitude` moves the probe to the camera's height.
- Discs are drawn at pixel coverage (`get_celestial_discs`) so their summed light does not jump with jitter. TAA resamples history in compressed space because Catmull-Rom's negative lobes next to the sun disc would go below zero.
- **Froxel fog** (`passes/volumetric_fog.lua`, `froxel_fog.lua`): one medium of clear air and a ground fog layer (`atmosphere.SetGroundFog*`), lit by the shadowed sun, the other celestial body, the DDGI probes (looked up by their own lower resolution pass, `volumetric_froxel_gi`, kept out of the per froxel scatter for cost) and the sky ambient. Consumers read it through `get_volumetric_fog` / `froxel_sample`, never `texture()` on the volume. Texels of -1 mean unknown.
  - Rule: every in-scatter term from the precomputed tables assumes open sky and must be scaled by a visibility toward its own source direction, or haze glows in front of mountains. The froxel GI pass stores sun and glow visibility rays plus a screen space march for that; far terrain needs `ShadowExtent`. Repro with a huge far box and the sun behind it.
  - Never use a single point shadow lookup for a medium segment. The surface clamp uses the depth pass's farthest depth over the tile, not a jittered sample, or alpha tested holes flicker; check with a holed plane against the sky and `FrameRecorder` (`Clip:Variance`).
  - The fog's light is a fitted model referenced against a path tracer; change it only with a reference to compare to.
- **Fog volumes** are procedural `fluid_volume`s ([fluid-volumes.md](fluid-volumes.md)). Procedural fluids and the ground fog layer are also optical volume records in the ray material prefix, so lighting, probes and hit rays see light through them (`optical_inscatter`); simulated fluids are not. Every consumer of the ground fog layer is per channel (`media.presets.fog`).
- **Clouds** (`clouds.lua`, `passes/clouds.lua`; `weather.SetCloudLayers`, presets in `climate.lua`): baked noise, beer shadow map, low resolution checkerboard trace with reconstruct, composited in the fog pass; the sun is dimmed by `get_cloud_shadow`. Phases come from `media.presets.cloud` and multiple scattering from `medium_multiple_scattering` in `medium_common.lua`. Durable rules: baked noise must span 0..1; tiling noise needs a per region offset; transmittance must fade out smoothly near zero because the sun is far brighter than a cloud. Cost knobs: `cloud_layer_steps`, `cloud_detail_min_mip`, `clouds.VIEW_DIVISOR`. `clouds.SetCoverageMap` overrides large scale coverage over a world square.
- **Snow** (`surface_weather.lua`, `snow_cover.lua`, `snow_backdrop.lua`): `weather.SetSnowDepth(meters)` covers surfaces masked by a shelter map from the precipitation direction; with depth, gbuffer fragments march a relief snow map in light space, and a backdrop sphere draws snow in front of sky or buried geometry. The shelter map only redraws when the camera crosses a snap step, so a scratch scene that never moves the camera has no relief.
- **Heat mirage** (`mirage.lua`, `passes/mirage.lua`, `passes/mirage_shimmer.lua`, `r_mirage`): surface heat from an energy balance, mirrored view rays below the critical angle, and angle of arrival shimmer after AA.
- **Camera inside a medium body** (`passes/camera_medium.lua`, `r_camera_medium`): per pixel ray query to find whether the camera is inside a glass or medium body; dims and in-scatters the view, sun shafts through `medium_common.GetShaftGLSL`. Rippled tops and their caustics come from `medium_surface.lua`, shared with water volumes. `addons/examples/lua/examples/render3d/media_scene.lua` is the test scene for media (env `MODE`, `NIGHT`, `FOG`, `CAM`; cameras are printed on load).

## Planet

The world is a planet of radius `planet.RADIUS` (`goluwa/planet.lua`) touching the origin. Terrain and ocean are lowered by `planet.GetDrop(x, z)` (GLSL `planet_drop`, written to stay precise in float32); static meshes, gameplay and gravity stay flat. `addons/examples/lua/examples/render3d/earth.lua` checks it from orbit.

- Anything that compares a world position with the sea or a fog layer uses height above the datum (`y + planet_drop(xz)`); rays against the sea use `planet_sphere_intersect` / `planet_surface_t`.
- Surface lighting uses `get_sun_transmittance_from_origin` and `get_sky_light_from` so far points get their own sun elevation and sky light.
- For a high camera, the fog composite hands the air in front of geometry from the froxels to a per pixel march (`apply_far_air`). Do not take the difference of two path integrated table lookups for rays that end on the ground: it draws an arc where the end point crosses the table's horizon.
- Pixel footprints from the projection need `abs(projection[1][1])`: Vulkan's y flip makes it negative, and a negative footprint silently disables distance filters.
- Distant clouds past the shape mips switch to a homogeneous model (`cloud_unresolved`); coarse environment mips are sampled with a B-spline to avoid rings on large smooth reflectors.

## Reflections, translucency, materials

- **Reflections** are the specular lobe of the radiance pass (no pass of their own): one ray per pixel in `radiance_trace` (mirror below `REFLECTION_MIRROR_THRESHOLD`, GGX visible normal sample above), accumulated and filtered with the GI, read through `gi.GetScreenSpecularTexture`. Durable rules:
  - The specular texture holds weighted light in rgb and the masking weights in alpha; the lighting divides. A sample below the surface counts as zero, never folded and counted.
  - Hits are limited by a firefly clamp in exposed units; with `radiance_specular_reuse` the clipped part comes back from the GI reservoir, so the sum stays unbiased. Never weight samples by brightness.
  - Smooth surfaces reproject history along the reflected image (`radiance_specular_tracking`), not the surface. The image lies behind the surface along the view ray by the hit distance for a mirror and by less as the lobe widens (the dominant direction factor), so a rough lobe's image slides back onto the surface.
  - The image is tracked by the inverse distance from the camera to it, averaged and weighted by the light each ray brought: an average of distances puts every image a sky ray touches at infinity, and the bright part of a glossy reflection is what is seen. Use this frame's ray on smooth surfaces and the accumulated distance as the lobe widens.
  - The image history replaces the surface's outright; a blend smears. A broad lobe sees near and far things whose images drift apart, so its history is capped at the frames they take to separate by half the lobe's screen footprint, and by half a lobe width of view rotation. Down-weighting history by tracking-distance disagreement only applies to mirrors; glossy rays always disagree.
  - The fp16 filter textures hold pre-exposed radiance because a mirror reflects the sun disc, far above fp16 in absolute units.
  - Surface features that change the lobe go through `surface_features.GetReflectionGLSL`.
  - Without ray queries there is no traced reflection; the lighting falls back to the environment and probes.
  - Tests to run after touching any of this: `test/tests/render3d/radiance_specular.lua` (metals and dielectrics over roughness against the path tracer) and `radiance_furnace.lua` (spheres in a closed room of emitters must read the wall radiance). Indirect-only lighting is the sensitive case. Scenes: `reflection_plates.lua`, `reflection_furnace.lua` in the render3d examples.
- **Translucent surfaces** (`passes/translucent.lua`, `translucent_extras.lua`): exact dielectric Fresnel, absorption from the material's `Medium` or albedo; with ray queries a closed refractive body takes its exit point and normal from its own geometry (`get_body_exit`, per channel for dispersion), otherwise it falls back to a pane or curvature model. They trace their own mirror ray and rely on TAA for noise. A graphics pipeline has one descriptor set unless `DescriptorSetCount` says more, and sets in use cannot be rewritten, so per frame TLAS/material bindings need a set per swapchain image.
- **Diffuse transmission** (`features/transmission.lua`) is for leaves and cloth: the albedo is the reflectance and `DiffuseTransmission` scales transmittance over it. Reflectance plus transmittance above 1 adds energy; that is the material's to avoid.
- **Subsurface** (`features/subsurface.lua`, `passes/subsurface.lua`): screen space diffusion of irradiance (divided by albedo, re-multiplied after the blur so texture detail stays sharp), scaled by `r_subsurface_width` and the material's `SubsurfaceRadius` / `SubsurfaceColor`; `SubsurfaceSkinTone` restricts it to skin-like albedo. Specular, reflections and GI rays do not see it. Thin back lit skin is `DiffuseTransmission`.
- **Gbuffer roughness**: `gbuffer_roughness()` returns GGX alpha; `sqrt` it for perceptual.
- **Shading model** (`surface_lighting.lua`, `ibl.lua`): GGX D, height correlated Smith, a dielectric Fresnel from the index implied by F0 (metals Schlick), Kulla-Conty style energy compensation on direct and ambient specular, and EON diffuse (energy preserving Oren-Nayar) with its own `DiffuseRoughness`, separate from specular roughness. Ambient diffuse multiplies irradiance by `EON_directional_albedo`, not albedo. Ray hits shade with `ibl.GetHitBRDFGLSL`, the same lobes with an analytic split sum fit; metals reflect with their F0 colour, never as diffuse albedo.
- **Local lights**: `Lumen` is flux; falloff `1 / (d^2 + source_radius^2)` times a range window; spots integrate their cone (`GetEmissionSolidAngle`) so a lamp emits exactly its lumens; colour is normalised to luminance. Lights and their maps read the entity's world transform, so a light can be nested in a prefab or prop.
- **Emissive surfaces** are not lights; they reach surroundings only through GI. `EmitsLight` false keeps the glow but drops the emitter. For Source maps `source_loader/fixture_emission.lua` clears it on fixture materials next to authored light entities and sets `IgnoredByLightOcclusion` (`MASK_LIGHT_BODY`) so a light inside its bulb mesh is not occluded by it.
- **Validating shading**: a colour chart under a locked EV against analytic matte values, furnace rooms where nothing may look brighter or darker than the walls, and the path tracer. When a surface looks washed out, render it with black albedo and remove terms one at a time to find additive light.

## Measuring lighting

- Control exposure: auto exposure brings a leaking room and a correct black room both to mid grey. Pin EV or read textures before exposure (`gi.GetScreenTexture`, `post_source.GetSceneSourceTexture`). Disable local exposure for absolute comparisons.
- One frame after a camera move measures unconverged GI; hold the view with `Shot.Capture` (`settle` or `converge`) and avoid pitch of exactly ±90. See [screenshots-and-cameras.md](screenshots-and-cameras.md).
- A converged mean says nothing about shimmer; compare captures far enough apart in time that history hysteresis does not correlate them.
- Test scenes set `MetallicMultiplier = 0` explicitly for non-metals.
- Compare GI against a reference (the path tracer, or ReSTIR's plain ray mode), not one GI system against another.

## Path traced reference

`r_pass_path_trace 1` (built only while on: `path_trace.lua`, `passes/path_trace.lua`) replaces lit opaque surfaces with a progressive path traced mean. It is a debugging tool, not a game renderer.

- It shares everything with the raster except light transport: the first surface is the gbuffer, every vertex uses the raster's lobes, lights use `get_light_vector_and_attenuation` (point and spot lights as spheres of `SourceRadius`), the sun is a cone with ground fog and cloud shadow, MIS with BSDF rays, the sky is the environment map. A difference is therefore the raster's integration. With `path_trace_caustics` it handles caster glass and water tops through photons.
- Not modelled (so differences there are expected): extra feature lobes (clearcoat, sheen, subsurface), media between surfaces, some hit-side normal detail and terrain blend rules, and emitter sampling (small emitters converge slowly).
- The mean restarts when camera, lights, materials, instances or environment change, or on `path_trace_reset`; `path_trace_max_samples` stops it.
- Compare with `path_trace_view` (`raster`, `split`, `difference`), the `path_trace_compare` command or `path_trace.Compare(step)` (absolute radiance over both targets).
- Check the tracer before blaming the raster: with `path_trace_bounces 0` and `lighting_debug_direct` both show direct light only and must agree closely. Coplanar surfaces z-fighting under TAA jitter look like a lighting difference.
- The raster's indirect diffuse uses the uniform EON response, so rough surfaces with the sun behind the viewer differ from the reference by design.

## Caustics

`r_pass_caustics` (off by default, built only while on: `caustics.lua` for settings, casters and photon GLSL, `passes/caustics.lua`) lights surfaces with sun and lamp light bent by glass bodies and water volume tops: visible surfaces through a screen buffer, ray hits through a world hash grid (`r_caustics_world`, `caustics.GetWorldGrid`). The path tracer uses the same photon code. Scene: `addons/examples/lua/examples/render3d/caustics.lua` (`caustics_demo.Goto(...)`, env `CAM`, `NIGHT`, `CAUSTICS`, `PATH_TRACE`).

- Casters are glass with `CastsCaustics` (needs `Refraction`) and water volumes. Emitter entries pair a light with a caster; the CPU ranks them by flux and gives `r_caustics_photons` by screen importance and measured landed share. Photons are kept only if they bent.
- Dispersive surfaces (`(n - 1) / AbbeNumber` above `r_caustics_dispersion_spread`) retrace a photon as three spectral bands.
- Filtering is spatial by photon density first, then temporal; sparse photons are noise, not motion, so they are neither clamped nor history capped.
- A light is handed to the photons only where a receiver lies in one of its entries and the light ray crosses caster glass or a water top (`caustic_covered`); elsewhere glass keeps its straight tint.
- Measure cost as `gpu_frame` with the pass on and off (the photon pass's GPU scope closes before its rays finish). Check against the path tracer with time frozen (`system.SetElapsedTime`) using regional means over whole caustics.
- Compute pass callbacks are `on_pre_draw(self, cmd, frame, desc)`; bind buffers to `desc`, not `frame`.

## Water

Ocean and `water_volume` components are drawn by the `water` compute pass (`passes/water.lua`, denoiser `water_resolve`; wave passes keep the `ocean_` prefix). Sea state is in `water.lua`, presets in `media.lua`. Examples: `water_showcase.lua`, `ocean_shore.lua`, `water_volume_test.lua`, `twilight_lake.lua`.

- **Water as a medium**: the ray material buffer starts with a typed prefix of optical volume records (ocean, water volumes, fog volumes; `optical_ranges`, written in one place), read by the water and froxel passes (`radiance_cache.BindOpticalVolumes`). `get_light_transmittance` and `get_optical_tint` apply the path inside each volume, so lighting and probes carry the depth falloff and the water pass only adds refraction and caustics. Local light in-scatter uses the shared medium light loop (`medium_lighting.GetLocalLightsGLSL`), so lights in water are shadowed as in air. More than `water.MAX_VOLUMES`: the nearest are drawn (`water.GetNearestVolumes`).
- **Waves**: inverse FFT cascades built on the CPU (`water.BuildOceanWaves`, `water.SetOceanGradually` spreads a rebuild over frames). Ocean level is the mean surface. `water.SampleOcean` (buoyancy) sums the strongest modes on the CPU. `WindDirection` is where waves go; `WindFromClimate` follows the climate wind.
- The trace from above is a sphere trace through the slab `level ± wave_bound` with a mean-plane fallback; a ray with no crossing must never show what is behind the sea.
- **Shallows**: `water_field.lua` is a floor height grid around the camera (`water_field.SetHeightSource`) that damps, refracts and shelters waves; `r_water_field_debug` draws it. `surf.lua` is a deterministic breaking wave train from an eikonal solve, with swash on land; the CPU mirrors it (`surf.ApplyToSample` via `water.SetShallowSampler`) and fires `SurfBreak` events for sounds.
- **Foam** is one lace pattern driven by a density (whitecaps, shore, flow streaks). Dense foam means more strands, not wider ones; keep foam albedo below lit white. Shore foam measures from the visible depth buffer, not ray hits (the ray traced geometry can differ from the rasterized terrain).
- **Reflections**: screen space first, rays fill where the screen finds nothing (`r_water_screenspace_roughness`, `water_rt_share`). Ambient under a roof uses probe visibility rays so covered water does not glow like open sky.
- **Cost**: the `r_water_*` feature pvars are runtime bits (no recompile); compare variants in one process interleaved with baselines, since GPU clocks drift. `r_water_waves` off gives a flat mirror.
- Pitfalls: `smoothstep(e, e, x)` is undefined; phase angle is `dot(-view_dir, light_dir)`; negative values (not only NaN/inf) become bright discs through bloom; screen space apparent depth refraction tears at edges; regular sine ripples show grids, use advected gradient noise.
- Map importers turn game fog into a medium with `media.MediumFromFog`.
