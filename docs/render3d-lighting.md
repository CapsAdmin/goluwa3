# Render3D lighting, GI, fog, sky, water and exposure

Physically based, absolute-unit lighting: scene radiance is in cd/m2 (daylight is thousands, the sun disc ~2e9), exposure is applied at the end. When something looks off, work out which term is wrong with an isolated scene (one light, locked exposure, other terms off) rather than tuning by eye. Pass sources are in `goluwa/render3d/passes/`, shared lighting glsl in `surface_lighting.lua`, `ibl.lua`, `atmosphere.lua`, `gi.lua`, `ddgi.lua`, `surfel_gi.lua`, `restir_gi.lua`, `scene_reflection.lua`. Every pass has an `r_pass_<name>` pvar. Ray traced consumers read the scene store ([gpu-scene.md](gpu-scene.md)); on hardware without ray queries they use stubs (test with `GOLUWA_NO_RAY_TRACING=1`: the GI passes are not built and GI falls back to the sky irradiance, no light occlusion, particle ray collision or traced reflections; screen space paths remain). `r_quality` (`goluwa/render3d/quality.lua`) gates passes and scales the fog grid, cloud steps, screen space shadow steps and sun shadow maps; consumers read `quality.Get()`.

## Exposure and units

- **Scene color is pre-exposed.** The fp16 scene targets (lighting, water, fog composite, translucent, taa) hold absolute radiance times `post_source.get_pre_exposure()` (last frame's exposure over `PRE_EXPOSURE_HEADROOM`), because fp16 cannot hold both a starlit night and the sun. Lighting math stays absolute: multiply when writing scene color, divide when reading it for shading, rescale histories by `get_pre_exposure() / get_previous_pre_exposure()`. Probe captures have pre-exposure 1 and stay absolute. New passes that read or write scene color must use `post_source.pre_exposure_block` + `GetPreExposureGLSL`. Radiance limits (firefly clamps) belong in exposed units (`post_source.GetExposureTexture(true)`), not absolute ones.
- Exposure is one number (EV100) with two front ends, both owned by `goluwa/render3d/exposure.lua` (plain state with `exposure.Get/Set`, no pvars; the Look desktop program edits it). Camera: normalises the metered mean to the grey midpoint `camera.grey` (percent, 18 is middle grey), clamped to `camera.ev_min`..`camera.ev_max` (equal values lock it). ISO, aperture and shutter are derived from the exposure along a program line (`camera.mode`: program, aperture/shutter priority, or manual which locks EV) and only drive effects: shutter the motion blur length, ISO the grain in the blit (`r_grain`), aperture the depth of field (`r_dof`, focused by a raycast through the screen centre that skips the active view's `Ignore` entity, the player's rig for the game camera). Effect shaders get them from the exposure texture through `exposure.GetCameraSettingsGLSL` + `exposure.camera_block`; the eye has no camera, so it reads as ISO 100, f/64, 1/60. Eye (`eye.*`): no normalisation. The exposure follows the adapting luminance (the wide-field log-average of the scene, clamped to `eye.adaptation_min`..`eye.adaptation_max` in log10 cd/m2, converted with EV = log2(L) + 3) along the Ferwerda 1996 cone and rod threshold curve (Ward 1994 above 64 cd/m2), with mesopic rod/cone mixing from 0.005 to 5 cd/m2. The rod branch takes scotopic luminance, not photopic: the meter also log-averages `2.3 * dot(rgb, ROD_RESPONSE)` (2.3 is the scotopic/photopic ratio of D65 white) and the curve uses the ratio of the two means, so blue-white light reads brighter to the rods than tungsten at the same cd/m2 (the curve is evaluated in the exposure shader, no baked table); rod vision (`eye.night_vision`) desaturates to the rod spectral response below `eye.night_vision_threshold`. The viewer's own adaptation to the screen dims the display in dim scenes: displayed white is the paper white times `min(L_adapt / 64 cd/m2, 1) ^ eye.viewer_adaptation` (0.33, Stevens' brightness exponent; fitted by eye from a candle lit room needing 25 nits at paper white 200), so one paper white (the display's real SDR white) serves candle rooms and daylight; camera mode is unaffected. There is no artistic layer in the eye: no compensation, no local adaptation, no tint. Bloom and glare are optics, built from the scene before anything else. Their angles become pixels through `eye.display_fov` (the vertical angle the screen takes up at the viewer's eye, `exposure.GetPixelsPerDegree`), never the game camera's field of view: the viewer's eye does not zoom with the picture, so a zoomed camera must not grow the glare, the defocus or the starburst. The camera's glare spreads a flat share of energy per octave of angle (Stiles-Holladay, 1/theta^2); the eye's is the CIE 1999 general glare spread function (Vos and van den Berg, steeper than 1/theta^2 below a few degrees, standard observer with pigmentation 0.5), integrated per bloom level in `passes/bloom.lua` (per channel for the primaries' dominant wavelengths 611, 549 and 464 nm against 560: the two core terms are diffraction, so their angles scale with wavelength; the 400/(1+(theta/0.1)^2) and theta^2 terms are particle scatter, scaled by wavelength^-4; the glare share is per channel; `r_eye_chromatic_halo`). The CIE function and the wavelength scaling live in `render3d/eye_glare.lua`. The starburst (`r_eye_starburst`, `passes/starburst.lua`) is the diffraction pattern of the eye: `render3d/eye_psf.lua` builds a pupil aperture (disc, hundreds of random radial lens fibres of random width, length and opacity, lashes, dust specks; each strip diffracts into its own spike, so spike length, width and brightness are random), FFTs it on the CPU (`math/fft.lua`, once, about 1.5 s on the first eye frame) and stores the power spectrum as a polar table (angle by log radius) normalised so every ring averages 1 (each ring is box filtered to 0.25 degrees of arc first, or the table aliases without mipmaps and the thin rays wobble with the TAA jitter). The table adds the rays of a star around bright compact light sources (only the part above the ring mean: subtracting the dark gaps overshoots the smooth bloom and eats the source's own pixels, which showed as black bursts around lamps): a tile pass (1/16 resolution) finds each tile's summed radiance above the local background (the 1/16 resolution blur, so a uniform sky or a big bright area contributes nothing: discrete tile sources cannot cancel the energy neutral pattern the way a continuous field does, and leave a grid at the tile pitch) and its centroid with a bilinear tent assignment (a tile is only a star source while the bright light within 16 half resolution pixels of it is compact, `COMPACT_SPREAD`: a long neon tube or a big sign becomes a row of separate stars 16 pixels apart whose thin rays do not overlap, and sums to blotches, where a real extended source averages its rays into smooth glare) (every pixel's energy is shared between neighbouring tiles with weights summing to 1, so a light on a tile boundary does not hop between tiles as the TAA jitter moves it), a half resolution gather adds `E * spread(theta) * (modulation - 1)` per channel, with theta scaled by pupil diameter (Moon-Spencer, from the adapting luminance) and by wavelength, when a temporal anti-aliasing mode is on (it jitters the camera) the result is blended 0.9 with the previous frame, reprojected by the camera rotation only and faded out as soon as the camera turns more than a third of a pixel or moves more than a few millimetres in a frame (the history only exists to average the jitter of a still camera; resampling it while moving diffuses the star into a smear, and a rotation only reprojection cannot follow a moving camera or light) (the star hangs on a direction, and a short average cannot remove a flicker with the 16 frame jitter period) (the source is the jittered scene before TAA, whose summed brightness of a tiny emitter flickers about 10% and whose position moves with the jitter; the centroid is shifted by half the jitter), then the glare pass adds it to the bloom. The afterimage (`r_eye_afterimage`, `passes/afterimage.lua`) is a per pixel retinal state at 1/8 resolution in screen space (it follows the view like an afterimage follows the eye), fed from `bloom_down3` plus the glare (the retinal image, so the blob's falloff is the eye's own), read back through a 3x3 gaussian blur at that resolution (display only, not fed back into the state, so it cannot diffuse over time) and bicubic: it is a blobby glow, not a copy of the picture. It has three parts. The source stage is the light's own colour (cone direction times a saturating level) held while it is lit and drained over `eye.afterimage_source_seconds` (0.3 s, not scaled). Behind it a single age value per pixel (held while lit, decaying as `level ^ eye.afterimage_falloff / (8 s * eye.afterimage_time_scale)`, held back while the source stage is strong) runs through a colour ramp, white, yellow, green, blue, purple, red (red lingers: it holds the last third of the age), with brightness fading with the level. The falloff: 0 is a plain exponential, below 0 the decay speeds up as it fades so the tail is cut off and it ends in a finite time (-1 is linear in light), above 0 it slows down and leaves a tail (1 is hyperbolic). Last, bleaching (`dB/dt = (L_cone / 4000 cd/m2)(1 - B) - B / (16 s * time scale)` per cone type, Hunt-Pointer-Estevez) multiplies the cone signals by `1 - B`, a complementary tinted darker patch on bright fields. A ramp reads about like the real flight of colours and is much cheaper than modelling the cone types. A light must be `eye.afterimage_adaptation_ratio` times the adapting luminance to excite it, and `eye.afterimage_intensity` scales the whole effect (0 skips the passes). The update gathers the retinal image along each pixel's motion (the velocity buffer, the camera reprojection for sky) at one sample per state pixel, taking the max, so a light sweeping over the screen leaves a continuous streak and not one dot per frame; a motion over 0.2 of the screen is a cut and is ignored. Two more eye-only effects live in the final shader in `passes/blit.lua`, each behind a pvar so it can be isolated: `r_eye_chromatic_defocus` (red and blue blurred by the pupil, Moon-Spencer diameter from the adapting luminance, 0.35 D and 0.9 D of longitudinal chromatic aberration against the focused green) and `r_eye_noise` (the grain of a dark adapted eye, in displayed units and faded out with adaptation: sd `strength * dark_adapted * (0.0025 + 0.015 * sqrt(shown))` of display white, 2x2 pixel blocks, tinted purple (a red 0.75, green 0.35, blue 1.0 weighting, as the dark adapted eye sees it), divided by the exposure and by the viewer adaptation scale so it is the size of what is finally shown: at night that scale is about 0.07 and would otherwise shrink the noise 14 times, `dark_adapted` 1 below 0.01 cd/m2 adapting luminance and 0 above 3; in absolute scene luminance the noise was a few thousandths of a code value and could not be seen). Both end in the same display transform (`r_tonemapper`, default `neutral`, SDR or HDR). The camera meter is a centre weighted mean of linear luminance with each pixel held at the 95th percentile of a log histogram; the eye adapts to the wide-field log-average (uniform weights, no clamp), so one lamp cannot dominate it. `eye.rod_adaptation` defaults to 1, the full rod+cone curve; lower values blend toward the cone-only curve, which is darker at night than the model's own data. A view's `ExposureLock` (EV100, `glw shot --ev`) fixes exposure in either mode. `ExposureHold` freezes the auto exposure at its current value while the view is active, so a screen effect that darkens most of the image is not metered back to grey. Shader in `passes/blit.lua`.
- Motion blur (`passes/motion_blur.lua`) runs after TAA from the velocity buffer (current minus previous screen position). By default it gathers only behind the motion (`r_motion_blur_trailing`), so the leading edge keeps its position and only the trail extends; the symmetric kernel stretches things ahead of where they are and hides a wrong velocity sign, so test sign changes with trailing on. The faster neighbour a pixel borrows its direction from is searched out to `r_motion_blur_max` with a reach test (the neighbour's blur must reach the pixel), never with a threshold on the cap. The search reads 16 pixel tiles, not the full resolution buffers: `motion_blur_tiles` keeps each tile's longest blur, `motion_blur_neighbors` the longest that can reach the tile, so a pixel with nothing moving near it exits after one read, and the rest test the tile maxima against their own distance to each tile.
- The `neutral` tonemapper leaves everything below 0.76 of exposed white unchanged (no toe or contrast); SDR and HDR are the same curve with different `peak`. The other curves (`agx`, `aces`, `gt7`) are looks. HDR output (`r_hdr_peak`, `r_hdr_paper_white`) cannot know the display; they are settings. With `--hdr` the window target is an fp16 layer of sRGB encoded values where 1.0 is `r_hdr_ui_white` nits, so the UI and anything else drawn to the screen blends exactly as in SDR (`RequiresManualGamma()` is true); the blit writes the scene into it encoded the same way (above 1.0 for highlights) and `goluwa/render/hdr_output.lua` decodes it to the scRGB or HDR10 swapchain at the end of the frame. Never blend UI in linear space: alpha and text antialiasing come out thinner and lighter.
- `goluwa/render3d/grade.lua`: a grade preset is a table of pvars (anything left out resets to default, so a preset is the whole look); `grade.SetPreset(name, seconds)` blends. Controls after the tonemapper (`r_grade_*`: brightness, contrast, saturation, warmth, lift/gain) are applied in `grade_display`. At defaults the grade is exactly the tonemapper.
- **Measuring lighting needs control of exposure.** Auto exposure brings a dark leaking room and a correct black room both to mid grey; pin EV (indoor ~10.5, outdoor ~15.5) or read a texture before exposure (the GI resolve texture `gi.GetScreenTexture`, or `post_source.GetSceneSourceTexture`). Local exposure also brightens dark regions, so disable it for absolute comparisons. A one-frame camera override measures unconverged GI: hold the view with `Shot.Capture` (`settle` or `converge`), and avoid pitch of exactly +-90. A converged mean says nothing about shimmer: compare captures spread over time (frames 3 apart are correlated by hysteresis and understate flicker many times; use ~20-frame gaps, and auto exposure for night scenes). Test scenes must set `MetallicMultiplier = 0` explicitly for non-metals.

## Choosing the GI

`r_gi` picks the global illumination: `ddgi`, `surfels` (surfel radiance cascades), `restir` (a ray per pixel resampled in space and time, over a world cache) or `none` (the sky's irradiance). Only the chosen system's pass is put in the default pass list, so the other one builds no pipelines and allocates nothing; changing it rebuilds the pipeline bundles. Under `restir`, `r_gi_cache` (`ddgi`, `surfels`, `none`) picks the world space system built alongside it. Bundles with an explicit pass list (tests, previews) get whichever GI pass they list. The active world system is the DDGI or surfel pass present and enabled in the current bundle (`gi.GetMode()`), not the pvar; `gi.IsPerPixel()` says whether the screen GI comes from ReSTIR instead.

- Consumers go through `goluwa/render3d/gi.lua`, never a system directly: screen textures (`gi.GetScreenTexture` and friends, the same three textures and conventions for both), the `ddgi_data` uniform block (`gi.GetProbeBlockLayout`, `gi.WriteProbeBlock`, it carries both systems' fields and `gi_mode`) and the world lookups in `gi.GetCommonGLSL`/`gi.GetMediumGLSL` (`gi_sample_irradiance`, `gi_sample_medium`, `gi_in_volume`, `gi_available`, `gi_covers`), which branch on `gi_mode` at runtime. Both lookups read only bindless textures, so a consumer needs no extra bindings for either system.
- `gi_covers(P, weight)` decides whether a lookup that found nothing means darkness or "unknown, use the sky fallback": DDGI probes inside the volume that were all hidden mean dark, surfels that do not exist mean the place was never seen.
- Irradiance is stored and returned as E/pi (the cosine weighted mean radiance) in both systems: shading multiplies it by albedo, never by albedo/pi. A new integrator that returns E is pi times too bright and makes the multi-bounce feedback run away.
- **Emitters** (`goluwa/render3d/scene/emitters.lua`, shared by DDGI probes, surfels and the fog): emissive surfaces reach GI only through emitter samples (rays alone give fireflies on small emitters). Every consumer draws `EMITTER_CANDIDATES` triangles from a light tree and keeps one in proportion to `facing * receiving / max(d^2, r^2)` (receiving being the receiver's cosine when it has a normal, `vec3(0)` for probes and the medium); surfels take all candidates as samples. The tree is an implicit binary tree over the emissive triangles in Morton order, rebuilt on the GPU in `emitters.Build`: the order (a bitonic sort of centroid keys) only when the emitter list changes, the leaves (world space bounds, power, emission cone) and the levels every frame, so moving emitters refit. Descending, each child is weighed by power times the bounded orientation and receiver cosines over a squared distance, clamped by the consumer's radius, and no single distance works: the distance to the node's box makes a big node that holds the receiver look as near as the radius clamp, so it starves a small emitter beside the receiver in another node; the distance to its centre underrates a big node whose near corner touches the receiver (emitters on the receiver's own wall). Half the candidates walk by each, and every candidate's pdf is the average of both walks' probabilities of the path it took (both are computed along the one path), so neither can leave an emitter all but unpicked. Brightness is the test for any change to the sampler, not only noise: compare long means against picking by power alone (unbiased with no spatial terms). Rare huge weights do not stay unbiased in practice: DDGI clips them in its fp16 storage and comes out dark, and smaller clusters or fewer candidates raise them, so `EMITTER_CLUSTER` is 16 and DDGI keeps 4 candidates; the ReSTIR candidate pass takes 2 (one per weighing), its reuse stands in for more. Picking by instance first (bounding sphere, then a triangle by area) fails where one instance holds many small emitters spread over a large area (a prop of hundreds of cave crystals): nearly every pick lands on a far crystal and the near ones that light a spot show as speckle. The traversal adds fresh random bits per level; rescaling one number down the tree runs out of precision after a few unlikely branches. The walk stops `EMITTER_CLUSTER` leaves above the bottom and picks among them by their own importance, and two exact tests give zero importance: a leaf keeps its triangle's plane (a receiver behind a one sided triangle), and any node whose box lies wholly below the receiver's tangent plane. Without them a receiver at the foot of an emitter (inside its triangles' bounds, where the cone and sphere bounds say nothing) spends most walks on faces that turn away and they die. The tree cannot see occlusion: where the nearby emitters are mostly hidden (crystals set in rock), the rare visible far ones carry the light and the noise is visibility noise, about as high as plain rays hitting the emitters. `emitter_light` turns a pick into radiance times solid angle over its pdf, divided by the receiving cosine. `emitter_random` is a per index low discrepancy sequence over frames (a hashed offset plus the frame times the 4d golden ratio constants), so a consumer must pass a stable index (a slot, a cell) and the frame, never a hash that includes the frame, or the history averages clumped samples. A consumer shadow-tests the point itself. While emitters are sampled, rays must not also add the emission of what they hit or it counts twice: DDGI's shade never adds it, surfel and fog rays define `SCENE_HIT_EMISSION` to `ddgi_emitter_count == 0`; reflections keep it. Additive materials are not emitters unless `gi_additive_emitters` (an additive billboard once held 99.9% of all emitter power); `gi_emitters` turns sampling off. A material that is "albedo alpha emissive" emits scaled by the albedo's average alpha. Check total emitter power before tuning samplers when emitter noise appears.

## DDGI

Probe-based GI over cascades (`goluwa/render3d/ddgi.lua` for glsl and lookup, `passes/ddgi.lua` for the passes: trace, shade, probe data/relocation, irradiance and distance integrate, guide, resolve).

- **Cascades**: `ddgi.CASCADES` (10) starting at `ddgi_probe_spacing` (0.5 m), spacing doubling, fitted to the scene bounds and `ddgi_max_coverage`; cascades past the one covering everything are dropped. A pixel samples at most two. Cascade n updates every `ddgi_update_interval_*` frames (spaced geometrically between `_min` and `_max`), and a cascade updates early when the sun moved (`ddgi_change_threshold`, `ddgi_change_min_interval`; plus a short fast-history window after a large change). Only the sun is tracked; other changes wait for the interval, or call `ddgi.ResetHistory()`. If far GI looks stale after a non-sun change, lower the relevant interval. The finest cascade updating every frame is the largest single DDGI cost.
- **History**: a texel keeps `ddgi_hysteresis` to the power of the cascade's update interval; a probe with few frames behind it weighs the new frame 1/(n+1). A probe whose mean ray brightness consistently drifts one way (detector follows the median of the mean, fast rate decided from the frames *before* this one) takes the new frame faster, so lights switching are followed but lone bright rays (spikes) are not taken in at the fast rate (that biases GI up and causes fireflies). Reset, moved or disabled probes start over.
- **Rays**: `ddgi_guided_rays` is the share aimed by the probe's own octahedral radiance guide (path guiding); guide cells also aim at their brightest spot (`ddgi_aim_share`, a build time define). Every pass that needs a ray direction recomputes it from the same guide, which is only replaced at the end of the frame so trace, shade and integrate agree (any guide is unbiased if they agree). Probe rays start `DDGI_ORIGIN_PULL` behind the probe so a probe lying on a thin wall hits it at once from the far side. `ddgi_ray_clamp` caps a ray's weighted contribution (radiance times its guide weight `s_q`) against the probe's mean, never its raw radiance: a guided ray aimed at a small bright patch (sky through a hole) has a tiny weight, and clamping its radiance undoes the guiding and leaves a room lit through a small opening nearly black. It still trims light that only rare rays find, so such rooms stay darker than unclamped and converge over hundreds of frames.
- **Moving instances stay out of the probes**: probe rays, the sun and emitter shadow rays of their hits and every visibility ray use `DDGI_SCENE_MASK` / `DDGI_VISIBILITY_MASK` (`instances.MASK_GI`, `MASK_GI_SOLID`), never `0xFF` or `MASK_SOLID`; a new trace against the probe scene must too. Probe history lasts about 30 frames, so a thing that moves through the lattice leaves a trail in probe light, distance moments and relocation. `bridge.lua` (`item_mask`) clears the GI bits of character gear (`character_movement`, `weapon_holder`; animation moves it without its transform changing) for good, and of any other dynamic visual (rigid bodies, attachments) while its world matrix changes, until `GI_SETTLE_FRAMES` frames after the last change. A house of props lights like static geometry once built, a carried or thrown prop is lit by the probes but does not write into them. Surfel traces are not masked this way.
- **Screen space AO** (`passes/ambient_occlusion.lua`, half resolution, temporal then a depth and normal weighted blur) is CORTAO: depth samples on a view facing circle become circular occluders that each block the cosine distributed rays (32, one bit each) they cover. Occlusion, the bent normal and the bounce all come from that bit mask: bounce light is the previous frame's colour at an occluder times the share of rays it newly blocks. Tunables are the `ambient_occlusion_*` pvars (`samples` is read when the shader is built), `ambient_occlusion_debug` shows one output (`occlusion_amplified` exaggerates small differences). The temporal pass has a trailing problem: a static surface keeps its velocity while a moving occluder passes it, so the reprojected history stays valid and the occluder's halo trails behind it (and lags ahead of it); it also trails the bent normal that the GI lookup follows. The temporal pass therefore lowers the history weight by how far the history's AO is from the 5x5 raw mean, in units of that window's standard deviation (the 3x3 sigma clamp is too loose for the 1 spp noise; in units of the mean's standard error the gate rejects the history of noisy pixels, so they never accumulate and the noise stays static). History is read at the reprojected texel; only when that texel is a hole or its depth is off by more than the current 3x3 raw depth spread plus 5% is a better matching texel searched among the 3x3 history texels (searching always picks along the depth iso-line of a sloped surface and the noise flows along it). Accepting history up to that spread is what lets alpha tested foliage accumulate, since it flips between leaf layers under the TAA jitter. `ambient_occlusion_debug` also has `bounce_raw` and `bounce_temporal` to measure each stage; the stage to blame is the one whose frame to frame change does not fall (for the bounce in dense foliage: raw about 0.10, after temporal 0.027, after blur 0.033, in sqrt luma). Measure noise against a long converged render of a still camera, not against the previous frame, which cannot see a static pattern. Measure it with a box walking past a wall, comparing the temporal AO alpha in strips behind and ahead of it with the raw AO (`GetAttachment(1)` of `ambient_occlusion` and `ambient_occlusion_blur`).
- **Lookup rules** (`ddgi_sample_cascade`): visibility uses a Chebyshev test plus ray queries (`DDGI_VISIBILITY_RAYS`; a pass must bind the TLAS to get them, and fog and any new distant consumer need it or sealed buildings leak outdoor probes). A probe is traced when its blend weight is >= `DDGI_VISIBILITY_MIN_WEIGHT` or when it would add more than `DDGI_VISIBILITY_MIN_SHARE` of the light so far (faint-weight probes outside a roof can be 1000x brighter than a dark room, so weight alone is not enough). Lookups at hit points (probe bounces, reflection hits: `DDGI_CONTRAST_VISIBILITY_RAYS`) trace probes `ddgi_contrast_ratio` times brighter than the point's reference. A probe a ray found hidden counts for nothing; there is no fallback to hidden probes (such points fade to the next cascade). `DDGI_NO_VISIBILITY_RAYS` is for consumers that cannot leak (the open sea surface). If a leak persists while visibility rays seem to do nothing, first check that the rays are even issued.
- Sky texels in `ddgi_resolve`'s geometry texture have a zero normal; their depth (1e30) is clamped by fp16 to about 65504, so never detect them by depth.
- The `ddgi_visibility` view of `ddgi_resolve` (RENDER VIEWS, pass views) shows what the visibility rays changed in the screen lookup: red is light they removed (a leak they stopped), blue is darkness they removed, green is the share of probe weight they rejected. Black means the Chebyshev test, the biases and the facing weight already agree with the rays; use it to find where those fail before tuning them.
- **Relocation**: probes in gaps narrower than twice the relocation distance, or inside thin sheets, must stay disabled (they jiggle and leak sky); moves go along the dominant axis of the backface direction only. A probe resting on a surface (its short backface hits all on one side, as on a floor that lies on a probe plane) is moved off it to the open side instead, if a ray along the move hits nothing (a box resting on another surface has its bottom face on a probe plane too, and moving through that would put its probes under the ground): disabled, every cascade would light that surface from a full spacing away and each cascade from a different height, which shows as steps at the cascade edges. Use `ddgi_debug_gi` and `ddgi_debug_probes` to inspect.
- `ddgi_strength` scales the light probes give every consumer (the lookup wrapper), not the probes' own bounce rays (`DDGI_UNSCALED`). `ddgi_resolve_resolution` fixes the pixel count of the screen GI. The resolve looks probes up along the AO bent normal; the AO pass must rotate it from the depth-derived geometric normal onto the shading normal (faint banding following mesh facets means something uses a depth derived normal), and its sideways lean fades on walls.
- A coarse cascade only keeps the shadow of an occluder taller than its probe spacing: a surface lookup takes part of its light from the probe layer one spacing out (the smooth blend and the facing weight favour it), and that layer sees past a lower occluder. The lookup biases (`NORMAL_BIAS`, `VIEW_BIAS`, in spacings) stop growing at `BIAS_MAX_SPACING` meters so coarse cascades sample close to the surface.
- Octahedral atlas borders (`ddgi_texel_direction`): edge texels mirror the interior row or column next to them, corner texels take the diagonally opposite interior corner. All four corners of the octahedral square are -z, so surfaces facing -z (and any lookup near it) sample a corner: a wrong corner texel shows as seams and static speckle wherever the bent normal's x or y crosses zero.
- Faceted/thin wall rules of thumb: planes without thickness cannot be traced reliably from a surface; sun shadows of zero-thickness quads leak along edges and are not a DDGI problem.
- Verify with `test/tests/render3d/ddgi.lua` (ray hits, emitters, pinhole rooms against slow averages). For noise, score the rms of many frames against their mean *and* print the mean luminance (flicker that comes with a darker room is bias); compare runs at the same warm up, since multi-bounce in a lit room takes thousands of frames to settle. Scratch scripts in `./tmp/`; set pvars that shaders are built from through `GOLUWA_PVARS`.

## Surfel radiance cascades

Radiance cascades on surfaces instead of in a volume (`goluwa/render3d/surfel_gi.lua` for constants, pvars, resources and the lookup glsl, `passes/surfel_gi.lua` for the passes). Surfels are cells of a spatial hash spawned from the gbuffer, so only surfaces the screen has seen get GI; the probe count per cascade drops 4x (cells are 2x larger on a 2D surface) while the directions per surfel grow 4x, so every cascade costs about the same.

- **Switches**: every surfel specific fix below is a pvar listed in `surfel_gi.FEATURES` (on by default; `surfel_fill`, `surfel_neighbour_masks`, `surfel_buried`, `surfel_filter`, ...), sent as a bitmask in both the pass block and `ddgi_data` (`SURFEL_ON(SURFEL_FEATURE_X)` in glsl), so any of them can be turned off in game to see what it does; `surfel_grid_shift` changes the cell keys and restarts the gi. `surfel_capacity` sets the cascade 0 table size (a power of two, 4096 to 262144, default 131072; the coarser cascades get 1/4, 1/16 and 1/32, `surfel_gi.CASCADE_SHIFTS`: a quarter each starved cascades 2 and 3, which need about 0.1 and 0.04 of cascade 0's surfels in a real scene because small objects, edges and buckets each keep their own coarse cell; watch `failed` in the `surfel_debug` stats. Cascade 0 fails inserts above about 70% load with `SURFEL_PROBES` 8, and doubling the probes cost more trace time than doubling the table): the shaders read the capacity from the blocks, so changing it only frees and recreates the tables (in the pvar callback, between frames, after a device wait).
- **Keys**: a surfel is (cell, level, normal bucket, cascade). Every cell computation (touch, lookup, merge, debug) goes through `surfel_cell_position`, which shifts the grid half a cell along the bucket's axis: surfaces on round coordinates sit exactly on cell boundaries along their normal, depth noise then splits one face between two cell layers and the GI shows stripes. The level is the distance LOD (`surfel_cell_size` near the camera, doubling past `surfel_lod_distance`), the bucket is a signed axis of the normal (a pixel touches every bucket its normal leans into, so 45 degree surfaces blend instead of seaming), and cascade c lives at level + c. Each cascade has its own open-addressing table (linear probing, `surfel_gi.PROBES` steps) in a storage buffer for the writers (lock hash, last touched frame, election, new flag) and in float textures for bindless readers (key, position + packed normal). When a table is full the samples that run first win the free slots, so the touch pass scrambles which screen cell a thread takes each frame; otherwise an overflowing table loses whole screen rows (bands), which looks like a bad hash (the hash itself is as uniform as random numbers). Slots are never emptied, only taken over once stale (`surfel_lifetime`), so a probe chain never has holes and a lookup can stop at the first empty slot. A slot keeps two stamps: any touch and screen touch. A screen sample may take over a slot the screen has not touched within the lifetime even if hits keep it alive, so hit spawns cannot crowd out what is on screen. With `surfel_debug` on, `surfel_update` logs live, screen and failed inserts per cascade every 2 s (also in its gpu_timing info): check it before tuning anything that looks like holes.
- **Passes**: `surfel_touch` samples the gbuffer on a jittered grid plus last frame's hit samples (below), finds or inserts the surfels of every cascade and votes with atomicMax; `surfel_elect` writes the winning pixel's position and normal (one representative per frame, it moves over the cell and the history averages the cell); `surfel_update` ages slots, marks which trace this frame (cascade c every 2^c frames, capped) and clears the votes; `surfel_trace` dispatches the cascades coarse to fine, each ray covers its interval and a miss takes the 2x2 child texels of the next coarser cascade's surfels around it (trilinear, same bucket); `surfel_integrate` turns cascade 0's directions into L1 SH irradiance; `surfel_resolve` writes the screen textures DDGI's resolve writes.
- **Intervals**: cascade 0 reaches `surfel_interval` cells, each cascade reaches 4x further, the last one reaches `surfel_max_ray_distance` and sees the sky. Every cascade's rays start at 0, not where the finer cascade ended: a coarse surfel can be most of its cell away from the finer one that reads it, and a coarse ray that starts part way out begins behind a nearby wall and fills a sealed room with sky (an overlap of 1.5 cells was not enough). Starting at 0 costs over-occlusion (a coarse surfel near a wall returns the wall instead of the far light: 25-30% darker than tracing every ray to the end in a room lit through a window) and about 25% more trace time. A miss with no visible coarser surfel around traces on to the end instead of guessing.
- **Visibility**: a merge only reads coarser surfels a short ray from the finer surfel reaches (8 rays per surfel per update, traced by `surfel_merge_visibility` before the trace and kept as a bitmask in the table's `merge_visible`), and the elect pass only moves a surfel's position while the screen has not confirmed it for `SURFEL_POSITION_CONFIRM` frames (the touch pass confirms a position when a screen sample lands within 3/4 of a cell of it, on its plane; a position re-elected every frame hops around its cell, and far, large cells then flash as squares because their visibility tests and plane weights change with it; electing the sample nearest the old position instead makes it random walk and flicker more), and only to a sample the current position can see, unless it is buried: a quarter or more of its last update's rays hit single sided backfaces (`backfaces` in the table, rays in the high 16 bits, the trace pass counts them). A confirmed position reconstructed slightly inside a wall at a floor edge would otherwise stay there and show as black squares along edges; a buried surfel moves to any sample and restarts its history, so a cell that straddles a wall (the floor inside and the ground outside are coplanar) stays on one side instead of averaging both; a screen sample may still move it to its side once the screen has not confirmed the old position for `SURFEL_POSITION_HOLD` frames, which restarts its history (walking in from outside otherwise keeps outdoor positions). Each cascade 0 surfel keeps a 26 bit mask of which neighbouring cells' surfels it can see (`surfel_visibility`, all 26 rays for a new surfel, a quarter per update after; the table holds it, the integrate pass copies it into the alpha of irradiance texels 1-3, 9 bits each, so any lookup reads it bindless), and a surface lookup drops the neighbours its own cell's surfel cannot see. Lookups at ray hits (bounces, reflections) also trace one ray to their own cell's surfel: a cell straddling a zero thickness wall can hold the surfel of the other side (the ground outside a floor 1 cm above it), and then that surfel is skipped and every neighbour is traced. The screen resolve skips that ray (`SURFEL_SCREEN_LOOKUP`): the screen moves such positions to its side within `SURFEL_POSITION_HOLD`. The lookup's fill from coarser levels traces a ray to each surfel it uses (`ddgi_probe_hidden`, needs `DDGI_VISIBILITY_RAYS` and the TLAS): those cells are 2-4x larger and reach across walls. Measure leaks against DDGI in `addons/examples/lua/examples/render3d/gi_reflections.lua` (the sealed, thin wall, roof gap and two floor rooms) with a locked EV: the mean screen GI of a sealed room should be about as close to 0 as DDGI's.
- Directions are a world space octahedral map over the whole sphere (so parents and children line up across surfels and buckets); texels below the surfel's horizon are marked invalid (alpha -1) and skipped by merges and the integration, texels straddling it are weighted by the share above it.
- **History** (`surfel_hysteresis`) is per frame: a cascade updating every n frames keeps it to the power n, or coarse cascades lag n times longer (a room entered from sunlight took over 10 s to darken).
- **Level blend and holes**: past `surfel_lod_distance` a lookup blends its level and the next across the whole doubling of distance (the touch pass keeps both alive), each weighted by the coverage it found; what is still missing is filled from the next one or two coarser levels (with visibility rays), fading the fill out as the coverage found rises from `surfel_gi.FILL_FULL` to `FILL_NONE` (the result is divided by its coverage anyway, and filling every partial gap made the coarse lookups most of the cost of every lookup), and only a pixel where no level has anything falls back to the sky. The two levels differ most near walls and corners, so a narrow blend band, or refilling holes of the coarse level from the fine one (which stops at the switch), shows as a curve around the camera on walls. Never fade toward the sky by partial coverage: shadowed surfaces next to a hole get a bright ring.
- **Ray start**: rays start `0.01 + 0.05 * cell` along the surfel's normal, which points into open space. Do not shorten it with a ray from the surfel's position: positions reconstructed from depth sit a hair behind their surface, the ray hits that surface and the rays then start behind thin walls. Keep the gbuffer normal for surfels; the depth-derived geometric normal is noisier at grazing angles and leaked more in thin-walled rooms.
- **Masks**: surfel light rays (trace, emitter shadows, the fog gather) leave out decals (`SCENE_REFLECTION_MASK`, all but `MASK_DECAL`; reflections keep them): a decal floats a hair above the ground, and surfels on that ground traced into its underside and stayed black under every decal. Surfel visibility rays (neighbour masks, position moves) only test solid instances (`MASK_SOLID`, like DDGI's `DDGI_VISIBILITY_MASK`): with every instance as opaque, grass cards hid neighbours and pinned positions, which left blocky holes in grass that never filled.
- **Crease samples**: a screen sample at a crease (where two planes meet) lies on both, and far from the origin the depth reconstruction puts it a hair outside one of them (about 1 mm at 100 m), so its rays start behind the other plane and see through it: a sealed room of zero thickness planes then glows along every seam under auto exposure while DDGI stays black. Screen samples are pulled `SURFEL_SAMPLE_PULL` along the sum of their own normal and the normals of the neighbouring pixels whose plane they lie on (off both planes, to their open side); hit samples are pulled back along their ray. Never pull toward the camera: it sees through single sided planes from behind (a camera outside a plane room sees its ceiling), which pulls those samples out of the room. The leak tests compare a sealed room with the same room opened, and a leak a millionth of daylight passes them while still lighting a dark room under auto exposure: check sealed rooms in the game view with auto exposure too.
- Hits are shaded by `shade_scene_reflection_hit` (the reflection hit shading split out of `trace_scene_reflection_hit`), whose GI lookup reads the surfels themselves, which gives the bounces; `SURFEL_UNSCALED` keeps `surfel_strength` out of them.
- **Emitters**: `surfel_emitters` takes `EMITTER_CANDIDATES` emitter samples per cascade 0 surfel per frame into its own history texture (same L1 layout as the irradiance, `surfel_hysteresis`), which the integrate pass adds. Light through an opening (an emitter mostly hidden behind a doorway) is visibility noise: a surfel sees few of its samples, and per surfel history alone leaves cell sized blotches.
- **Filter**: the integrate pass writes `irradiance_raw`, and two neighbour filter passes (`surfel_irradiance_blur`, `surfel_irradiance_filter`) make the irradiance every lookup reads: the 26 neighbours of the same level and bucket, tent weighted, skipping those the visibility masks hide (read from the table, current this frame), plus a plane and normal weight; texel 0's sky visibility is filtered, the masks in texels 1-3 are copied. Histories (atlas, emitter texture) stay unfiltered so the blur does not compound over frames. One pass left emitter blotches behind a doorway, two match DDGI. It barely helps the slow bounce wobble, which is coherent over areas larger than the filter. Without the receiver cosine in the candidate weights most samples land behind the surface and the room is blotchy per cell.
- **Curved surfaces**: every surfel to surfel or point to surfel visibility ray (neighbour masks, own cell check, merges, position switches) lifts both ends by `surfel_curvature_lift` (distance times the normals' difference over 4, about twice a circle's bulge) on top of the fixed bias. Without it the chord between two points on a sphere runs through the sphere, neighbours read as hidden, and each cell's own mask drops a different set: hard lines across spheres (squares on flat surfaces with bumps) that move as masks are re-tested. Equal normals get no lift, so walls and floors on either side of a thin wall keep their tests.
- **Idle surfels**: lookups match the key only and never check whether a surfel is still updated, so a surfel the screen and hit samples stopped touching must not freeze: past `surfel_lifetime` it is refreshed `SURFEL_IDLE_INTERVAL_SCALE` (16) times less often, never not at all. Frozen idle surfels (behind the camera, far ground, room interiors) kept daytime light after a switch to night and fed it back through bounces until the camera flew past them. The state texture's y is the frames since the last update, which every history blend uses as the hysteresis exponent.
- **Moving geometry**: surfels are world space cells, not attached to objects. The update pass reads the boxes of instances that moved this frame (`bridge.CreateDirtyQueue`/`ConsumeDirty`, a queue per consumer; light occlusion has its own; more than 64 boxes means "everything" and surfels then skip it) and every surfel within the box grown by 1.5 cells or half the box's size gets its sample count capped at 1 (next update weighs 1/2), is updated that frame whatever its interval, has its position unconfirmed and retests all 26 neighbours. A change detector catches what moved geometry does not cover (moving lights, light changes): the trace sums each ray's luminance ratio to the direction's history (`ratio_sum`), the next update keeps a fast average of its log2 (`drift`) and above `SURFEL_CHANGE_THRESHOLD` raises the hysteresis exponent `SURFEL_CHANGE_BOOST` times for that update. Measured on a box moved away from a wall: recovery is limited by room wide multi-bounce feedback (every surface catches up through its own history, about 1/(1 - albedo) times the hysteresis time constant), not by the surfels near the box; a lower detector threshold only multiplied false triggers.
- **Cost**: the trace grows with live surfels (all cascades' due surfels times their directions), and every hit is fully shaded, so dense open maps cost several times DDGI's fixed probe budget (the Crysis quarry traced 15 ms at 100k live cascade 0 surfels). `surfel_ray_budget` caps it: the update pass adds each due surfel's rays to a counter in the hit buffer header (three slots rotating per frame) and next frame skips due surfels at random with probability 1 - budget / total; skipped surfels stretch their hysteresis exponent by the same factor, new and moved surfels are never skipped. The fog's surfel gather traces two shaded rays and one emitter sample per cell, and only within `surfel_fog_gather_distance` (faded stochastically over the last quarter, the sky ambient beyond).
- **Hit spawning** (`surfel_hit_spawns`): rays of surfels the screen touched this frame append a sample of their hits (at most `surfel_gi.HIT_SAMPLES`, thinned by last frame's attempt count) that the next frame's touch pass treats like gbuffer samples, so a wall behind the camera has surfels for reflections and bounces before it is seen. Only screen-touched surfels spawn: surfels spawned by hits spawning more would creep over the whole map.
- Limits: surfaces neither on screen nor hit by a screen surfel's ray have no surfels (hits there use the sky fallback), and a cell that holds both sides of a zero thickness wall has one surfel for both. `surfel_debug` shows the cells a pixel reads, the coverage or the sky visibility; the resolve's `surfel_debug` view is in RENDER VIEWS.
- Verify against DDGI in the same scene with a locked EV: mean screen GI of a sealed room with a window and of open ground should be close (`GOLUWA_PVARS="r_gi=surfels"`).

## ReSTIR GI

A per pixel final gather (`goluwa/render3d/restir_gi.lua` for pvars, `passes/restir_gi.lua` for the passes), after ReSTIR GI (Ouyang et al. 2021) with the generalized RIS weights of Lin et al. 2022. It only produces the screen GI: world lookups (fog, water, glass, reflection hits, hits off screen) still go to the cache picked by `r_gi_cache`, whose own screen resolve is skipped. Everything runs at `restir_gi_resolution`; a GI pixel samples one screen pixel of its 2x2 quad, a different one each frame.

- **Passes**: `restir_gi_candidates` traces this frame's candidates (one ray shaded by `shade_scene_reflection_hit`, cosine or probe guided, and one emitter sample) for the pixels whose turn it is (`restir_gi_rays`: every, a checkerboard half, or one of each 2x2), one thread per tracing pixel; `restir_gi_temporal` resamples them against each pixel's reservoir from last frame (a pixel that did not trace enters with confidence 0 and keeps its history); `restir_gi_spatial` combines each pixel's reservoir with `restir_gi_spatial_samples` neighbours, writes irradiance (a pixel without a reservoir yet averages its valid neighbours only) and resamples one reservoir from the same weights, which is next frame's temporal history; `restir_gi_accumulate`, `restir_gi_blur` and `restir_gi_filter` denoise (temporal accumulation with luminance moments, then two edge stopping disc blurs whose radius falls to zero by `restir_gi_filter_history` frames, so only newly revealed pixels are blurred; settled ones would lose contact detail and look screen space blurred, and longer accumulation is the better denoiser for them. A small radius only enlarges the noise into blotches that look worse than pixel noise; it must be wide enough to actually average, 24 pixels). The screen textures follow the GI conventions (E/pi, sky visibility in alpha, geometry for the bilateral upsample); there is no specular texture, rough reflections fall back to the irradiance.
- **Resolution, rays and temporal reuse**: the default is full resolution, every pixel tracing, temporal reuse on. A half resolution GI pixel stands for a different screen pixel of its 2x2 every frame and loses its history in foliage. Temporal reuse is what keeps rare bright samples (sky through a small opening, a mostly hidden emitter) from flashing as fireflies, which the denoiser's accumulation alone leaves as dots. Every reuse step must weigh samples with the same target: the candidates weigh from the lifted ray origin, so the temporal pass rescales the fresh reservoir to the surface point's target before combining (mixed targets darken creases). A pixel's history is capped by how far it moved since last frame (`max_history / (1 + pixels)`), or one sample is carried along the motion and draws streaks. `restir_gi_rays` half or quarter needs temporal reuse: a pixel that did not trace has no sample of its own hemisphere otherwise, and neighbours cannot see its gaps (canopy shade came out at 2/3). With reduced rays, a pixel keeps its reservoir and the receiver it was found from on frames it does not trace, so the next traced frame reconnects it once against a new sample, and it only permutes on frames it traces.
- **Rare light needs spatiotemporal chaining**: where a small share of the rays carries most of the light (a sunlit patch seen through a doorway), each pixel finds it once in hundreds of frames. Temporal history fed only by the temporal pass keeps each pixel's find to itself (a few dozen samples) and shows as slow drifting grain; the spatial pass's resampled reservoir as history spreads one pixel's find to its neighbours and on, so a region learns where the opening is within a few frames. Confidence stays capped at `restir_gi_max_history` when the temporal pass reads it, or chained samples snowball. Check with a converged reference of the same view, not only frame to frame change (slow grain barely changes between frames).
- **Guided rays**: `restir_gi_guided` of the candidate rays are drawn from the nearest DDGI probe's radiance guide (`ddgi_guide_sample`, the same octahedral guide and aim the probes trace with; the probe is picked half a spacing along the normal, on the receiver's side) and every ray is weighted by the mixture density `(1 - share) cos/pi + share * ddgi_guide_density`, which the cosine part keeps positive everywhere, so it is unbiased with any guide. A guided ray below the surface is not traced (it is a sample of zero). Only cosine rays count toward the sky visibility (scaled by their share), or rays aimed at openings raise it. Without the DDGI cache (surfels) every ray is cosine.
- **Moving emitters**: an emitter sample in a reservoir is kept as its triangle and barycentrics and placed on it when read, and its contribution weight crosses frames per area of the emitter, turned into solid angle at the receiver from where the emitter is now. A world space point and a solid angle weight leave samples lighting the spot a moving emitter left, and flaring where it moved closer.
- **Neighbour count**: each spatial neighbour costs two alpha tested visibility rays per pixel (both pairwise directions), most of the spatial pass. With every pixel tracing and the denoiser accumulating, 2 neighbours measured as quiet as 4 or 8 in foliage, emitter lit rooms and during camera motion, and none only somewhat noisier; reuse matters most where the denoiser has no history.
- **Samples are points**: a reservoir holds a hit point, its normal and the radiance it sends (or a sky direction), its contribution weight `W` and confidence `M`. Reuse reconnects the point to the new receiver with the solid angle Jacobian. Rays leave emission to the emitter candidates (`SCENE_HIT_EMISSION` off while emitters exist), so the two candidates cover disjoint parts of the integrand and both enter one reservoir with weight `p_hat * W`; an emitter sample's radiance is emission only, a ray sample's is reflected light only, and anything that retraces a sample (validation) must keep them apart.
- **Weights, not counts**: both reuse steps use balance heuristic weights (temporal: fresh against history; spatial: pairwise MIS of each neighbour against the pixel itself), each strategy's density converted into the receiver's solid angle, with `M` as confidence. Weighting by `M` alone is biased dark in creases (neighbours cannot find the wall right beside a pixel) and, with a receiver that moves every frame, lets a hit point next to it ratchet `W` up frame after frame until it shows as a bright dot. The spatial output is the mean of every candidate's estimate (Rao-Blackwellized), not one picked sample.
- **History** is reprojected for the GI pixel's centre, not the screen pixel it samples this frame (otherwise a still camera resamples it bilinearly every frame and it diffuses into swirls), and the receiver position is stored so temporal reuse can apply the Jacobian. Permutation sampling (the frame swaps reservoirs with a 2x2 neighbour, only for pixels that traced this frame) is off by default: it brings back fireflies, and the motion capped history already stops streaks; `restir_gi_max_history` bounds how long a sample lasts. Never drop or rewrite reservoirs by a test that correlates with their brightness: reservoirs keep samples whose noisy hit shading came out high, so re-shading them under the same `W` (validation) or dropping the brightest of a group (`restir_gi_boiling_filter`) or the ones a retrace from a slightly different origin no longer reaches (light through leaf gaps) darkens the result, 15-50% under a canopy. Both are off by default; a changed light is followed within the history length.
- **Hits read the screen**: a hit the camera saw last frame takes its light past the first bounce from last frame's raw spatial output (`SCENE_HIT_SCREEN_GI` hook in `shade_scene_reflection_hit`), only hits off screen read the cache. Probe caches leak at the foot of walls, and reuse turns such a sample into a dot that lasts its whole history. Never feed back the denoised output: every bounce then waits on its history, and light changes take the accumulation length divided by one minus the bounce gain to settle. A hit closer to the ray's origin than a twentieth of its view depth (the other wall at a crease) reads the accumulated estimate instead: the raw one there is partly the receiver's own light, the reservoirs keep the samples whose noisy read came out high, and with temporal reuse the loop between two walls glows along every crease. Reading the world cache there instead leaves dark lines, the probes are low next to corners.
- **Following light changes**: exponential accumulation follows a drop in as many history lengths as the drop has orders of magnitude, so day to night took seconds while night to day looked instant. Shortening the history by comparing it with the neighbourhood of this frame's estimate does not work in shade lit through gaps: most neighbourhoods miss the rare bright samples, and clamping drops the correct history (a canopy came out at 2/3 of its brightness, twice as noisy). Instead a change of the sun or moon (`restir_gi_light_change`, like DDGI's) caps the history everywhere for 60 frames. Local lights that move or switch are not detected and trail by the accumulation length. Local changes are found the same way, at the source, not per pixel: `restir_gi.GetChangedLights` lists the emitters and local lights that moved, turned or changed brightness in the last second (a turning spot moves everything it lights) (the sphere around their recent path and their intensity), and a pixel that sees such a light (one ray toward the sphere around its path, so lights in the next room do not count) and gets more than a tenth of its last denoised GI from it keeps a few frames of denoiser history and one of reservoir history. The temporal pass decides once and flags the reservoir, the denoiser reads the flag. The test must never read this frame's or a reservoir's own noisy estimate: cutting the history of the dark samples and keeping the bright ones made a dark room several times too bright. A per pixel detector (a short average against the long one, outside the pixel's own noise) fires on heavy tailed noise and brings fireflies back all over static scenes.
- **Cost of the cache at hits**: the world cache lookup at off screen hits is most of the candidate pass (DDGI's visibility rays to bright probes, `ddgi_contrast_ratio`, under a sunlit canopy nearly every probe). `restir_gi_hit_gi_share` below 1 runs it for a whole thread group on a share of frames and scales it up: linear in the lookup, so unbiased, and the lookup is unchanged, so no leaks, but the groups that looked up show as mottling during camera motion (the denoiser has little history), so it defaults to every frame. The group phase must be hashed: a linear pattern lines groups up into screen space diagonal bands. Never thin the probe visibility rays themselves: without them sealed rooms leak, and russian roulette per probe is renormalised by the probe weights and darkens rooms (3% at a quarter, 15% at a tenth). The decision must be the same for the whole group, or a lane that does the lookup holds its warp.
- **Ray origins** are lifted off every neighbouring pixel's plane the pixel lies on (crease pixels reconstruct a hair behind one of the two planes). Visibility rays to a neighbour's hit point aim at the point lifted off its own surface. The pairwise weights trace visibility both ways (this pixel to the neighbour's sample and the neighbour to this pixel's sample): a neighbour credited with a sample it cannot see takes weight from light found through gaps. Rays skip decals (`instances.MASK_SURFACES`): decal and road meshes float a few centimetres above the ground they are blended onto, and rays from the ground hit their black undersides.
- The lighting pass skips the screen space AO and its bounce for diffuse under ReSTIR (`gi_per_pixel`): the rays already hold near field occlusion and light; specular occlusion still uses the AO. DDGI with the AO bounce reads brighter than ReSTIR in enclosed spaces; compare GI against a reference, not against DDGI.
- **Verifying**: `restir_gi_debug` shows the irradiance. A reference is `restir_gi_temporal`, `restir_gi_spatial` and `restir_gi_filter` off with `restir_gi_accumulation` 100000 and a still camera: plain unbiased rays. With temporal reuse on the long average converges slower (a kept sample repeats), so compare strips along creases and walls over 15 s or more, and count fireflies with `MotionRecorder` with TAA off (TAA's clamp hides them). Compare means of the accumulated texture over hundreds of frames, never one frame: the estimator is heavy tailed (sun patches, emitters) and a single frame's mean over half a million pixels still moves 20%. Look at creases in a dark exposure with the contrast stretched; most failures show there first. The raw per-frame estimate (`restir_gi_spatial`) is fp32 and unclamped: next to an emitter one frame's estimate exceeds fp16, and a clamp there darkens everything near emitters, the more the better a sampler finds them; compare emitter samplers with no clamp in the path. `test/tests/render3d/restir_gi.lua` (the slow test compares reuse against plain rays).

## Shadows

- Sun shadow maps are built by `weather.BuildSunShadows`: one map whose cascades follow the camera frustum; cascade 1 is the `exact` one (fixed reach, own size, depth format, min caster size and update interval, redrawn every frame), the rest are `cascades`. Vegetation animates only in the exact cascade; coarser cascades draw plants at rest. Change settings with `weather.SetSunShadow(category, {name = value})` (names are `SUN_SHADOW_DEFAULTS` in `weather.lua`; `enabled`, `count`, `size`, `format` recreate the maps, the rest is written live). The Sun dial window's Shadows tab edits them and shows the reach and texel size. The exact cascade works without the others.
- A cascade fits the bounding sphere of its frustum slice divided by `zoom`; the radius is kept at or above half the slice depth so narrow fields of view still cover mid range. Texel sizes are what matters: compute them from `UpdateCascadeLightMatrices` before choosing a layout.
- The lookup is pushed toward the sun by `texel_world_size * 0.25` (in `directional_shadows.lua`); a full texel push leaked light under ceilings in far cascades. Thin geometry leaks in far cascades scale with texel size: look at receiver offsets before adding cascades. Translucency in shadow maps is dithered by `Material:GetShadowOpacity`. Prefer physically derived filtering (PCSS with the sun's real angular size) over fixed filters even when it changes the old look.
- Contact shadows march the depth buffer toward each light.
- The sun shadow map is blind in three places: occluders within a few texels (single sided walls meeting at a corner or pierced by beams, common in Source 2 maps, leave the occluder's silhouette on the receiver), occluders inside the soft filter's receiver plane tolerance (slope times filter radius, up to a meter at grazing angles on round geometry), and the soft filter's penumbra estimate, which averages near and far blockers and spreads light from a nearby gap over points a close occluder fully hides. No bias fixes these. A ray toward the sun from every lit point covers them: `bias.contact` shadow texels, the tolerance the lookup used (`shadow_ray_reach`), and blockers closer than `texel / sun_tan` (whose penumbra is under a texel, so one ray is exact). It only tests solid instances, and only for the sun. `single_sided_room` in `addons/examples/lua/examples/render3d/gi_reflections.lua` reproduces every case without game assets (inward facing planes on stilts, beams and a pipe under a slotted roof, balls half in a wall, in an oversized wall hole plugged by a `ViewHidden` blocker and sunk in a mirror floor, and a scaffold far overhead whose stripes make the soft filter mix near and far blockers); compare zoomed shots with the contact ray (`bias.contact` 0) and the blocker off.
- Ray origins off a gbuffer surface move by a fraction of a millimetre scaled by view distance (enough for depth reconstruction error), never by centimetres: in Source 2 maps props and floors meet within millimetres, and a centimetre offset starts sun or reflection rays on the far side of the floor or wall, where they see the sunlit outside.
- Glass tints light with a ray, not a shadow map: while `r_glass_light` is on, glass casts no shadow of its own (`Material.GlassCastsShadow()` false) and `get_glass_light` (see `glass_light.lua`) traces only instances with the glass mask bit (`instances.MASK_GLASS`, set for `Material:IsGlass()`, refractive and not additive, which includes Source `refract` water), so decals and cutouts cost it nothing. Surface lighting skips it for a light whose contribution is under `GLASS_LIGHT_MIN_SCREEN_RADIANCE`, since glass only darkens. A large glass mesh still makes every bright light's ray near it walk that mesh. Where it runs: the lighting and translucent passes, `ddgi_shade` (sun, lights, emitters, probe rays) and scene reflections. A penumbra can reach into an opaque frame's footprint, so it traces a few offset rays (`search_radius`). `ddgi.HasGlass()` gates every ray so scenes without glass pay nothing. Without ray queries glass falls back to the dithered shadow.

## Sky, atmosphere, fog, clouds

- **Atmosphere** (`atmosphere.lua`, `atmosphere_scattering.lua`): sun illuminance times `GetTransmittance(sun_dir)` times `SUN_COLOR` (one physical sun color, luminance 1, used for direct light, sky, fog, clouds, disc and rain). The transmittance LUT is parameterised by distance to the atmosphere top (Bruneton) so the horizon is resolved, and rays below the local horizon return 0 in the lookup. Multiple scattering is a precomputed table (`scattering.LOOKUP_GLSL` / `sc_lookup`), baked on aerosol change; single scattering is marched in `integrate_scattering`. Visibility is two parameters in meters: `atmosphere.SetAerosolVisibility` (clear air) and `SetGroundFogVisibility` (the ground hugging layer); climates and the `atmosphere_controller` take the same pair. Sky LUT caches must be baked at the quantised key they are keyed by, or the result depends on who asked first. The sky has no ground: rays below the horizon use `get_sky_lookup_dir`; the environment probe draws a ground (`get_ground_radiance`), never over an environment map, which holds its own lower half. Marching code that integrates atmosphere must clip at the planet (density functions clamp altitude at 0, so underground air integrates at ground density); every consumer of `integrate_scattering` must do it. Tables are for a unit sun: multiply by `ATMOSPHERE_SUN_ILLUMINANCE * SUN_COLOR`. `envprobe.UpdateEnvironmentAltitude` moves the environment probe to the camera's height so high-up reflections and ambient are right. `weather.SetEnvironmentMap(path)` (an HDRI) disables sun and atmosphere; intensity defaults to 1000 cd/m2 per unit because probes clamp at 65504. Equirect samplers must clamp vertically.
- **Froxel volume** (`passes/volumetric_fog.lua`, `froxel_fog.lua`): one medium holds clear air and ground fog (extinction from `ground_fog_visibility` at the fog's start height, falling off with `GROUND_FOG_SCALE_HEIGHT` above it, constant below it, faded out between the end height minus 0.3 km and the end height; all three are runtime values, `atmosphere.SetGroundFogStartHeight`, `SetGroundFogEndHeight` (default start plus 1.1 km), `SetGroundFogScaleHeight`, and `atmosphere_controller` properties) out to `froxel_fog.FAR` (100 km), lit by the shadowed sun (including cloud shadows), DDGI probes where they exist and the analytic sky ambient in open air (mixed by overhead sky visibility). With surfel GI there is nothing to look up in the air (surfels live on surfaces), so in surfel mode `volumetric_froxel_gi` traces two opposite rays per cell instead (the upper one's miss is the sky visibility, a single ray cannot estimate a 0..1 visibility without clamping bias), shades hits with `shade_scene_reflection_hit` and adds one emitter sample; the medium lookup is DDGI only. Its texels are -1 where nothing is known (hidden or outside the GI range). DDGI is looked up by its own pass (`volumetric_froxel_gi`) on a grid of half the froxel width and height (each cell evaluates one random froxel of its 2x2 per frame, the temporal pass averages them) through `ddgi_sample_medium` (`ddgi.GetMediumGLSL`): one probe loop and one visibility ray per probe give both the mean radiance (irradiance at a random direction and its opposite) and the overhead sky visibility, with no surface normal weighting. Keep DDGI lookups out of the per-froxel scatter: their visibility rays dominate the cost of the fog. The integrate pass writes per channel in-scatter (first half of a double-depth 3D texture) and transmittance (second half): consumers go through `get_volumetric_fog` / `froxel_sample`, never `texture()` on the volume. The fog lights the air from the primary directional light (the moon once it outranks the sun's direct light, e.g. sun just below the horizon) plus the other body (sun or moon) as a second term in the froxel scatter (the multiple scattering terms, primary and other, are scaled by one jittered ray per froxel from the froxel GI pass toward the dominant sky glow body (the sun above -11 degrees, else the moon), raised above the horizon when the body is below it, so ridges and buildings between the air and the horizon glow block it; stored in the emitter texture's alpha, the terrain shadow is the fallback without ray queries) and the sky tail (`integrate_air`); without it a twilight sky goes black. Rule: every in-scatter term read from the precomputed tables (`sc_lookup`, `integrate_scattering`) assumes open sky and is unoccluded unless scaled by a visibility toward its own source direction (shadow maps only cover the primary light's direct term, overhead sky visibility says nothing about a ridge between the air and the horizon glow); a new light or term added through the tables brings the glowing haze in front of mountains back. Sky pixels are composed (volume + transmittance * tail past FAR + layer transmittance * background), not subtracted from the sky LUT; the tail is integrated for every ray that leaves the atmosphere past FAR, never gated by the altitude the ray has at FAR (at twilight only the high air is sunlit, and a gate shows as a hard line across the sky); with clouds the sum splits at the cloud depth. Never use a single point shadow lookup for a medium segment. Noise: the temporal pass blurs each froxel along the sun direction (fading with depth, `line_fade`) plus a light neighbour blur, history is weighted by a validity alpha, scatter picks a random corner for occluded froxels (history lookups stay deterministic). The depth that clamps a froxel to the surface and marks it covered (`froxel_point`) is the depth pass's farthest depth over the tile and its margin, never a jittered pixel sample: a few samples per froxel miss alpha tested holes smaller than the sample spacing, so the froxels behind the geometry flip between clamped to the surface and open air every frame and the sky seen through the holes flickers and darkens. Check it with a plane of small round holes against the sky, aa, ao and gi off, flicker measured with `MotionRecorder` against the same scene with `r_pass_volumetric_fog` off. `froxel_fog.TILE` sets sharpness and cost. The fog's light is a fitted model (`get_ground_fog_sun` octaves, `get_ground_fog_sky_ambient`, `get_ground_fog_sun_transmittance`, which also dims direct light on surfaces by height), referenced against a path tracer; change it only with a reference to compare to.
- **Fog volumes** (`fog_volume` component, `fog_volumes.lua`, a `Medium` from `media.presets` such as `fog`, `steam`, `smoke` or `dust`, a `Density` in 1/m, `EdgeSoftness` and a `HeightFalloff` scale height, 0 is uniform): an oriented box of a participating medium inside the froxel volume. The scatter pass adds `scattering * density` lit by the sun (attenuated through the volumes by `optical_range_tint`, with the phase of the medium), the ambient and the local lights (`get_fog_volume_light_scattering`, the shared loop with the volume tint as its attenuation, so lamps are shadowed by the shadow maps and dimmed by the fog on the way) and the integrate pass adds the per channel `(absorption + scattering) * density` to the extinction, so smoke darkens and dust tints what is behind it. The same boxes are `optical_volume` records of kind 1 in the ray material prefix (centred box, uniform density with the falloff sampled at the middle of the path), so the lighting pass, the probes and the reflection hit rays see the sun and lamps through them. The layer is also a volume record (kind 3 in the optical prefix: start and top height in world metres, the scale height, the tint times the extinction per metre, the precipitation extinction and the albedo; `write_layer_record`, present while the extinction is above 0), and `optical_inscatter` applies every fog volume and the layer to a ray: `get_optical_tint` multiplies the transmittance (the layer only for rays that hit something, `with_layer`, because a ray that misses samples the environment map, which is baked with the layer) and `optical_inscatter` adds `(ambient + sun * visibility * transmittance to the sun * phase) * albedo` per volume over three segments (`T(0, s_k) - T(0, s_k+1)` weights each segment's own source), the sun visibility being one shadow ray per segment at its middle (`optical_inscatter_lit`, `get_medium_sun_visibility`), with the closed form optical depth of the exponential profile for the layer (`optical_layer_depth`). The DDGI probe, surfel, ReSTIR and reflection hit rays all use it, so fog volumes and the layer light and dim bounce and reflection hits the same way; the sun skips the layer (`get_glass_light`) because it applies it analytically while the other lights dim by it (`get_glass_light_local`, and the froxel lamp in-scatter through `optical_layer`), the layer top is cut halfway into the atmosphere's fade (`GROUND_FOG_TOP_SOFTNESS`), and the froxel volume keeps reading the atmosphere. Every hit ray takes the ambient from the environment irradiance over pi (`ddgi_fog_ambient`) and the sun from the atmosphere or the DDGI sun. The sun transmittance through the layer is one function (`exponential_layer_transmittance` in `medium_common`, used by the atmosphere and by the DDGI direct light). The ground fog layer takes its extinction tint, albedo and phase from `media.presets.fog` (a `Scattering` and `Absorption` per channel), and every consumer of the layer is per channel: the froxel integrate pass, the sun transmittance through the layer (`ground_fog_sun_transmittance`, `ddgi_ground_fog_sun_transmittance`, the surface lighting, the particles and the DDGI direct light) and the non froxel segment integral (`GroundFogSegment`). The precipitation extinction is added to the same extinction, so it is tinted with the layer in the froxel integral but grey in the sun transmittance.
- **Snow** (`surface_weather.lua`, `snow_cover.lua`): `weather.SetSnowDepth(meters)` whitens surfaces in the surface shader, masked by the shelter map (an orthographic depth map from the precipitation direction, the top surface within 96 m of the camera). Dynamic instances are in it, so snow caps them and leaves a sheltered pit under them. With depth it is relief: when the shelter map re-centres or the depth changes, a compute pass (recorded in the frame's command buffer; `SubmitAndWait` there stalled 30 ms) turns the shelter depth into a snow map, rg = snow top and top surface in light space depth, lifted by the depth on ground flatter than about 60 degrees and tapered to nothing over 1.5 times the depth towards any ledge, drops (roof and wall edges) and rises (wall bases, the footprint of an overhang) alike, so snow falls off smoothly into shelter instead of ending in a wall; the shelter map cannot tell a wall from an overhang, so snow no longer banks against walls. Every gbuffer fragment marches its view ray in light space (`march_snow_cover`, a step of a twentieth of the depth, at most `snow_march_steps` steps (pvar, 128) over the part of the ray inside the shelter map; two levels of min tiles (8 and 64 texels, `snow_cover.lua`, holding the highest snow top of a tile and its margin) let a ray that stays above a tile skip to its exit, snapped to the step lattice so skipped and marched rays sample the same points. The march is bound by dependent texture fetches, so cost follows the number of fine steps (grazing views over a long window are the expensive ones, and a cap of 256 doubled the cost of one such view for a smoother silhouette): coarser steps scallop the rim of a drift seen at a grazing angle, for example from below; the fine window ahead of the fragment is 1.2 times the depth over the cosine to the precipitation direction, floored at 0.02, and 48 coarse steps cover the rest of the ray back to the eye, because snow on higher ground between the eye and a far fragment is outside that window and would let the geometry behind a deep drift show through) and is snow where a point lies between the snow top and the surface under it, so caps hang over the walls below them. The test blends each texel's own inside distance (bilinearly while marching, with a 4x4 cubic B-spline at the fragment's own position and in the bisection, which rounds outlines and caps; B-spline on every step costs about 2 ms) clamped to the ledge jump (on one surface that is the bilinear snow top, across a ledge the outline runs between texel centres instead of in steps), with 5 cm of tolerance under the top surface (more only for an upward facing fragment at its own position) capped by the snow's thickness: filtering the snow top itself or a texel sized tolerance hangs snow curtains under every balcony, and an uncapped tolerance whitens steep faces in texel sized patches (acne, worst with wind slanting the map). Two separable passes smooth the snow top with a Gaussian over texels on the same surface (top within the ledge jump), radius half the depth, at most 24 texels, which rounds caps and the taper steps of the 9 cm shelter texels (`SHELTER_SIZE` 2048 over 192 m). An edge pass writes, per texel, a Gaussian blurred coverage of its own surface; after the blur the thickness is multiplied by a smoothstep of it, so snow eases to zero at a ledge (the blur skips ledge neighbours, so an edge texel would otherwise keep the thickness of its interior neighbours and the snow would end in a step that ambient occlusion picks up). The snow normal is a finite difference of the snow top whose neighbours fade out smoothly across a ledge; sampling across one made the shading jagged along every edge. The shelter map only redraws when the camera crosses a snap step, so a scratch scene that spawns geometry and never moves the camera has no relief (move the view away and back before a shot). The snow map is rebuilt when the shelter map was actually redrawn (`last_rendered_frame`), not when it moved; a fixed delay read stale depth. The gbuffer only marches fragments of geometry, so snow in front of the sky or of far geometry it buries is drawn by a backdrop (`snow_backdrop.lua`): a sphere of 90 m around the camera queued in the gbuffer pass (`Draw3DGeometry`, `gbuffer_instancing.Queue`) with a `SnowBackdrop` material, which writes fragment depth like a decal or relief material. Its fragments run the normal snow march and are discarded on a miss, or when the ray rises more than 0.5 along the precipitation direction, so a hit is a real gbuffer surface with the same lighting, gi, shadows and texture as the snow around it. It adds a march per pixel while snow lies, since writing depth disables early depth rejection. A hit forces snow cover by its thickness (full at `SNOW_FULL_COVER_DEPTH`), so the cover fades out with the taper instead of ending in a line. A hit writes its depth (lighting reconstructs the position from it; without it the shading happened at the buried surface and shadows speckled), which the culling pass gets by routing every main view row to its nearer pipeline variant while a snow map exists: the height map pipeline promises `depth_less` for shells, and snow moves depth nearer. Snow cannot stick out against the sky, nothing behind it marches. The default climate wind is 0; wind slants the shelter map and the snow with it.
- **Clouds** (`clouds.lua`, `passes/clouds.lua`; layers via `weather.SetCloudLayers`, presets in `climate.lua`): noise bake, beer shadow map, sky dome, quarter-res checkerboard trace with half-res reconstruct, composited in the fog pass. The sun is dimmed per pixel by `get_cloud_shadow` (surface lighting, the medium shadow function, DDGI shade). Rules learned: baked noise must be stretched to 0..1; lattice streaks need the domain warp with the warp mip offset by its frequency; the sun disc is ~1e5-1e6 times a cloud's brightness, so transmittance must fade out smoothly (not cut off) near zero; detail volume resolution (`clouds.DETAIL_NOISE_SIZE`) limits close-up detail more than step count; the main-view detail is filtered by pixel footprint, not step size. Cost knobs: `cloud_layer_steps`, `cloud_detail_min_mip`, `clouds.VIEW_DIVISOR`.

## Reflections, translucency, materials

- **Reflections** are two passes in `passes/reflections.lua`: `reflections_trace` (one thread per block of `reflection_resolution`, raw samples written per pixel at full resolution) and `reflections` (spatial disc filter over the blocks' traced pixels, temporal history, both per pixel at full resolution; keep them separate, a shared-memory tile filter shows a grid at tile borders). A block traces the rays of one pixel, rotating each frame (`get_reflection_gbuffer_pos`); a block whose pixels all need a single ray (smooth) traces every pixel, one ray each, the same ray budget as one rough pixel. Untraced pixels are written with alpha -1. Never keep the history at the block resolution: the rotating pixel then averages the block's four reflections and a normal mapped mirror turns into a blur. Screen space (`reflection_screenspace_roughness`) is traced first when above 0, rays fill the rest; with 0 and ray queries the nearest scene hit is authority. Above `reflection_gi_threshold` (fading over `reflection_gi_fade`) a reflection is the GI/environment light instead of traced, except metals (`reflection_trace_metals`, `get_reflection_traced_weight`): the GI stand-in is irradiance around the dominant direction, a cosine lobe far wider than a rough GGX lobe that sees the sky a rough metal does not, so a metal crossing the threshold changes brightness. Specular occlusion (from AO) multiplies only the GI share; traced rays already find their occluders. Do not weight or clamp samples by brightness relative to roughness (a firefly weight of `1 / (1 + lum / k)` halves the mean of a reflection that mixes a sunlit and a shaded surface); hits are limited only at `SCENE_REFLECTION_MAX_HIT_LUMINANCE` times exposure white, the environment has no sun disc. Rays hit shading: albedo at the ray cone's mip, alpha test, single bounce, lambert from the scene material (`ddgi_material`), so surface feature lobes and transmission do not appear in reflections or GI. Traced hits get analytic ground fog (`apply_ground_fog_ray`, `r_rt_reflection_fog`) and read the environment at `roughness * (1 - rough_mix)` (the direction is already sampled over the lobe, blurring twice is wrong). Max ray distance `r_rt_reflection_distance`; rays only see the scene store within `r_rt_distance`. The history is read where the *image* sits behind the surface (`reflection_hit_reprojection`), and its alpha is clamped to the neighbouring alphas (otherwise silhouettes fill with environment light as halos). Odd sizes: block bounds round up, never truncate the ratio (`get_reflection_gbuffer_pos`, `get_reflection_block` in `ibl.lua`). `addons/examples/lua/examples/render3d/reflection_plates.lua` has mirrors with and without normal maps, roughness sweeps and a dirty metal. Against a numerical GGX reference (height correlated Smith plus Kulla-Conty multiple scattering, white metal under an environment that is a step at the horizon) the traced ray average is within about 2% up to roughness 0.9 and about 6% at 1 (it gives the multiple scattering energy the single scattering lobe's shape), while the GI stand-in is off by 20% at roughness 0.5 and 40% at 0.4 and only within about 3% from 0.7 up. A history clamp box built from the spatial samples alone biases a lobe whose bright samples are rare (a few rays reaching a bright region) towards the frames that missed them (15% darker with 4 rays, 40% with 1); the box also takes the history's own spread from a second luminance moment kept with it, which brings every ray count within a few percent. Only rough pixels get that widening: a mirror's single ray is deterministic, its spread over time is motion, and widening its box lets the history smear the reflection when the camera moves. With that, a very rough pixel traces a single ray (`reflection_single_ray_roughness`): the trace's cost follows the ray count, so this saves what tracing rough surfaces at quarter density would, while keeping a sample in every block (skipping blocks instead leaves lanes idle in every warp). A rough reflection's history is not moved with the reflected image, so motion lowers its weight, but only by the image's shift relative to the reflection's own blur: a shift smaller than the blur cannot show as lag, and cutting the history for it shows the one ray noise as crawling grain while the camera moves. The filter disc turns every frame so its taps do not stay fixed on screen.
- **Translucent surfaces** (`passes/translucent.lua`, moments/accumulate with `translucent_extras.lua`): refraction is exact unpolarised dielectric Fresnel; a thin wall is a pane, a solid has far-side Fresnel and total internal reflection; absorption comes from the material's `Medium` (Beer-Lambert, `optical_transmittance`) or its albedo; with ray queries a refractive body takes its refraction from its own geometry: `get_glass_body_exit` (a ray query over the glass masked instances along the refracted direction, per colour channel with dispersion) returns the distance to the nearest back face of a glass or medium body and its smoothed outward normal (`scene_hit_smooth_normal`, the interpolated vertex normals), the exit position and the exit refraction use that point and normal, the absorption of a material with a medium runs over that distance, and an exit face parallel to the entry face (`dot > 0.98`) takes the slab formula with its inner reflections; this replaces the curvature sphere and slab model and the thin pane model for every closed body, a single sheet or a miss falls back to them (`RefractionThickness`, the curvature estimate, `MEDIUM_PANE_PATH`); a refracting material has one `Roughness` (rough glass averages several rays with hashed, not interleaved-gradient, noise). They trace their own mirror ray (`scene_reflection` direction/lobe code), have no denoiser of their own (TAA averages the rays). A graphics pipeline has one descriptor set unless `DescriptorSetCount` says more; sets used by a command buffer cannot be rewritten, so per-frame TLAS/material bindings need a set per swapchain image. Diffuse transmission (`features/transmission.lua`) is for leaves and cloth: a normalised lobe lights the front with light arriving at the back, and the opaque diffuse is scaled by `1 - T`.
- **Gbuffer roughness**: `gbuffer_roughness()` returns GGX alpha (readers `sqrt` it for perceptual); the raw channel holds perceptual roughness and is written through `gbuffer_encode_roughness(alpha)`.
- **Subsurface scattering** (`features/subsurface.lua`, `passes/subsurface.lua`) is a screen space diffusion. The lighting pass writes the diffuse light (direct plus ambient, divided by albedo, pre-exposed) of every pixel with `Subsurface > 0` to its second target with the strength in alpha. `subsurface_blur` blurs it horizontally and `subsurface` vertically with a heavy tailed profile (two exponentials per channel, in millimeters; the material's `SubsurfaceColor` sets how far each channel travels, normalised to luminance 1 like the transmission tint, and `r_subsurface_width` times `SubsurfaceRadius` scales it, neither is clamped). The gbuffer is near its 8 target limit, so the tint and radius are 4 bit fields, then adds `strength * (blurred - original) * albedo` to the lighting color in place, so everything that reads the lit scene (ocean, previews, history) sees it. Blurring irradiance and re-applying albedo keeps pores and texture detail sharp. `SubsurfaceSkinTone` multiplies the strength by how skin-like the albedo is per pixel (hue, saturation, brightness), which separates skin from hair, eyes and clothes inside one texture; the Source VMT loader sets it for flesh and player class materials. Taps are rejected by depth and by neighbours without the feature. The effect is faint on smooth lit surfaces; it shows at shadow edges, terminators and fine features, and a larger `SubsurfaceRadius` with a wide `SubsurfaceColor` (wax, marble) makes it obvious. Specular is untouched, and reflections and GI rays do not see it. Thin back lit skin (ears) is `DiffuseTransmission`, a separate term. A change to the gbuffer shaders can take minutes of driver pipeline compile on the first run.
- **Shading model** (`surface_lighting.lua`, `ibl.lua`): GGX D, height-correlated Smith visibility, Schlick Fresnel with `F90(f0)` (a fixed F90 of 1 makes `SpecularMultiplier 0` surfaces sheen edge-on), Kulla-Conty style energy compensation applied to direct *and* ambient specular (`GGXEnergyCompensation = 1 / (1 - (1 - E) F_avg)`), and a Fresnel-coupled EON diffuse (`Fd_EON`, energy preserving Oren-Nayar) for direct light; the ambient diffuse is Lambert since irradiance is already a cosine integral. When a surface looks washed out, render it with black albedo and remove terms (specular, transmission, fog) one at a time to find additive light; thin geometry seen edge-on gets large direct GGX specular.
- **Local lights**: a point light's `Lumen` is flux, intensity `Lumen / (4 pi)`, falling off as `1 / (d^2 + source_radius^2)` times Frostbite's window (`range`; the automatic range is where the light reaches ~0.0005 lux). Spots integrate the cone including the smoothstep taper (`GetEmissionSolidAngle`), so a lamp emits exactly its lumens. Light color is divided by its luminance so hue does not change brightness. Lights, their shadow maps and occlusion maps read the entity's world transform (`GetWorldPosition`, `GetWorldRotation`), so a light can be a nested prefab node or a child of a prop.
- **Emissive surfaces**: emissive luminance is `color * strength (* albedo alpha) * EMISSIVE_REFERENCE_LUMINANCE`; an emissive surface is not a light, its effect on surroundings is GI only. Probe spacing causes an overshoot directly under tiny emitters. A material with `EmitsLight` false still glows but is not an emitter. Source maps author a light entity next to every bulb, so `source_engine/fixture_emission.lua` clears the flag on a material once most of its emissive area (half) lies within 0.5 m of a light entity; everything else (screens, signs, windows, lone lamps) keeps emitting. It also sets `IgnoredByLightOcclusion`: the instance mask bit `MASK_LIGHT_BODY` that the light occlusion trace skips, because a light placed inside its own bulb mesh would otherwise see that mesh on every ray.
- **Ambient occlusion** weighs below-horizon directions by `BELOW_HORIZON_WEIGHT` and the probes' sky visibility (the ground is already in the probes' lobes, so counting it as an occluder twice darkens near-ground surfaces); the bent normal of an unoccluded surface must equal its normal. A slice whose directions all weigh 0 (a ceiling, every direction below the horizon, with the GI reporting some sky visibility) uses the unweighted open fraction: dividing gave 0/0 and a black ceiling.
- Validate shading changes against references: a ColorChecker chart under a locked EV compared with analytic matte values, furnace rooms (walls that emit and reflect, nothing may look brighter or darker than the walls; `reflection_furnace.lua` in the examples), and a random-walk multiple scattering reference for metals. Scratch scripts in `./tmp/`.

## Water

Ocean and `water_volume` components are both drawn by the `water` compute pass (`passes/water.lua`, with its denoiser `water_resolve`; the wave passes keep the `ocean_` prefix). Sea state is in `goluwa/render3d/water.lua` and the water presets in `goluwa/render3d/media.lua`; the in-scatter of the local lights and the specular highlights of lamps on the surface in the water pass use the loop the fog and particles use (`get_medium_light_visibility`) (`medium_lighting.GetLocalLightsGLSL` with the water phase and the underwater attenuation as `options`: point and local directional shadow maps and light occlusion decide the visibility, the water block carries the `scene_lights` shadow layout and the occlusion block and binds the occlusion map), so a light in the water is shadowed by what shadows it in the air; a water volume or the ocean is also a participating medium for the light tint walk: the ray material buffer starts with a typed prefix, the one place the volumes are written (`optical_ranges` gives the first index and count of the water and the fog volumes after the ocean record; a record carries everything a consumer needs: for water the absorption, scattering, phase, index of refraction, Abbe number, roughness, foam, caustics, wave length and direction, flow, whether it is closed and the surface height, for fog the scattering, phase, edge softness and height falloff; the water pass and both froxel passes read it through their own storage binding of that buffer, `ddgi.BindOpticalVolumes`, the water pass with ray queries through its materials binding, and `water.GetNearestVolumes` picks the nearest when there are more than `water.MAX_VOLUMES`). The prefix holds the header (`optical_counts` with the glass, medium body and volume counts, so a ray skips a query with nothing to find, then `optical_volume` records: the oriented box, the extinction `media.GetExtinction` (absorption, the molecular scattering `Molecular` of the preset, which only water presets have, and the particle scattering; the molecular part has its own phase, `medium_scatter_phase`), the index of refraction and the wave height, the tint averages three top heights `-h, 0, +h` for the wave statistics) and the material array after it (`ddgi.GetMaterialEntries`, `ddgi.GetOpticalVolumes`; material indices are unchanged); `get_glass_light` (every direct light) multiplies the path inside each volume, refracted at the top for a light above the water (the in-water path of a light ray is `depth / cos(theta_water)`), and `get_optical_tint` (probe shading, reflection, ReSTIR and surfel hit rays) adds the medium bodies, both through `optical_transmittance`, so the lighting pass and the probes carry the depth falloff and the water pass `relight_submerged` only adds the sun refraction and caustics (`sun_tinted`, `gi_tinted`, it falls back to its own `exp(-sigma * depth)` without ray queries); examples `water_showcase.lua`, `ocean_shore.lua`, `water_volume_test.lua`, `twilight_lake.lua`.

- The wave field is three inverse-FFT tiles (`water.FFT_CASCADES`) built from spectra on the CPU when ocean params change (`water.BuildOceanWaves`); the three wave textures sample them and write height, slope and foam. Foam is a world space field with memory (per cascade foam pass), driven by the Jacobian of waves 4 m and longer and the wind (`water.GetWhitecapFactor`). `water.SampleOcean` (buoyancy) sums the strongest modes on the CPU, so bodies follow the visible sea but not its smallest waves. Ocean level is the *mean* surface.
- The trace from above is a sphere trace through the slab `level +- wave_bound` (`height_map_tracing`) with a mean-plane fallback; never let a ray with no crossing show what is behind the sea. The camera start is the near plane (`WATER_NEAR_PLANE`) per pixel, so the waterline is a soft edge instead of a whole-screen flip.
- Reflections: screen first (`r_water_screenspace_roughness`), marching the depth buffer and reading the last fogged frame (`post_source.GetFoggedOpaqueSceneTexture`), rays fill where the screen finds nothing; the water shares the reflection pass's direction code and a denoiser (`water_resolve`, radius scaled by `water_rt_share`). Water lit through what covers it: the ambient is sampled at a few points along the path with probe visibility rays and sun shafts trace toward the sun, otherwise water under a roof glows like open sky. A volume at sea level blends into the ocean's surface within `VOLUME_EDGE_BLEND` of its sides (`mix_water`).
- Cost: use the `r_water_*` feature pvars (bits of `ocean_data.features`, so toggling does not recompile) to find what is expensive; compare variants in one process, interleaved with baselines (the gpu clock drifts several ms inside a process). The big costs are inscatter (shaft caustics, ambient GI), refraction, ripples and the reflection trace; `r_water_waves` off is a flat mirror.
- Pitfalls: never `smoothstep(e, e, x)` (undefined); phase angle sign is `dot(-view_dir, light_dir)`; negative values (not just NaN/inf) cause bright discs through bloom (`integrate_attenuated` must not divide by a negative); flat apparent-depth refraction in screen space tears at edges (refraction only offsets the straight-behind pixel); the submerged-floor test must be "is the point inside the volume"; discrete sine waves show grids, use advected gradient noise ripples; shader recompiles can take minutes, run shots in the background.
- Map importers turn game fog into a medium with `media.MediumFromFog`. More than `water.MAX_VOLUMES` volumes: the nearest are drawn.
