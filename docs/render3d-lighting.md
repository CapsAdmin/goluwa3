# Render3D lighting, GI, fog, sky and water

Notes collected from past sessions. Dated numbers are snapshots; re-measure before relying on them.

## DDGI long range cascades and faceted bent normals

*10 cascades from 0.5 m, sun-change triggered updates, bent normal rotated onto the smooth normal*

- 2026-10-14: `ddgi.CASCADES` 10, `ddgi_probe_spacing` 0.5, ratio 2 (0.5 m to 256 m spacing, about 6 km wide), update intervals 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64 x4, `ddgi_max_coverage` and `ddgi_max_ray_distance` 4000. Cascades are fitted to the scene bounds and the ones that already cover everything are dropped, so small maps don't pay. A pixel samples at most two cascades.
- Slow far cascades are rescued by `ddgi_change_threshold` (0.05, sun direction in radians plus relative brightness, 0 = off) and `ddgi_change_min_interval` (2): a cascade updates early when the sun moved that much since its last update. Local lights, emissives and the sky map are not tracked, `ddgi.ResetHistory()` resets all.
- The resolve looks up probes along the AO bent normal. The AO pass computes it against `get_geometric_normal` (depth derivatives, flat per triangle), which showed as faint vertical bands on a low poly sphere. The AO pass now rotates the bent normal from the geometric to the shading normal before writing it (`rotate_between`).

**How to apply:** faint banding that follows mesh facets in GI means something in the lookup uses a depth-derived normal. Related: [DDGI software trace](#ddgi-software-trace).

## DDGI software trace

*only GI left; compute scene_bvh fallback; GOLUWA_NO_RAY_TRACING=1 to test*

Since 2026-09-27 radiance cascades and voxel GI are deleted; DDGI is the only GI. On a gpu without ray tracing `passes/ddgi.lua` swaps the ray tracing `ddgi_trace` pass for a compute one over `scene_bvh` (same hit buffer), and the shade pass shadows local lights with `scene_bvh_occluded`. Probe visibility rays are still skipped without ray queries.

`GOLUWA_NO_RAY_TRACING=1 luajit glw ...` makes the device report no ray tracing/ray query, so every fallback (ddgi, reflections, fog, lighting) can be tested on this machine; the env var reaches test workers. `test/tests/render3d/ddgi.lua` passes in both modes.

**Why:** the user prefers DDGI over RC and wanted the non-RT path working first, optimizing it later.
**How to apply:** when touching DDGI or anything gated on `ray_query_supported`/`ray_tracing_supported`, run the tests with and without the env var. Open items are in docs/refactoring-notes.md (GI section).

## DDGI emitter sampling

*additive FX were 99.9% of emitter power; filter + grid; ddgi_emitter_info*

Faint constant probe flashing on the Dear Esther crystal cave (2026-10-01) was the emitter samples, and the root cause was not the sampler.

- `Lightshaft_005` (an additive billboard) held 4.09e6 of 4.096e6 total emitter power. scene_bvh treats any additive material, or albedo-alpha-emissive, or emissive texture as emissive; DDGI then lights with it at emissive x albedo x 2000 cd/m2. Waterfalls, flowing water, wake masks, glass and sparkles were the same kind of mistake. The real crystals total about 250.
- With power-proportional global draws, every draw was the shaft, and a crystal drawn that way weighed g x total / (1 - share): fireflies for any grid share below 1.
- Fix: scene_bvh's Emitter records `additive` (additive and not albedo-alpha-emissive and no emissive texture); ddgi leaves those out unless `ddgi_additive_emitters 1`. Total power went 4.096e6 to 245.7 and the noise was gone.
- `ddgi_emitter_info` prints totals, the grid, and every emissive block with its materials' flags (blocks left out are marked). Use it first when emitter noise shows up.
- The emitter grid (`ddgi_emitter_grid`, `ddgi_emitter_cell_size`, a hash of 4 m cells the candidates were drawn from) and the resampled candidates (`ddgi_emitter_candidates`, 8, one kept by the light it would bring) were removed 2026-10-15: they were built for the shaft's tail (above), measured no better once the additive effects were out (30 tiny emitters 1.5 m apart: 1.4 to 2.4 percent frame to frame noise with them, 2.5 to 3.8 without; 30 emitters 25 m apart none was right and the default was the noisiest). An emitter sample is now an emitter drawn by its power (`ddgi_pick_emitter`, a cdf in `ddgi.GetEmitters`, 3 words an emitter after a 2 word header: total power and count), a point on its triangle, weight `facing / max(d^2, r^2) * total power` (`ddgi_pick_emitter_sample`), the shade pass rebuilds the point from the same random numbers. What it does not do is find the emitters near a probe on a map with many far apart, if that shows up a light bvh is the answer, not a grid of cells.
- Not checked: crystals should be blue. For albedo-alpha-emissive materials the sampler uses the texture's average colour and ignores its alpha mask, so a crystal texture with a white core may come out wrong and too bright.

**Why:** the tail of a single big emitter ruined the global proposal, and brute force (more candidates) only smoothed it. (The candidates and the grid are gone, see above; the emitter samples themselves stay, rays alone flicker on small emitters: Emissive surfaces below.)

**How to apply:** check emitter power with `ddgi_emitter_info` before tuning samplers. See [DDGI guided rays](#ddgi-guided-rays) and [DDGI fireflies came from fast-adapt](#ddgi-fireflies-came-from-fast-adapt).

## DDGI fireflies came from fast-adapt

*ratio vs ~0 night history; now needs 6 agreeing frames; measure at auto exposure, 20-frame gaps*

DDGI fireflies came from the irradiance fast-adapt snapping the history on noise. A probe sees a lit room through a doorway with 0, 1 or 2 rays per frame. Two approaches failed:
- **Single-frame ratio test:** fired on noise.
- **4-frame trend EMA vs history ratio, threshold 16 (2026-09-23):** still fired at night. The history on dark ground is ~0, so any flash is a huge ratio, and the flash lingers in the trend for many frames. It showed as probe-cell blotches all over open ground.

Fix (2026-09-24, `goluwa/render3d/passes/ddgi.lua` IRRADIANCE_INTEGRATE.adapt):
- It snaps only when each frame's own estimate differs from history by more than the `ddgi_irradiance_threshold` pvar (2) in the same direction for `ddgi_adapt_frames` (6) frames in a row.
- The run count is stored signed in the trend atlas alpha.

Results on the gi_reflections night overview, where ffly = pixels > 2× their median over captures 20 frames apart:

| setup | ffly | spot room light off | light on |
|---|---|---|---|
| old | 13.7% | 124 frames | 117 frames |
| fast-adapt disabled | 3.1% | 124 | 118 |
| new | 3.8% | 21 | 94 |

The old threshold of 16 never fired on real light switches (a 10× change); a threshold of 2 only became possible because runs filter out noise.

**Why:** exposure hides this. Screenshots at a pinned EV 6 look calm, but night auto exposure is around EV -3, about 700× brighter (see [GI measurement needs an exposure lock and a real camera](#gi-measurement-needs-an-exposure-lock-and-a-real-camera)). Captures 3 frames apart are correlated by hysteresis 0.97 and understate flicker about 7×.

**How to apply:**
- Measure flicker by capturing at auto exposure with captures 20 frames apart (the old `tmp/ddgi_fireflies.lua` rig with FF_GAP/FF_EXPOSURE/FF_MAP env knobs and the light switch rig `tmp/ddgi_response.lua` are gone; rebuild with `Shot.Sequence`).

Emissive surfaces were the other half. The brightest-ray clamp erased lone hits on small emitters, under-counting them about 12×. Since 2026-09-24 they reach the probes only through emitter samples (`ddgi.EMITTER_SAMPLES`, resampled candidates until 2026-10-15, a draw by power since), which brought night flicker down to 2.9%. Details are in docs/vulkan-raytrace.md, "Night fireflies and emitter sampling". Related: [DDGI software trace](#ddgi-software-trace).

## DDGI guided rays

*ddgi_guided_rays, per-probe radiance map steers rays; test/harnesses/guided_rays_scene.lua; ray clamp stays off*

DDGI fireflies from small bright openings are now attacked with path guiding (2026-09-30). `ddgi_guided_rays` (default 0.5, 0 = old uniform) is the share of a probe's 128 rays aimed by its own octahedral radiance map. Code: `pass_guide` in passes/ddgi.lua (last of the probe passes), direction and weight functions in `GetRayDirectionGLSL` in ddgi.lua.

- Guide texel: r = mean luma, g = cell probability, b = CDF, a = stamp hash of (world, cascade). A slot a different probe scrolled into has a mismatched stamp, so it falls back to uniform rays until its own guide exists.
- Every pass that needs a ray direction recomputes it from the guide; the guide is only replaced at the end of the frame, so trace, shade and integrate always agree. Any guide is unbiased as long as they agree, so a stale one only costs efficiency.
- Ray weight q = (1/4π) / (share/4π + (1-share) * density), density = prob * 16 / |d|_1^3; irradiance and distance integrates multiply their cosine weight by q. The relocation/back face statistics only look at the uniform rays.
- `ddgi_ray_clamp` (replaces the brightest-ray clamp, default 0/off) caps radiance at N x the probe's mean ray luma without its brightest. It only fixes fireflies, not dark "shadowflies", and darkens GI a bit.
- The view of it: `luajit glw --3d lua test/harnesses/guided_rays_scene.lua` (dark sealed room, small window, sun-lit ground outside; GR_WINDOW sets the size). 0.5 vs 0 was obviously more stable there, but barely visible in the main scene.

**Why:** hysteresis only turned the noise into slow bubbling; reducing the variance at the source was the correct fix. See [DDGI fireflies came from fast-adapt](#ddgi-fireflies-came-from-fast-adapt).

**How to apply:** if the guide seems to do nothing, check that stamps match (probes scrolled in are uniform for a frame) and that the cell size (about 22 degrees) is not larger than the opening.

## DDGI long range and bent normal facets

*10 cascades from 0.5 m, sun-change triggered updates, bent normal rotated onto the smooth normal*

Since 2026-10-14 DDGI has 10 cascades (`ddgi.CASCADES`), `ddgi_probe_spacing` 0.5 and ratio 2, so the coarsest is 256 m apart and about 6 km wide. Cascades are fit to the scene bounds and `ddgi_max_coverage` (4000), and any cascade past the one that covers everything is dropped, so small maps pay for fewer. Update intervals are 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, then 64, which keeps the rays per frame close to the old 4 cascade setup. `ddgi_max_ray_distance` is 4000. A cascade also updates ahead of its interval when the sun moved or changed brightness by more than `ddgi_change_threshold` (radians plus relative luma, 0 is off) since its last update, at most every `ddgi_change_min_interval` frames. Only the sun is tracked, local lights, emitters and sky changes still wait for the interval.

The AO pass computes the bent normal against a depth derived geometric normal, which is flat per triangle. DDGI looks the probes up along it, so a low poly sphere showed faint stripes per facet. The bent normal is now rotated from the geometric normal onto the smooth gbuffer normal (`rotate_between` in passes/ambient_occlusion.lua) before it is written.

**Why:** the user wanted GI over large terrains with sparse far probes and unchanged near detail.
**How to apply:** if far GI looks stale after a lighting change that is not the sun, lower the relevant `ddgi_update_interval_N`. Any new consumer of the bent normal gets the smooth-normal-relative one.

## DDGI relocation: cramped probes

*probes in narrow gaps jiggle and leak sky; disable them*

DDGI probe relocation (passes/ddgi.lua probe_data pass, offset packed into probe data xyz as world + offset/spacing):
probes squeezed in a gap narrower than 2×RELOCATION_DISTANCE (e.g. furniture 0.2 m from a wall) get pushed back and forth,
jiggle every frame with the random ray rotation, and caused a large blue sky leak across the whole room. Fix: when the
averaged push mostly cancels ("cramped") keep the offset and disable the probe (w = 2). RTXGI's "move toward the farthest
hit" rule made ground probes drift sideways (farthest = random sky miss); averaged push away from close hits is used instead.

**Why:** the leak looked like exterior (roof) probes bleeding through the Chebyshev floor, but it was the two jiggling
crevice probes — the GI-only view (`ddgi_debug_gi 1`, low exposure) plus `ddgi_debug_probes 1` found it.

**How to apply:** when relocation changes look wrong, inspect with the probe debug view before tuning visibility. See
[DDGI fireflies came from fast-adapt](#ddgi-fireflies-came-from-fast-adapt), [GI measurement needs an exposure lock and a real camera](#gi-measurement-needs-an-exposure-lock-and-a-real-camera).

## DDGI visibility min-weight leak

*faint probes skipped rays but were 1000x brighter; contribution-share rule fix, gm_construct room repro*

On gm_construct (brick room, grass on the roof) the room walls/ceiling glowed green and a bright band ran along the wall tops. `ddgi_visibility_rays` 0/1/2 changed nothing. Cause: commit 8d81b89c ("optimizations") only traces a probe's visibility ray if its blend weight is >= `DDGI_VISIBILITY_MIN_WEIGHT` (0.02). Probes outside the roof get weight ~0.004 after Chebyshev + the w<0.2 crush, so they were never ray-tested, but their irradiance is 100-1000x the dark room's, so a 1% share is the whole result.

Fix in `ddgi_sample_cascade` (goluwa/render3d/ddgi.lua): fetch the probe's irradiance before the ray test and trace if `w >= min_weight` OR `w * luma(E) > DDGI_VISIBILITY_MIN_SHARE (0.02) * luma(sum so far)`. Resolve cost +0.3 ms (0.86 -> ~1.2 ms), tracing everything was +0.95 ms. The shade pass still passes `DDGI_NO_VISIBILITY_RAYS` (now explicitly skips the contrast rule too); turning rays on there changed nothing visible.

Diagnosis tricks that worked: `ddgi.SKY_INTENSITY = 0` showed the green was sun-lit grass bounce, not sky; a debug return of hidden-weight fraction showed 0 everywhere (rays never fired); `behind_weight` showed no probe behind a surface contributed, so it was not a plane-side problem. `INSTANCE_FACING_CULL_DISABLE` is set on all TLAS instances, so `ddgi_visibility_front_faces_only` is a no-op.

A thin-walled sealed-box test in test/tests/render3d/ddgi.lua did not reproduce it (open case read 0 GI), so no regression test was added.

**Why:** the optimization assumed faint probes cannot show if they leak; at high indoor/outdoor contrast they dominate.
**How to apply:** if a leak persists while visibility rays seem to do nothing, first check whether the rays are even issued for the leaking probes (weight/share thresholds), not whether the rays hit. Related: [Fog needs DDGI visibility rays](#fog-needs-ddgi-visibility-rays), [DDGI relocation: cramped probes](#ddgi-relocation-cramped-probes), [GI measurement needs an exposure lock and a real camera](#gi-measurement-needs-an-exposure-lock-and-a-real-camera).

## Fog needs DDGI visibility rays

*DDGI sampled without the TLAS leaks through thin walls on coarse cascades*

`ddgi_sample_cascade` only rejects probes behind thin walls via ray queries under `#ifdef DDGI_VISIBILITY_RAYS`, and a pass has to bind the TLAS (`ddgi_scene`) to get them. Without that, only the Chebyshev test is left, and it can't resolve a wall thinner than a coarse cascade's spacing. So points far from the camera inside a sealed building pick up the outdoor probes. The froxel fog scatter pass (`passes/volumetric_fog.lua`) had this bug: bluish blotchy fog inside a tall gm_construct building, visible only from a distance. Fixed by binding the TLAS like `passes/reflections.lua` does (+0.5 ms scatter GPU time on gm_construct).

**How to apply:** when a new pass samples DDGI and leaks only at a distance, check that it defines DDGI_VISIBILITY_RAYS first. Repro rig: `test/harnesses/fogleak/rig.lua` (sealed dark hall, fog on/off at locked EV, several distances). A gm_construct shot needs about 20 s settle before DDGI shows the leak; 6 s showed nothing. Related: [GI measurement needs an exposure lock and a real camera](#gi-measurement-needs-an-exposure-lock-and-a-real-camera)

## Air and fog share the froxel volume

*FAR = shadow range, analytic only beyond; no single-point shadow lookups*

Since 2026-09-27 the aerial perspective ("blue haze") lives in the froxel volume with the ground fog, lit by the shadowed sun and the DDGI/sky ambient. froxel_fog.FAR = 3000 (about the sun shadow map's 2700 m), SLICES = 96, which keeps near slice resolution close to the old 64 slices / 150 m. The composite integrates the air and fog analytically only beyond FAR, fully lit. (Since 2026-10-15 FAR is 100 km and nothing is integrated analytically beyond it, see "One medium in the froxel volume".)

**Why:** the old composite applied the air from the camera with sun/sky visibility = 1 (blue haze indoors and in shade), and the far segment used ONE shadow lookup at a representative point, which projected hard, misplaced shadow shapes (V shapes, blue horizontal bands in the sky).
**How to apply:** don't reintroduce single-point shadow lookups for a medium segment. Every froxel holds air; for sky pixels the composite takes the unshadowed air of the first FAR out of the sky color instead (skipping air per froxel tile made halos at silhouettes). The ocean no longer applies its own aerial perspective, the fog does. The open-air ambient (get_ground_fog_sky_ambient) is the sky averaged over all directions (upper sky from get_sky_irradiance, sky-lit ground below), never the sky along the view ray: that put the sunset glow in front of mountains hiding the sun. Froxel points use froxel_point: when the sample's pixel can't see the point, it moves to the ray of the froxel's pixel that sees furthest (fixes halos around foliage; clamping only per slice made staircase leaks on grazing walls).. Related: [Fog needs DDGI visibility rays](#fog-needs-ddgi-visibility-rays), [Scene color is pre-exposed](#scene-color-is-pre-exposed)

## GI measurement needs an exposure lock and a real camera

*both silently invalidate screenshot numbers*

- **Auto exposure** amplifies a dark interior back to mid grey, so a fixed leak and a fixed black room both measure ~128. Pin it with a view's `ExposureLock` (EV100, `glw shot --ev`; the `r_exposure_lock` pvar is gone, `r_camera_mode manual` is the same for the player's view) (`goluwa/render3d/passes/blit.lua`; no argument = automatic; it takes an EV, indoor is about 10.5 and outdoor 15.5). `r_exposure_info` prints the metered EV. Local exposure also brightens dark regions per pixel, so set `r_eye_local_shadows 0` and `r_eye_local_highlights 0` (or `r_eye 0`) for absolute comparisons. The DDGI resolve texture (`ddgi.GetScreenTexture`) is unaffected by any of this and is the better thing to read for GI numbers.
- **A one-frame camera override measures unconverged GI** (a sealed box read 0.71 where a held camera measures ~108). Use `Shot.Capture` (`goluwa/render3d/shot.lua`, `glw shot`), which holds a view for `settle` seconds (use 6 or more when numbers matter, 2.5 s is not always converged) and can wait for `converge`, or a priority-100 `View` with `ExposureLock`. Avoid pitch +-90, it yields a degenerate view matrix and a bogus reading.
- A converged mean says nothing about shimmer, and shimmer is what reads as "dirty": compare two captures a few frames apart from a stationary camera (mean absolute difference). Captures 3 frames apart are correlated by hysteresis 0.97 and understate flicker about 7x, use 20-frame gaps, and measure at auto exposure for night scenes (see the fast-adapt section).

## Scene color is pre-exposed

*multiply on write, divide on read, rescale histories; probes stay absolute*

Since 2026-09-27 the fp16 scene color targets (lighting, ocean, fog composite, translucent, taa) hold absolute cd/m2 times post_source.get_pre_exposure() = last frame's exposure / PRE_EXPOSURE_HEADROOM (32). The sun disc is physical (~2e9 cd/m2), which only fits because of this.
- Lighting math stays absolute. Multiply by get_pre_exposure() when writing scene color, divide when reading it for shading (ocean refraction, translucent refraction, fog composite, the reflections' last frame uses get_previous_pre_exposure()).
- History buffers (taa, ocean_resolve) scale by get_pre_exposure() / get_previous_pre_exposure(). The previous value is GetExposureTexture(false), valid only before this frame's exposure pass.
- blit/bloom: exposed = pre-exposed * exposure / pre_exposure_from_exposure(prev exposure). Metering converts back to absolute.
- Probe capture bundles have no exposure pipeline, so their pre-exposure is 1 and probes stay absolute.

**Why:** fp16 can't hold both a starlit night and the real sun; pre-exposure keeps the metered average near 0.28/32.
**How to apply:** new passes that sample or write scene color must use post_source.pre_exposure_block + GetPreExposureGLSL. Related: [Perf measurement pitfalls](performance.md#measuring-correctly).

## Shadow push wall leak

*full-texel push toward sun leaked under ceilings in far cascade; now 0.25 texel*

On gm_construct, a sawtooth of sunlight appeared along the wall/ceiling junction at distance (user view pos 2.64,-1.49,-21.2). Cause: in `sampleShadowMap` (render3d/directional_shadows.lua) the lookup point was moved `light_dir * max(texel_world_size, depth_step_world)` toward the sun. The sun's far cascade is 4096 d16 over 1350 m = 0.48 m texels (the 16 m inset is 0.01 m), so a wall point within ~0.5 m of the ceiling was pushed above the slab. Normal offset was not involved (NORMAL_K=0 changed nothing, push=0 fixed it).

Fix: push is now `texel_world_size * 0.25`. Checked for acne at 2 outdoor views and two sun angles; the diff against the full push was only at shadow edges.

**Why:** the user prefers fixing leaks in the shadow map over adding a ray-traced fix.
**How to apply:** thin-geometry leaks in far cascades scale with texel size; look at receiver offsets before adding cascades. Cascade layout lives in weather.lua (2 cascades, zoom 3 and 1, plus inset). The push is `light_dir * max(texel_world_size * 0.25, depth_step_world)` in directional_shadows.lua. Related: [DDGI visibility min-weight leak](#ddgi-visibility-min-weight-leak).

## Foliage quad shadows = RT contact ray

*TLAS has no alpha; CastShadows=false test tells shadow map vs ray terms apart*

Card-shaped dark quads under alpha-tested foliage (Crysis bigpatch_medium in Quarry, 2026-09-26) were the sun contact-shadow ray in `passes/lighting.lua` (`shadow_contact_visibility`), which traces the scene_bvh TLAS. The soup has no UVs, so it can't alpha test. Its reach is 8 cascade texels, which is metres in a level with a 2700 m shadow distance, so low ground cards darken the terrain. That's why it only shows in big levels and never in small test scenes.

Fix: alpha-tested visuals get the TLAS instance flag FORCE_NO_OPAQUE, and the contact ray uses gl_RayFlagsCullNoOpaqueEXT. The other TLAS rays force opaque via ray flags, so they're unaffected.

**Why:** toggling CastShadows=false on the entity did NOT remove the artifact. That's the quick test that separates the shadow map from ray-traced terms.
**How to apply:** for shadow artifacts only seen in levels, check the contact ray and other BVH/TLAS consumers before the shadow map paths (soup cascades 2+ also ignore alpha, see shadow_map.lua DrawSoup). Related: [GI measurement needs an exposure lock and a real camera](#gi-measurement-needs-an-exposure-lock-and-a-real-camera)

## Glass tints light with a ray

*glass_light.lua, test/harnesses/glass_rooms.lua*

Glass (`Material:IsGlass()`: Refraction > 0, not additive) casts no shadow of its own while `r_glass_light` is on (`Material.GlassCastsShadow()` is false, so the shadow maps and the soup skip it). `get_glass_light(origin, dir, distance)` in the lighting pass traces a ray query with `gl_RayFlagsCullOpaqueEXT`: only see-through TLAS instances make candidates, each glass triangle multiplies the light by `refraction * 0.9 * albedo + (1 - refraction) * (1 - covering)` and is never confirmed. It runs for the sun and every local light that survived its shadow, and for the DDGI sun term in `ddgi_shade`. It replaced `glass_tint.lua` (four fitted 1024 maps, 25 taps, its own pass); the shadow edge is now as sharp as the geometry and point lights tint too.

- A closed glass box is counted once, by the faces the ray enters through (`!FrontFace`, the soup's faces wind the other way); `DoubleSided` glass counts every hit.
- Only instances flagged see-through (`scene_bvh` `see_through`, more than half the block's triangles refractive or alpha tested) are non opaque. Glass triangles in a mostly opaque model are opaque to the ray and stay untinted.
- The albedo of a glass hit is the texture's lowest mip, there are no uvs. `ddgi_material` carries `glass`, `glass_refraction`, `glass_covering`; `ddgi.HasGlass()` gates every ray (`glass_light` int in the lighting, ddgi and scene_reflection blocks) so a scene without glass pays nothing.
- Where it runs: the lighting pass and the translucent surface pass (sun and local lights; translucent carpets and glass surfaces get tinted light like opaque ones), `ddgi_shade` (the sun, local light samples, emitter samples, and every probe ray, which is multiplied by the glass between the probe and its hit or the sky), and `scene_reflection` (the sun and lights at a traced reflection hit, and the reflection ray itself, so a mirror shows a tinted view through a window). The screen space part of reflections reads lit scene colour and needs nothing. The local light visibility ray in `ddgi_shade` confirms every non opaque candidate except glass, the sun's trace any-hit shader ignores glass, and probe rays ignore refractive surfaces.
- A shadow map's penumbra reaches into the footprint of an opaque frame, where the sun's ray has no glass (the glass stops at the frame). The light there passed beside the frame, through the glass next to it. `get_glass_light`'s `search_radius` (the lighting pass gives it for a partly lit sun, 3 shadow texels clamped to 0.05 to 0.5 m) traces four more rays offset sideways when the first found none and takes the most absorbing result. Without it every frame shadow in gm_construct_m3_401's lobby had an untinted tan fringe on both sides (pos 185.84, 185.37, 214.62, sun quat -0.2567, -0.6705, -0.6424, 0.2680). Found with a debug view that drew the glass candidates per pixel: white (T = 1) bands wider than the dark cores.
- Cost on gm_construct_m3_401 (lobby, culled): lighting +0.3 ms, ddgi_shade +0.14, reflections +0.05, frame 11.5 to 12.0 ms.
- Not covered: the view ray through glass is the translucent pass's job, the software BVH trace without ray queries, and glass inside a mostly opaque model (see above).
- A graphics pipeline has one descriptor set unless `DescriptorSetCount` says more, and a set a command buffer still uses can't be rewritten (validation error on `vkUpdateDescriptorSets`). The TLAS and material buffer are per frame, so `translucent_surface` asks for `render.GetSwapchainImageCount()` sets like ocean does, and `glass_light.Bind` only writes what changed.
- **Translucent surfaces reflect the scene with a ray.** The forward translucent pass (glass, water-like panes, translucent carpets) had only the sky and the env probes, so a smooth pane showed a sky gradient instead of what stands in front of it (test room `reflect` in `test/harnesses/glass_rooms.lua`, `REFL_YAW`, `REFL_FOV`). `translucent_surface` now binds the TLAS, triangles, uvs and materials through `scene_reflection` (`SCENE_BINDINGS` 22 to 25, no light grid since it has its own) and the shader calls `trace_scene_reflection` along the mirror direction, weighted `1 - smoothstep(0.1, 0.35, perceptual_roughness)` against the probe reflection. One ray, no history, so rough surfaces keep the probes. `scene_reflection.GetDeclarationGLSL(bindings, true)` and `GetGLSL(block, true)` are the shared modes for a pass that has the light grid, `ddgi.GetCommonGLSL` and `get_glass_light` already (the visibility ray define stays off so the probe sampling is unchanged). The traced hit is lit through `get_glass_light` like any other.
- Without ray queries glass falls back to the dithered shadow (`GlassCastsShadow` true).
- Scenes: `luajit glw --3d --background --validate lua "import('test/harnesses/glass_rooms_shot.lua')"` writes `tmp/glass_rooms_<room>.png` for three rooms (red/green/blue ceiling panes, a blue pane with a point light, a blue glass box over an opaque floor; chrome balls in the first and last show the reflections). `SUN_EV=14` locks the exposure of the sunlit rooms (auto exposure washes the glass box out), `GLASS_OFF=1` turns the effect off, `LAMP_EV`/`LAMP_LUMEN` tune the lamp room.

## Reflection halo around objects on glossy floors

*gm_construct_m3_401 lobby, pos 169.97 179.46 204.18 ang -0.1807 -1.885 0 sun quat -0.2229 0.3602 0.0891 0.9015; also pos 184.59 187.46 206.04 ang -0.4527 -0.7677 0 sun quat -0.0115 0.9875 0.1302 0.0876*

A one pixel rim (a halo after taa) along the base of steps, columns and benches on a glossy dark floor, the colour of the sky (black with the sun down), worst indoors under a bright sky. Not the traced reflection (it showed with ray queries off), not direct light (0 there), not taa (it only widens it). Found by drawing the reflections texel's alpha raw, after the lighting's upsample and the roughness through a debug return in `get_reflection`:

- The reflections pass's history alpha was the cause. History is read bilinearly at the texel's own position, and at a silhouette that blends in the alpha of what is beside the surface (an object with roughness over the cutoff has alpha 0). The history feeds itself, so the floor's alpha settled at 0.4 to 0.6 while its neighbours were 0.6 (current frame raw alpha and the filtered one were both fine). Where the alpha is low `combine_reflections` mixes in `env_reflection`, which is the bright unoccluded sky indoors. Fix in `passes/reflections.lua`: the history alpha is clamped to the range of the similar texels' alphas the spatial filter just read (`alpha_min`, `alpha_max`).
- `get_reflection_blend_weight` (ibl.lua) was `(1 - smoothstep(0.08, 0.45, r))^2`, so a floor at perceptual roughness 0.13 to 0.2 already gave a third of the reflection to the environment. It is `1 - smoothstep(0.3, 0.45, r)` now, in line with the 0.45 the reflections and traced paths stop at. This alone halved the rim and removed the glowing tile grout.
- Tried and dropped, they changed nothing: widening the lighting's reflection upsample to 16 taps, four depth weighted history taps, a roughness weight in the reflections pass's spatial filter, capping the specular antialiasing of `get_antialiased_roughness` for glossy surfaces.
- Test room `reflection_floor` in `test/harnesses/glass_rooms.lua` (`TILE_ROUGHNESS`, `FLOOR_LAMP`, `PVARS=key=value,..`) does not reproduce it, its rim was the traced hit at the contact line.

## Global gi strength

`ddgi_strength` (default 1, 0 to 4) scales the light the probes give every consumer: `ddgi_sample_irradiance` is a wrapper that multiplies the real lookup (`ddgi_sample_irradiance_unscaled`), and the resolve pass's out-of-volume environment fallback is scaled the same way, so the lighting pass's diffuse gi and reflection fallback, the translucent pass, traced reflection hits, ocean and fog all follow it. The probes' own bounce rays (`ddgi_shade`, which defines `DDGI_UNSCALED`) are not scaled, that would compound with every update. 0 gives no indirect light from the probes (only the direct light, reflections and the sky in the reflection), 2 doubles it.

## Reflections are two passes

Naming: "reflections" is the whole system (the passes, their pvars, `reflection_data`, `reflection_tex`), "screenspace" is the part that marches the depth buffer and reads last frame's colour (`SCREENSPACE_*`, `trace_screenspace_direction`, `reflection_screenspace_roughness`, the ocean's `screenspace_*`). The screen space march is what traces the reflection of a smooth surface when `reflection_screenspace_roughness` is above 0 and the whole reflection where there are no ray queries (`reflection_ray_query` off or no hardware).

`passes/reflections.lua` holds `reflections_trace` (the rays, written raw to `reflection_raw`, `reflection_rough_rays` rays per rough pixel, see `blue_noise` for the sample numbers) and `reflections` (the 3x3 spatial filter and the temporal history, writes `reflections` and `reflection_depth`, which lighting and the history read). They were one pass whose filter read its neighbours from an 8x8 tile of shared memory, so a texel on a tile border averaged 4 or 6 neighbours and not 9 and the border showed as a grid every 16 pixels (the pass is half resolution) in the noise of a rough reflection: a high-pass scan of a rendered sphere at roughness 0.8, one ray, no aa, had its strongest period at 16 px in x and y with 1.8x the energy on the tile borders, 1.2x (the level of the other periods) after the split. The filter reads the raw texture and the gbuffer directly now. `shared_glsl` is the constants and helpers both passes use. `reflection_gi_threshold` (default 0.5, 1 is never) is the perceptual roughness where a reflection starts to be the light computed from the gi (`get_reflection`'s `env_reflection`: the probes' irradiance, the sky weighted by sky visibility, the env probes) and not what the rays traced, fully the gi `reflection_gi_fade` (default 0.15) above (`ibl.GetReflectionGIRange`, `get_reflection_blend_weight`). `reflections_trace` casts no ray above the end of the fade (`reflection_data.roughness_cutoff`), so a lower threshold also saves the rays. Furnace test, 2026-10-13: within 0.2 percent of the all traced result for the metal row and 0.2 percent for the dielectric row (the gi is no darker than the traced reflection), the blended sphere is a flat blur of the gi's colour, with none of the angular detail of the rays. `reflection_resolution` (`full`, `half`, `quarter`, default half) is the resolution both run at, changed at runtime by recreating their framebuffers (the history starts over). `reflection_filter_radius` is in texels of the half resolution, the filter scales it so the blur is the same on screen at every resolution. Full resolution costs about 4 times the rays, filter taps and memory of half; it improved the flicker of thin leaves in a mirror reflection a little but did not remove it (2026-10-13, the 4 phase pixel pick was ruled out with a toggle, nothing changed).

## Translucent shadow measurement

*alpha_measure.lua recipe, soup per-vertex opacity, LocalExposure=0 pitfall*

test/harnesses/alpha_scene.lua + test/harnesses/alpha_measure.lua (CAM_H=12 FOV=90 near/inset, CAM_H=25 FOV=60 far/soup, TOGGLE=1 flips material props at runtime) print "sun passed" per material vs 1-opacity. Results match within ~0.05.

- Floor of 60 m with the camera 25 m up got no far shadows at all, 200 m works; not investigated.

## Gbuffer roughness is GGX alpha

*gbuffer_roughness() is alpha, mra.g stores perceptual; clamp radiance in exposed units*

`gbuffer_roughness()` returns GGX alpha. Readers must `sqrt` it to get perceptual roughness. reflections.lua used to read it raw, and that mismatch hid bugs (fixed 2026-09-24). Since 2026-09-29 the raw mra.g channel holds perceptual roughness (write through `gbuffer_encode_roughness(alpha)`): 8-bit alpha put alpha 0.002 at 0, and the sun lobe's (alpha / lobe_alpha)^2 energy then removed the glint from mirrors.

Also, scene radiance is in absolute units (daylight at EV 12 is thousands). A fixed luminance clamp like "8.0" crushes everything to black. Apply such limits in exposed units via `post_source.GetExposureTexture(true)`, the way bloom and reflections do.

**Why:** the gm_flatgrass crack streaks came from the reflections' sky fallback bleeding through its non-alpha-weighted filters. Fixing the roughness then exposed the absolute clamp.
**How to apply:** any new pass reading mra.g or clamping radiance should follow the above. Before debugging reflection/DDGI-RT visuals, wait for BVHSceneReady (see [GI measurement needs an exposure lock and a real camera](#gi-measurement-needs-an-exposure-lock-and-a-real-camera)).

## Grazing fresnel ignored specular

*F90(F0) fix in ibl.lua; debug washed-out surfaces with black albedo*

Until 2026-09-26 the ambient specular was `reflection * (F0 * envBRDF.x + envBRDF.y)`: the grazing term assumed F90 = 1, so SpecularMultiplier 0 still gave a strong white sheen edge-on. Fixed Filament-style with `F90(f0) = clamp(dot(f0, 50 * 0.33), 0, 1)` in ibl.lua (F_Schlick, F_SchlickRoughness), lighting.lua and translucent.lua. Only F0 < 0.02 (specular multiplier < 0.5) changes; water is unaffected. The user said this "massively improved everything".

**Why:** grass looked grey-white at eye level; the cause was additive specular, not albedo or transmission.

**How to apply:** when a surface looks washed out or grey, render it with `set_albedo(vec3(0))` and remove terms one at a time (specular, transmission, fog) to find additive light. Thin geometry seen edge-on (grass blades) also gets huge direct GGX specular even at tiny F0; grass uses roughness 0.7 and specular 0.02 for that reason. Related: [Gbuffer roughness is GGX alpha](#gbuffer-roughness-is-ggx-alpha).

## HDR environment override

*weather.SetEnvironmentMap(path) disables sun/atmosphere, EXR PIZ float pitfall, intensity clamp*

`weather.SetEnvironmentMap(path)` (also `envmap <path> [intensity]` command, and atmosphere_controller `EnvironmentMap` / `EnvironmentMapIntensity`) sets `atmosphere.environment_map_path`. `atmosphere.IsEnabled()` then returns false (sun lux 0, no fog, clouds, aerial perspective), and `GetGLSLMainCode` samples the equirect first (u = atan(z,x)/2pi+0.5, v = 0.5-asin(y)/pi, top row up). envprobe re-renders via `atmosphere.GetEnvironmentMapVersion()` in `HasSkyChanged`, so IBL, DDGI and reflections all use the map. Sky is black until the async texture is ready.

**Why:** intensity defaults to 1000 cd/m2 per map unit because probes are b10g11r11 and clamp at 65504, so a bright sun pixel in an HDRI clips. Raise it only with that in mind; auto exposure hides the absolute level.

**How to apply:** test with `test/harnesses/envmap_scene.lua` sphere grid + `shot --setup`. exr.lua decodes none/RLE/ZIPS/ZIP/PIZ; PIZ wavelet must run once per 16-bit word plane (`start + j`, stride = words per pixel), float channels decoded as garbage without it. magick only writes half EXRs, so float PIZ is only verified against the user's 1K file (matches magick PFM to 4e-6).

## Underwater look-up artifact

*negative inscatter, not NaN; probe signs; tank test scene*

Looking steeply up inside water showed a huge white disc with a black ring. Cause: `integrate_attenuated` in passes/ocean.lua divided by `max(x, 1e-3)`, but x is negative when the path climbs faster than the light fades (dir.y > mu, ~48 deg up for the sky's mu 0.75). That gave large negative inscatter; bloom then spread the negatives (negative bloom_tex), the tonemap saw negative exposed values (black) next to huge ones (white). Fixed by dividing by x with near-zero entries swapped for 1.

**Why:** no NaN/inf anywhere, so isnan/isinf probes found nothing. Colour-coding `col * exposure` in blit.lua (nan/inf/negative/>1e4) and then the bloom texture's sign located it.

**How to apply:** for "inf/nan looking" artifacts, probe for negative values too. Test scene: addons/examples/lua/examples/render3d/water_volume_test.lua (40x12x40 tank, Position.y = surface, Size.y goes down). Related: [Water rendering](#water-rendering), [Scene color is pre-exposed](#scene-color-is-pre-exposed).

## Volumetric clouds

*how clouds hook into sky/shadows/fog; noise range and lattice-streak pitfalls*

Volumetric clouds (built 2026-09-29): `goluwa/render3d/clouds.lua` (layers, presets, GLSL) and `passes/clouds.lua` (noise bake, beer shadow map, sky dome, quarter-res checkerboard trace + half-res reconstruct). Composited in `passes/volumetric_fog.lua`. `weather.SetCloudLayers(layers)` (read back with `GetCloudLayers`/`GetCloudCover`; presets such as `windy` live in `climate.lua`).

- Sun is no longer dimmed by `(1 - cover)`. Per-pixel `get_cloud_shadow` is multiplied in: surface_lighting (type 0), the medium shadow fn (froxels, ocean), DDGI shade. Shadow maps are only disabled when `clouds.GetMaxTransmittance` is tiny (coverage 1, no gaps).
- The sky dome is published via `atmosphere.SetCloudSky` from `clouds.Update` (the weather Update listener). The lighting pass skips it in the main view (`sky_clouds`), since the fog composite draws screen-space clouds.
- Pitfall: baked perlin-worley/worley fbm values only span ~0.54-0.80 (`test/harnesses/clouds/noise_stats.lua` measures this). Channels must be stretched to 0-1, or any coverage fills the sky.
- Pitfall: radial streaks at the horizon come from the noise tile lattice, not step aliasing. Fixed by a domain warp from a turned, low-frequency copy of the base noise, ramped in with distance in tiles (CLOUD_WARP_*). The warp mip must be offset by log2(warp frequency), or far away it reads a flat mip and does nothing. At full strength up close it shears clouds into swirls; the user liked that, so it is the `swirl` layer field (preset `windy`).
- Block structs (`{"shadows", {...}}`) are NOT flattened by glsl_metadata. Splice fields in with `unpack(...)` last.
- Texture:GenerateMipmaps / BlitImage now support 3D (image depth) and take `{cmd, src_stage, dst_stage}`.
- Pitfall: detail_scale of 500-900 m reads as no detail at all (a cumulus 3 km away is ~3 m per half-res pixel). Presets use 150-400 m. A steep density ramp (the old `* 3`) gives hard cartoon outlines; the rim must be thin density for erosion to show.
- Weather map channels: r coverage, g regional heap height, b cirrus fibres (flat layers), a base height within `base_variation`. Heap tops also vary per cloud from a coarse read of base noise .g. Layers get distinct shape/detail offsets in SetLayers, so a view shows a different noise region than before 2026-09-29; judging coverage from one view is noisy (one view spans ~1-2 shape tiles).
- Pitfall: from high up, sky pixels look below the horizon, and nothing clipped them at the planet: atmosphere density functions clamp altitude at 0, so underground air/fog integrates at full ground density and whites everything out. integrate_scattering never clips; its callers must (volumetric_fog composite uses get_planet_distance, froxel far fog clips for is_sky, cloud_march clips t_max). Test scene ground is only a 16 km box.
- Pitfall: the sun disc is ~1e5-1e6x a cloud's brightness, so cloud transmittance decides its visibility down to ~1e-6. A hard march cutoff cuts the disc along a contour; no cutoff shows it through grey cloud (Wrenninge octaves underestimate forward diffuse light). Now T fades out between 1e-3 and 1e-4 (CLOUD_LIT/HIDDEN_TRANSMITTANCE). Fog is split at cloud_depth in the composite, else ground haze paints over nearer clouds below the horizon.
- 2026-10-14 port from tmp/atmosjs/sky/Clouds.js, what stayed: `cloud_density_shaded` returns the sample's height in its column, the march lights with it (powder from density and height, base shadow, a diffusion term, upward sky probes `CLOUD_SKY_PROBE_*` scaling the ambient, scattering orders `CLOUD_ORDER_*`, `CLOUD_SUN_SCALE`/`CLOUD_AMBIENT_SCALE`), and the light's optical depth is marched every `CLOUD_LIGHT_REUSE` (3) lit samples with jittered steps (0.70 to 0.57 ms). `clouds.VIEW_DIVISOR` (1) is the render size over the cloud view's size, each frame traces a quarter of it (full resolution trace 0.8-1.4 ms, GPU timing in `test/harnesses/clouds/gpu_time.lua`). A JS style distance field skip was not built: coverage comes from 3D shape noise, a conservative occupancy field would skip little.
- 2026-10-14, what was tried and dropped: JS's density model (a steep ramp on the shape value minus crease erosion from a worley distance detail noise, a low density fringe, lifted bottoms, small puffs from a second detail fetch). It made crisp, pebbly, smeared clouds that looked worse than the old soft `pow(.., 0.7)` ramp against reference photos: the old ramp is what gives cotton edges. Pitfalls met on the way: a coarse empty space test skipping faint density cuts far clouds off at a horizontal line; the harness ground is a 16 km box so a pink band under the horizon is the harness; judge from elevated views and compare tonemappers, EV 13 reads washed out.
- 2026-10-14 detail footprint: `cloud_density_shaded` filters the detail by what one view pixel covers (`min(dt, ts * pixel_angle)`, `pixel_angle` from `projection[1][1]` and `render_size.y`) in the main view (`quality >= 1`), not by the step `dt`, which grows with distance and blurred every cloud past ~1 km. This is what made edges puffy again, a second finer detail fetch (`CLOUD_FINE_DETAIL`, turned against the first) adds the small scallops. The sky dome (`quality < 1`) still uses `dt`. From above the layer the thick tops blow out white at EV 14 with `CLOUD_SUN_SCALE` 2.2.
- Detail variety: read sharp (footprint filtering above), the detail noise's single lattice showed as evenly spaced beads of one size. The detail lookup is warped by a low frequency field of the shape noise (`CLOUD_DETAIL_WARP_*`, amount above ~1 smears puffs into streaks), and the coarse, a mid (`CLOUD_MID_DETAIL` 1.6) and a fine (`CLOUD_FINE_DETAIL` 2.7) fetch at unrelated scales and turns are mixed with weights that vary across the sky (`CLOUD_FINE_WARP_*`). The puffs only reach the rim: the erosion `remap` leaves a dense cloud body smooth.
- Lumpy body: the density `remap` only erodes a thin rim and a thick cloud saturates to 1 inside, so the body had no surface structure. `cloud_density_shaded` now dents the outer shape with big 3D billows (`CLOUD_BODY_*`: a detail fetch at 2.2x the detail scale, `CLOUD_BODY_DEPTH` 0.9, reaching in to shape value `CLOUD_BODY_SHELL` 0.8) and fades them out with the mip level. Seen in `pose.sh` against the cumulus photo this is what made the bodies read as cauliflower.
- Fine pixel level detail was limited by the detail volume's texel size, not by the march or the noise content: `clouds.DETAIL_NOISE_SIZE` 32 (7.8 m texels at a 250 m tile, magnified blobs) to 128 is what brought it back. Tried without effect before finding it: a finer fine step (8 m), a 6x "finest" fetch of the same low resolution volume, lump lighting contrast (adds unnatural noise), nearest history sampling in the reconstruct, more detail steps in the light march and lighting every sample. Bloom and TAA soften clouds only a little. A steeper rim (`pow` above 0.7) makes thin clouds vanish.
- Cloud cost (2026-10-14, `test/harnesses/clouds/bench.sh <tag>`, mixed preset from 600 m looking at the horizon, no ground, frozen wind, exact frame so two runs agree to 0.06% rmse (GPU float noise, kept by dither, TAA and the ground), `cmpsky.sh` diffs the sky): `clouds_trace` is about 4 ms at 1080p, texture bound. The detail in the main march is about half of it (multi scale puffs 0.9 ms, lumpy body 0.85 ms), the light march 1 ms, the sky probes 0.1 ms. Pvars `cloud_layer_steps` (default 100, the fine step is thickness over this, 8 to 30 m, 120 was the old default: 100 is about 20% faster for 0.2% difference) and `cloud_detail_min_mip` (default 0, the finest mip the detail volume is read at: 2 is about 15% faster and costs the fine detail of the nearest clouds). No effect on time or image: nearest history sampling, skipping the detail deep in a cloud (few samples are core), empty space steps, the lit transmittance cutoff. Large difference for little time: coarse steps 6 (3.4%), light steps 4 (1.5%). Detail volume size is the cost: 128 3.45 ms, 64 2.97, 32 2.40 (1.2% and 2.2% difference).
- Reference comparison: `test/harnesses/clouds/pose.sh <tag> [preset]` renders one saved pose and sun next to a photo, `TONEMAP=neutral` sets the tonemapper for the session, `shoot.sh` takes views from inside and above the layer. Cloud positions depend on elapsed time (wind offsets), compare looks and not shapes.
- Before/after: `test/harnesses/clouds/compare.sh <tag>` renders a fixed set of views into a jpg per tag.
- Views/shots: `test/harnesses/clouds/views.lua` with CLOUDS, VIEWS, EV, OUT env vars.

**Why:** Tuning took several rounds; these are the things that weren't obvious from the code.
**How to apply:** Read this before touching cloud density/lighting or adding a pass that lights with the sun. Related: [Scene color is pre-exposed](#scene-color-is-pre-exposed), [Air and fog share the froxel volume](#air-and-fog-share-the-froxel-volume).

## Water rendering

*ocean + water volumes in passes/ocean.lua; mean sea level, phase sign, ripple and streaming pitfalls*

Ocean and water_volume components are both drawn by the "ocean" pass (name kept for fog/envprobe/post_source). Sea state and presets live in goluwa/render3d/water.lua; showcase is addons/examples/lua/examples/render3d/water_showcase.lua (views via `water_view N`).

- Ocean level is now the MEAN surface (old Seascape heights were all positive).
- Phase function angle is dot(-view_dir, light_propagation_dir); the wrong sign made murky water glow milky.
- Never smoothstep(e, e, x) (footprint 0) — undefined in GLSL, broke all wave heights.
- A few discrete sine waves give visible crisscross grids; per-pixel ripples use advected gradient noise with analytic gradients instead.
- Submerged-floor test must be "is the point inside the volume", not a distance comparison along different rays.
- Ocean pass cost (island example from the sea, 8.3 ms before): ablating parts in one process (a bitmask uniform cycled every 3 s, compare modes inside one process, the process to process variance is bigger than the wins) showed the refraction side at 6.4 ms, of which the light shafts in `get_water_inscatter` were 5: 8 samples each with a `get_caustic` (newton steps, 4 wave fetches and the ripples a step). The reflection ray, detail ripples, shore ray, point lights and the 32 to 96 step trace were each under 1 ms. The caustic of the shafts is now faded out with distance from the camera (full under 25 m, gone at 140 m, where its lines are thinner than a pixel and average out to no change), 3.1 to 4.0 ms. A fade by `get_pixel_footprint` did not cut anything at the thresholds that matched those distances (the ocean renders at a size where the footprint is about half of what the formula for the display gives), so it is by distance. Looking straight down in shallow water has no distance to fade by, so the shafts take 2, 4 or 8 samples by the length of the path through the water and their caustic skips the fine ripples and the newton step (only the floor caustic has them): 13.9 to 9.0 ms in one-process A/B at that view. `height_map_tracing` takes `scene_limit` and stops where the scene is in front, so land pixels don't march the waves.
- The parts of the water that cost can be turned off with pvars (`r_water_caustics` for the floor's, `r_water_shaft_caustics`, `r_water_shafts`, `r_water_ripples`, `r_water_lights`, `r_water_reflection_trace`, `r_water_shore_foam`, `r_water_inscatter`, `r_water_refraction`, `r_water_sun_shadows`, `r_water_ambient_gi`, all on by default, defined at the top of passes/ocean.lua). They are bits of one uniform (`ocean_data.features`), so toggling doesn't recompile the shader. `test/harnesses/scatter/water_toggles.lua` turns each off in turn in one process at a shallow, straight down camera, with a baseline between every two (the gpu clock wanders by several ms inside a process, an un-interleaved run gave a 3.6 ms and a 6.6 ms "all on"). 6.6 ms all on at first, 5.3 after the lines below; what each saves (they overlap, so they don't add up): inscatter 2.2 (ambient gi 1.1, shafts 0.6), ripples 1.6, refraction 1.6, floor caustics 1.25, shaft caustics 0.65, reflection trace 0.7, sun shadows 0.45, lights 0.15, shore foam 0.07.
- The water's ambient light (`get_ambient_irradiance`, the ddgi probes) was the biggest piece of inscatter: it looks up 27 probes (smooth blend) and with `DDGI_VISIBILITY_MIN_WEIGHT` each of those above the weight fired a visibility ray query, up to 27 per pixel. It passes `DDGI_NO_VISIBILITY_RAYS` now, the surface of the sea is open sky so there is no wall to leak through, and the shots with and without are the same to the eye (2.15 to 1.1 ms). `get_caustic` also drops the newton steps and the small ripples once it is half blurred by depth. The alternating of shaft caustic samples between frames was tried and gained 0.25 ms, not kept.
- Terrain streaming lags ~5 s after camera jumps; shot sequences need settle ≥6 s or you see chunk skirts/ocean through land.

- Refraction only offsets the straight-behind pixel by the ripples' deviation from flat refraction. Flat (apparent depth) refraction in screen space tore apart at screen edges and behind objects crossing the surface (ghosts, missing water at the screen bottom); don't bring it back.
- Caustics: gradient noise (value noise lattice made triangles) + full refraction Jacobian by finite differences of the slope; filter only depth*0.012, short ripples are what make pool caustics.

- Map importers: water.MediumFromFog(linear color, extinction, albedo_scale) turns game water fog into our medium. BSP uses $fogcolor/$fogend (albedo scale 4; the vdf decoder already makes "{r g b}" a Color). Cry uses FogColor*FogColorMultiplier/FogDensity, albedo scale 4 (12 was milky).

## Prefers physical over legacy look

*keep physically derived results even when the old look changes*

When a physically correct model changes the established look (e.g. PCSS with the real 0.27 deg sun made clear-day shadows slightly soft near the camera), the user liked the result better than the old fixed filter.

**Why:** said "much better than before, even at a clear day" on 2026-09-27 after the cloud-cover PCSS change.
**How to apply:** don't add special cases just to preserve the old look; derive from the physics or the reference engine and mention the visible change. Related: [Crysis vegetation bending](crysis.md#crysis-vegetation-bending).

## Ray-traced reflection material detail and visibility

With `reflection_screenspace_roughness` at 0 (default) and ray-query hardware, opaque and water reflections use the nearest scene hit as the authority for visibility and the screen space trace is skipped. Blending an unrelated screen-space hit over a solid RT hit can show distant buildings through the reflected balcony wall. Below the perceptual roughness it is set to, the screen is traced first and is the reflection where it holds one, and the same trace (cutouts alpha tested, the hit's own textures) fills the rest. There is no cheaper trace for those fills any more. Translucent surfaces have no screen space reflection and always trace.

Reflection history in motion: a reflection is an image behind the surface, `hit_distance` past it along the view ray, and it moves with the camera by another amount than the surface does. Reading the history where the surface was made reflections drag along with their surface and bend while the camera moved (no TAA, so the history is the only accumulation, 10 to 30 frames). The filter pass reads it where the image was (`reflection_hit_reprojection`, on): the image sits at `hit_distance / (1 + 2 * curvature * hit_distance)` behind the surface (the mirror equation, curvature from how far the normal turns over a texel, a concave surface can have no image), blended into the surface position over roughness 0.1 to 0.5 where the hit distance is an average, and the history weight falls by up to half with what is not corrected. The surface's own depth validates the history, not the image's. Measured on the sphere scene, camera strafing 2.4 m over 60 frames against a still capture of the same pose: error 8.4 off, 4.3 on (1.8 between two still captures). Foliage animates, so regions with leaves can't be measured this way.

Reflection pipelines bind the soup UV buffer (`uvs = 10`) as well as triangles and materials. Hit UVs and vertex blend weights are interpolated from ray-query barycentrics, the albedo texture is sampled at the hit and the second albedo/blend-modulate textures are applied where present. DDGI's averaged albedo is fine for diffuse probe lighting but loses the texture detail reflections need. Candidate intersections also run the shared alpha-cutout test, so cutout geometry is skipped. Terrain keeps its world-space material sampling. This is single-bounce hit shading, so secondary specular reflections are not reproduced.

Reflection rays run in the half-resolution reflections pass. Odd framebuffer sizes round the half size up, so the full/half ratio must not be truncated to an integer. `get_reflection_gbuffer_pos` in ibl.lua partitions the G-buffer by integer division for both ray generation and the reflection upsample (see test/tests/render3d/reflection_mapping.lua).

## Exposure modes and the neutral tonemapper

*2026-10-14, `passes/blit.lua`, `compute_helpers.lua`*

- Exposure is one number, EV100, and there are two independent front ends for it (`r_eye`: off, the default, is the camera, on is the eye), both ending in the same display transform (the tonemapper, SDR or HDR). A shared meter reads the frame (the centre weighted mean luminance in linear light, each pixel held at the 95th percentile of a log histogram; it was the 40th to 95th percentile mean of the log luminance until 2026-10-15) and each mode decides what to do with it. The settings live in `goluwa/render3d/exposure.lua`, the shader in `passes/blit.lua`.
- `camera` normalises: the metered average is shown at 18 percent grey (`KEY` 0.18, K = 12.5, so it was 0.28 before). It has the settings of a camera, EV100 = log2(N^2 / t) - log2(ISO / 100): `r_camera_mode` (`program`, `aperture_priority`, `shutter_priority`, `manual`), `r_camera_iso`, `r_camera_aperture`, `r_camera_shutter` (seconds) for what you fix, and `r_camera_iso_min/max` (100-6400), `r_camera_aperture_min/max` (f/1.4-f/16), `r_camera_shutter_min/max` (1/8000-1/30) for what auto exposure may change. The limits give the range of scenes it can expose properly, EV100 -0.1 to 21 for the defaults, and a scene outside it is shown at the limit (this replaced `r_exposure_min_ev/max_ev`). Only the product matters for the image: `r_exposure_info` prints the ISO, f-number and shutter that the program line would use for the current EV (it moves aperture and shutter evenly away from f/8 and 1/125 and lets the ISO make up the rest), there is no depth of field, motion blur or noise. Manual keeps its three settings and the exposure no longer follows the scene. `r_camera_white_balance` (kelvin, 6500 is neutral, 0 automatic) and `r_camera_white_balance_tint` are the camera's white balance, see the ColorChecker section. Camera mode has no local exposure and no rod vision.
- `eye` does not normalise, dim scenes stay dim. It has what a visual system has, in the `exposure.eye` table and the `r_eye_rod_adaptation`, `r_eye_local_shadows`/`r_eye_local_highlights` and `r_eye_night_vision*` pvars: its own range (EV100 -4 to 18), the adaptation curve (Ward's function in daylight, cone and rod thresholds below it, mesopic blend, anchored to zero at EV 15 so a sunlit scene looks the same as in camera mode and only dim light differs), local exposure (per region adaptation, and its grid passes only run in this mode), rod vision with a blue tint in the dark, and slow adaptation. Tune these by hand. A view's `ExposureLock` (EV100, what `glw shot --ev` sets) fixes the exposure in either mode, the measurement harnesses use it. Compensation in stops is `r_camera_compensation` for the camera (the real feature, it biases what the meter picks) and `r_eye_compensation` for the eye, a view's `ExposureCompensation` overrides whichever is active. Both modes keep the bloom and glare.
- `r_tonemapper` default is `neutral` (2026-10-14): up to 0.76 of exposed white the colour is unchanged (no toe, no black offset, no contrast, grey 0.18 is 0.18), above it the brightest channel rolls off with a slope of 1 at the start to the display's peak, the ratio of the channels kept, and a colour that is compressed hard moves towards white. It is the Khronos PBR Neutral shoulder without the black offset. The other curves (`agx`, `agx_punchy`, `aces`, `gt7`) are looks. The Crysis curve is gone.
- SDR and HDR are the same curve with a different `peak` (display peak over paper white: 1 for SDR, 4.9 for 1000 nits at 203): `neutral(x, peak)` has the same start for both, so everything below 0.76 is identical and HDR keeps more highlight. The other curves keep their own HDR mapping in `tonemap_hdr`.
- Check: at a locked EV 15 the clear zenith at 60 degrees sun reads linear 0.09, 0.13, 0.23 in the output (Y 0.13, a blue a bit under middle grey), which is what a sunny-16 exposure of a blue sky gives.

### Metering and the eye model, measured

*harnesses `test/harnesses/exposure/meter.lua` and `eye.lua`, 2026-10-15*

`meter.lua` puts emissive walls of known luminance (`emissive * albedo * 2000 cd/m2`) in front of the camera in a dark scene (no sun, ground, ddgi, reflections or ambient occlusion), reads the engine's metered EV (the g channel of the exposure texture) and takes the frame's own luminance map (the scene before exposure on the meter's 160 x 90 grid, absolute cd/m2) as the truth for four references: the centre weighted arithmetic mean (an averaging meter integrates linear light, ISO 2720, EV100 = log2(L S / K), K = 12.5), the same with every pixel clamped at the 95th percentile, the centre weighted geometric mean, and a replica of the shader's histogram (128 bins over log2 -10 to 22, weights `1 + 3 (1 - smoothstep(0.2, 1, r))`, the 40th to 95th percentile of the log luminance). The replica matches the engine to 0.00 stops in every layout, so the shader does what the code says, and the histogram's quantisation (0.25 stops a bin) puts a uniform field -0.07 stops off.
- Engine minus the arithmetic mean, in stops (negative: the engine exposes brighter): uniform -0.07, a half at 1/4 of the other +0.24, 1/16 +0.11, 1/64 -0.19, a bright centre spot (20 percent of the width, 16 times the surround) **-1.01**, a dark centre spot +0.10, a 2 percent highlight 100 times the surround -0.91, a bright sky over a dark lower half (1/32 the luminance) **+0.50**. The geometric mean is 0.6 to 1.8 stops under the engine in the split and sky over ground layouts. The clamped arithmetic mean equals the arithmetic one except the lone highlight (engine -0.08).
- So the old percentile window was neither of the usual meters: it was about the arithmetic mean for plain layouts, exposed for the background when a bright subject filled the middle (the subject went a stop over) and for the sky when it was over dark ground (half a stop darker than an averaging meter, more ground detail lost), and ignored a small highlight like a matrix meter does. **Replaced (2026-10-15, on request, as the standard): the meter is the centre weighted arithmetic mean of the luminance in linear light with each pixel held at the 95th percentile of the histogram's weight** (`exposure_feedback_shader` in `passes/blit.lua`: a histogram of the log luminance finds the percentile, a second pass over the grid sums `min(L, percentile) * weight` and the weights, reduced across the workgroup; `low_percent` is gone from `exposure.camera` and `exposure.eye`). Engine minus the clamped arithmetic mean now: uniform +0.01, the three splits 0.00 to +0.01, bright spot 0.00, dark spot -0.01, highlight 0.00 (-0.83 against the unclamped mean, the highlight is held on purpose), sky over ground -0.01. The camera's range matches its limits (`GetCameraRange`: EV100 -0.1 to 20.9, f/1.4 1/30 ISO 6400 to f/16 1/8000 ISO 100). The eye mode shares the meter, so its exposure moved the same way for non uniform scenes.
- The eye model (`eye.lua`: uniform fields from 0.001 to 100000 cd/m2 in eye mode, night vision and local exposure off, adapted for 12 seconds, the SDR output decoded) shows the exposed grey the curve defines, `0.18 * 2^stops(EV)` with the stops from the visibility matching response (luminance over Ward Larson's threshold, rods and cones blended in the mesopic range, Ward's 1994 function above EV 9, 0 at EV 15): 0.0030 (0.001 cd/m2, the 8 bit floor), 0.0018, 0.0056, 0.0185, 0.063, 0.130, 0.175, 0.175, 0.545 (100000 cd/m2, past the eye's EV 18 ceiling) against 0.0037, 0.0020, 0.0057, 0.0178, 0.065, 0.129, 0.166, 0.186, 0.549 predicted, a ratio of 0.94 to 1.05 from 0.1 cd/m2 up (the histogram's quantisation moves the metered EV by up to 0.1 stops, 5 percent in the grey). The curve is flat above about 80 cd/m2 (Weber's law: the threshold grows with the luminance, so luminance over threshold stays 18) so a cloudy day and a sunny one look alike in eye mode, and a scene a tenth of the sun's luminance is 25 percent darker, not the 2 times a brightness power law would give; that is the model, not an error.

### The sky's haze flickering while the sun moves

*2026-10-15, found with the sun debug script (`addons/debug/lua/autorun/render_3d/sun.lua`, numpad), `tmp`-style sweep: the sun moved 0.5 degrees a frame, the sky at a fixed direction read from the composited scene every frame next to the sky view LUT for the current sun*

- Through the volumetric fog pass a sky pixel is `(sky LUT - the air's analytic scattering out to 3 km) * T + the froxel volume's air`, so the haze of the sky is the froxel volume's, and the volume lit its clear air with `get_ambient`, the DDGI probes' irradiance in a random direction (averaged over the frames, it is the mean radiance round the point) wherever there are probes. Converged, that is the analytic ambient: composited sky over the LUT 1.055 with the probes, 1.060 without them. But the probes' sky follows a moving sun with a lag and in steps (the environment probe re-renders every degree, the cascades update every 1, 2, 4, 8 frames and the froxels along a ray sample different cascades), and the sky read 0.89 of the LUT for the first frames after the sun moved and 1.055 afterwards (0.80 with `ddgi_strength 0`), in motion a toggling haze. With `r_pass_ddgi=false` it was a constant 1.06 from the first frame. It got noticeable when the ambient was corrected (2.2 times brighter, see Ground fog), the same lag that made a 5 percent step before made a 10 to 15 percent one.
- `get_ambient` (passes/volumetric_fog.lua) is now `mix(probe ambient, analytic sky ambient, overhead sky visibility)`: one more DDGI lookup per froxel, straight up, whose alpha is the sky visibility there. Open air gets the analytic ambient (validated against the path tracer, independent of the probes' state), a room or a roof gets the probes' light. Closed room with black walls, camera inside, the far wall through 13 m of air: 0.000 cd/m2; with the roof off 18, 22, 33. The sweep: composited over LUT 1.060 to 1.056 over the whole sweep, no step. Fog against the path tracer unchanged (0.875, 0.993, 1.03 at 2 degrees).
- Also found, not the cause: `weather.SetSunDirection` solves a latitude, a date and a time of day for the direction, and moved the date by days a step near the solstice (the moon's phase and place jump while the sun is moved; left as it is, the sun is what is controlled); and the sun entity's rotation was a shortest arc quaternion undefined at the horizon in the default azimuth, its roll (the shadow maps' orientation) turned up to 53 degrees in a 0.005 degree step within 0.01 degrees of it (`weather.lua get_light_rotation`, undefined only for a sun straight down now).
- The cause of the flicker the user kept seeing (found 2026-10-15 with two nearly identical views, 0.85 degrees of sun apart, one grey and one blue): the sky view LUT cache was keyed by the camera in 2 km cells but baked at the exact camera of whoever asked first. The environment probe asks from the origin (1.8 m up), the view from its own place (671 m up), both in the cell `0:6372:0`, so the bucket held the sea level bake (7 percent lower mean view transmittance, a grey zenith, a pale haze) or the 671 m bake depending on which was asked first, and the order changes as the sun walks through buckets. Same key, same inputs on the Lua side, different table: compare the downloaded LUTs and the `DBG` camera at the bake. The table is now baked at the quantised camera (height in 100 m steps, position in 5 km) and the quantised sun elevation of its key, so it is a pure function of the key. A cache keyed more coarsely than it is baked has this bug, always bake at the key.
- Reproduce with `tmp/sweep_views.lua` style: `weather.SetSunRotation` at a few lerped quaternions between two suns, read sky pixels of the blit scene source over the exposure. Smooth over 0.85 degrees now (zenith red 5077, 5063, 5044), t=0 identical at the start and end of the run.

### Multiple scattering is a table

*2026-10-15, `goluwa/render3d/atmosphere_scattering.lua`*

- The isotropic multi scatter LUT and its fitted gain are gone. A Bruneton style table holds the light scattered two to sixteen times for every camera height, view direction, sun elevation and angle to the sun (`scattering.LOOKUP_GLSL`, `sc_lookup(table, r, mu, mu_s, nu)`). `integrate_scattering` marches the single scattering as before (clouds, fog, phase functions) and adds the table at the ray's start less `view_transmittance` times the table at its end. Nothing is fitted: it follows the sun, the viewer's height and the aerosol, a camera at 671 m and one at sea level read the same table, and it holds where the gain did not (visibility under 12 km, sun under 6 degrees).
- Layout: one 512x2048 fp16 atlas (8 MB), x = sun (32) * azimuth (16), y = radius (32) * (2 halves of 32 elevations split at the horizon, sqrt spaced away from it). The altitude axis is cubic (`RADIUS_POWER`), the azimuth is 0 to pi (view round the zenith from the sun, mirror symmetric). Interpolation is by hand across radius and sun and by the hardware inside a block, texel centres are clamped so it never blends two blocks. Bake: raw single scattering (rayleigh and aerosol apart, no phase, so the aerosol lobe is not tabulated), then per order the scattering density (16x32 directions; the ground reflects the direct sun for order 2 and the indirect irradiance of the order before after), the order's radiance, an accumulation and the ground's irradiance. 0.33 s once per aerosol change, the sky view bake after it is under a millisecond.
- Measured against the path tracer (`SPECTRAL=1 MC=8000 SUN_ELEVS=60,25,8`, rms log2 of engine over reference, bias in stops): visibility 20 km 0.030, 0.036, 0.054 (bias -0.02 to -0.04), 55 km 0.031, 0.044, 0.047 (+0.01 to +0.03), 150 km 0.060, 0.067, 0.056 (+0.03 to +0.05), 6 km haze 0.09 and 0.125 (-0.085, -0.12, the orders do not converge fully there, 40 orders gave the same as 16). The fitted gain scored 0.066, 0.073, 0.097 at the default and was invalid below 12 km. Colorchecker white patch 1.005 1.001 0.994.
- What made it accurate, in the order found (each one was measured with the single and multiple parts split, `MS=1` in `lut.lua` prints the split; the engine single scattering against `ss_reference` and the table's share against the path tracer's multiple part):
  - **The march.** 32 quadratic steps over a horizon path (1100 km) put 10 steps in the first 10 km. The engine's own single scattering read 0.85 to 0.90 of the reference at 1 degree and, in a 6 km haze (1.5 km near field), the whole sky read 0.15 stops dark. The sky view bake and the table passes now space the steps geometrically from 20 m (`ATMOSPHERE_LOG_MARCH`, `get_march_distance`), 128 steps; every other pass keeps the quadratic spacing. It cost nothing measurable.
  - **The radius axis.** With sqrt spaced altitude the horizon of slice 0 and slice 1 (104 m up) differ by 0.3 degrees, and the table interpolates between them at the same elevation parameter: the horizon read +11 percent. 64 slices of that axis fixed it (1.004) at 4 times the memory, a cubic altitude axis fixed it with 32 (0.98). The elevation count (32 or 64) and the directions of the density pass (8x16 to 32x64) did not matter.
  - **Orders.** 4 orders left it 0.076 stops dark, 8 converge for the default aerosol (+0.044 over 4).
  - Not the cause (tested and removed): a spectral view transmittance for the march. exp(-tau) of three coefficients reads a long path wrong (150 km: the folded red is 2.3 times it), but folding 41 wavelengths in the march changed the score by 0.006 stops and cost 4 times the bake.
- Pitfalls: a `Texture:Shade` pipeline is cached by its source and `pipeline:GetTextureIndex` hands out indices per pipeline in order of first use, so hardcoded `TEXTURE(1)` breaks as soon as the same shader runs with other textures (a second order, a rebaked table). Pass the indices in the push constants through `fragment_push_constants.get_data(face, texture, pipeline)`. The sky view bake had the same latent bug after an aerosol change and uses it now. Also: the transmittance LUT is fp16 and underflows for grazing paths, so ratios of it cannot give the transmittance between two points; the bake's ground paths are marched. `Texture.Shade` has no additive blend (`blend` is alpha blending), accumulate with a pass that reads both tables.
- Left: the engine's single scattering reads 3 to 5 percent bright at the zenith (its sun transmittance LUT or the reference, not looked at), the table is 4 to 5 percent bright there too and 3 percent dark towards the sun; `lut.lua`'s `MS=1` shows both.

### One medium in the froxel volume

*2026-10-15, `passes/volumetric_fog.lua`, `froxel_fog.lua`*

- The volume reaches the horizon: `froxel_fog.FAR` is 100 km (96 slices, knee 2 m, 12 percent per slice where 3 km had 8), and the analytic aerial perspective of the composite beyond it (`apply_atmospheric_aerial_perspective`, `apply_ground_fog`, `apply_aerial_perspective`, `get_aerial_perspective_segment`) is gone. A terrain pixel at 40 km is the volume's in-scatter and transmittance at its slice, with the shadowed sun and the probes' light where there are probes, as at 300 m. Beyond FAR a surface gets FAR's fog (nothing past 100 km is drawn in the alpine scene).
- Extinction is per channel now. The scatter pass no longer stores one for the air (it was a luminance, "a fraction of a percent within 3 km", 20 percent at 60 km). The integrate pass finds it per slice from the medium (`compute_view_tau` of the densities at the slice's middle along the ray, plus the fog and the precipitation) and writes the in-scatter and the transmittance as rgb. The integrated volume is one 3D texture twice as deep as the slice count, in-scatter in the first half, transmittance in the second (a consumer samples both with a z of `(S + z) / 2S`, clamped inside each half), so `get_volumetric_fog(uv, distance, out transmittance)` is the only interface: composite and translucent changed, nothing needed a second binding.
- The clear air's multiple scattering comes from the scattering source table (`atmosphere.GetScatteringSourceTexture()`, the sum of the scattering densities of orders 2 and up, baked with the scattering table, `atmosphere_scattering_source_texture_index` in the block): per froxel `sc_lookup(source, r, mu, mu_s, nu) * illuminance * SUN_COLOR`, weighted by the overhead sky visibility, the probes' light times the air's scattering coefficient for the rest (`get_ambient` returns `open_sky` and `probe_ambient`). It replaces `ambient * air_scattering`, whose ambient (`get_ground_fog_sky_ambient`) was the mean radiance of the sky including its single scattering. The ground fog keeps that ambient for now. The table's integral along a ray is the table's radiance (`M(start) - T M(end)`), so the volume's air and the sky's agree.
- Measured with `test/harnesses/fog/sky.lua` (the composited sky against the path tracer, engine over reference): default fog layer 0.98 to 1.04 (0.94 to 1.19 before, the zenith rows were 8 to 19 percent bright), no fog layer 1.00 to 1.04 (0.95 to 1.20), a 2 km visibility fog 0.74 to 1.09 (0.9 to 1.13 where it was not broken, and the committed version read 0.01 in the sun's direction at 2 to 10 degrees, a bug of the old analytic far fog or its history, now 0.75 to 0.81; that dense fog towards the sun is under-lit, in the fog model and not this change). Colorchecker white patch 1.008 1.005 0.998.
- The sky is composed, not subtracted (same day): the lighting pass writes only what is behind the air for the main view's sky pixels (`sky_air` is 0 when the volumetric fog pass is on and there is no render context; the sun disc, the moon and the stars, already attenuated by the air, `GetGLSLMainCode` option `air`), and the composite builds the rest: `volume scattering + volume transmittance * tail scattering + fog layer transmittance * background`. The tail is the air past FAR, `integrate_scattering(origin, dir, FAR, edge)` over 32 geometric steps (`ATMOSPHERE_LOG_MARCH`), skipped where the ray is above 50 km at FAR (a few thousandths of the ground's density). The fog layer's own transmittance (fog and precipitation, grey) is the alpha of the volume's transmittance half, `get_fog_layer_transmittance`, because the background is attenuated by the clear air already. With clouds the same sum splits at the cloud's depth: `front_scattered + front_transmittance * cloud.rgb + cloud.a * (behind_scattered + layer * background)`, the front from the volume at the cloud's depth (or the volume plus a tail march when the cloud is past FAR), what scatters behind it weighted by the light that reaches it. The old route subtracted the analytic air of the volume's range from the sky LUT and added the volume's: two integrators of one integral whose difference showed. The sky LUT is no longer read by the main view; reflections, the env probe and the ambient still use it. With the pass off (or in a render context) the lighting pass writes the whole sky as before.
- The clouds' shaft correction (the shadowed air less the unshadowed, added in front of a cloud) is gone: the froxel pass lights its air with the sun's visibility that includes the cloud shadow map, so the crepuscular rays are in the volume out to 100 km (they stopped at 3 km before the volume grew, and the old branch added them from 3 to 60 km analytically).
- A bug found on the way: `integrate_scattering` looked the multiple scattering table up at the ray's origin, not at `t_near`, so any segment that did not start at the camera got the radiance of the whole ray (a sky ray's tail read 1.6 times too bright at 25 degrees). It looks up at the start point now.
- Measured (`fog/sky.lua`, engine over reference): default fog layer 0.97 to 1.04 at every elevation (0.98 to 1.06 with the subtraction), colorchecker 1.006 1.002 0.994, the cloud harness (`clouds/shoot.sh`, cumulus, three views) looks the same as before in the sky and the crepuscular rays; under the cloud deck the far ground is warmer (the cloud shadow on the air below it) and keeps more of its own colour (per channel transmittance).
- The fog layer's ambient is the table's (same day): `get_ground_fog_sky_ambient` took the mean radiance and the irradiance of the sky from a 4x8 quadrature of the sky view LUT (32 lookups per froxel); they depend only on the height and the sun's elevation, so the scattering bake writes them as a 64x32 table (`atmosphere.GetScatteringAmbientTexture()`, `sc_ambient`, `ATMOSPHERE_SCATTERING_AMBIENT_TEXTURE_INDEX`, the mean radiance in the first 32 columns, the irradiance in the second 32, radius down the rows) from the single scattering plus the light of orders 2 and up over the upper hemisphere. At 60 and 25 degrees it matches a 32x64 quadrature of the LUT within 1 percent (the 4x8 one was 5 percent off). The cloud dome is folded in by a quadrature of the dome alone (`get_sky_hemisphere_integrals`, the clear sky's value times the mean transmittance plus the mean cloud light), and `get_ground_radiance` (the probes' ground below the horizon) reads the table too. Pitfalls: the tables are for a unit sun, every use multiplies by `ATMOSPHERE_SUN_ILLUMINANCE * SUN_COLOR` (forgetting it gave a fog 30 percent too dark that looked like a calibration problem for an hour: print the table with a raw fetch scaled up before blaming the maths); and the fog's own diffuse term (`GROUND_FOG_DIFFUSE_FRACTION`) was fitted with the ambient taken at the camera, per froxel point it fades with the altitude and the 2 km fog harness read 0.7, so the froxel pass still passes the camera's origin.
- The fog's in-scatter is one function (`get_ground_fog_in_scatter(point, ray_dir, sun_dir, sun_visibility, ambient)`, per km): the froxel scatter pass and the analytic `integrate_ground_fog_segment` call it. The analytic integration is marched now (the sun's beam through the layer and the extinction at every sample), where it lit the whole segment with the sun and the extinction at the ray's origin in one slab (`get_ground_fog_color`, gone). The env probe's sky capture (`apply_ground_fog_ray` over the sky LUT, the LUT keeps the moon's sky and the cloud dome) therefore has the same fog as the camera sees. Measured against the 2 km fog path tracer at a 60 degree sun (probe over reference, toward the sun / sideways / away, per elevation of 5, 10, 25, 60): before 0.72 0.90 1.03, 0.72 0.89 1.05, 0.72 0.89 1.07, 0.79 0.85 1.01; now 0.75 0.92 1.05, 0.77 0.92 1.07, 0.82 0.95 1.11, 0.94 0.97 1.10, the same pattern as the camera's volume (the dense fog's sun side is under lit and the far side over lit, in the fog model). Without fog the probe's sky is unchanged and its ground moves 0.15 percent. `tmp`-style probe dump: `envprobe.environment_probe.color_equirect:Download()` after 40 frames, the equirect's rows run up from the bottom.
- Left (each can go in its own change): the fog layer's ambient is evaluated at the camera, not at the point (refit `GROUND_FOG_DIFFUSE_FRACTION` first), the sky view LUT could be rebuilt from the same medium, the cost of the longer integrate pass and the scatter pass over 100 km of froxels is not measured.

### The fog's light is a fitted model

*2026-10-15, `get_ground_fog_sun`, `get_ground_fog_sky_ambient` in `atmosphere.lua`, fit tools in `test/harnesses/fog/fit/`*

- Why dense fog read 25 percent dark towards the sun: `ORDERS=1,2 FOG=2000 luajit glw ... fog/sky.lua` prints the reference limited to that many scatterings. In a 2 km fog the single scattering is 10 to 25 percent of the light (5389 of 21082 cd/m2 at 5 degrees towards a 60 degree sun), the rest is light that scattered more than once, and that is forward peaked too: the reference is 1.9 times brighter towards the sun than away from it, the engine's isotropic ambient 1.36 times. At a 25 degree sun, which the earlier calibration never looked at, the old model was far worse: 300 m fog 1.4 to 2.0 times too bright (rms 0.45), 1 and 2 km 0.2.
- Two things were wrong. The fog's light does not scale with the sky's mean radiance (nearly the same at a 25 and a 60 degree sun) but with the irradiance entering the top of the layer (0.33 of the dense fog's light at 25 against 60 degrees); and the old diffuse term, `E (1 - T(point)) * 0.25 / pi`, is zero at the top of the layer and largest at the bottom where the real light is dimmest.
- The model now: the sun's beam scatters as three octaves (Wrenninge's multiple scattering approximation, `get_ground_fog_sun`: octave n has its weight, extinction and phase asymmetry scaled by 0.95^n, 0.544^n, 0.669^n; the octaves from the second on are weighted by `thickness^0.929`, with `thickness = 1 - exp(-vertical optical depth of the layer)`, the octaves alone gave 1.2 in a 5 km fog and 0.6 in a 300 m one); the sky's mean radiance, the thin fog's source, fades out as `(1 - thickness)`; and a thick fog is lit by what enters its top, `(sky irradiance + sun irradiance) * 0.33 * thickness^1.685 / pi` (`get_ground_fog_sky_ambient`). `GROUND_FOG_DIFFUSE_FRACTION` and the `(1 - T)` term are gone.
- How it was fitted, because the next change to the fog needs it again: `tmp/fogref/sun<elevation>_vis<visibility>.txt` from `fit/reference.sh` (15 views, the engine and the path tracer, 3 minutes a file), then `fit/model.lua`, the engine's fog along a ray in luminance, which reproduced the engine within 1 percent (rms 0.012) so a parameter set costs milliseconds and not a GPU run, and `fit/opt.lua`, a Nelder-Mead over 135 rows (visibilities 300 m to 20 km, suns of 25 and 60 degrees, 15 views each). The tried structures, rms over the 135 rows: octaves alone with the old diffuse term fitted 0.14; octaves and the old term 0.078 at 60 degrees only (and 1.4 to 2 times at 25, it did not generalise); a depth profile `1 / (1 + k tau)` or a second diffuse term added nothing (0.076 against 0.078); with the irradiance term and every parameter free 0.090; with the physical constraints (the sky's mean at full weight in a thin fog, no depth profile) 0.0957, the one used. Fitted on 60 degrees only it scores 0.116 on both suns, on 25 only 0.102: the structure generalises. A parameter at a bound is a sign that the data does not constrain it (the unconstrained fit put the sky's ambient at its lower bound because a thin fog barely shows it), so the physical form was imposed instead.
- Engine over path tracer now (rms of the log over the rows, 15 views, `fit/compare.sh <sun> <visibility>`): 60 degrees 300 m 0.113, 1 km 0.098, 2 km 0.104, 5 km 0.078, 20 km 0.050, no fog 0.016; 25 degrees 300 m 0.180, 1 km 0.058, 2 km 0.081, 5 km 0.087, 20 km 0.064, no fog 0.031. Before: 60 degrees about 0.19 at 300 m and 0.2 at 1 and 2 km; 25 degrees 0.449, 0.222, 0.211. The default 140 km fog is unchanged (0.97 to 1.04), the colorchecker too, the env probe's fog follows the camera's (same function).
- Left: looking steeply up through a 1 to 5 km fog (60 degrees) the model is 10 to 27 percent bright, and a 300 m fog at a 25 degree sun is up to 34 percent bright at the horizon and 20 percent dark overhead; no structure tried removed the elevation trend (the sky's ambient decaying with depth, a depth profile on the diffuse term, a second diffuse term), it likely needs the radiance to depend on the view's elevation, which a source along the ray does not.

### The sun on surfaces under the fog

The ground fog used to attenuate the sun only in its own in-scatter; the direct light on surfaces (`light.color` from the CPU, the air's transmittance) never saw the layer, so a surface under 300 m fog still got the full sun. `get_ground_fog_sun_transmittance` (atmosphere.lua, `ground_fog_sun_transmittance` with plain numbers, `atmosphere.GetGroundFogSunTransmittance` on the CPU) is the closed form of the layer's optical depth above a point along the sun's slant, `exp(-(ext0 H (exp(-h / H) - exp(-top / H)) + precipitation (top - h)) / sin(elevation))`, and now multiplies the sun's radiance by the surface's height: in `surface_lighting.lua` (the lighting and the translucent pass, diffuse and specular alike), and in the DDGI probe hit shading (`ddgi_direct_light`, `ddgi_ground_fog` in the probe block, so the bounce light under the fog is right too). Measured with the colour chart before the fog pass (`PREFOG=1`, `FOG_VIS=<m>`), the sun's direct light as the sunlit minus the shaded run (the ambient changes with fog, the fog's own light is in front of the chart after the pass): relative to the 140 km default, 5 km 0.785, 1 km 0.288 and 300 m 0.014, the closed form 0.787, 0.292, 0.016. The default fog takes 1 percent of the sun at 60 degrees. What still does not see the fog: traced reflection hits (`scene_reflection.lua`), the ocean pass, and the cloud lighting (a cloud layer sits above the fog layer anyway).

## Local lights: units

*harness `test/harnesses/lights/point.lua`, 2026-10-15*

Matte white plates (albedo 0.9, specular off) in a night scene (sun at -30 degrees, black ground, `ddgi_strength 0`, reflections and ambient occlusion off) lit by one light; the plates' luminance read from the scene source texture, absolute cd/m2 (the pre exposure undone), turned into lux with `L * pi / albedo` and compared with the light's definition. `KIND=point|spot H= LUMEN= RANGE= RADIUS= QUAD= LINEAR= CONSTANT= INNER= OUTER= COLOR= OFFSETS= EV=`.
- Point: `Lumen` is luminous flux, intensity `Lumen / (4 pi)` cd (`GetInverseEmissionSolidAngle`), illuminance `I * cos(incidence) * window^2 / (source radius^2 + constant + linear d + quadratic d^2)`, quadratic 1 by default, so inverse square. Measured against that at 0.5, 1, 2 and 4 m, at 0 to 56 degrees of incidence: 0.99 to 1.02 on the axis and under the light (the +2 percent is the diffuse BRDF: a white 0.9 plate under EON sends back 1.02 of Lambert's at normal incidence), and +13 percent at 45 degrees with the camera on the light's side, -8 percent on the far side, which is EON's retro reflection (computed by hand for the +13 percent: 1.136 of Lambert's at that geometry, measured 1.126), not the light.
- The window `(1 - (d / range)^4)^2` is Frostbite's, so an explicit `Range` distorts the inverse square well inside it: -12 percent at half the range, a tenth of the light at 90 percent of it (measured against it: 0.99 to 1.04). The automatic range (the distance where the light falls to 0.0005 lux, capped at 200 m, 31.6 m for 3000 lm) leaves it at 1.0 for any plausible distance.
- `SourceRadius` adds its square to the falloff, `1 / (d^2 + r^2)` (UE4's `d^2 + 1` regularisation with a radius): finite at the light, 6.5 percent under the physical sphere light's `1 / d^2` at 3.3 radii, 1 percent at 10. Left as designed, a radius of 0 is exact. `GlowRadius` only widens the specular lobe.
- Colour: the light's colour is divided by its luminance, so hue does not change the brightness (60.88 lux for white, orange and blue lights alike, plate rgb over luminance 1.71, 0.855, 0.342 for (1, 0.5, 0.2)).
- Spot: the shader's cone is full strength inside the inner angle and `smoothstep(cos outer, cos inner, cos angle)` out to the outer one, but the intensity was `Lumen / (2 pi (1 - cos outer))`, the outer cone's solid angle, so the taper took light away: a 10 to 20 degree spot emitted 63 percent of its lumens, a 0 to 30 degree one 50 percent, 15 to 25 68 percent. `light_spot.lua` `GetEmissionSolidAngle` is now the integral of the cone, `2 pi (1 - (cos inner + cos outer) / 2)`, and the lamp emits exactly its lumens (the harness integrates the cone, 1.000), so spots set by hand in lumens are 1.6 times brighter at 10/20, twice at 0/30. Source engine lights go in as candela and out through the same function, so they do not change. Measured illuminance on the axis and across the cone against the formula: 0.99 to 1.04.

## PBR shading audit (direct light)

*harness `test/harnesses/brdf/audit.lua`, 2026-10-14. `luajit test/harnesses/brdf/audit.lua`, no gpu*

The direct light's BRDF in `surface_lighting.lua` and `ibl.lua`: GGX normal distribution, height correlated Smith visibility, Schlick Fresnel (F90 from `F90(f0)`, 1 for any F0 of 0.02 and up), diffuse `kD * albedo * Fd_Burley` with `kD = (1 - F(L.H)) (1 - metallic)`, `F0 = 0.04 * specular` or the albedo for a metal, alpha = perceptual roughness squared. The audit evaluates exactly these formulas in lua and integrates them over the hemisphere (directional albedo: the share of the light a surface sends back for a given view, a physical surface can send back at most 1).
- **No multiple scattering energy for the direct light (fixed 2026-10-15).** `GGXEnergyCompensation` (Kulla-Conty) was applied to the ambient specular (`passes/lighting.lua`, `translucent.lua`) and not to the sun and the lights. A white metal (F0 1) sends back, per view at n.v 0.1 / 0.5 / 1.0: 0.95 / 1.00 / 1.00 at perceptual roughness 0.1, 0.89 / 0.86 / 0.92 at 0.5, 0.86 / 0.70 / 0.70 at 0.7, and 0.76 / 0.45 / 0.31 at 1.0. `get_direct_light` (`surface_lighting.lua`) multiplies the specular by `1 + F0 (1 / E - 1)` with E from the brdf lut, once per surface. Checked on the gpu with `test/harnesses/brdf/shade.lua` (white metal plates, the sun's light alone as the difference between a picture in the sun and one with a cube in front of it, the shader against the same BRDF in lua with the compensation): measured over expected at roughness 0.3 / 0.5 / 0.7 / 0.85 / 1.0 was 0.93 / 0.87 / 0.68 / 0.52 / 0.39 before (the uncompensated energy, the same as the audit) and 0.94 / 0.98 / 0.99 / 0.99 / 1.00 after. The 6 percent at 0.3 is the sun's finite size: the lobe is widened by the sun's angular radius and its peak scaled down (`lobe_energy`), for a sharp lobe that is a few percent at the highlight. The same plates in white dielectric agree within 0.3 percent from roughness 0.5.
- **Burley diffuse was not energy conserving, and the Fresnel coupling gained energy (both fixed 2026-10-15).** Alone, a white Burley surface sent back (relative to Lambert) 1.38 at n.v 0.1 and roughness 1, 1.15 at 0.3, 1.04 at 0.6 and 1.0, and 0.69 at n.v 0.1 at roughness 0 (the Frostbite energy factor was not in the code). The diffuse was scaled by `1 - F(L.H)` while the specular reflects `F` at the view angle, so at a grazing view a white dielectric sent back specular + diffuse = 1.28 at roughness 0.1 and n.v 0.1, 1.18 at 0.3, 1.14 at 0.5, 1.20 at 0.7, 1.36 at 1.0 (0.98-1.03 at n.v 0.5 and 1.0).
- **The diffuse is EON now, Fresnel coupled.** `get_direct_light` uses `Fd = Fd_EON(albedo, perceptual roughness, N.L, N.V, L.V) (1 - E(n.v)) (1 - F(n.l)) / (1 - average F)`. `Fd_EON` (`ibl.lua`) is the energy preserving Oren-Nayar of Portsmouth, Kutz and Hill (EON, JCGT 2025, arXiv 2410.18026): Fujii's Oren-Nayar single scatter term `rho / pi (A + B s / t)` (`A = 1 / (1 + (1/2 - 2 / (3 pi)) r)`, `B = r A`, `s = L.V - N.L N.V`, `t = max(N.L, N.V)` where `s > 0` and 1 otherwise, r the perceptual roughness) plus the multiple scatter term `rho^2 / pi <E> / (1 - rho (1 - <E>)) (1 - E(N.L)) (1 - E(N.V)) / (1 - <E>)` with the paper's quartic fit of the directional albedo (`g1..g4` 0.0571085289, 0.491881867, -0.332181442, 0.0714429953). It keeps the retro reflection of a rough surface at a grazing light and a white surface sends back exactly 1 (the audit's EON table, 1.000 at every roughness and view, and 0.456-0.485 at an albedo of 0.5). The coat: what the view's specular does not take (`1 - E(n.v)`, E from the brdf lut with the compensation), entering through `1 - F(n.l)` and divided by the average of that, `average F = F0 + (F90 - F0) / 21`. White dielectric, compensated specular + coupled EON diffuse, directional albedo over 15 roughness and view combinations: 0.96 to 1.007 (the audit's last column; the Lambert version of the coupling was exactly 1.000, EON's angular shape against the `1 - F(n.l)` weight costs up to 4 percent at a grazing view and roughness 1). A surface with no coat (F0 0, `SpecularMultiplier` 0) is EON alone. Metals have no diffuse and `average F` is clamped so a white metal does not divide by zero (a NaN, found by the plate harness). Checked on the gpu with `test/harnesses/brdf/shade.lua`: white dielectric plates agree with the same formulas in lua (EON included) within 0.3 percent from roughness 0.5.
- The ambient diffuse of the opaque lighting pass (`passes/lighting.lua`) uses the same `1 - E(n.v)` now (the brdf lut's directional albedo with the compensation, it was `1 - F_SchlickRoughness`) and stays Lambert, the irradiance is already the cosine integral so EON's angular shape does not apply (its average is what `1 - E` divides by). `translucent.lua` keeps `1 - F_SchlickRoughness` for its ambient diffuse and its transmission: with the lut version the refractive materials test (`test/tests/render3d/translucent.lua`, the clear glass must differ from the scene behind it) failed, the glass reflected too little, so it was left alone, look at it separately.
- Schlick against the exact dielectric Fresnel (n 1.5): equal at 0, 75 degrees, 21 percent low at 60 degrees (0.070 against 0.089), 6 percent high at 85. Negligible for dielectrics, Schlick loses the tint at the grazing edge of metals.
- Fine as it is: GGX D (`alpha^2 / (pi (n.h^2 (alpha^2 - 1) + 1)^2)`), `V_SmithGGXCorrelated` is Filament's with `a2 = alpha^2` (clamped at 1e-3, which makes alpha under 0.03 behave as 0.03), the ambient specular's split sum with the energy compensation, `F0 = 0.04 * specular`.

### Metals: Schlick against the exact Fresnel

*harness `test/harnesses/brdf/metals.lua` (plain lua), 2026-10-15*

A metal is `F0 = albedo`, `F_Schlick` with a white grazing reflectance (`F90` is 1 for any bright F0). The harness compares that with the exact unpolarised Fresnel equations of a conductor (complex index n + ik per rgb channel, the usual pbrt / mitsuba fits, good for the shape of the curve, not for absolute F0) and with the F82 tint model (Hoffman 2023, the Adobe standard material), Schlick minus a dip that passes through the exact value at 82 degrees.
- Gold, silver and copper: Schlick is within 0.02 absolute at every angle, within 3 percent in the directional albedo of the specular lobe. Nothing to fix.
- Aluminium and iron: Schlick is too bright towards grazing, aluminium by 0.04 at 70 degrees and 0.12 at 82, iron by 0.08 and 0.17 (the exact iron curve is 0.51 at 70 degrees, 0.58 at 82, Schlick 0.59 and 0.75). In the specular lobe's directional albedo (green) it is 1.09 for aluminium and 1.27 for iron at n.v 0.1 and roughness 0.15, 1.06 and 1.20 at roughness 0.4, within 4 percent for n.v of 0.4 and up. So only low F0 grey metals (iron, steel, chrome, nickel) seen at a glancing angle, mostly smooth ones.
- F82 tint brings both within 0.02 of the exact curve, but needs the exact reflectance at 82 degrees as a per material value (a tint colour or a scalar dip). It is not a function of F0: iron and aluminium dip (0.77 and 0.89 of Schlick at 82 degrees), gold, silver and copper do not (0.97 to 1.01). The engine has no such parameter (`specular_tint` is the dielectric's), the IBL's BRDF LUT would need a third term (the integral of the dip) and so would the energy compensation, and imported content has no F82 data, so it would stay Schlick. Not done.
- Reference F0 for a metal's albedo (linear rgb, Filament's table after Hoffman): silver 0.972, 0.960, 0.915; aluminium 0.913, 0.921, 0.925; gold 1.000, 0.766, 0.336; copper 0.955, 0.638, 0.538; iron 0.562, 0.565, 0.578; titanium 0.542, 0.497, 0.449; platinum 0.673, 0.637, 0.585; brass 0.910, 0.778, 0.423. ## Translucency and refraction

Measured with `test/harnesses/translucency/` (each prints the engine against the exact reference): `fresnel.lua`, `pane.lua` (a pane against emissive walls, absolute radiance, one and two interface formulas), `sphere.lua` (a ball in front of a stripe wall against an exact ray trace of the ball, stripes encode their index) and `transmission.lua` (diffuse transmission, below).

- **Refraction** (`features/refraction.lua`, `passes/translucent.lua`) is glass: the exact unpolarised dielectric Fresnel (not Schlick; the split sum is scaled by exact over Schlick), a thin wall is a pane (two interfaces, `2F / (1 + F)`), a solid has its far side's Fresnel and total internal reflection, and `AttenuationDistance` is glTF's attenuation distance for Beer-Lambert. Reflection depends on the index (`((n - 1) / (n + 1))^2` head on), so a ball at index 1 does not reflect and one at 40 is a mirror; that is the physics, a constant gloss would be a clearcoat. The index is clamped to 1 or more.
- A refracted ray that leaves the screen is ray traced when it can (`trace_scene_reflection`, a hit only), otherwise the screen's edge it left through stands in for the scene, blurred and fading to the probes. Not a probe alone: a probe is dark where the scene is not.
- **Rough glass:** one ray through a rough interface lands on one object, so a blurred lookup at where it landed kept the hard edge of whatever the ray met (sharp silhouettes of the geometry behind). From roughness 0.04 up, four rays across the cone are averaged. Their noise must be a hash of pixel and frame: interleaved gradient noise has diagonal structure that slides across the glass as the frame moves (stripes that follow the framerate), and deriving the cone's rotation from the same value as the step jitter made it worse.
- **Transmission** (`features/transmission.lua`) is diffuse transmission for leaves and cloth, not glass: light arriving at the back lights the front side (lambert, blended by `TransmissionScattering` to a Henyey-Greenstein lobe around the light, g = 0.6), the opaque diffuse is scaled by `1 - T`.
