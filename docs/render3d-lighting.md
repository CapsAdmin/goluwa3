# Render3D lighting, GI, fog, sky, water and exposure

Physically based, absolute-unit lighting: scene radiance is in cd/m2 (daylight is thousands, the sun disc ~2e9), exposure is applied at the end. When something looks off, work out which term is wrong with an isolated scene (one light, locked exposure, other terms off) rather than tuning by eye. Pass sources are in `goluwa/render3d/passes/`, shared lighting glsl in `surface_lighting.lua`, `ibl.lua`, `atmosphere.lua`, `ddgi.lua`, `scene_reflection.lua`. Every pass has an `r_pass_<name>` pvar. Ray traced consumers read the scene store ([gpu-scene.md](gpu-scene.md)); on hardware without ray queries they use stubs (test with `GOLUWA_NO_RAY_TRACING=1`: no DDGI trace, light occlusion, particle ray collision or traced reflections; screen space paths remain).

## Exposure and units

- **Scene color is pre-exposed.** The fp16 scene targets (lighting, ocean, fog composite, translucent, taa) hold absolute radiance times `post_source.get_pre_exposure()` (last frame's exposure over `PRE_EXPOSURE_HEADROOM`), because fp16 cannot hold both a starlit night and the sun. Lighting math stays absolute: multiply when writing scene color, divide when reading it for shading, rescale histories by `get_pre_exposure() / get_previous_pre_exposure()`. Probe captures have pre-exposure 1 and stay absolute. New passes that read or write scene color must use `post_source.pre_exposure_block` + `GetPreExposureGLSL`. Radiance limits (firefly clamps) belong in exposed units (`post_source.GetExposureTexture(true)`), not absolute ones.
- Exposure is one number (EV100) with two front ends, both owned by `goluwa/render3d/exposure.lua` (plain state with `exposure.Get/Set`, no pvars; the Look desktop program edits it). Camera: normalises the metered mean to the grey midpoint `camera.grey` (percent, 18 is middle grey), clamped to `camera.ev_min`..`camera.ev_max` (equal values lock it). ISO, aperture and shutter are derived from the exposure along a program line (`camera.mode`: program, aperture/shutter priority, or manual which locks EV) and only drive effects: shutter the motion blur length, ISO the grain in the blit (`r_grain`), aperture the depth of field (`r_dof`, focused by a raycast through the screen centre that skips the active view's `Ignore` entity, the player's rig for the game camera). Effect shaders get them from the exposure texture through `exposure.GetCameraSettingsGLSL` + `exposure.camera_block`; the eye has no camera, so it reads as ISO 100, f/64, 1/60. Eye (`eye.*`): no normalisation. The exposure follows the adapting luminance (the wide-field log-average of the scene, clamped to `eye.adaptation_min`..`eye.adaptation_max` in log10 cd/m2, converted with EV = log2(L) + 3) along the Ferwerda 1996 cone and rod threshold curve (Ward 1994 above 64 cd/m2), with mesopic rod/cone mixing from 0.005 to 5 cd/m2. The rod branch takes scotopic luminance, not photopic: the meter also log-averages `2.3 * dot(rgb, ROD_RESPONSE)` (2.3 is the scotopic/photopic ratio of D65 white) and the curve uses the ratio of the two means, so blue-white light reads brighter to the rods than tungsten at the same cd/m2 (the curve is evaluated in the exposure shader, no baked table); rod vision (`eye.night_vision`) desaturates to the rod spectral response below `eye.night_vision_threshold`. The viewer's own adaptation to the screen dims the display in dim scenes: displayed white is the paper white times `min(L_adapt / 64 cd/m2, 1) ^ eye.viewer_adaptation` (0.33, Stevens' brightness exponent; fitted by eye from a candle lit room needing 25 nits at paper white 200), so one paper white (the display's real SDR white) serves candle rooms and daylight; camera mode is unaffected. There is no artistic layer in the eye: no compensation, no local adaptation, no tint. Bloom and glare are optics, built from the scene before anything else. Their angles become pixels through `eye.display_fov` (the vertical angle the screen takes up at the viewer's eye, `exposure.GetPixelsPerDegree`), never the game camera's field of view: the viewer's eye does not zoom with the picture, so a zoomed camera must not grow the glare, the defocus or the starburst. The camera's glare spreads a flat share of energy per octave of angle (Stiles-Holladay, 1/theta^2); the eye's is the CIE 1999 general glare spread function (Vos and van den Berg, steeper than 1/theta^2 below a few degrees, standard observer with pigmentation 0.5), integrated per bloom level in `passes/bloom.lua` (per channel for the primaries' dominant wavelengths 611, 549 and 464 nm against 560: the two core terms are diffraction, so their angles scale with wavelength; the 400/(1+(theta/0.1)^2) and theta^2 terms are particle scatter, scaled by wavelength^-4; the glare share is per channel; `r_eye_chromatic_halo`). The CIE function and the wavelength scaling live in `render3d/eye_glare.lua`. The starburst (`r_eye_starburst`, `passes/starburst.lua`) is the diffraction pattern of the eye: `render3d/eye_psf.lua` builds a pupil aperture (disc, hundreds of random radial lens fibres of random width, length and opacity, lashes, dust specks; each strip diffracts into its own spike, so spike length, width and brightness are random), FFTs it on the CPU (`math/fft.lua`, once, about 1.5 s on the first eye frame) and stores the power spectrum as a polar table (angle by log radius) normalised so every ring averages 1 (each ring is box filtered to 0.25 degrees of arc first, or the table aliases without mipmaps and the thin rays wobble with the TAA jitter). The table adds the rays of a star around bright compact light sources (only the part above the ring mean: subtracting the dark gaps overshoots the smooth bloom and eats the source's own pixels, which showed as black bursts around lamps): a tile pass (1/16 resolution) finds each tile's summed radiance above the local background (the 1/16 resolution blur, so a uniform sky or a big bright area contributes nothing: discrete tile sources cannot cancel the energy neutral pattern the way a continuous field does, and leave a grid at the tile pitch) and its centroid with a bilinear tent assignment (a tile is only a star source while the bright light within 16 half resolution pixels of it is compact, `COMPACT_SPREAD`: a long neon tube or a big sign becomes a row of separate stars 16 pixels apart whose thin rays do not overlap, and sums to blotches, where a real extended source averages its rays into smooth glare) (every pixel's energy is shared between neighbouring tiles with weights summing to 1, so a light on a tile boundary does not hop between tiles as the TAA jitter moves it), a half resolution gather adds `E * spread(theta) * (modulation - 1)` per channel, with theta scaled by pupil diameter (Moon-Spencer, from the adapting luminance) and by wavelength, when a temporal anti-aliasing mode is on (it jitters the camera) the result is blended 0.9 with the previous frame, reprojected by the camera rotation only and faded out as soon as the camera turns more than a third of a pixel or moves more than a few millimetres in a frame (the history only exists to average the jitter of a still camera; resampling it while moving diffuses the star into a smear, and a rotation only reprojection cannot follow a moving camera or light) (the star hangs on a direction, and a short average cannot remove a flicker with the 16 frame jitter period) (the source is the jittered scene before TAA, whose summed brightness of a tiny emitter flickers about 10% and whose position moves with the jitter; the centroid is shifted by half the jitter), then the glare pass adds it to the bloom. The afterimage (`r_eye_afterimage`, `passes/afterimage.lua`) is a per pixel retinal state at 1/8 resolution in screen space (it follows the view like an afterimage follows the eye), fed from `bloom_down3` plus the glare (the retinal image, so the blob's falloff is the eye's own), read back through a 3x3 gaussian blur at that resolution (display only, not fed back into the state, so it cannot diffuse over time) and bicubic: it is a blobby glow, not a copy of the picture. It has three parts. The source stage is the light's own colour (cone direction times a saturating level) held while it is lit and drained over `eye.afterimage_source_seconds` (0.3 s, not scaled). Behind it a single age value per pixel (held while lit, decaying as `level ^ eye.afterimage_falloff / (8 s * eye.afterimage_time_scale)`, held back while the source stage is strong) runs through a colour ramp, white, yellow, green, blue, purple, red (red lingers: it holds the last third of the age), with brightness fading with the level. The falloff: 0 is a plain exponential, below 0 the decay speeds up as it fades so the tail is cut off and it ends in a finite time (-1 is linear in light), above 0 it slows down and leaves a tail (1 is hyperbolic). Last, bleaching (`dB/dt = (L_cone / 4000 cd/m2)(1 - B) - B / (16 s * time scale)` per cone type, Hunt-Pointer-Estevez) multiplies the cone signals by `1 - B`, a complementary tinted darker patch on bright fields. A ramp reads about like the real flight of colours and is much cheaper than modelling the cone types. A light must be `eye.afterimage_adaptation_ratio` times the adapting luminance to excite it, and `eye.afterimage_intensity` scales the whole effect (0 skips the passes). The update gathers the retinal image along each pixel's motion (the velocity buffer, the camera reprojection for sky) at one sample per state pixel, taking the max, so a light sweeping over the screen leaves a continuous streak and not one dot per frame; a motion over 0.2 of the screen is a cut and is ignored. Two more eye-only effects live in the final shader in `passes/blit.lua`, each behind a pvar so it can be isolated: `r_eye_chromatic_defocus` (red and blue blurred by the pupil, Moon-Spencer diameter from the adapting luminance, 0.35 D and 0.9 D of longitudinal chromatic aberration against the focused green) and `r_eye_noise` (the grain of a dark adapted eye, in displayed units and faded out with adaptation: sd `strength * dark_adapted * (0.0025 + 0.015 * sqrt(shown))` of display white, 2x2 pixel blocks, tinted purple (a red 0.75, green 0.35, blue 1.0 weighting, as the dark adapted eye sees it), divided by the exposure and by the viewer adaptation scale so it is the size of what is finally shown: at night that scale is about 0.07 and would otherwise shrink the noise 14 times, `dark_adapted` 1 below 0.01 cd/m2 adapting luminance and 0 above 3; in absolute scene luminance the noise was a few thousandths of a code value and could not be seen). Both end in the same display transform (`r_tonemapper`, default `neutral`, SDR or HDR). The camera meter is a centre weighted mean of linear luminance with each pixel held at the 95th percentile of a log histogram; the eye adapts to the wide-field log-average (uniform weights, no clamp), so one lamp cannot dominate it. `eye.rod_adaptation` defaults to 1, the full rod+cone curve; lower values blend toward the cone-only curve, which is darker at night than the model's own data. A view's `ExposureLock` (EV100, `glw shot --ev`) fixes exposure in either mode. Shader in `passes/blit.lua`.
- Motion blur (`passes/motion_blur.lua`) runs after TAA from the velocity buffer (current minus previous screen position). By default it gathers only behind the motion (`r_motion_blur_trailing`), so the leading edge keeps its position and only the trail extends; the symmetric kernel stretches things ahead of where they are and hides a wrong velocity sign, so test sign changes with trailing on. The faster neighbour a pixel borrows its direction from is searched out to `r_motion_blur_max` with a reach test (the neighbour's blur must reach the pixel), never with a threshold on the cap.
- The `neutral` tonemapper leaves everything below 0.76 of exposed white unchanged (no toe or contrast); SDR and HDR are the same curve with different `peak`. The other curves (`agx`, `aces`, `gt7`) are looks. HDR output (`r_hdr_peak`, `r_hdr_paper_white`) cannot know the display; they are settings.
- `goluwa/render3d/grade.lua`: a grade preset is a table of pvars (anything left out resets to default, so a preset is the whole look); `grade.SetPreset(name, seconds)` blends. Controls after the tonemapper (`r_grade_*`: brightness, contrast, saturation, warmth, lift/gain) are applied in `grade_display`. At defaults the grade is exactly the tonemapper.
- **Measuring lighting needs control of exposure.** Auto exposure brings a dark leaking room and a correct black room both to mid grey; pin EV (indoor ~10.5, outdoor ~15.5) or read a texture before exposure (the DDGI resolve texture `ddgi.GetScreenTexture`, or `post_source.GetSceneSourceTexture`). Local exposure also brightens dark regions, so disable it for absolute comparisons. A one-frame camera override measures unconverged GI: hold the view with `Shot.Capture` (`settle` or `converge`), and avoid pitch of exactly +-90. A converged mean says nothing about shimmer: compare captures spread over time (frames 3 apart are correlated by hysteresis and understate flicker many times; use ~20-frame gaps, and auto exposure for night scenes). Test scenes must set `MetallicMultiplier = 0` explicitly for non-metals.

## DDGI

Probe-based GI over cascades (`goluwa/render3d/ddgi.lua` for glsl and lookup, `passes/ddgi.lua` for the passes: trace, shade, probe data/relocation, irradiance and distance integrate, guide, resolve). It is the only GI.

- **Cascades**: `ddgi.CASCADES` (10) starting at `ddgi_probe_spacing` (0.5 m), spacing doubling, fitted to the scene bounds and `ddgi_max_coverage`; cascades past the one covering everything are dropped. A pixel samples at most two. Cascade n updates every `ddgi_update_interval_*` frames (spaced geometrically between `_min` and `_max`), and a cascade updates early when the sun moved (`ddgi_change_threshold`, `ddgi_change_min_interval`; plus a short fast-history window after a large change). Only the sun is tracked; other changes wait for the interval, or call `ddgi.ResetHistory()`. If far GI looks stale after a non-sun change, lower the relevant interval. The finest cascade updating every frame is the largest single DDGI cost.
- **History**: a texel keeps `ddgi_hysteresis` to the power of the cascade's update interval; a probe with few frames behind it weighs the new frame 1/(n+1). A probe whose mean ray brightness consistently drifts one way (detector follows the median of the mean, fast rate decided from the frames *before* this one) takes the new frame faster, so lights switching are followed but lone bright rays (spikes) are not taken in at the fast rate (that biases GI up and causes fireflies). Reset, moved or disabled probes start over.
- **Rays**: `ddgi_guided_rays` is the share aimed by the probe's own octahedral radiance guide (path guiding); guide cells also aim at their brightest spot (`ddgi_aim_share`, a build time define). Every pass that needs a ray direction recomputes it from the same guide, which is only replaced at the end of the frame so trace, shade and integrate agree (any guide is unbiased if they agree). Probe rays start `DDGI_ORIGIN_PULL` behind the probe so a probe lying on a thin wall hits it at once from the far side. `ddgi_ray_clamp` exists but is off (it darkens GI).
- **Emitters**: emissive surfaces reach probes only through emitter samples (rays alone give fireflies on small emitters): `ddgi_pick_emitter_sample` draws `DDGI_EMITTER_CANDIDATES` emitters by power and keeps one in proportion to the light it brings (resampled importance sampling), weights `facing / max(d^2, r^2)`. The shade pass rebuilds the same point from the same numbers. Additive materials are not emitters unless `ddgi_additive_emitters` (an additive billboard once held 99.9% of all emitter power). A material that is "albedo alpha emissive" emits scaled by the albedo's average alpha. Check total emitter power before tuning samplers when emitter noise appears. A light bvh, not a grid, is the answer if far apart emitters become a problem.
- **Lookup rules** (`ddgi_sample_cascade`): visibility uses a Chebyshev test plus ray queries (`DDGI_VISIBILITY_RAYS`; a pass must bind the TLAS to get them, and fog and any new distant consumer need it or sealed buildings leak outdoor probes). A probe is traced when its blend weight is >= `DDGI_VISIBILITY_MIN_WEIGHT` or when it would add more than `DDGI_VISIBILITY_MIN_SHARE` of the light so far (faint-weight probes outside a roof can be 1000x brighter than a dark room, so weight alone is not enough). Lookups at hit points (probe bounces, reflection hits: `DDGI_CONTRAST_VISIBILITY_RAYS`) trace probes `ddgi_contrast_ratio` times brighter than the point's reference. A probe a ray found hidden counts for nothing; there is no fallback to hidden probes (such points fade to the next cascade). `DDGI_NO_VISIBILITY_RAYS` is for consumers that cannot leak (the open sea surface). If a leak persists while visibility rays seem to do nothing, first check that the rays are even issued.
- **Relocation**: probes in gaps narrower than twice the relocation distance, or inside thin sheets, must stay disabled (they jiggle and leak sky); moves go along the dominant axis of the backface direction only. A probe resting on a surface (its short backface hits all on one side, as on a floor that lies on a probe plane) is moved off it to the open side instead: disabled, every cascade would light that surface from a full spacing away and each cascade from a different height, which shows as steps at the cascade edges. Use `ddgi_debug_gi` and `ddgi_debug_probes` to inspect.
- `ddgi_strength` scales the light probes give every consumer (the lookup wrapper), not the probes' own bounce rays (`DDGI_UNSCALED`). `ddgi_resolve_resolution` fixes the pixel count of the screen GI. The resolve looks probes up along the AO bent normal; the AO pass must rotate it from the depth-derived geometric normal onto the shading normal (faint banding following mesh facets means something uses a depth derived normal), and its sideways lean fades on walls.
- A coarse cascade only keeps the shadow of an occluder taller than its probe spacing: a surface lookup takes part of its light from the probe layer one spacing out (the smooth blend and the facing weight favour it), and that layer sees past a lower occluder. The lookup biases (`NORMAL_BIAS`, `VIEW_BIAS`, in spacings) stop growing at `BIAS_MAX_SPACING` meters so coarse cascades sample close to the surface.
- Octahedral atlas borders (`ddgi_texel_direction`): edge texels mirror the interior row or column next to them, corner texels take the diagonally opposite interior corner. All four corners of the octahedral square are -z, so surfaces facing -z (and any lookup near it) sample a corner: a wrong corner texel shows as seams and static speckle wherever the bent normal's x or y crosses zero.
- Faceted/thin wall rules of thumb: planes without thickness cannot be traced reliably from a surface; sun shadows of zero-thickness quads leak along edges and are not a DDGI problem.
- Verify with `test/tests/render3d/ddgi.lua` (sealed rooms of single and double sided planes, partitions, pinhole rooms against slow averages). For noise, score the rms of many frames against their mean *and* print the mean luminance (flicker that comes with a darker room is bias); compare runs at the same warm up, since multi-bounce in a lit room takes thousands of frames to settle. Scratch scripts in `./tmp/`; set pvars that shaders are built from through `GOLUWA_PVARS`.

## Shadows

- Sun shadow maps are built by `weather.BuildSunShadows`: `cascades` (follow the camera frustum) and `inset` (a close fine map). Change settings with `weather.SetSunShadow(category, {name = value})` (names are `SUN_SHADOW_DEFAULTS` in `weather.lua`; `enabled`, `count`, `size`, `format` recreate the maps, the rest is written live). The Sun dial window's Shadows tab edits them and shows the reach and texel size. The inset works without cascades.
- A cascade fits the bounding sphere of its frustum slice divided by `zoom`; the radius is kept at or above half the slice depth so narrow fields of view still cover mid range. Texel sizes are what matters: compute them from `UpdateCascadeLightMatrices` before choosing a layout.
- The lookup is pushed toward the sun by `texel_world_size * 0.25` (in `directional_shadows.lua`); a full texel push leaked light under ceilings in far cascades. Thin geometry leaks in far cascades scale with texel size: look at receiver offsets before adding cascades. Translucency in shadow maps is dithered by `Material:GetShadowOpacity`. Prefer physically derived filtering (PCSS with the sun's real angular size) over fixed filters even when it changes the old look.
- Contact shadows march the depth buffer toward each light.
- Glass tints light with a ray, not a shadow map: while `r_glass_light` is on, glass casts no shadow of its own (`Material.GlassCastsShadow()` false) and `get_glass_light` (see `glass_light.lua`) traces see-through instances only. Where it runs: the lighting and translucent passes, `ddgi_shade` (sun, lights, emitters, probe rays) and scene reflections. A penumbra can reach into an opaque frame's footprint, so it traces a few offset rays (`search_radius`). `ddgi.HasGlass()` gates every ray so scenes without glass pay nothing. Without ray queries glass falls back to the dithered shadow.

## Sky, atmosphere, fog, clouds

- **Atmosphere** (`atmosphere.lua`, `atmosphere_scattering.lua`): sun illuminance times `GetTransmittance(sun_dir)` times `SUN_COLOR` (one physical sun color, luminance 1, used for direct light, sky, fog, clouds, disc and rain). Multiple scattering is a precomputed table (`scattering.LOOKUP_GLSL` / `sc_lookup`), baked on aerosol change; single scattering is marched in `integrate_scattering`. Visibility is two parameters in meters: `atmosphere.SetAerosolVisibility` (clear air) and `SetGroundFogVisibility` (the ground hugging layer); climates and the `atmosphere_controller` take the same pair. Sky LUT caches must be baked at the quantised key they are keyed by, or the result depends on who asked first. The sky has no ground: rays below the horizon use `get_sky_lookup_dir`; the environment probe draws a ground (`get_ground_radiance`). Marching code that integrates atmosphere must clip at the planet (density functions clamp altitude at 0, so underground air integrates at ground density); every consumer of `integrate_scattering` must do it. Tables are for a unit sun: multiply by `ATMOSPHERE_SUN_ILLUMINANCE * SUN_COLOR`. `envprobe.UpdateEnvironmentAltitude` moves the environment probe to the camera's height so high-up reflections and ambient are right. `weather.SetEnvironmentMap(path)` (an HDRI) disables sun and atmosphere; intensity defaults to 1000 cd/m2 per unit because probes clamp at 65504. Equirect samplers must clamp vertically.
- **Froxel volume** (`passes/volumetric_fog.lua`, `froxel_fog.lua`): one medium holds clear air and ground fog out to `froxel_fog.FAR` (100 km), lit by the shadowed sun (including cloud shadows), DDGI probes where they exist and the analytic sky ambient in open air (mixed by overhead sky visibility). DDGI is looked up by its own pass (`volumetric_froxel_gi`) on a grid of half the froxel width and height (each cell evaluates one random froxel of its 2x2 per frame, the temporal pass averages them) through `ddgi_sample_medium` (`ddgi.GetMediumGLSL`): one probe loop and one visibility ray per probe give both the mean radiance (irradiance at a random direction and its opposite) and the overhead sky visibility, with no surface normal weighting. Keep DDGI lookups out of the per-froxel scatter: their visibility rays dominate the cost of the fog. The integrate pass writes per channel in-scatter (first half of a double-depth 3D texture) and transmittance (second half): consumers go through `get_volumetric_fog` / `froxel_sample`, never `texture()` on the volume. Sky pixels are composed (volume + transmittance * tail past FAR + layer transmittance * background), not subtracted from the sky LUT; with clouds the sum splits at the cloud depth. Never use a single point shadow lookup for a medium segment. Noise: the temporal pass blurs each froxel along the sun direction (fading with depth, `line_fade`) plus a light neighbour blur, history is weighted by a validity alpha, scatter picks a random corner for occluded froxels (history lookups stay deterministic). `froxel_fog.TILE` sets sharpness and cost. The fog's light is a fitted model (`get_ground_fog_sun` octaves, `get_ground_fog_sky_ambient`, `get_ground_fog_sun_transmittance`, which also dims direct light on surfaces by height), referenced against a path tracer; change it only with a reference to compare to.
- **Clouds** (`clouds.lua`, `passes/clouds.lua`; layers via `weather.SetCloudLayers`, presets in `climate.lua`): noise bake, beer shadow map, sky dome, quarter-res checkerboard trace with half-res reconstruct, composited in the fog pass. The sun is dimmed per pixel by `get_cloud_shadow` (surface lighting, the medium shadow function, DDGI shade). Rules learned: baked noise must be stretched to 0..1; lattice streaks need the domain warp with the warp mip offset by its frequency; the sun disc is ~1e5-1e6 times a cloud's brightness, so transmittance must fade out smoothly (not cut off) near zero; detail volume resolution (`clouds.DETAIL_NOISE_SIZE`) limits close-up detail more than step count; the main-view detail is filtered by pixel footprint, not step size. Cost knobs: `cloud_layer_steps`, `cloud_detail_min_mip`, `clouds.VIEW_DIVISOR`.

## Reflections, translucency, materials

- **Reflections** are two passes in `passes/reflections.lua`: `reflections_trace` (rays at `reflection_resolution`, written raw) and `reflections` (3x3 spatial filter, temporal history; keep them separate, a shared-memory tile filter shows a grid at tile borders). Screen space (`reflection_screenspace_roughness`) is traced first when above 0, rays fill the rest; with 0 and ray queries the nearest scene hit is authority. Above `reflection_gi_threshold` (fading over `reflection_gi_fade`) a reflection is the GI/environment light instead of traced (`ibl.GetReflectionGIRange`, `get_reflection_blend_weight`). Rays hit shading: albedo at the ray cone's mip, alpha test, single bounce, lambert from the scene material (`ddgi_material`), so surface feature lobes and transmission do not appear in reflections or GI. Traced hits get analytic ground fog (`apply_ground_fog_ray`, `r_rt_reflection_fog`) and read the environment at `roughness * (1 - rough_mix)` (the direction is already sampled over the lobe, blurring twice is wrong). Max ray distance `r_rt_reflection_distance`; rays only see the scene store within `r_rt_distance`. The history is read where the *image* sits behind the surface (`reflection_hit_reprojection`), and its alpha is clamped to the neighbouring alphas (otherwise silhouettes fill with environment light as halos). Half resolution with odd sizes: round up, never truncate the ratio (`get_reflection_gbuffer_pos` in `ibl.lua`).
- **Translucent surfaces** (`passes/translucent.lua`, moments/accumulate with `translucent_extras.lua`): refraction is exact unpolarised dielectric Fresnel; a thin wall is a pane, a solid has far-side Fresnel and total internal reflection; `AttenuationDistance` is Beer-Lambert; a refracting material has one `Roughness` (rough glass averages several rays with hashed, not interleaved-gradient, noise). They trace their own mirror ray (`scene_reflection` direction/lobe code), have no denoiser of their own (TAA averages the rays). A graphics pipeline has one descriptor set unless `DescriptorSetCount` says more; sets used by a command buffer cannot be rewritten, so per-frame TLAS/material bindings need a set per swapchain image. Diffuse transmission (`features/transmission.lua`) is for leaves and cloth: a normalised lobe lights the front with light arriving at the back, and the opaque diffuse is scaled by `1 - T`.
- **Gbuffer roughness**: `gbuffer_roughness()` returns GGX alpha (readers `sqrt` it for perceptual); the raw channel holds perceptual roughness and is written through `gbuffer_encode_roughness(alpha)`.
- **Shading model** (`surface_lighting.lua`, `ibl.lua`): GGX D, height-correlated Smith visibility, Schlick Fresnel with `F90(f0)` (a fixed F90 of 1 makes `SpecularMultiplier 0` surfaces sheen edge-on), Kulla-Conty style energy compensation applied to direct *and* ambient specular (`GGXEnergyCompensation = 1 / (1 - (1 - E) F_avg)`), and a Fresnel-coupled EON diffuse (`Fd_EON`, energy preserving Oren-Nayar) for direct light; the ambient diffuse is Lambert since irradiance is already a cosine integral. When a surface looks washed out, render it with black albedo and remove terms (specular, transmission, fog) one at a time to find additive light; thin geometry seen edge-on gets large direct GGX specular.
- **Local lights**: a point light's `Lumen` is flux, intensity `Lumen / (4 pi)`, falling off as `1 / (d^2 + source_radius^2)` times Frostbite's window (`range`; the automatic range is where the light reaches ~0.0005 lux). Spots integrate the cone including the smoothstep taper (`GetEmissionSolidAngle`), so a lamp emits exactly its lumens. Light color is divided by its luminance so hue does not change brightness.
- **Emissive surfaces**: emissive luminance is `color * strength (* albedo alpha) * EMISSIVE_REFERENCE_LUMINANCE`; an emissive surface is not a light, its effect on surroundings is DDGI only. Probe spacing causes an overshoot directly under tiny emitters.
- **Ambient occlusion** weighs below-horizon directions by `BELOW_HORIZON_WEIGHT` and the probes' sky visibility (the ground is already in the probes' lobes, so counting it as an occluder twice darkens near-ground surfaces); the bent normal of an unoccluded surface must equal its normal.
- Validate shading changes against references: a ColorChecker chart under a locked EV compared with analytic matte values, furnace rooms (walls that emit and reflect, nothing may look brighter or darker than the walls; `reflection_furnace.lua` in the examples), and a random-walk multiple scattering reference for metals. Scratch scripts in `./tmp/`.

## Water

Ocean and `water_volume` components are both drawn by the `ocean` pass (`passes/ocean.lua`; the name is kept for fog/envprobe/post_source). Sea state and presets are in `goluwa/render3d/water.lua`; examples `water_showcase.lua`, `ocean_shore.lua`, `water_volume_test.lua`, `twilight_lake.lua`.

- The wave field is three inverse-FFT tiles (`water.FFT_CASCADES`) built from spectra on the CPU when ocean params change (`water.BuildOceanWaves`); the three wave textures sample them and write height, slope and foam. Foam is a world space field with memory (per cascade foam pass), driven by the Jacobian of waves 4 m and longer and the wind (`water.GetWhitecapFactor`). `water.SampleOcean` (buoyancy) sums the strongest modes on the CPU, so bodies follow the visible sea but not its smallest waves. Ocean level is the *mean* surface.
- The trace from above is a sphere trace through the slab `level +- wave_bound` (`height_map_tracing`) with a mean-plane fallback; never let a ray with no crossing show what is behind the sea. The camera start is the near plane (`WATER_NEAR_PLANE`) per pixel, so the waterline is a soft edge instead of a whole-screen flip.
- Reflections: screen first (`r_water_screenspace_roughness`), marching the depth buffer and reading the last fogged frame (`post_source.GetFoggedOpaqueSceneTexture`), rays fill where the screen finds nothing; the water shares the reflection pass's direction code and a denoiser (`ocean_resolve`, radius scaled by `water_rt_share`). Water lit through what covers it: the ambient is sampled at a few points along the path with probe visibility rays and sun shafts trace toward the sun, otherwise water under a roof glows like open sky. A volume at sea level blends into the ocean's surface within `VOLUME_EDGE_BLEND` of its sides (`mix_water`).
- Cost: use the `r_water_*` feature pvars (bits of `ocean_data.features`, so toggling does not recompile) to find what is expensive; compare variants in one process, interleaved with baselines (the gpu clock drifts several ms inside a process). The big costs are inscatter (shaft caustics, ambient GI), refraction, ripples and the reflection trace; `r_water_waves` off is a flat mirror.
- Pitfalls: never `smoothstep(e, e, x)` (undefined); phase angle sign is `dot(-view_dir, light_dir)`; negative values (not just NaN/inf) cause bright discs through bloom (`integrate_attenuated` must not divide by a negative); flat apparent-depth refraction in screen space tears at edges (refraction only offsets the straight-behind pixel); the submerged-floor test must be "is the point inside the volume"; discrete sine waves show grids, use advected gradient noise ripples; shader recompiles can take minutes, run shots in the background.
- Map importers turn game fog into a medium with `water.MediumFromFog`. More than `water.MAX_VOLUMES` volumes: the nearest are drawn.
