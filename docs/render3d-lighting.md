# Render3D lighting, GI, fog, sky, water and exposure

Physically based, absolute-unit lighting: scene radiance is in cd/m2 (daylight is thousands, the sun disc ~2e9), exposure is applied at the end. When something looks off, work out which term is wrong with an isolated scene (one light, locked exposure, other terms off) rather than tuning by eye. Pass sources are in `goluwa/render3d/passes/`, shared lighting glsl in `surface_lighting.lua`, `ibl.lua`, `atmosphere.lua`, `gi.lua`, `radiance_cache.lua`, `radiance.lua`, `scene_hits.lua`. Every pass has an `r_pass_<name>` pvar. Ray traced consumers read the scene store ([gpu-scene.md](gpu-scene.md)); on hardware without ray queries they use stubs (test with `GOLUWA_NO_RAY_TRACING=1`: the GI passes are not built and GI falls back to the sky irradiance, no light occlusion, particle ray collision or traced reflections; screen space paths remain). `r_quality` (`goluwa/render3d/quality.lua`) gates passes and scales the fog grid, cloud steps, screen space shadow steps and sun shadow maps; consumers read `quality.Get()`.

## Exposure and units

- **Scene color is pre-exposed.** The fp16 scene targets (lighting, water, fog composite, translucent, taa) hold absolute radiance times `post_source.get_pre_exposure()` (last frame's exposure over `PRE_EXPOSURE_HEADROOM`), because fp16 cannot hold both a starlit night and the sun. Lighting math stays absolute: multiply when writing scene color, divide when reading it for shading, rescale histories by `get_pre_exposure() / get_previous_pre_exposure()`. Probe captures have pre-exposure 1 and stay absolute. New passes that read or write scene color must use `post_source.pre_exposure_block` + `GetPreExposureGLSL`. Radiance limits (firefly clamps) belong in exposed units (`post_source.GetExposureTexture(true)`), not absolute ones.
- Exposure is one number (EV100) with two front ends, both owned by `goluwa/render3d/exposure.lua` (plain state with `exposure.Get/Set`, no pvars; the Look desktop program edits it). Camera: normalises the metered mean to the grey midpoint `camera.grey` (percent, 18 is middle grey), clamped to `camera.ev_min`..`camera.ev_max` (equal values lock it). ISO, aperture and shutter are derived from the exposure along a program line (`camera.mode`: program, aperture/shutter priority, or manual which locks EV) and only drive effects: shutter the motion blur length, ISO the grain in the blit (`r_grain`), aperture the depth of field (`r_dof`, focused by a raycast through the screen centre that skips the active view's `Ignore` entity, the player's rig for the game camera). Effect shaders get them from the exposure texture through `exposure.GetCameraSettingsGLSL` + `exposure.camera_block`; the eye has no camera, so it reads as ISO 100, f/64, 1/60. Eye (`eye.*`): no normalisation. The exposure follows the adapting luminance (the wide-field log-average of the scene, clamped to `eye.adaptation_min`..`eye.adaptation_max` in log10 cd/m2, converted with EV = log2(L) + 3) along the Ferwerda 1996 cone and rod threshold curve (Ward 1994 above 64 cd/m2), with mesopic rod/cone mixing from 0.005 to 5 cd/m2. The rod branch takes scotopic luminance, not photopic: the meter also log-averages `2.3 * dot(rgb, ROD_RESPONSE)` (2.3 is the scotopic/photopic ratio of D65 white) and the curve uses the ratio of the two means, so blue-white light reads brighter to the rods than tungsten at the same cd/m2 (the curve is evaluated in the exposure shader, no baked table); rod vision (`eye.night_vision`) desaturates to the rod spectral response below `eye.night_vision_threshold`. The viewer's own adaptation to the screen dims the display in dim scenes: displayed white is the paper white times `min(L_adapt / 64 cd/m2, 1) ^ eye.viewer_adaptation` (0.33, Stevens' brightness exponent; fitted by eye from a candle lit room needing 25 nits at paper white 200), so one paper white (the display's real SDR white) serves candle rooms and daylight; camera mode is unaffected. There is no artistic layer in the eye: no compensation, no local adaptation, no tint. Bloom and glare are optics, built from the scene before anything else. The bloom reads the temporally anti aliased scene (a jittered source makes sub pixel highlights flicker with the jitter), but under TAA its own linear resolve (`post_source.GetBloomSceneTexture`, the third TAA attachment), never the displayed one: the displayed TAA blends in compressed space, where anything far above white reads as about 1, so decompressing it gives a brightness that has nothing to do with the light (the sun disc came out 3 times too bright and a mirror's reflection of it 30 times, both pulsing with the jitter). The linear resolve averages radiance, reads its history with the same tap clamped Catmull-Rom (a bilinear history blurs while the camera turns and any clamp then cuts the blurred fringe), is not clamped to the neighbourhood (a sub pixel edge sits outside it half the time, so the mean drops) and instead blends faster wherever the displayed history was clamped and, in camera mode only, `r_bloom_threshold` (exposed luminance, soft knee, 0 is off) keeps only highlights in it, and `r_bloom_floor` softly cuts the blurred glow below an exposed level so dark areas beside a bright one stay clean (the wide glare of a bright patch is what veils its neighbours, a pre blur threshold cannot remove it); the blit takes the same thresholded part out of the base so energy is conserved. Their angles become pixels through `eye.display_fov` (the vertical angle the screen takes up at the viewer's eye, `exposure.GetPixelsPerDegree`), never the game camera's field of view: the viewer's eye does not zoom with the picture, so a zoomed camera must not grow the glare, the defocus or the starburst. The camera's glare spreads a flat share of energy per octave of angle (Stiles-Holladay, 1/theta^2); the eye's is the CIE 1999 general glare spread function (Vos and van den Berg, steeper than 1/theta^2 below a few degrees, standard observer with pigmentation 0.5), integrated per bloom level in `passes/bloom.lua` (per channel for the primaries' dominant wavelengths 611, 549 and 464 nm against 560: the two core terms are diffraction, so their angles scale with wavelength; the 400/(1+(theta/0.1)^2) and theta^2 terms are particle scatter, scaled by wavelength^-4; the glare share is per channel; `r_eye_chromatic_halo`). The CIE function and the wavelength scaling live in `render3d/eye_glare.lua`. The starburst (`r_eye_starburst`, `passes/starburst.lua`) is the diffraction pattern of the eye: `render3d/eye_psf.lua` builds a pupil aperture (disc, hundreds of random radial lens fibres of random width, length and opacity, lashes, dust specks; each strip diffracts into its own spike, so spike length, width and brightness are random), FFTs it on the CPU (`math/fft.lua`, once, about 1.5 s on the first eye frame) and stores the power spectrum as a polar table (angle by log radius) normalised so every ring averages 1 (each ring is box filtered to 0.25 degrees of arc first, or the table aliases without mipmaps and the thin rays wobble with the TAA jitter). The table adds the rays of a star around bright compact light sources (only the part above the ring mean: subtracting the dark gaps overshoots the smooth bloom and eats the source's own pixels, which showed as black bursts around lamps): a tile pass (1/16 resolution) finds each tile's summed radiance above the local background (the 1/16 resolution blur, so a uniform sky or a big bright area contributes nothing: discrete tile sources cannot cancel the energy neutral pattern the way a continuous field does, and leave a grid at the tile pitch) and its centroid with a bilinear tent assignment (a tile is only a star source while the bright light within 16 half resolution pixels of it is compact, `COMPACT_SPREAD`: a long neon tube or a big sign becomes a row of separate stars 16 pixels apart whose thin rays do not overlap, and sums to blotches, where a real extended source averages its rays into smooth glare) (every pixel's energy is shared between neighbouring tiles with weights summing to 1, so a light on a tile boundary does not hop between tiles as the TAA jitter moves it), a half resolution gather adds `E * spread(theta) * (modulation - 1)` per channel, with theta scaled by pupil diameter (Moon-Spencer, from the adapting luminance) and by wavelength, when a temporal anti-aliasing mode is on (it jitters the camera) the result is blended 0.9 with the previous frame, reprojected by the camera rotation only and faded out as soon as the camera turns more than a third of a pixel or moves more than a few millimetres in a frame (the history only exists to average the jitter of a still camera; resampling it while moving diffuses the star into a smear, and a rotation only reprojection cannot follow a moving camera or light) (the star hangs on a direction, and a short average cannot remove a flicker with the 16 frame jitter period) (the source is the jittered scene before TAA, whose summed brightness of a tiny emitter flickers about 10% and whose position moves with the jitter; the centroid is shifted by half the jitter), then the glare pass adds it to the bloom. The afterimage (`r_eye_afterimage`, `passes/afterimage.lua`) is a per pixel retinal state at 1/8 resolution in screen space (it follows the view like an afterimage follows the eye), fed from `bloom_down3` plus the glare (the retinal image, so the blob's falloff is the eye's own), read back through a 3x3 gaussian blur at that resolution (display only, not fed back into the state, so it cannot diffuse over time) and bicubic: it is a blobby glow, not a copy of the picture. It has three parts. The source stage is the light's own colour (cone direction times a saturating level) held while it is lit and drained over `eye.afterimage_source_seconds` (0.3 s, not scaled). Behind it a single age value per pixel (held while lit, decaying as `level ^ eye.afterimage_falloff / (8 s * eye.afterimage_time_scale)`, held back while the source stage is strong) runs through a colour ramp, white, yellow, green, blue, purple, red (red lingers: it holds the last third of the age), with brightness fading with the level. The falloff: 0 is a plain exponential, below 0 the decay speeds up as it fades so the tail is cut off and it ends in a finite time (-1 is linear in light), above 0 it slows down and leaves a tail (1 is hyperbolic). Last, bleaching (`dB/dt = (L_cone / 4000 cd/m2)(1 - B) - B / (16 s * time scale)` per cone type, Hunt-Pointer-Estevez) multiplies the cone signals by `1 - B`, a complementary tinted darker patch on bright fields. A ramp reads about like the real flight of colours and is much cheaper than modelling the cone types. A light must be `eye.afterimage_adaptation_ratio` times the adapting luminance to excite it, and `eye.afterimage_intensity` scales the whole effect (0 skips the passes). The update gathers the retinal image along each pixel's motion (the velocity buffer, the camera reprojection for sky) at one sample per state pixel, taking the max, so a light sweeping over the screen leaves a continuous streak and not one dot per frame; a motion over 0.2 of the screen is a cut and is ignored. Two more eye-only effects live in the final shader in `passes/blit.lua`, each behind a pvar so it can be isolated: `r_eye_chromatic_defocus` (red and blue blurred by the pupil, Moon-Spencer diameter from the adapting luminance, 0.35 D and 0.9 D of longitudinal chromatic aberration against the focused green) and `r_eye_noise` (the grain of a dark adapted eye, in displayed units and faded out with adaptation: sd `strength * dark_adapted * (0.0025 + 0.015 * sqrt(shown))` of display white, 2x2 pixel blocks, tinted purple (a red 0.75, green 0.35, blue 1.0 weighting, as the dark adapted eye sees it), divided by the exposure and by the viewer adaptation scale so it is the size of what is finally shown: at night that scale is about 0.07 and would otherwise shrink the noise 14 times, `dark_adapted` 1 below 0.01 cd/m2 adapting luminance and 0 above 3; in absolute scene luminance the noise was a few thousandths of a code value and could not be seen). Both end in the same display transform (`r_tonemapper`, default `neutral`, SDR or HDR). The camera meter is a centre weighted mean of linear luminance with each pixel held at the 95th percentile of a log histogram; the eye adapts to the wide-field log-average (uniform weights, no clamp), so one lamp cannot dominate it. `eye.rod_adaptation` defaults to 1, the full rod+cone curve; lower values blend toward the cone-only curve, which is darker at night than the model's own data. A view's `ExposureLock` (EV100, `glw shot --ev`) fixes exposure in either mode. `ExposureHold` freezes the auto exposure at its current value while the view is active, so a screen effect that darkens most of the image is not metered back to grey. Shader in `passes/blit.lua`.
- Motion blur (`passes/motion_blur.lua`) runs after TAA from the velocity buffer (current minus previous screen position). By default it gathers only behind the motion (`r_motion_blur_trailing`), so the leading edge keeps its position and only the trail extends; the symmetric kernel stretches things ahead of where they are and hides a wrong velocity sign, so test sign changes with trailing on. The faster neighbour a pixel borrows its direction from is searched out to `r_motion_blur_max` with a reach test (the neighbour's blur must reach the pixel), never with a threshold on the cap. The search reads 16 pixel tiles, not the full resolution buffers: `motion_blur_tiles` keeps each tile's longest blur, `motion_blur_neighbors` the longest that can reach the tile, so a pixel with nothing moving near it exits after one read, and the rest test the tile maxima against their own distance to each tile.
- The `neutral` tonemapper leaves everything below 0.76 of exposed white unchanged (no toe or contrast); SDR and HDR are the same curve with different `peak`. The other curves (`agx`, `aces`, `gt7`) are looks. HDR output (`r_hdr_peak`, `r_hdr_paper_white`) cannot know the display; they are settings. With `--hdr` the window target is an fp16 layer of sRGB encoded values where 1.0 is `r_hdr_ui_white` nits, so the UI and anything else drawn to the screen blends exactly as in SDR (`RequiresManualGamma()` is true); the blit writes the scene into it encoded the same way (above 1.0 for highlights) and `goluwa/render/hdr_output.lua` decodes it to the scRGB or HDR10 swapchain at the end of the frame. Never blend UI in linear space: alpha and text antialiasing come out thinner and lighter.
- `goluwa/render3d/grade.lua`: a grade preset is a table of pvars (anything left out resets to default, so a preset is the whole look); `grade.SetPreset(name, seconds)` blends. Controls after the tonemapper (`r_grade_*`: brightness, contrast, saturation, warmth, lift/gain) are applied in `grade_display`. At defaults the grade is exactly the tonemapper.
- **Measuring lighting needs control of exposure.** Auto exposure brings a dark leaking room and a correct black room both to mid grey; pin EV (indoor ~10.5, outdoor ~15.5) or read a texture before exposure (the GI resolve texture `gi.GetScreenTexture`, or `post_source.GetSceneSourceTexture`). Local exposure also brightens dark regions, so disable it for absolute comparisons. A one-frame camera override measures unconverged GI: hold the view with `Shot.Capture` (`settle` or `converge`), and avoid pitch of exactly +-90. A converged mean says nothing about shimmer: compare captures spread over time (frames 3 apart are correlated by hysteresis and understate flicker many times; use ~20-frame gaps, and auto exposure for night scenes). Test scenes must set `MetallicMultiplier = 0` explicitly for non-metals.

## Choosing the GI

There is one screen GI, the `radiance` pass (a per pixel ReSTIR final gather over a world cache), and a world space cache, the `radiance_cache` pass (DDGI probe cascades). `r_pass_radiance` turns the screen GI off (the lighting then falls back to the sky's irradiance); `r_radiance_cache` (`probes`, `none`) picks whether the probes are built, and changing it rebuilds the pipeline bundles. Bundles with an explicit pass list (tests, previews) get whichever GI passes they list. The `radiance_cache` pass present and enabled in the current bundle is the world cache (`gi.GetMode()`), not the pvar; `gi.IsPerPixel()` says whether the screen GI is on.

- Consumers go through `goluwa/render3d/gi.lua`, never a system directly: screen textures (`gi.GetScreenTexture` and friends, ReSTIR's three textures), the `radiance_cache_data` uniform block (`gi.GetProbeBlockLayout`, `gi.WriteProbeBlock`, it carries the probes' fields, the sun, sky and emitter data and `gi_mode`) and the world lookups in `gi.GetCommonGLSL`/`gi.GetMediumGLSL` (`gi_sample_irradiance`, `gi_sample_medium`, `gi_in_volume`, `gi_available`, `gi_covers`), which are the probe lookups. They read only bindless textures, so a consumer needs no extra bindings.
- `gi_covers(P, weight)` decides whether a lookup that found nothing means darkness or "unknown, use the sky fallback": DDGI probes inside the volume that were all hidden mean dark.
- Irradiance is stored and returned as E/pi (the cosine weighted mean radiance) in both systems: shading multiplies it by a reflectance (`EON_directional_albedo`), never by albedo/pi. A new integrator that returns E is pi times too bright and makes the multi-bounce feedback run away.
- **Emitters** (`goluwa/render3d/scene/emitters.lua`, shared by DDGI probes and the fog): emissive surfaces reach GI only through emitter samples (rays alone give fireflies on small emitters). Every consumer draws `EMITTER_CANDIDATES` triangles from a light tree and keeps one in proportion to `facing * receiving / max(d^2, r^2)` (receiving being the receiver's cosine when it has a normal, `vec3(0)` for probes and the medium). The tree is an implicit binary tree over the emissive triangles in Morton order, rebuilt on the GPU in `emitters.Build`: the order (a bitonic sort of centroid keys) only when the emitter list changes, the leaves (world space bounds, power, emission cone) and the levels every frame, so moving emitters refit. Descending, each child is weighed by power times the bounded orientation and receiver cosines over a squared distance, clamped by the consumer's radius, and no single distance works: the distance to the node's box makes a big node that holds the receiver look as near as the radius clamp, so it starves a small emitter beside the receiver in another node; the distance to its centre underrates a big node whose near corner touches the receiver (emitters on the receiver's own wall). Half the candidates walk by each, and every candidate's pdf is the average of both walks' probabilities of the path it took (both are computed along the one path), so neither can leave an emitter all but unpicked. Brightness is the test for any change to the sampler, not only noise: compare long means against picking by power alone (unbiased with no spatial terms). Rare huge weights do not stay unbiased in practice: DDGI clips them in its fp16 storage and comes out dark, and smaller clusters or fewer candidates raise them, so `EMITTER_CLUSTER` is 16 and DDGI keeps 4 candidates; the ReSTIR candidate pass takes 2 (one per weighing), its reuse stands in for more, and its reflection sample reweighs the same two by the lobe rather than walking the tree again (each pick's light is evaluated at the diffuse origin and normal its tree weight was computed for). Picking by instance first (bounding sphere, then a triangle by area) fails where one instance holds many small emitters spread over a large area (a prop of hundreds of cave crystals): nearly every pick lands on a far crystal and the near ones that light a spot show as speckle. The traversal adds fresh random bits per level; rescaling one number down the tree runs out of precision after a few unlikely branches. The walk stops `EMITTER_CLUSTER` leaves above the bottom and picks among them by their own importance, and two exact tests give zero importance: a leaf keeps its triangle's plane (a receiver behind a one sided triangle), and any node whose box lies wholly below the receiver's tangent plane. Without them a receiver at the foot of an emitter (inside its triangles' bounds, where the cone and sphere bounds say nothing) spends most walks on faces that turn away and they die. The tree cannot see occlusion: where the nearby emitters are mostly hidden (crystals set in rock), the rare visible far ones carry the light and the noise is visibility noise, about as high as plain rays hitting the emitters. `emitter_light` turns a pick into radiance times solid angle over its pdf, divided by the receiving cosine. `emitter_random` is a per index low discrepancy sequence over frames (a hashed offset plus the frame times the 4d golden ratio constants), so a consumer must pass a stable index (a slot, a cell) and the frame, never a hash that includes the frame, or the history averages clumped samples. A consumer shadow-tests the point itself. While emitters are sampled, rays must not also add the emission of what they hit or it counts twice: DDGI's shade never adds it, fog rays define `SCENE_HIT_EMISSION` to `radiance_cache_emitter_count == 0`; reflections keep it. Additive materials are not emitters unless `gi_additive_emitters` (an additive billboard once held 99.9% of all emitter power); `gi_emitters` turns sampling off. A material that is "albedo alpha emissive" emits scaled by the albedo's average alpha. A leaf's power is its triangle's own emission (`emitter_triangle_luminance`: the emission texture at the triangle's UV centre, at the mip where the triangle is about one texel, floored at 1% of the texture's mean) times its area, and a pick's light divides by that same per triangle value (`emitter_luminance`), never by the material's average: masked emitters (Source 2 self illumination, a big panel that is black but for a few lit strips) gave every triangle the panel's mean, so the lit ones were weighed hundreds of times too low and their rare picks were single pixels bright enough to bloom, and most picks landed on black. Check total emitter power before tuning samplers when emitter noise appears.

## radiance cache (DDGI probes)

Probe-based GI over cascades (`goluwa/render3d/radiance_cache.lua` for glsl and lookup, `passes/radiance_cache.lua` for the passes: trace, shade, probe data/relocation, irradiance and distance integrate, guide). It is ReSTIR's world cache and the source of the fog, particle, water and glass ambient; it no longer lights the screen itself.

- **Cascades**: `radiance_cache.CASCADES` (10) starting at `radiance_cache_probe_spacing` (0.5 m), spacing doubling, fitted to the scene bounds and `radiance_cache_max_coverage`; cascades past the one covering everything are dropped. A pixel samples at most two. Cascade n updates every `radiance_cache_update_interval_*` frames (spaced geometrically between `_min` and `_max`), and a cascade updates early when the sun moved (`radiance_cache_change_threshold`, `radiance_cache_change_min_interval`; plus a short fast-history window after a large change). Only the sun is tracked; other changes wait for the interval, or call `radiance_cache.ResetHistory()`. If far GI looks stale after a non-sun change, lower the relevant interval. The finest cascade updating every frame is the largest single DDGI cost.
- **History**: a texel keeps `radiance_cache_hysteresis` to the power of the cascade's update interval; a probe with few frames behind it weighs the new frame 1/(n+1). A probe whose mean ray brightness consistently drifts one way (detector follows the median of the mean, fast rate decided from the frames *before* this one) takes the new frame faster, so lights switching are followed but lone bright rays (spikes) are not taken in at the fast rate (that biases GI up and causes fireflies). Reset, moved or disabled probes start over.
- **Rays**: `radiance_cache_guided_rays` is the share aimed by the probe's own octahedral radiance guide (path guiding); guide cells also aim at their brightest spot (`radiance_cache_aim_share`, a build time define). Every pass that needs a ray direction recomputes it from the same guide, which is only replaced at the end of the frame so trace, shade and integrate agree (any guide is unbiased if they agree). Probe rays start `RADIANCE_CACHE_ORIGIN_PULL` behind the probe so a probe lying on a thin wall hits it at once from the far side. `radiance_cache_ray_clamp` caps a ray's weighted contribution (radiance times its guide weight `s_q`) against the probe's mean, never its raw radiance: a guided ray aimed at a small bright patch (sky through a hole) has a tiny weight, and clamping its radiance undoes the guiding and leaves a room lit through a small opening nearly black. It still trims light that only rare rays find, so such rooms stay darker than unclamped and converge over hundreds of frames.
- **Moving instances stay out of the probes**: probe rays, the sun and emitter shadow rays of their hits and every visibility ray use `RADIANCE_CACHE_SCENE_MASK` / `RADIANCE_CACHE_VISIBILITY_MASK` (`instances.MASK_GI`, `MASK_GI_SOLID`), never `0xFF` or `MASK_SOLID`; a new trace against the probe scene must too. Probe history lasts about 30 frames, so a thing that moves through the lattice leaves a trail in probe light, distance moments and relocation. `bridge.lua` (`item_mask`) clears the GI bits of character gear (`character_movement`, `weapon_holder`; animation moves it without its transform changing) for good, and of any other dynamic visual (rigid bodies, attachments) while its world matrix changes, until `GI_SETTLE_FRAMES` frames after the last change. A house of props lights like static geometry once built, a carried or thrown prop is lit by the probes but does not write into them.
- **Screen space AO** (`passes/ambient_occlusion.lua`, half resolution, temporal then a depth and normal weighted blur) is CORTAO: depth samples on a view facing circle become circular occluders that each block the cosine distributed rays (32, one bit each) they cover. Occlusion, the bent normal and the bounce all come from that bit mask: bounce light is the previous frame's colour at an occluder times the share of rays it newly blocks. Tunables are the `ambient_occlusion_*` pvars (`samples` is read when the shader is built), `ambient_occlusion_debug` shows one output (`occlusion_amplified` exaggerates small differences). The temporal pass has a trailing problem: a static surface keeps its velocity while a moving occluder passes it, so the reprojected history stays valid and the occluder's halo trails behind it (and lags ahead of it); it also trails the bent normal that the GI lookup follows. The temporal pass therefore lowers the history weight by how far the history's AO is from the 5x5 raw mean, in units of that window's standard deviation (the 3x3 sigma clamp is too loose for the 1 spp noise; in units of the mean's standard error the gate rejects the history of noisy pixels, so they never accumulate and the noise stays static). History is read at the reprojected texel; only when that texel is a hole or its depth is off by more than the current 3x3 raw depth spread plus 5% is a better matching texel searched among the 3x3 history texels (searching always picks along the depth iso-line of a sloped surface and the noise flows along it). Accepting history up to that spread is what lets alpha tested foliage accumulate, since it flips between leaf layers under the TAA jitter. `ambient_occlusion_debug` also has `bounce_raw` and `bounce_temporal` to measure each stage; the stage to blame is the one whose frame to frame change does not fall (for the bounce in dense foliage: raw about 0.10, after temporal 0.027, after blur 0.033, in sqrt luma). Measure noise against a long converged render of a still camera, not against the previous frame, which cannot see a static pattern. Measure it with a box walking past a wall, comparing the temporal AO alpha in strips behind and ahead of it with the raw AO (`GetAttachment(1)` of `ambient_occlusion` and `ambient_occlusion_blur`).
- **Lookup rules** (`radiance_cache_sample_cascade`): visibility uses a Chebyshev test plus ray queries (`RADIANCE_CACHE_VISIBILITY_RAYS`; a pass must bind the TLAS to get them, and fog and any new distant consumer need it or sealed buildings leak outdoor probes). A probe is traced when its blend weight is >= `RADIANCE_CACHE_VISIBILITY_MIN_WEIGHT` or when it would add more than `RADIANCE_CACHE_VISIBILITY_MIN_SHARE` of the light so far (faint-weight probes outside a roof can be 1000x brighter than a dark room, so weight alone is not enough). Lookups at hit points (probe bounces, reflection hits: `RADIANCE_CACHE_CONTRAST_VISIBILITY_RAYS`) trace probes `radiance_cache_contrast_ratio` times brighter than the point's reference. A probe a ray found hidden counts for nothing; there is no fallback to hidden probes (such points fade to the next cascade). `RADIANCE_CACHE_NO_VISIBILITY_RAYS` is for consumers that cannot leak (the open sea surface). If a leak persists while visibility rays seem to do nothing, first check that the rays are even issued.
- **Relocation**: probes in gaps narrower than twice the relocation distance, or inside thin sheets, must stay disabled (they jiggle and leak sky); moves go along the dominant axis of the backface direction only. A probe resting on a surface (its short backface hits all on one side, as on a floor that lies on a probe plane) is moved off it to the open side instead, if a ray along the move hits nothing (a box resting on another surface has its bottom face on a probe plane too, and moving through that would put its probes under the ground): disabled, every cascade would light that surface from a full spacing away and each cascade from a different height, which shows as steps at the cascade edges. Use `radiance_cache_debug_probes` to inspect.
- `radiance_cache_strength` scales the light probes give every consumer (the lookup wrapper), not the probes' own bounce rays (`RADIANCE_CACHE_UNSCALED`).
- A coarse cascade only keeps the shadow of an occluder taller than its probe spacing: a surface lookup takes part of its light from the probe layer one spacing out (the smooth blend and the facing weight favour it), and that layer sees past a lower occluder. The lookup biases (`NORMAL_BIAS`, `VIEW_BIAS`, in spacings) stop growing at `BIAS_MAX_SPACING` meters so coarse cascades sample close to the surface.
- Octahedral atlas borders (`radiance_cache_texel_direction`): edge texels mirror the interior row or column next to them, corner texels take the diagonally opposite interior corner. All four corners of the octahedral square are -z, so surfaces facing -z (and any lookup near it) sample a corner: a wrong corner texel shows as seams and static speckle wherever the bent normal's x or y crosses zero.
- Faceted/thin wall rules of thumb: planes without thickness cannot be traced reliably from a surface; sun shadows of zero-thickness quads leak along edges and are not a DDGI problem.
- Verify with `test/tests/render3d/radiance_cache.lua` (ray hits, emitters, pinhole rooms against slow averages). For noise, score the rms of many frames against their mean *and* print the mean luminance (flicker that comes with a darker room is bias); compare runs at the same warm up, since multi-bounce in a lit room takes thousands of frames to settle. Scratch scripts in `./tmp/`; set pvars that shaders are built from through `GOLUWA_PVARS`.

## Global illumination (ReSTIR)

A per pixel final gather (`goluwa/render3d/radiance.lua` for pvars, `passes/radiance.lua` for the passes), after ReSTIR GI (Ouyang et al. 2021) with the generalized RIS weights of Lin et al. 2022. It produces the screen GI and the reflection (the specular lobe, below): world lookups (fog, water, glass, hits off screen, its own and the reflection rays') still go to the cache picked by `r_radiance_cache`, whose own screen resolve is skipped. Everything runs at `radiance_resolution`; a GI pixel samples one screen pixel of its 2x2 quad, a different one each frame.

- **Passes**: `radiance_candidates` traces this frame's candidates (one ray shaded by `shade_scene_hit`, cosine or probe guided, and one emitter sample) for the pixels whose turn it is (`radiance_rays`: every, a checkerboard half, or one of each 2x2), one thread per tracing pixel; `radiance_temporal` resamples them against each pixel's reservoir from last frame (a pixel that did not trace enters with confidence 0 and keeps its history); `radiance_spatial` combines each pixel's reservoir with `radiance_spatial_samples` neighbours, writes irradiance (a pixel without a reservoir yet averages its valid neighbours only) and resamples one reservoir from the same weights, which is next frame's temporal history; `radiance_accumulate` accumulates with luminance moments, and four a-trous passes (`radiance_filter_0` to `_2`, then `radiance_filter`, steps 1 to 8) filter it, each tap stopped by plane, normal and a luminance difference beyond `radiance_filter_luminance` standard deviations of the pixel's accumulated mean (from the moments, carried in alpha between the passes; the last pass hands back the sky visibility): noisy pixels blur wide and settled ones keep their contact detail without switching the filter off. The spatial pass writes two estimates: plain irradiance (the screen hits read it, and its own accumulation for hits near the ray origin) and each sample weighted by `Fd_EON` at the pixel over the uniform response the lighting multiplies by (accumulated and filtered for the lighting; irradiance does not say where the light came from, and a window lit room seen from the window side is about 15% too dark without it). Noise rules: the 64x64 blue noise tile takes a random shift per tile copy and frame (pixels a tile apart otherwise take the same rays forever; check the autocorrelation of the result at a 64 pixel offset); a filter whose tap pattern changes every frame shows as flicker; never clamp bright samples against the history, in rooms lit through windows most of the light arrives in rare bright samples and a clamp at six deviations removed 25-40% of it. A pixel with under 4 frames of history blurs by plane and normal only: its own luminance as the noise estimate makes a dark single sample turn away brighter neighbours, and every newly revealed area (the screen edge while turning) came out 40% dark for a few frames. Below full resolution a gi pixel rotates only among the screen pixels under it within 2% of the nearest one's distance, so beside thin geometry and edges it keeps one surface; rotating across the edge reset its history every frame and the filter spread the unsettled value into the wall beside it (dashed flicker next to thin poles). Measure noise both settled and a few frames after a camera cut (spatial deviation from a 9x9 mean on flat surfaces and frame to frame change): a still camera hides how slowly a pass converges. The screen textures follow the GI conventions (E/pi, sky visibility in alpha, geometry for the bilateral upsample). The specular lobe has its own ray per pixel in the candidates pass and goes through the accumulate and filter passes beside the irradiance (see Reflections); it is read without the sky mix and the AO specular occlusion since its rays already hold sky and visibility. The reflection is the lobe's own radiance, which the lighting multiplies by the specular albedo with Fresnel and the energy compensation.
- **Resolution, rays and temporal reuse**: the default is full resolution, every pixel tracing, temporal reuse on. A half resolution GI pixel stands for a different screen pixel of its 2x2 every frame and loses its history in foliage. Temporal reuse is what keeps rare bright samples (sky through a small opening, a mostly hidden emitter) from flashing as fireflies, which the denoiser's accumulation alone leaves as dots. Every reuse step must weigh samples with the same target: the candidates weigh from the lifted ray origin, so the temporal pass rescales the fresh reservoir to the surface point's target before combining (mixed targets darken creases). A pixel's history is capped by how far it moved since last frame (`max_history / (1 + pixels)`), or one sample is carried along the motion and draws streaks. `radiance_rays` half or quarter needs temporal reuse: a pixel that did not trace has no sample of its own hemisphere otherwise, and neighbours cannot see its gaps (canopy shade came out at 2/3). With reduced rays, a pixel keeps its reservoir and the receiver it was found from on frames it does not trace, so the next traced frame reconnects it once against a new sample, and it only permutes on frames it traces. Below full resolution the lighting upsamples the GI and the reflection (`radiance_cache.GetScreenSampleGLSL`) by bilinear weights times the distance to each other's plane (never a depth ratio, which rejects the same floor at grazing angles), the normal and, for the reflection, the angle between reflection directions from the roughness; only where no texel of the quad lies on the pixel's surface does it read the 4x4 ring around it, then the single closest match. A plain bilinear fallback there blends the surface behind an edge into it (bright dashes beside poles, mirror spheres smeared into the wall behind), and a wider kernel everywhere blurs reflected edges within one flat mirror, which no geometric test can sharpen. A quarter resolution GI pixel samples the middle 2x2 of its 4x4 screen pixels, so the upsample can treat it as the texel centre.
- **Rare light needs spatiotemporal chaining**: where a small share of the rays carries most of the light (a sunlit patch seen through a doorway), each pixel finds it once in hundreds of frames. Temporal history fed only by the temporal pass keeps each pixel's find to itself (a few dozen samples) and shows as slow drifting grain; the spatial pass's resampled reservoir as history spreads one pixel's find to its neighbours and on, so a region learns where the opening is within a few frames. Confidence stays capped at `radiance_max_history` when the temporal pass reads it, or chained samples snowball. Check with a converged reference of the same view, not only frame to frame change (slow grain barely changes between frames).
- **Guided rays**: `radiance_guided` of the candidate rays are drawn from the nearest DDGI probe's radiance guide (`radiance_cache_guide_sample`, the same octahedral guide and aim the probes trace with; the probe is picked half a spacing along the normal, on the receiver's side) and every ray is weighted by the mixture density `(1 - share) cos/pi + share * radiance_cache_guide_density`, which the cosine part keeps positive everywhere, so it is unbiased with any guide. A guided ray below the surface is not traced (it is a sample of zero). Only cosine rays count toward the sky visibility (scaled by their share), or rays aimed at openings raise it. Without the DDGI cache every ray is cosine.
- **Moving emitters**: an emitter sample in a reservoir is kept as its triangle and barycentrics and placed on it when read, and its contribution weight crosses frames per area of the emitter, turned into solid angle at the receiver from where the emitter is now. A world space point and a solid angle weight leave samples lighting the spot a moving emitter left, and flaring where it moved closer.
- **Neighbour count**: each spatial neighbour costs two alpha tested visibility rays per pixel (both pairwise directions), most of the spatial pass. With every pixel tracing and the denoiser accumulating, 2 neighbours measured as quiet as 4 or 8 in foliage, emitter lit rooms and during camera motion, and none only somewhat noisier; reuse matters most where the denoiser has no history, so a pixel whose reservoir has under 2 frames of confidence (disoccluded, or moving fast) takes `radiance_disocclusion_samples` neighbours instead. A neighbour that lands on another surface or has no reservoir is drawn again from half the radius (`radiance_spatial_retries`) rather than wasted (foliage, thin geometry); the choice depends only on the geometry, never on the samples, so the pairwise weights stay unbiased.
- **Samples are points**: a reservoir holds a hit point, its normal and the radiance it sends (or a sky direction), its contribution weight `W` and confidence `M`. Reuse reconnects the point to the new receiver with the solid angle Jacobian. Rays leave emission to the emitter candidates (`SCENE_HIT_EMISSION` off while emitters exist), so the two candidates cover disjoint parts of the integrand and both enter one reservoir with weight `p_hat * W`; an emitter sample's radiance is emission only, a ray sample's is reflected light only, and anything that retraces a sample (validation) must keep them apart.
- **Weights, not counts**: both reuse steps use balance heuristic weights (temporal: fresh against history; spatial: pairwise MIS of each neighbour against the pixel itself), each strategy's density converted into the receiver's solid angle, with `M` as confidence. Weighting by `M` alone is biased dark in creases (neighbours cannot find the wall right beside a pixel) and, with a receiver that moves every frame, lets a hit point next to it ratchet `W` up frame after frame until it shows as a bright dot. The spatial output is the mean of every candidate's estimate (Rao-Blackwellized), not one picked sample.
- **History** is reprojected for the GI pixel's centre, not the screen pixel it samples this frame (otherwise a still camera resamples it bilinearly every frame and it diffuses into swirls), and the receiver position is stored so temporal reuse can apply the Jacobian. When none of the 4 bilinear history texels holds the pixel's surface (thin things under the jitter, an edge that moved), the reservoir and the denoiser take their history from the best matching texel of the 3x3 around the reprojected position (`radiance_temporal_search`) instead of starting over. Permutation sampling (the frame swaps reservoirs with a 2x2 neighbour, only for pixels that traced this frame) is off by default: it brings back fireflies, and the motion capped history already stops streaks; `radiance_max_history` bounds how long a sample lasts. Never drop or rewrite reservoirs by a test that correlates with their brightness: reservoirs keep samples whose noisy hit shading came out high, so re-shading them under the same `W` (validation) or dropping the brightest of a group (`radiance_boiling_filter`) or the ones a retrace from a slightly different origin no longer reaches (light through leaf gaps) darkens the result, 15-50% under a canopy. Both are off by default; a changed light is followed within the history length. The temporal pass traces no visibility toward the kept sample either: a pairwise test needs the old receiver's ray origin, which without last frame's depth cannot be lifted off its planes reliably, and under the TAA jitter a receiver moves among grass blades every frame, so one way only darkened foliage by about a tenth and both ways brightened it by a few percent. A sample an occluder moved in front of keeps lighting its own pixel until fresh samples outweigh it, about the history length (neighbours only take it after their own visibility ray).
- **Hits read the screen**: a hit the camera saw last frame takes its light past the first bounce from last frame's raw spatial output (`SCENE_HIT_SCREEN_GI` hook in `shade_scene_hit`), only hits off screen read the cache. Probe caches leak at the foot of walls, and reuse turns such a sample into a dot that lasts its whole history. Never feed back the denoised output: every bounce then waits on its history, and light changes take the accumulation length divided by one minus the bounce gain to settle. A hit closer to the ray's origin than a twentieth of its view depth (the other wall at a crease) reads the accumulated estimate instead: the raw one there is partly the receiver's own light, the reservoirs keep the samples whose noisy read came out high, and with temporal reuse the loop between two walls glows along every crease. Reading the world cache there instead leaves dark lines, the probes are low next to corners.
- **Following light changes**: exponential accumulation follows a drop in as many history lengths as the drop has orders of magnitude, so day to night took seconds while night to day looked instant. Shortening the history by comparing it with the neighbourhood of this frame's estimate does not work in shade lit through gaps: most neighbourhoods miss the rare bright samples, and clamping drops the correct history (a canopy came out at 2/3 of its brightness, twice as noisy). Instead a change of the sun or moon (`radiance_light_change`, like DDGI's) caps the history everywhere for 60 frames. Local lights that move or switch are not detected and trail by the accumulation length. Local changes are found the same way, at the source, not per pixel: `radiance.GetChangedLights` lists the emitters and local lights that moved, turned or changed brightness in the last second (a turning spot moves everything it lights) (the sphere around their recent path and their intensity), and a pixel that sees such a light (one ray toward the sphere around its path, so lights in the next room do not count) and gets more than a tenth of its last denoised GI from it keeps a few frames of denoiser history and one of reservoir history. The temporal pass decides once and flags the reservoir, the denoiser reads the flag. The test must never read this frame's or a reservoir's own noisy estimate: cutting the history of the dark samples and keeping the bright ones made a dark room several times too bright. A per pixel detector (a short average against the long one, outside the pixel's own noise) fires on heavy tailed noise and brings fireflies back all over static scenes.
- **Cost of the cache at hits**: the world cache lookup at off screen hits is most of the candidate pass (DDGI's visibility rays to bright probes, `radiance_cache_contrast_ratio`, under a sunlit canopy nearly every probe). `radiance_hit_gi_share` below 1 runs it for a whole thread group on a share of frames and scales it up: linear in the lookup, so unbiased, and the lookup is unchanged, so no leaks, but the groups that looked up show as mottling during camera motion (the denoiser has little history), so it defaults to every frame. The group phase must be hashed: a linear pattern lines groups up into screen space diagonal bands. Never thin the probe visibility rays themselves: without them sealed rooms leak, and russian roulette per probe is renormalised by the probe weights and darkens rooms (3% at a quarter, 15% at a tenth). The decision must be the same for the whole group, or a lane that does the lookup holds its warp.
- **Ray origins** are lifted off every neighbouring pixel's plane the pixel lies on (crease pixels reconstruct a hair behind one of the two planes). Visibility rays to a neighbour's hit point aim at the point lifted off its own surface. The pairwise weights trace visibility both ways (this pixel to the neighbour's sample and the neighbour to this pixel's sample): a neighbour credited with a sample it cannot see takes weight from light found through gaps. Rays skip decals (`instances.MASK_SURFACES`): decal and road meshes float a few centimetres above the ground they are blended onto, and rays from the ground hit their black undersides.
- The lighting pass skips the screen space AO and its bounce for diffuse under ReSTIR (`gi_per_pixel`): the rays already hold near field occlusion and light; specular occlusion still uses the AO. DDGI with the AO bounce reads brighter than ReSTIR in enclosed spaces; compare GI against a reference, not against DDGI.
- **Verifying**: `radiance_debug` shows the irradiance. A reference is `radiance_temporal`, `radiance_spatial` and `radiance_filter` off with `radiance_accumulation` 100000 and a still camera: plain unbiased rays. With temporal reuse on the long average converges slower (a kept sample repeats), so compare strips along creases and walls over 15 s or more, and count fireflies with `MotionRecorder` with TAA off (TAA's clamp hides them). Compare means of the accumulated texture over hundreds of frames, never one frame: the estimator is heavy tailed (sun patches, emitters) and a single frame's mean over half a million pixels still moves 20%. Look at creases in a dark exposure with the contrast stretched; most failures show there first. The raw per-frame estimate (`radiance_spatial`) is fp32 and unclamped: next to an emitter one frame's estimate exceeds fp16, and a clamp there darkens everything near emitters, the more the better a sampler finds them; compare emitter samplers with no clamp in the path. `test/tests/render3d/radiance.lua` (the slow test compares reuse against plain rays).

## Shadows

- Sun shadow maps are built by `weather.BuildSunShadows`: one map whose cascades follow the camera frustum; cascade 1 is the `exact` one (fixed reach, own size, depth format, min caster size and update interval, redrawn every frame), the rest are `cascades`. Vegetation animates only in the exact cascade; coarser cascades draw plants at rest. Change settings with `weather.SetSunShadow(category, {name = value})` (names are `SUN_SHADOW_DEFAULTS` in `weather.lua`; `enabled`, `count`, `size`, `format` recreate the maps, the rest is written live). The Sun dial window's Shadows tab edits them and shows the reach and texel size. The exact cascade works without the others.
- A cascade fits the bounding sphere of its frustum slice divided by `zoom`; the radius is kept at or above half the slice depth so narrow fields of view still cover mid range. Texel sizes are what matters: compute them from `UpdateCascadeLightMatrices` before choosing a layout.
- The lookup is pushed toward the sun by `texel_world_size * 0.25` (in `directional_shadows.lua`); a full texel push leaked light under ceilings in far cascades. Thin geometry leaks in far cascades scale with texel size: look at receiver offsets before adding cascades. Translucency in shadow maps is dithered by `Material:GetShadowOpacity`. Prefer physically derived filtering (PCSS with the sun's real angular size) over fixed filters even when it changes the old look.
- Contact shadows march the depth buffer toward each light.
- `r_ray_shadows` shadows every light in surface lighting (the lighting and translucent passes) with rays instead: a ray toward a point on the sun's disc or on a sphere of the light's `SourceRadius` (`r_ray_shadow_samples` per light and pixel for sized lights), replacing the sun shadow map, the sun contact ray, light occlusion maps, point and local directional shadow maps and screen space shadows there; the maps are still built for the other consumers (fog, GI, particles). The visibility test is `ray_shadow_visible` (`ray_materials.GetShadowRayGLSL`: solid and alpha tested surfaces, transparent materials skipped since glass tints through `get_light_transmittance`, light bodies skipped for local lights), which the path tracer uses too, so a difference between them is sampling, not geometry. It is built into the shaders: the call in the light loop costs registers even when skipped at runtime, so the pvar reloads the passes that include surface lighting (`render3d.ReloadPasses`). With `r_ray_shadow_denoise` (default on) the lighting pass does not trace them itself: the `ray_shadows` passes trace one visibility per pixel for two groups (the directional lights and the local lights, each a weighted average by the light's unshadowed light at the point), accumulate it over frames (`r_ray_shadow_history`, reprojected by velocity, history clamped to the raw neighbourhood so moving shadows do not trail) and filter it by plane, normal and the accumulated deviation, and the lighting multiplies every light of a group by the group's visibility. Grouping is exact for one light per group and approximate where several lights cast different shadows on the same point. A pixel without a group's light stores a placeholder visibility of one: every average (reprojection taps, the temporal blend, the neighbourhood clamp, the spatial filter) must weigh visibility by the group's presence, or the placeholder lights the shadowed side of edges and contacts that the jitter or motion alternates with a surface facing away from the light (bright rims, worst with the sun). History taps must also match the pixel's normal, not only its depth. A light behind the surface must not enter a group unless the surface is lit from behind (`gbuffer_is_lit_from_behind`), or it drags the group's visibility down where it adds no light. The samples are blue noise with an R2 step per frame: interleaved gradient noise with a frame offset leaves a moire drifting across the screen that no history resolves. Off, the lighting traces each light per pixel and the noise is left to TAA, which cannot average one-ray visibility.
- Point and spot lights without a shadow map are shadowed by their light occlusion map (`light_occlusion.lua`): an octahedral map of hit distance moments traced from the light, blurred and mipmapped, read with a Chebyshev test. A light with a `SourceRadius` (the light block's `direction.w`) gets a PCSS style lookup (`light_occlusion_soft`): coarse and sharp reads skip lit and umbra pixels, taps find the blockers, the penumbra is the source radius times the mean of 1 / blocker distance minus 1 / receiver distance (averaging the distance and inverting it reads a too narrow penumbra), and taps over it test against the receiver's plane (flat ground near the light otherwise counts as its own blocker). Bleed reduction fades out above a few texels of penumbra, where it only darkens. Measure changes against the path tracer in an isolated scene (one light, occluders standing on a plane, direct light only, error split into lit, penumbra, umbra and a contact band around the occluder bases): penumbra width, umbra leak and contact leak (peter panning) move against each other.
- The sun shadow map is blind in three places: occluders within a few texels (single sided walls meeting at a corner or pierced by beams, common in Source 2 maps, leave the occluder's silhouette on the receiver), occluders inside the soft filter's receiver plane tolerance (slope times filter radius, up to a meter at grazing angles on round geometry), and the soft filter's penumbra estimate, which averages near and far blockers and spreads light from a nearby gap over points a close occluder fully hides. No bias fixes these. A ray toward the sun from every lit point covers them: `bias.contact` shadow texels, the tolerance the lookup used (`shadow_ray_reach`), and blockers closer than `texel / sun_tan` (whose penumbra is under a texel, so one ray is exact). It only tests solid instances, and only for the sun. `single_sided_room` in `addons/examples/lua/examples/render3d/gi_reflections.lua` reproduces every case without game assets (inward facing planes on stilts, beams and a pipe under a slotted roof, balls half in a wall, in an oversized wall hole plugged by a `ViewHidden` blocker and sunk in a mirror floor, and a scaffold far overhead whose stripes make the soft filter mix near and far blockers); compare zoomed shots with the contact ray (`bias.contact` 0) and the blocker off.
- Everything that lights a surface with the sun (surface lighting, `radiance_cache_direct_light`, `shade_scene_hit`, the path tracer) multiplies it by the cloud shadow and the ground fog's sun transmittance; the cloud shadow fields are in the probe block (`radiance_cache_data`) so every ray hit shader reads them.
- Ray origins off a gbuffer surface move by a fraction of a millimetre scaled by view distance (enough for depth reconstruction error), never by centimetres: in Source 2 maps props and floors meet within millimetres, and a centimetre offset starts sun or reflection rays on the far side of the floor or wall, where they see the sunlit outside.
- Ray tracing layers, lowest first: `scene/hit.lua` (geometry only: `scene_trace`, `scene_occluded`, no materials bound; audio, particles and light occlusion use it), `scene/ray_materials.lua` (the material buffer every material aware ray reads, `ray_material`/`ray_materials`, the optical volume records, alpha test and hit albedo, the `r_rt_hit_*` pvars), `optical_rays.lua` (below) and `scene_hits.lua` (trace and shade a hit with lights, GI and fog: `trace_scene_hit`, `scene_hit_visible`, `shade_scene_hit`, `trace_scene_radiance`; reflections, refraction and restir GI use it). The scene acceleration structure is `scene_tlas` in every shader.
- `optical_rays.lua` is the shared toolkit for straight rays through transparent bodies: transmittance along a segment (`get_optical_tint_ray`, `get_optical_tint` with the media volumes), light through them (`get_light_transmittance`, `_local`), the exit of a closed body (`get_body_exit`) and media inscatter along a segment. View rays that bend at interfaces (translucent refraction, the camera inside a body, water) use it for the exit and the tint of each straight segment; light paths are bent only by the caustic photons (see Caustics). It binds through `scene_hits.GetDescriptorSets`/`Bind` under the one scene name `scene_tlas`.
- A glass layer passes `mix(1, albedo * texel.rgb, texel.a)` of the light, its texture read where the ray crosses it (`optical_glass_through`): Source 2 glass keeps dirt and print in a colour texture whose alpha is their coverage, black where the pane is clear, so the colour alone made clear glass opaque.
- Glass tints light with a ray, not a shadow map: while `r_light_transmittance` is on, glass casts no shadow of its own (`Material.GlassCastsShadow()` false) and `get_light_transmittance` (see `optical_rays.lua`) traces only instances with the glass mask bit (`instances.MASK_GLASS`, set for `Material:IsGlass()`, refractive and not additive, which includes Source `refract` water), so decals and cutouts cost it nothing. Surface lighting skips it for a light whose contribution is under `LIGHT_TRANSMITTANCE_MIN_SCREEN_RADIANCE`, since glass only darkens. A large glass mesh still makes every bright light's ray near it walk that mesh. Where it runs: the lighting and translucent passes, `radiance_cache_shade` (sun, lights, emitters, probe rays) and scene reflections. A penumbra can reach into an opaque frame's footprint, so it traces a few offset rays (`search_radius`). `radiance_cache.HasGlass()` gates every ray so scenes without glass pay nothing. Without ray queries glass falls back to the dithered shadow.
- The terrain heightfield shadow (`terrain_shadow.lua`, `ShadowExtent` on the terrain) is multiplied into the directional shadow at every distance, not only far away: a mountain too far for the cascades still shadows ground right next to the camera, and a receiver's distance says nothing about its caster's.

## Sky, atmosphere, fog, clouds

- **Atmosphere** (`atmosphere.lua`, `atmosphere_scattering.lua`): sun illuminance times `GetTransmittance(sun_dir)` times `SUN_COLOR` (one physical sun color, luminance 1, used for direct light, sky, fog, clouds, disc and rain). The transmittance LUT is parameterised by distance to the atmosphere top (Bruneton) so the horizon is resolved, and rays below the local horizon return 0 in the lookup. Multiple scattering is a precomputed table (`scattering.LOOKUP_GLSL` / `sc_lookup`), baked on aerosol change; single scattering is marched in `integrate_scattering`. Visibility is two parameters in meters: `atmosphere.SetAerosolVisibility` (clear air) and `SetGroundFogVisibility` (the ground hugging layer); climates and the `atmosphere_controller` take the same pair. Sky LUT caches must be baked at the quantised key they are keyed by, or the result depends on who asked first. The sky has no ground: rays below the horizon use `get_sky_lookup_dir`; the environment probe draws a ground (`get_ground_radiance`), never over an environment map, which holds its own lower half. Marching code that integrates atmosphere must clip at the planet (density functions clamp altitude at 0, so underground air integrates at ground density); every consumer of `integrate_scattering` must do it. Tables are for a unit sun: multiply by `ATMOSPHERE_SUN_ILLUMINANCE * SUN_COLOR`. `envprobe.UpdateEnvironmentAltitude` moves the environment probe to the camera's height so high-up reflections and ambient are right. `weather.SetEnvironmentMap(path)` (an HDRI) disables sun and atmosphere; intensity defaults to 1000 cd/m2 per unit because probes clamp at 65504. Equirect samplers must clamp vertically.
- **View directions** for the sky, stars and discs must be built by rotating the view space ray (`mat3(inv_view) * view_pos.xyz`), never as `world_pos - camera_position`: subtracting two large world coordinates quantizes the direction by more than the sun's 0.27 degree radius a couple of km from the origin, which turns the disk, the moon and the stars into blocks and makes them come and go with position and angle (`screen_reconstruct.GetWorldRayGLSL` does it right).
- **The sun and moon discs reach reflections through mip 0 of the environment probe only** (`envprobe.prefilter_pipeline`, roughness 0, environment probe only): the pass adds `get_sun_disc` and `get_moon_disc` supersampled 4x4 over the texel (analytic, so the disc keeps its size whatever the probe resolution). The real disc is about 1e9 to 2e9 cd/m2 and a probe is fp16, so the environment probe is stored divided by a power of two scale picked from the sun's peak radiance (`get_probe_scale`) with the scale in the alpha channel of every texel (mips and irradiance alike, so filtering stays valid); every reader multiplies `.rgb` by `.a` (`sample_environment_specular`, `sample_environment_irradiance`, `radiance_cache_sky`, `pt_sky`), and a new reader of the environment textures must do the same. The mips above 0, the irradiance and `radiance_cache_sky` (lod 1) stay disc free, or the sun would light everything twice next to the direct light (the path tracer also reads mip 1 inside the disc because `pt_sun_hit` adds it); the direct sun lobe fades out below about 2.6 times the sun's angular radius in alpha (`lobe_energy` in `surface_lighting.lua`, material alpha is clamped to 0.002 so even a mirror keeps a small lobe) so the two hand off. The probe has no occlusion, so reflection probes do not get the disc. A mirror through the radiance pass shows the full disc with its glare because the filtered specular texture is pre-exposed (below). Stochastic lobe rays (roughness above `REFLECTION_MIRROR_THRESHOLD`) read the probe at lod 1 or higher (`sample_environment_specular_lod`): their directions already carry the lobe, so a miss would otherwise read mip 0 and a ray landing in the disc brings back 1e9, which the filters spread into speckle across every glossy surface; the sun's highlight on those surfaces comes from the direct light.
- **The sun and moon discs are drawn at the coverage of the pixel** (`get_celestial_discs`, 4x4 sub samples each with a quarter pixel edge, from the sky pass and the environment probe): a disc a few pixels wide is 1e4 times display white, so point sampling it makes its summed light jump about 14% with its position within the pixels, which the bloom and glare spread over a large area as a pulsing glow while the camera turns or jitters. The disc's normalisation includes the edge width so its illuminance does not change with the pixel size.
- **The TAA history is resampled in compressed space** (`sample_history`, Catmull-Rom on `compress`ed taps): the filter has negative lobes, and applied to linear radiance next to something 1e5 times brighter than its surroundings (the sun disc) it goes below zero and clamps to black, leaving dark pixels around the disc that flicker with the jitter.
- **The sun and the moon always both light the air; the primary light only decides who gets the shadowed direct light.** `ATMOSPHERE_SUN_ILLUMINANCE` is the primary directional light's illuminance (the moon's while it is up and outranks the sun), but `sc_ambient`, `sc_lookup` and the sky view LUT are baked for `ATMOSPHERE_SKY_SUN_DIRECTION` and work for any body by looking them up at the body's direction. Every radiometric term (ambient hemisphere integrals, ground irradiance, the fog's air and ground fog direct light, the multiple scattering table) is therefore a sum over the sun (`ATMOSPHERE_SUN_DISC_ILLUMINANCE`) and the moon (`ATMOSPHERE_SUN_DISC_ILLUMINANCE * ATMOSPHERE_MOON_SKY_SCALE`) with each body's own direction, never a function of which one is primary; scaling sun baked tables by the primary's lux makes twilight ambient jump by an order of magnitude at moonset. The only primary dependent input is the visibility (shadow maps, `sun_visibility`), and the other body uses the froxel glow ray. `fog_primary_light.lua` forces each body primary and requires the same fog.
- **Scattering tables are interpolated in log space along the sun axis** (`sc_ambient`): twilight radiance changes by an order of magnitude per few degrees while the sun axis has 32 nodes over mu_s -0.2 to 1 (about 2.2 degrees each near the horizon), and linear interpolation of that curve leaves a slope kink at every node (fog and ambient brightening 6 times faster after the sun crosses -4.81 or -2.59 degrees). `fog_twilight_smooth.lua` checks the slope on both sides of a node. `sc_lookup` (the multiple scattering table) still interpolates the sun axis linearly; if its kinks ever show in the sky or the fog, give it the same treatment.
- **Froxel volume** (`passes/volumetric_fog.lua`, `froxel_fog.lua`): one medium holds clear air and ground fog (extinction from `ground_fog_visibility` at the fog's start height, falling off with `GROUND_FOG_SCALE_HEIGHT` above it, constant below it, faded out between the end height minus 0.3 km and the end height; all three are runtime values, `atmosphere.SetGroundFogStartHeight`, `SetGroundFogEndHeight` (default start plus 1.1 km), `SetGroundFogScaleHeight`, and `atmosphere_controller` properties) out to `froxel_fog.FAR` (100 km), lit by the shadowed sun (including cloud shadows), DDGI probes where they exist and the analytic sky ambient in open air (mixed by overhead sky visibility). Its texels are -1 where nothing is known (hidden or outside the GI range). DDGI is looked up by its own pass (`volumetric_froxel_gi`) on a grid of half the froxel width and height (each cell evaluates one random froxel of its 2x2 per frame, the temporal pass averages them) through `radiance_cache_sample_medium` (`radiance_cache.GetMediumGLSL`): one probe loop and one visibility ray per probe give both the mean radiance (irradiance at a random direction and its opposite) and the overhead sky visibility, with no surface normal weighting. Keep DDGI lookups out of the per-froxel scatter: their visibility rays dominate the cost of the fog. The integrate pass writes per channel in-scatter (first half of a double-depth 3D texture) and transmittance (second half): consumers go through `get_volumetric_fog` / `froxel_sample`, never `texture()` on the volume. The fog lights the air from the primary directional light (the moon once it outranks the sun's direct light, e.g. sun just below the horizon) plus the other body (sun or moon) as a second term in the froxel scatter (the multiple scattering terms, primary and other, are scaled by one jittered ray per froxel from the froxel GI pass toward the dominant sky glow body (the sun above -11 degrees, else the moon), raised above the horizon when the body is below it, so ridges and buildings between the air and the horizon glow block it; stored in the emitter texture's alpha, the terrain shadow is the fallback without ray queries) and the sky tail (`integrate_air`); without it a twilight sky goes black. Rule: every in-scatter term read from the precomputed tables (`sc_lookup`, `integrate_scattering`) assumes open sky and is unoccluded unless scaled by a visibility toward its own source direction (shadow maps only cover the primary light's direct term, overhead sky visibility says nothing about a ridge between the air and the horizon glow); a new light or term added through the tables brings the glowing haze in front of mountains back. The direct sun term needs a visibility that reaches past the shadow cascades and past `r_rt_distance` (instances beyond it are not in the ray traced scene, so a far mountain is invisible to rays): the froxel GI pass stores one sun visibility per 2x2 cell in the emitter texture's red channel (a jittered ray toward the sun times `screen_sun_visibility`, a log spaced march along the sun's screen space path against the depth buffer that only counts surfaces beyond the froxel and at most their own depth behind the ray), and the scatter pass multiplies it into the cascade and terrain visibility at every distance (the far cascades of a terrain scene miss distant ridges, so fading it in only past the last cascade leaves a glow disc in front of a mountain that is a few km away); the green channel holds the same march toward the raised glow direction (a jittered cone of about 6 degrees: a wider one lets the glow lobe see over a ridge and the air in front of it glows), and the glow terms also multiply by `get_terrain_shadow`. Without them the air in front of a mountain with the sun behind it glows as bright as the sky. The march cannot see geometry when the sun is off screen, so far terrain needs the heightfield shadow (`ShadowExtent` on the terrain; with none, nothing shadows air past the cascades and ray range). Repro with a huge box far away and the sun behind it. Sky pixels are composed (volume + transmittance * tail past FAR + layer transmittance * background), not subtracted from the sky LUT; the tail is integrated for every ray that leaves the atmosphere past FAR, never gated by the altitude the ray has at FAR (at twilight only the high air is sunlit, and a gate shows as a hard line across the sky); with clouds the sum splits at the cloud depth. Never use a single point shadow lookup for a medium segment. Noise: the temporal pass blurs each froxel along the sun direction (fading with depth, `line_fade`) plus a light neighbour blur, history is weighted by a validity alpha, scatter picks a random corner for occluded froxels (history lookups stay deterministic). The depth that clamps a froxel to the surface and marks it covered (`froxel_point`) is the depth pass's farthest depth over the tile and its margin, never a jittered pixel sample: a few samples per froxel miss alpha tested holes smaller than the sample spacing, so the froxels behind the geometry flip between clamped to the surface and open air every frame and the sky seen through the holes flickers and darkens. Check it with a plane of small round holes against the sky, aa, ao and gi off, flicker measured with `MotionRecorder` against the same scene with `r_pass_volumetric_fog` off. `froxel_fog.TILE` sets sharpness and cost. The fog's light is a fitted model (`get_ground_fog_sun` octaves, `get_ground_fog_sky_ambient`, `get_ground_fog_sun_transmittance`, which also dims direct light on surfaces by height), referenced against a path tracer; change it only with a reference to compare to.
- **Fog volumes** are procedural `fluid_volume`s (see fluid-volumes.md): `Simulation = "procedural"`, `Fill = 1`, a `Medium`, `Density` in 1/m per unit of the medium, `HeightFalloff` (scale height, 0 uniform) and `EdgeSoftness`, raymarched when small and in the froxels with `Raymarch = false` when large. Procedural fluids are also `optical_volume` records of kind 1 in the ray material prefix (an analytic centred box with the uniform density times the falloff sampled at the middle of the path, noise ignored), so the lighting pass, the probes and the hit rays see the sun and lamps through them; simulated fluids are not. The layer is also a volume record (kind 3 in the optical prefix: start and top height in world metres, the scale height, the tint times the extinction per metre, the precipitation extinction and the albedo; `write_layer_record`, present while the extinction is above 0), and `optical_inscatter` applies every kind 1 record and the layer to a ray: `get_optical_tint` multiplies the transmittance (the layer only for rays that hit something, `with_layer`, because a ray that misses samples the environment map, which is baked with the layer) and `optical_inscatter` adds `(ambient + sun * visibility * transmittance to the sun * phase) * albedo` per volume over three segments (`T(0, s_k) - T(0, s_k+1)` weights each segment's own source), the sun visibility being one shadow ray per segment at its middle (`optical_inscatter_lit`, `scene_occluded`), with the closed form optical depth of the exponential profile for the layer (`optical_layer_depth`). The DDGI probe, ReSTIR and reflection hit rays all use it, so procedural fluids and the layer light and dim bounce and reflection hits the same way; the sun skips the layer (`get_light_transmittance`) because it applies it analytically while the other lights dim by it (`get_light_transmittance_local`, and the froxel lamp in-scatter through `optical_layer`), the layer top is cut halfway into the atmosphere's fade (`GROUND_FOG_TOP_SOFTNESS`), and the froxel volume keeps reading the atmosphere. Every hit ray takes the ambient from the environment irradiance over pi (`radiance_cache_fog_ambient`) and the sun from the atmosphere or the DDGI sun. The sun transmittance through the layer is one function (`exponential_layer_transmittance` in `medium_common`, used by the atmosphere and by the DDGI direct light). The ground fog layer takes its extinction tint, albedo and phase from `media.presets.fog` (a `Scattering` and `Absorption` per channel), and every consumer of the layer is per channel: the froxel integrate pass, the sun transmittance through the layer (`ground_fog_sun_transmittance`, `radiance_cache_ground_fog_sun_transmittance`, the surface lighting, the particles and the DDGI direct light) and the non froxel segment integral (`GroundFogSegment`). The precipitation extinction is added to the same extinction, so it is tinted with the layer in the froxel integral but grey in the sun transmittance. Froxel slices are about 12% of their depth deep (5 m at 40 m), so both the scatter pass (scattering) and the integrate pass (extinction) take each froxel fluid box as its overlap with the froxel's slice segment times the density there (`fluid_segment_sample`, clipped at the surface behind the froxel); a density sampled at one point per slice makes every box edge a staircase seen from outside, and a jittered point only converges while the camera holds still, so moving forward or back sweeps the slices through the volume.
- **Snow** (`surface_weather.lua`, `snow_cover.lua`): `weather.SetSnowDepth(meters)` whitens surfaces in the surface shader, masked by the shelter map (an orthographic depth map from the precipitation direction, the top surface within 96 m of the camera). Dynamic instances are in it, so snow caps them and leaves a sheltered pit under them. With depth it is relief: when the shelter map re-centres or the depth changes, a compute pass (recorded in the frame's command buffer; `SubmitAndWait` there stalled 30 ms) turns the shelter depth into a snow map, rg = snow top and top surface in light space depth, lifted by the depth on ground flatter than about 60 degrees and tapered to nothing over 1.5 times the depth towards any ledge, drops (roof and wall edges) and rises (wall bases, the footprint of an overhang) alike, so snow falls off smoothly into shelter instead of ending in a wall; the shelter map cannot tell a wall from an overhang, so snow no longer banks against walls. Every gbuffer fragment marches its view ray in light space (`march_snow_cover`, a step of a twentieth of the depth, at most `snow_march_steps` steps (pvar, 128) over the part of the ray inside the shelter map; two levels of min tiles (8 and 64 texels, `snow_cover.lua`, holding the highest snow top of a tile and its margin) let a ray that stays above a tile skip to its exit, snapped to the step lattice so skipped and marched rays sample the same points. The march is bound by dependent texture fetches, so cost follows the number of fine steps (grazing views over a long window are the expensive ones, and a cap of 256 doubled the cost of one such view for a smoother silhouette): coarser steps scallop the rim of a drift seen at a grazing angle, for example from below; the fine window ahead of the fragment is 1.2 times the depth over the cosine to the precipitation direction, floored at 0.02, and 48 coarse steps cover the rest of the ray back to the eye, because snow on higher ground between the eye and a far fragment is outside that window and would let the geometry behind a deep drift show through) and is snow where a point lies between the snow top and the surface under it, so caps hang over the walls below them. The test blends each texel's own inside distance (bilinearly while marching, with a 4x4 cubic B-spline at the fragment's own position and in the bisection, which rounds outlines and caps; B-spline on every step costs about 2 ms) clamped to the ledge jump (on one surface that is the bilinear snow top, across a ledge the outline runs between texel centres instead of in steps), with 5 cm of tolerance under the top surface (more only for an upward facing fragment at its own position) capped by the snow's thickness: filtering the snow top itself or a texel sized tolerance hangs snow curtains under every balcony, and an uncapped tolerance whitens steep faces in texel sized patches (acne, worst with wind slanting the map). Two separable passes smooth the snow top with a Gaussian over texels on the same surface (top within the ledge jump), radius half the depth, at most 24 texels, which rounds caps and the taper steps of the 9 cm shelter texels (`SHELTER_SIZE` 2048 over 192 m). An edge pass writes, per texel, a Gaussian blurred coverage of its own surface; after the blur the thickness is multiplied by a smoothstep of it, so snow eases to zero at a ledge (the blur skips ledge neighbours, so an edge texel would otherwise keep the thickness of its interior neighbours and the snow would end in a step that ambient occlusion picks up). The snow normal is a finite difference of the snow top whose neighbours fade out smoothly across a ledge; sampling across one made the shading jagged along every edge. The shelter map only redraws when the camera crosses a snap step, so a scratch scene that spawns geometry and never moves the camera has no relief (move the view away and back before a shot). The snow map is rebuilt when the shelter map was actually redrawn (`last_rendered_frame`), not when it moved; a fixed delay read stale depth. The gbuffer only marches fragments of geometry, so snow in front of the sky or of far geometry it buries is drawn by a backdrop (`snow_backdrop.lua`): a sphere of 90 m around the camera queued in the gbuffer pass (`Draw3DGeometry`, `gbuffer_instancing.Queue`) with a `SnowBackdrop` material, which writes fragment depth like a decal or relief material. Its fragments run the normal snow march and are discarded on a miss, or when the ray rises more than 0.5 along the precipitation direction, so a hit is a real gbuffer surface with the same lighting, gi, shadows and texture as the snow around it. It adds a march per pixel while snow lies, since writing depth disables early depth rejection. A hit forces snow cover by its thickness (full at `SNOW_FULL_COVER_DEPTH`), so the cover fades out with the taper instead of ending in a line. A hit writes its depth (lighting reconstructs the position from it; without it the shading happened at the buried surface and shadows speckled), which the culling pass gets by routing every main view row to its nearer pipeline variant while a snow map exists: the height map pipeline promises `depth_less` for shells, and snow moves depth nearer. Snow cannot stick out against the sky, nothing behind it marches. The default climate wind is 0; wind slants the shelter map and the snow with it.
- **Clouds** (`clouds.lua`, `passes/clouds.lua`; layers via `weather.SetCloudLayers`, presets in `climate.lua`): the phase lobes come from `media.presets.cloud` (`PhaseG`, `PhaseBackG`, `PhaseBackShare`) and the multiple scattering orders are `medium_multiple_scattering` in `medium_common`, which fog volumes and the layer use too (their higher orders are weighted by `1 - sun transmittance`, so thin fog stays single scattered); noise bake, beer shadow map, sky dome, quarter-res checkerboard trace with half-res reconstruct, composited in the fog pass. The sun is dimmed per pixel by `get_cloud_shadow` (surface lighting, the medium shadow function, DDGI shade). Rules learned: baked noise must be stretched to 0..1; lattice streaks need the domain warp with the warp mip offset by its frequency; the sun disc is ~1e5-1e6 times a cloud's brightness, so transmittance must fade out smoothly (not cut off) near zero; detail volume resolution (`clouds.DETAIL_NOISE_SIZE`) limits close-up detail more than step count; the main-view detail is filtered by pixel footprint, not step size. Cost knobs: `cloud_layer_steps`, `cloud_detail_min_mip`, `clouds.VIEW_DIVISOR`.
- **Camera inside a medium body** (`passes/camera_medium.lua`, `get_camera_body_view` in that pass, pvar `r_camera_medium`): a compute pass after the fog composite, active while the scene has medium or glass bodies (a glass body without a medium absorbs by its albedo tint over `medium_thickness`, as the light tint walk does). Every pixel casts a ray query along its view ray to the nearest medium body face; a face seen from its inside (an exit face) first means the camera is inside that body, and the pixel is dimmed by the body's transmittance over the path to the wall (or to the surface, if nearer) and gets the ambient in-scatter `ambient * wall transmittance * body reflectance * (1 - T)`. Sky pixels are tinted too. There is no camera state to track. `post_source.GetFoggedOpaqueSceneFramebuffer` returns this pass's output, so translucent surfaces and particles draw over the dimmed view (they are not dimmed themselves). The sun adds single scattering along the path through the shared jittered shaft sampler (`medium_common.GetShaftGLSL` generates a function that integrates a per sample expression times the transmittance over stratified samples, denser near the camera; the water pass builds its shaft visibility with the same generator): per sample the sun's path to the body's exit face through `get_body_exit`, one shadow ray past that wall and the shared multiple scattering orders, and the scene behind the wall is the refracted view (`screen_refraction_project` of the refracted ray's point in the fog output; a grazing ray that cannot leave reflects the body's own colour). The sun needs `medium_ior` in the material entry. Water volumes, fog volumes and the ocean have their own camera-inside handling (water pass, froxel volume). A body can have a rippled top surface (`MediumWaveHeight` in meters, `MediumWaveLength`, `MediumCaustics`): the top wall's normal is perturbed by the shared ripples for the refracted view, and the sun's shafts through it are modulated by the caustic focus. The ripples and the caustic solve live in `medium_surface.lua` (`GetGLSL(block)`, `GetCausticGLSL(block, {name, params, gradient})`, `block` and `WriteBlock` for the uniform fields it reads: `time`, `view_scale`, `ripple_pairs`, `bump_tex`, `bump_info`); the water pass uses the same code for water volumes and keeps the ocean's cascade textures and detail ripples as its own gradient. `addons/examples/lua/examples/render3d/media_scene.lua` is a scene for the media systems (ice, glass, water volumes, fog volumes, particles, the bodies to stand inside): env `MODE=all|ocean`, `NIGHT=1`, `FOG=<visibility m>`, `CAM=<camera>`, or `media_scene.Goto(name)` live; the cameras are printed on load and the `inside_*` ones are in the bodies.

## Reflections, translucency, materials

- **Reflections** are the specular lobe of the radiance pass (`radiance_candidates` traces one ray per pixel after the diffuse candidate, `radiance_accumulate` and the `radiance_filter` passes average it, the lighting reads it through `gi.GetScreenSpecularTexture`; there is no pass of their own). The ray is a mirror ray from `reflection_direction` below `REFLECTION_MIRROR_THRESHOLD` and a GGX visible normal sample above it, hit by `trace_scene_radiance` (the shading of every other hit, ground fog, the environment at `roughness * (1 - rough_mix)` because the direction is already sampled over the lobe) and limited at `REFLECTION_MAX_HIT_LUMINANCE` times exposure white above the mirror blend (a firefly weight relative to roughness halves the mean of a reflection that mixes a sunlit and a shaded surface, never weight by brightness). From `radiance_specular_emitter_roughness` up a pixel may leave the emissive hits to one emitter sample instead (two light tree picks resampled by their light times the VNDF density of their direction, one shadow ray); it chooses between that and the rays with the emission at random, with a share that falls to none between roughness 0.35 and 0.6, because the emitter sample reads low across a broad lobe (a white metal in a furnace of emitters lost half the emission at roughness 1; the cause is not found, the estimator matches a numerical integral of the density). The rays follow the visible normals, but the lobe also masks the light along each one: every ray carries the weight `(1 + lambda_v) / (1 + lambda_v + lambda_l)` and the specular texture holds the weighted light in rgb and the weights in alpha, which the lighting divides, so the average is the light over the lobe (unweighted a rough lobe came out 16% bright in a sky lit room). A sample whose direction falls below the surface is a sample of zero: it is still traced folded back above the surface (`reflection_above`, for the hit distance) but weighs 0. Folded and counted, it adds weight to the low directions in proportion to how many samples the lobe loses below the horizon, which only shows when the light is not uniform (rough metals 4 to 5% bright under sky light alone, invisible in a furnace). Surface features that change the lobe (clearcoat, secondary specular) bend the roughness and normal the ray uses (`surface_features.GetReflectionGLSL`); the lighting's layers read the same texture. The accumulation reads a smooth surface's reflection history where last frame showed the reflected thing, not the surface (`radiance_specular_tracking`, after NRD's virtual motion): its image lies behind the surface along the view ray, the hit distance times the lobe's dominant direction factor, and the history is taken from the texels on this pixel's plane where the line from last frame's camera to the image crosses it. The tracking distance is the inverse distance from the camera to the image, averaged weighted by each ray's light (averaging distances lets rays that escape to the sky put every image at infinity, and unweighted the dark things right beside a glossy surface pull its image close and the highlight trails), this frame's ray on a mirror and the accumulated one as the lobe widens (NRD's curve). The image history replaces the surface's outright: a share of the surface's, misplaced every frame, builds up into a smear over a long history. A broad lobe sees near and far things at once, which move apart on the screen however the history moves, so its history is capped by how long their images (one deviation of the tracked inverse distance either side) take to drift apart by half the lobe's footprint on the screen; a mirror keeps all of its history. Project far images with the point and the direction weighted (`P / (1 + d) - V d / (1 + d)` in clip space): a point a million meters out loses its precision and the drift test fires with a still camera. Without tracking, or where its texels do not hold the plane, the history follows the surface with a length set by the lobe width over how far the view turned (a mirror keeps none while the camera moves). Separate weights for the specular in the a-trous filter (its own luminance deviation, the lobe angle between normals, a roughness match) and a reach capped by the lobe's footprint at the hit distance both measured worse on a glossy floor than sharing the irradiance's weights. On a smooth surface only (a clamp to the neighbourhood darkens the heavy tailed samples of a rough one) the history is clamped to the neighbourhood of this frame's rays, so a reflected thing that moved does not leave a trail; the a-trous filter averages a pixel's reflection only as far as its lobe is wide (`specular_reach`, none for a mirror, the full radius from roughness 0.6). The hit limit is a firefly clamp and drops real light (rough reflections of small strong emitters, of sunlit patches at a low exposure: 8 to 14% of a rough metal floor's reflection in a window and emitter lit room), so with `radiance_specular_reuse` the cut part comes from the pixel's gi reservoir instead: its sample weighed by the lobe, `D G2 / (4 NoV)`, through the same `W`, with the same clamp applied to its light (none on sky samples, none on emission when the ray left emission to its emitter sample that frame) and only what lies above it added to the ray's weighted light. Clamped ray plus reservoir excess is the unclamped integral, at the clamped ray's noise; samples drawn by light find the bright things the lobe's rays hit rarely. Spreading the clipped light over neighbouring pixels instead keeps the mean but not the noise: the denoiser already averages far wider than any spread, the hits are too rare. Taking the whole rough reflection from the reservoirs gains no noise and makes it follow light changes as slowly as the diffuse gi. Under ray reconstruction the history and the filter are off and the network gets the single ray. The hit distance of the ray is its own candidate attachment (the DLSS guide reads it). Without ray queries there is no traced reflection: the lighting falls back to the environment and the probes' light. `test/tests/render3d/radiance_specular.lua` measures metals and dielectrics over roughness against the path tracer (mean luminance ratio, within 3 to 5%: metals 0.96 to 1.01 of the path, dielectrics within 1%), and `radiance_furnace.lua` puts white metal and diffuse spheres in a closed room of emitters, where every sphere should read the wall radiance (the diffuse spheres read 0.93 of the path, the diffuse gi's own loss, the metals 0.95 to 0.97): the tests to run after touching any of this. Indirect light alone (the sun at 1 lux) is the sensitive case for the lobe weighting: a furnace cannot see it, and under the sun the direct light hides it. `addons/examples/lua/examples/render3d/reflection_plates.lua` has mirrors with and without normal maps, roughness sweeps and a dirty metal. The accumulated specular is fp32 absolute radiance, but the fp16 `radiance_filter_*_specular` textures the lighting reads hold radiance times the pre-exposure (the first filter iteration multiplies it in, the lighting divides it out): absolute radiance in fp16 caps at 65504 cd/m2, far below the sun disc (about 1e9), which a mirror reflects.
- **Translucent surfaces** (`passes/translucent.lua`, moments/accumulate with `translucent_extras.lua`): refraction is exact unpolarised dielectric Fresnel; a thin wall is a pane, a solid has far-side Fresnel and total internal reflection; absorption comes from the material's `Medium` (Beer-Lambert, `optical_transmittance`) or its albedo; with ray queries a refractive body takes its refraction from its own geometry: `get_body_exit` (a ray query over the glass masked instances along the refracted direction, per colour channel with dispersion) returns the distance to the nearest back face of a glass or medium body and its smoothed outward normal (`scene_hit_smooth_normal`, the interpolated vertex normals), the exit position and the exit refraction use that point and normal, the absorption of a material with a medium runs over that distance, and an exit face parallel to the entry face (`dot > 0.98`) takes the slab formula with its inner reflections; this replaces the curvature sphere and slab model and the thin pane model for every closed body, a single sheet or a miss falls back to them (`RefractionThickness`, the curvature estimate, `MEDIUM_PANE_PATH`); a refracting material has one `Roughness` (rough glass averages several rays with hashed, not interleaved-gradient, noise). They trace their own mirror ray (`scene_hits` direction/lobe code), have no denoiser of their own (TAA averages the rays). A graphics pipeline has one descriptor set unless `DescriptorSetCount` says more; sets used by a command buffer cannot be rewritten, so per-frame TLAS/material bindings need a set per swapchain image. Diffuse transmission (`features/transmission.lua`) is for leaves and cloth: a normalised lobe lights the front with light arriving at the back, and the opaque diffuse is scaled by `1 - T`.
- **Gbuffer roughness**: `gbuffer_roughness()` returns GGX alpha (readers `sqrt` it for perceptual); the raw channel holds perceptual roughness and is written through `gbuffer_encode_roughness(alpha)`.
- **Subsurface scattering** (`features/subsurface.lua`, `passes/subsurface.lua`) is a screen space diffusion. The lighting pass writes the diffuse light (direct plus ambient, divided by albedo, pre-exposed) of every pixel with `Subsurface > 0` to its second target with the strength in alpha. `subsurface_blur` blurs it horizontally and `subsurface` vertically with a heavy tailed profile (two exponentials per channel, in millimeters; the material's `SubsurfaceColor` sets how far each channel travels, normalised to luminance 1 like the transmission tint, and `r_subsurface_width` times `SubsurfaceRadius` scales it, neither is clamped). The gbuffer is near its 8 target limit, so the tint and radius are 4 bit fields, then adds `strength * (blurred - original) * albedo` to the lighting color in place, so everything that reads the lit scene (ocean, previews, history) sees it. Blurring irradiance and re-applying albedo keeps pores and texture detail sharp. `SubsurfaceSkinTone` multiplies the strength by how skin-like the albedo is per pixel (hue, saturation, brightness), which separates skin from hair, eyes and clothes inside one texture; the Source VMT loader sets it for flesh and player class materials. Taps are rejected by depth and by neighbours without the feature. The effect is faint on smooth lit surfaces; it shows at shadow edges, terminators and fine features, and a larger `SubsurfaceRadius` with a wide `SubsurfaceColor` (wax, marble) makes it obvious. Specular is untouched, and reflections and GI rays do not see it. Thin back lit skin (ears) is `DiffuseTransmission`, a separate term. A change to the gbuffer shaders can take minutes of driver pipeline compile on the first run.
- **Shading model** (`surface_lighting.lua`, `ibl.lua`): GGX D, height-correlated Smith visibility, Schlick Fresnel with `F90(f0)` (a fixed F90 of 1 makes `SpecularMultiplier 0` surfaces sheen edge-on), Kulla-Conty style energy compensation applied to direct *and* ambient specular (`GGXEnergyCompensation = 1 / (1 - (1 - E) F_avg)`), and a Fresnel-coupled EON diffuse (`Fd_EON`, energy preserving Oren-Nayar) for direct light. Ambient diffuse multiplies irradiance by `EON_directional_albedo` (the share of uniform incoming light the EON lobe reflects toward the viewer), not by the albedo: Lambert reflects 10-15% more than EON on rough surfaces. Ray hits (`shade_scene_hit` and DDGI's probe rays, roughness from `ray_hit_roughness`) light the sun and lights with `Fd_EON` toward the ray (rough surfaces reflect up to twice their uniform response back toward the light, so sunlit ground seen through a window with the sun behind the viewer needs it) and probe or screen light with `EON_directional_albedo`. Irradiance does not say where the light came from: a window lit room seen from the window side is about 30% too dark with any irradiance based indirect; ReSTIR weighs each sample by `Fd_EON` at the pixel and divides by the uniform response the lighting pass multiplies by. When a surface looks washed out, render it with black albedo and remove terms (specular, transmission, fog) one at a time to find additive light; thin geometry seen edge-on gets large direct GGX specular.
- **Local lights**: a point light's `Lumen` is flux, intensity `Lumen / (4 pi)`, falling off as `1 / (d^2 + source_radius^2)` times Frostbite's window (`range`; the automatic range is where the light reaches ~0.0005 lux). Spots integrate the cone including the smoothstep taper (`GetEmissionSolidAngle`), so a lamp emits exactly its lumens. Light color is divided by its luminance so hue does not change brightness. Lights, their shadow maps and occlusion maps read the entity's world transform (`GetWorldPosition`, `GetWorldRotation`), so a light can be a nested prefab node or a child of a prop.
- **Emissive surfaces**: emissive luminance is `color * strength (* albedo alpha) * EMISSIVE_REFERENCE_LUMINANCE`; an emissive surface is not a light, its effect on surroundings is GI only. Probe spacing causes an overshoot directly under tiny emitters. A material with `EmitsLight` false still glows but is not an emitter. Source maps author a light entity next to every bulb, so `source_engine/fixture_emission.lua` clears the flag on a material once most of its emissive area (half) lies within 0.5 m of a light entity; everything else (screens, signs, windows, lone lamps) keeps emitting. It also sets `IgnoredByLightOcclusion`: the instance mask bit `MASK_LIGHT_BODY` that the light occlusion trace skips, because a light placed inside its own bulb mesh would otherwise see that mesh on every ray.
- **Ambient occlusion** weighs below-horizon directions by `BELOW_HORIZON_WEIGHT` and the probes' sky visibility (the ground is already in the probes' lobes, so counting it as an occluder twice darkens near-ground surfaces); the bent normal of an unoccluded surface must equal its normal. A slice whose directions all weigh 0 (a ceiling, every direction below the horizon, with the GI reporting some sky visibility) uses the unweighted open fraction: dividing gave 0/0 and a black ceiling.
- Validate shading changes against references: a ColorChecker chart under a locked EV compared with analytic matte values, furnace rooms (walls that emit and reflect, nothing may look brighter or darker than the walls; `reflection_furnace.lua` in the examples), and a random-walk multiple scattering reference for metals. Scratch scripts in `./tmp/`.

## Path traced reference

`r_pass_path_trace 1` (an optional pass, built only while on: `goluwa/render3d/path_trace.lua`, `passes/path_trace.lua`) replaces the lit opaque surfaces with a progressive path traced mean, a reference to hold GI, reflections and shadows against. It is a debugging tool, not a game renderer.

- It shares everything with the raster lighting except light transport, so a difference is the raster's integration: the first surface is the gbuffer (every surface feature decoded), every vertex uses the raster's core lobe (GGX with energy compensation, EON diffuse with the Fresnel scale), lights use `get_light_vector_and_attenuation`, point and spot lights are spheres of their `SourceRadius` (the light block's `direction.w`: the attenuation at the centre is spread over the cone the sphere subtends, which softens shadows, while raster shadows ignore the radius), the sun is a cone of `SUN_ANGULAR_RADIUS` with the ground fog transmittance and cloud shadow, both combined with BSDF rays by MIS, and the sky is the environment map (captured without the sun disc). With `path_trace_caustics` (default on) paths refract and reflect at caster glass (dielectric Fresnel, the material's roughness as a microfacet lobe, Beer-Lambert absorption over the distance inside, a sampled wavelength at a dispersive surface), next event estimation is blocked by caster glass and water tops, and the sun and lamp light that reaches the surface the camera sees through glass or a water top comes from the caustic photons (`path_trace_photons` per frame, deposited with the surface's full BSDF for the photon's direction); a path from that surface that finds the sun or a lamp through glass or a water top gets weight 0 where that light's photons cover it and 1 elsewhere. Non-caster glass and other transparent instances are passed through with the optical tint, and the passes after lighting (fog, water, translucent) draw over it as usual.
- Hits read textures at lod 0 and bend the smooth vertex normal by the material's normal maps (`NormalTexture` with its bump transform and multiplier, `Normal2Texture` by the texture blend, SSBump, `r_normal_map_strength`); terrain hits take the terrain's world normal texture and the layer normal maps triplanar at the layer scales, weighted by the material texture. The first surface's geometric normal and position come from a camera ray when it lands on the gbuffer surface (depth reconstruction tilts at grazing angles and rejects mirror rays), else from the depth.
- Not modelled: the extra lobes of features (clearcoat, sheen, transmission; subsurface is turned off where the reference shows), media between surfaces, the bending of camera paths at water tops (only photons bend there), point lights' glow widening, at hits the detail and micro detail normals, height map normals, parallax and overlays, and on terrain the slope, cover and height blend rules, far scales and anti-tiling. Emissive triangles are found by BSDF rays only (no emitter sampling), so small emitters converge slowly.
- The mean restarts when the camera, lights, materials, instance count or environment change, or on `path_trace_reset`. Motion does not restart it unless `path_trace_reset_on_scene_change` (the idling player alone would keep it from converging); `path_trace_max_samples` stops tracing.
- Compare with `path_trace_view` (`raster` flips to the normal lighting while the mean keeps accumulating, `split`, `difference`: the ratio of the two raw targets presented straight to the screen past every later pass, red where the raster is brighter, blue where darker, full at `path_trace_difference_stops`), the `path_trace_compare` command or `path_trace.Compare(step)` in scripts (mean luminance and error over both rgba32f targets, absolute radiance; the raster target holds only the last frame).
- Check the tracer before blaming the raster: with `path_trace_bounces 0` and `lighting_debug_direct` both show direct light only and must agree within about a percent (shadow map against ray differences aside). Coplanar surfaces z-fight under TAA jitter: the mean averages both while the raster target holds one, which looks like a lighting difference.
- The raster's indirect diffuse is Lambert while its direct is EON, so on rough surfaces viewed with the sun behind the viewer the reference is brighter (EON backscatters the bright sky around the sun, around a quarter more sky light on rough ground).

## Caustics

`r_pass_caustics` (an optional pass, off by default and built only while on: `goluwa/render3d/caustics.lua` for the settings, casters and photon GLSL, `passes/caustics.lua` for the passes) lights surfaces with sun and lamp light that glass bodies and water volume tops bent: the surfaces the camera sees through a screen buffer, and every ray hit (reflections, refraction, ReSTIR GI, DDGI probes) through a world grid. The path tracer uses the same photon code for its reference (see Path traced reference). `addons/examples/lua/examples/render3d/caustics.lua` is the scene: a rippling pool, a crown and a flint glass ball and a flint prism at minimum deviation throwing a spectrum (`caustics_demo.Goto(pool|ball|prism|mirror|hall|hall_ceiling|overview)`; the hall is a closed room whose pool has an underwater spot throwing caustics on the ceiling, env `CAM`, `NIGHT=1` for lamps only, `CAUSTICS=0`, `PATH_TRACE=1`).

- Casters are glass instances whose material casts caustics (`CastsCaustics`, default on, needs `Refraction`; double sided glass is a sheet and only tints) and water volumes. Emitters are entries of a light and a caster: for the sun a rectangle around the caster in the plane facing the sun, for a point or spot lamp in range the cone from the lamp around the caster's bounding sphere. A lamp inside a water volume aims its cone at the volume's top, and photons that start under water know it. Each frame the CPU keeps the `caustics.MAX_CASTERS` entries with the largest rank (the flux that reaches the caster, a lamp's intensity times its cone's solid angle or the sun's illuminance times the rectangle, times screen importance, times the measured landed share without the floor, so a measured dark entry leaves and is only measured again once its record has expired), and gives those photons by weight: their size (rectangle area, a caster's cross section, a water top) times screen importance (over squared camera distance) times the share of their photons that landed on visible pixels in recent frames (plus a tenth of those that only reached the world grid, read back per frame slot from counters the photon pass adds per workgroup, floored so a blocked entry keeps being measured). Weighting the photons by luminous flux gives a sun entry under a roof, or casters outside the camera's room, nearly every photon, so flux only picks the entries; `r_caustics_photons` are shared by that weight in jittered grids of 8x8 tiles (coherent rays, a Bayer stratum per tile for the wavelength). A photon whose cell lies in an earlier entry of the same light (rectangle, or cone of the same lamp) is dropped, so overlaps count once. A sun photon carries the illuminance (ground fog, cloud shadow) times its cell's area; a lamp photon its intensity times its cell's solid angle, and where it lands the falloff of an unbent ray of the same length from the lamp (`get_light_vector_and_attenuation` at the lamp plus the emitted direction times the path length, times that length squared), so spot cones and range windows match the raster lights.
- A photon is traced through caster glass (Fresnel picks reflection or refraction, roughness samples a microfacet, absorption over the path inside) and water tops (the water pass's own ripple gradient at the photon's cell size, absorption under the top until the box exit), passing non-caster glass with the straight tint. It is kept only if it bent at least once, and lands on the first other surface, which it adds to in a screen buffer only where that surface is the gbuffer's (plane test against the reconstructed position), as irradiance: its flux over the world area the pixel covers there (`caustic_pixel_area`), pre-exposed, in fixed point with stochastic rounding, with a photon count.
- Dispersion: a photon that reaches a surface whose `(n - 1) / AbbeNumber` is at least `r_caustics_dispersion_spread` is traced again from the same random state as three bands of the spectrum (stratified wavelengths, Cauchy fit through the material's index and Abbe number, CIE fit to linear sRGB clamped to positive and normalised so the bands average to white). Per photon wavelengths instead make colour noise everywhere; water and crown glass split by under a millimetre per metre, so the realtime threshold skips them (the path tracer always splits).
- Filtering: a separable bilateral blur at two widths, blended per pixel by how many photons the narrow one gathered (`r_caustics_filter_photons`, widest `r_caustics_filter_radius`), then a temporal pass with a neighbourhood clamp (`r_caustics_history`, and `r_caustics_water_history` while a lit caster is moving water: waves move the pattern many pixels a frame, so a long history averages it into flat blobs whatever the photon count). A clamp over a few pixels of sparse photons resets the history to zero, so filter spatially by density first.
- The lighting adds the irradiance shaded as light from the lights whose entries cover the pixel (softly past the entry's edge) and lie in front of it, weighted by their unshadowed irradiance there (photons keep no direction, and the ambient response misjudges a focus badly). A light is handed to the photons only where a receiver lies in one of its entries and the light ray crosses caster glass or a water top (either way) (`caustic_covered(light, light_position, P)`): there `get_light_transmittance_caustic` and `_local_caustic` block it at caster glass and water tops, elsewhere glass keeps the straight tint, so glass past the entry limit never casts a black shadow. The water pass skips its relight and analytic floor caustic for covered pixels under a volume; its shaft caustics stay analytic.
- World grid (`r_caustics_world`): every landed photon also adds to an open addressed hash grid in a device address buffer (`caustics.GetWorldGrid`, cells of `caustics.WORLD_CELL` doubling every distance octave from `caustics.WORLD_NEAR`, keyed by cell, level and the dominant axis of the normal, eight probes, lookups never stop at an empty slot so evicted cells do not hide later ones). `caustics_world` turns each frame's sums into a smoothed irradiance per cell (`r_caustics_world_history`) and evicts dark cells. The probe block (`radiance_cache_data`, so every ray hit shader) carries the grid address and the entries: `shade_scene_hit` and the DDGI probe rays add the cell's irradiance and block covered lights exactly like the lighting. The grid is a frame behind for the passes before `caustics`.
- Sparse photons (glass scattering light widely) are noise, not motion: the temporal pass neither clamps nor caps the history of pixels with few photons.
- Cost follows the photons that bend (three times for those that split) plus the screen sized filters; off it costs nothing. The photon pass's GPU scope closes before its rays finish and the next scope waits for them, so compare `gpu_frame` with the pass on and off. Check against the path tracer with time frozen (`system.SetElapsedTime`) and regional means of `path_trace.GetTexture` and `GetRasterTexture` over windows that hold a whole caustic: the blur moves light within it.
- Pitfall: compute pass callbacks are `on_pre_draw(self, cmd, frame, desc)`; binding a buffer to `frame` instead of `desc` binds it to the wrong descriptor set and reads stale data most frames.

## Water

Ocean and `water_volume` components are both drawn by the `water` compute pass (`passes/water.lua`, with its denoiser `water_resolve`; the wave passes keep the `ocean_` prefix). Sea state is in `goluwa/render3d/water.lua` and the water presets in `goluwa/render3d/media.lua`; the in-scatter of the local lights and the specular highlights of lamps on the surface in the water pass use the loop the fog and particles use (`get_medium_light_visibility`) (`medium_lighting.GetLocalLightsGLSL` with the water phase and the underwater attenuation as `options`: point and local directional shadow maps and light occlusion decide the visibility, the water block carries the `scene_lights` shadow layout and the occlusion block and binds the occlusion map), so a light in the water is shadowed by what shadows it in the air; a water volume or the ocean is also a participating medium for the light tint walk: the ray material buffer starts with a typed prefix, the one place the volumes are written (`optical_ranges` gives the first index and count of the water and the fog volumes after the ocean record; a record carries everything a consumer needs: for water the absorption, scattering, phase, index of refraction, Abbe number, roughness, foam, caustics, wave length and direction, flow, whether it is closed and the surface height, for fog the scattering, phase, edge softness and height falloff; the water pass and both froxel passes read it through their own storage binding of that buffer, `radiance_cache.BindOpticalVolumes`, the water pass with ray queries through its materials binding, and `water.GetNearestVolumes` picks the nearest when there are more than `water.MAX_VOLUMES`). The prefix holds the header (`optical_counts` with the glass, medium body and volume counts, so a ray skips a query with nothing to find, then `optical_volume` records: the oriented box, the extinction `media.GetExtinction` (absorption, the molecular scattering `Molecular` of the preset, which only water presets have, and the particle scattering; the molecular part has its own phase, `medium_scatter_phase`), the index of refraction and the wave height, the tint averages three top heights `-h, 0, +h` for the wave statistics) and the material array after it (`radiance_cache.GetMaterialEntries`, `radiance_cache.GetOpticalVolumes`; material indices are unchanged); `get_light_transmittance` (every direct light) multiplies the path inside each volume, refracted at the top for a light above the water (the in-water path of a light ray is `depth / cos(theta_water)`), and `get_optical_tint` (probe shading, reflection and ReSTIR hit rays) adds the medium bodies, both through `optical_transmittance`, so the lighting pass and the probes carry the depth falloff and the water pass `relight_submerged` only adds the sun refraction and caustics (`sun_tinted`, `gi_tinted`, it falls back to its own `exp(-sigma * depth)` without ray queries); examples `water_showcase.lua`, `ocean_shore.lua`, `water_volume_test.lua`, `twilight_lake.lua`.

- The wave field is three inverse-FFT tiles (`water.FFT_CASCADES`) built from spectra on the CPU when ocean params change (`water.BuildOceanWaves`); the three wave textures sample them and write height, slope and foam. Foam is a world space field with memory (per cascade foam pass), driven by the Jacobian of waves 4 m and longer and the wind (`water.GetWhitecapFactor`). `water.SampleOcean` (buoyancy) sums the strongest modes on the CPU, so bodies follow the visible sea but not its smallest waves. Ocean level is the *mean* surface.
- The trace from above is a sphere trace through the slab `level +- wave_bound` (`height_map_tracing`) with a mean-plane fallback; never let a ray with no crossing show what is behind the sea. The camera start is the near plane (`WATER_NEAR_PLANE`) per pixel, so the waterline is a soft edge instead of a whole-screen flip.
- Reflections: screen first (`r_water_screenspace_roughness`), marching the depth buffer and reading the last fogged frame (`post_source.GetFoggedOpaqueSceneTexture`), rays fill where the screen finds nothing; the water shares the reflection pass's direction code and a denoiser (`water_resolve`, radius scaled by `water_rt_share`). Water lit through what covers it: the ambient is sampled at a few points along the path with probe visibility rays and sun shafts trace toward the sun, otherwise water under a roof glows like open sky. A volume at sea level blends into the ocean's surface within `VOLUME_EDGE_BLEND` of its sides (`mix_water`).
- Cost: use the `r_water_*` feature pvars (bits of `ocean_data.features`, so toggling does not recompile) to find what is expensive; compare variants in one process, interleaved with baselines (the gpu clock drifts several ms inside a process). The big costs are inscatter (shaft caustics, ambient GI), refraction, ripples and the reflection trace; `r_water_waves` off is a flat mirror.
- Pitfalls: never `smoothstep(e, e, x)` (undefined); phase angle sign is `dot(-view_dir, light_dir)`; negative values (not just NaN/inf) cause bright discs through bloom (`integrate_attenuated` must not divide by a negative); flat apparent-depth refraction in screen space tears at edges (refraction only offsets the straight-behind pixel); the submerged-floor test must be "is the point inside the volume"; discrete sine waves show grids, use advected gradient noise ripples; shader recompiles can take minutes, run shots in the background.
- Map importers turn game fog into a medium with `media.MediumFromFog`. More than `water.MAX_VOLUMES` volumes: the nearest are drawn.
