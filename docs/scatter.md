# Scatter

Models grown over surfaces on the gpu: grass, flowers, rocks, bushes, trees. It replaced the grass shader that drew procedural blades. The sets are objects like materials (`goluwa/render3d/scatter_set.lua`, its header lists every key of a model's rules), the gpu side is `goluwa/render3d/scatter.lua`.

## How it fits together

*a material has a set object, a set is a list of models with placement rules, the instances are drawn by the gbuffer's multi draw pipeline*

- A material says what grows on it with `ScatterSet` (a set object, nil for nothing) and `ScatterDensity` (multiplies every density). `TerrainLayerScatter` is how much each of the terrain's first four layers grows, a prototype's `layers` picks from them, so one set can have grass on the grass layer and rocks on the rock layer. Terrain takes `ScatterSet = set` in `Terrain.New` and a `scatter = 1` per layer (default 0, so nothing grows on a layer that doesn't ask for it).
- `ScatterSet.New{Name = "meadow", Prototypes = {...}}` makes a set, `set:Use()` (called when a surface with that set shows up) loads its models and gives every prototype a global index, `set:Remove()` takes it out. A set is a property object like a material (`DensityMultiplier`, `DistanceMultiplier` and the `Name` are storable, so an editor lists them, `Prototypes` is the definitions it builds `set.protos` from when it is set), and a material or a terrain refers to it by the object, not by a name. Unknown keys are errors. `ScatterSet.GetGrass()` is the builtin set of tufts of blades made in lua (`scatter_grass_mesh.lua`), it needs no assets and is what Source materials with "grass" in them get. `addons/game/lua/autorun/render_3d/scatter_presets.lua` returns a table of `crysis_meadow`, `crysis_forest`, `crysis_jungle` and `crysis_rocky` by name when Crysis 1 is installed, `import("lua/autorun/render_3d/scatter_presets.lua").crysis_jungle`, and an empty one when not. `import("lua/foliage/scatter_sets.lua")` has `foliage_meadow`, `foliage_forest`, `foliage_tropical`, `foliage_rocky` and `foliage_wetland` of procedural models that need nothing installed (`docs/foliage.md`).
- `scatter.lua` is a gbuffer extra named `gbuffer_multi_draw`: it has no pipeline of its own. What the surfaces grow is **baked in units** and kept: a unit is a cell of one surface, cells of 64 m near the camera and 256 m and 1024 m further out (`BAKE_CELLS`: a cell is split into the 16 under it while the camera is nearer than `BAKE_SPLIT` = 1.5 times its size, so a world of 4.5 km reach is a thousand units and not a hundred thousand). A unit that is not baked yet, because the camera just came near enough to split a cell or far enough to merge cells, is drawn as the units it overlaps until it is. `PreDraw` bakes a few units a frame (`BAKE_BUDGET` 8, nearest first) with two compute passes, `scatter_tiles` (a workgroup per triangle, finds the 32x32 cell tiles of each prototype that fall in the unit and writes jobs) and `scatter_instances` (a job is a tile: its cells are hashed, jittered, thinned and tested against the triangle, the layer mask, slope, altitude, cluster noise and a ray query straight up, and the survivors are written as a matrix into the unit's room in the `baked` pool). A unit is baked for the band of distances its nearest point is in (`BAND_FIRST` 16 m, each band 1.6 times the last) and keeps every instance that the band's nearest distance keeps, so it is baked again when the camera comes into a nearer band, when the set, its multipliers, the surface or its density change (`bake_epoch`, the surface's stamp), or when the gpu counted more instances than its room (or far fewer, it is then baked again smaller). The matrix' bottom row carries what the camera part needs: `[0][3]` the density an instance needs the field to have (priority / ground amount), `[1][3]` the tint, `[2][3]` the prototype. Units nobody wanted for 30 frames are released, their room goes back to the pool after the frames in flight.
- Every frame `scatter_cull` goes through the baked instances (the units and the blocks of 64 instances of them are tables rewritten each frame), thins them by the exact distance (`lod_keep > need`), widens them (`distance_scale`), culls them by the view and writes them into the regions the draws read: a prototype's model, its billboard, and a separate list for the shadow maps (all of them, in view or not). `scatter_commands` then fills the instance counts of the indirect draws, and `Draw` does two `DrawIndexedIndirect`s, single and double sided, with the PBR batch records of `gbuffer_instancing.NewBatchTable`, so a scattered model is shaded exactly like the same model in the scene: textures, alpha test, transmission, wind bending.
- **In the scene bvh**: the scene bvh is built on the cpu from polygons, so scatter is made into polygons for it. `bvh` and `shadow` are true by default, a prototype that should cast nothing or stay out of the bvh (the pebbles) says `false`. A prototype with `bvh = true` (every one by default, the `tree`, `bush` and `rock` classes of the Crysis presets set it as well) has its instances in the units near the camera (`r_scatter_gi_distance`, 200 m, `r_scatter_gi` turns it off) copied to the host a few frames after they were baked (`update_proxies`, one unit a frame, a staging buffer of `PROXY_MAX_INSTANCES` instances for each frame in flight), and `scatter_proxy.lua` makes one polygon of them for each prototype and part: a copy of a stand-in moved by each instance matrix. What is a stand-in depends on the camera when the proxy is built (and it is built again when the camera has moved 40 m): the instances nearer than `r_scatter_gi_distance` and than the prototype's billboard distance are the model's own geometry, a prototype that has a billboard has it (6 triangles) for the instances past that, out to `r_scatter_gi_billboard_distance` (1500 m), and one that has none has no stand-in past `r_scatter_gi_distance`,  where it is the model's lowest level of detail (`proto.lod_parts`, the polygons the model loader makes of its other levels), else the model. They are entities (`scatter_proxy_<cx>_<cz>`, one per unit, a primitive for each part) with `Visual:SetRaysOnly(true)`: in the bvh (soup shadows, DDGI, reflections) and in no gbuffer, shadow map or culling draw. A proxy is built again when its unit is baked again and goes when the unit is far (1.25 times the distance) or released. The cascades that draw the bvh's soup (`ShadowMap:UsesSoup`) skip scatter's own shadow list, so the instanced model and billboard lists only draw in the others (the inset cascade). The instances are at most `r_scatter_gi_distance` from the camera when the unit was baked, a unit far from the camera has no proxy and casts nothing in a soup cascade.
- The instance matrix's bottom row is free (the vertex stage only reads the 3x4 part). `world[0][3]` is the dither fade the lod fade already used, `world[1][3]` is the tint, rgb in unorm8 at half scale as float bits (0 is no tint, which is what scene instances have). `in_instance_tint` multiplies the albedo in the gbuffer fragment shader, for all gbuffer pipelines.
- Placement is deterministic and baked once: a cell's hash decides its jitter, its priority and every random number of the instance, and the priority against the density at the camera's distance decides if it exists, so instances don't move or flicker when the camera does. Each prototype has its own stream.

- A set has `DensityMultiplier` and `DistanceMultiplier` (1 by default) that scale the density and the distances of its prototypes (a prototype takes the share of the distance one that its `distance_response` says, the Crysis classes have 0.1 for ground cover and flowers, 0.6 for bushes and rocks and 1 for trees, so a multiplier of 10 takes trees to the horizon and leaves grass near), and the density the same way with `density_response` (1 by default, above it is fine: 3 for the Crysis ground cover, so a density multiplier of 2 is 4 times the grass and 2 times everything else), `set:SetDensityMultiplier(2)` and `set:SetDistanceMultiplier(10)` change them live, and the room of the prototypes is estimated again.

- **Billboards**: a prototype with `billboard = {distance, fade}` (the `tree` class in the Crysis presets, 150 m) asks `render3d/billboard.lua` for the baked billboard of its model path when the model has loaded. Each prototype then has two counters and two instance regions (`proto.index * 2` and `+ 1`, the billboard is a batch whose slot is the odd one). The instance pass writes an instance to the model's region below the distance and to the billboard's beyond it, both in a band of `fade` (a share of the distance) around it, where they dither into each other with `world[0][3]` (positive for the outgoing model, negative for the incoming billboard, patterns that complement, like the lod fade). A billboard is frustum culled. A prototype that casts shadows has lists of its own for it (counters 2 and 3 of the four a prototype has): its model up to the billboard's distance and its billboard from there on, all of them, in view or not, and the shadow maps decide how far that reaches. A model without a billboard casts all the way. Drawing every cascade with the full model is what makes shadows expensive, which is what the billboard is for. `r_scatter_billboards` turns them all off and `r_scatter_billboard_distance` scales the distances (0.25 by default, so 150 m is 37 m). Harness: `BB_SCALE=0.25` or `BB_OFF=1`.

- **Steep slopes**: `align = 1` stands a model on the ground's normal, which on a wall of 50 degrees lays grass out of it nearly sideways, like a fan. `align_steep` (0.35 for the Crysis `ground_cover` class) is the share of `align` that is left at 55 degrees and over, fading in from 30 degrees, so the gentle slopes that grass should follow are untouched and a wall only leans it.
- **Conforming to the ground**: a rigid copy of a wide model aimed along the ground's normal at its root pokes through or floats off ground that curves under its footprint (a rim, a ridge, a hollow), which for grass patches showed as tufts hanging past the edge of the terrain. A prototype with `conform = true` (the Crysis `ground_cover` class) has the ground measured at bake time under the four edges of its footprint (`proto.footprint`, the model's mean half width): `scatter_instances` shoots a ray query along the instance's down axis from `reach * 1.5` above each of the points at the footprint's distance from the root, on the plane through the root, and keeps how far each hit is above or below that plane. The four numbers (5 bits each, in footprint radii, along the up axis) and the radius (6 bits, log scale) are packed into the free corner of the instance matrix, `[3][3]`, which is 1.0 in a scene instance, with the sign bit set to say so. `get_instance_conform_offset` in `model_pipeline.lua` (so in the gbuffer and the shadow maps, both go through `get_vertex_animation_offset`) moves each vertex along the up axis by a parabola through the four edge heights on each axis, and the less the higher the vertex is (zero a radius above its base), so the base meets the ground and the tips stay free. It needs ray queries (without them nothing conforms) and the ground in the scene bvh when the unit is baked. It does not move normals and the bvh proxies don't follow it.

## Pitfalls

- Models need an index buffer (the multi draw pulls vertices through the index pool). `shapes.Build*` meshes uploaded without indices fail with "a mesh without indices", pass `polygon:Upload(indices)`. Only LOD 0 polygons of a model are used.
- The room of a prototype in the instance buffer is dynamic. It starts from an estimate (density x thinning with distance x clusters x the part of the circle in view, with a margin) and then follows what the gpu wanted: the instance counter keeps counting past the room, `scatter_instances` copies the counters to a host buffer each frame and a prototype whose peak (slowly decaying) is more than its room grows to 1.5 times it, and one with three times too much shrinks. A frame or four of dropped instances after a jump is the price of the readback. A `max_instances` in the prototype holds it fixed instead. All prototypes in use share `MAX_INSTANCES` (2^21, 128 MB), when they want more every dynamic one is scaled down together. `MAX_PROTOTYPES` is 256.
- `bending` sets `Bending` on the model's materials, which are shared with every other use of the model (the Crysis level code does the same).
- Scattered instances are not in the scene BVH, so they are in no DDGI, reflection or ray traced hit. Grass was the same. A prototype with `shadow = true` is drawn into the shadow maps (an event listener on `DrawAllShadows` calling `ShadowMap:DrawBatches` with `shadow_commands`, non indexed draws the shadow pipeline wants), and is then not cut by the camera's frustum, so the instances behind the camera cast into the view. The shadow maps draw what the last `PreDraw` wrote, a frame behind when they run first.
- The frustum test and the tile jobs come from the camera, a second view (reflection plane, secondary camera) sees the instances of the main one.
- The `cover` ray query throws away an instance with anything above its root within its height (a road brush laid over a displacement, a bridge). It reads the scene BVH, which builds incrementally, so a freshly spawned object may take a few frames to clear grass.
- Dither fade only comes from thinning with distance. An early version also faded by density (cluster edges), which turned bushes into ghosts.
- Prefer a cluster `amount` over a low `density` for patches: a low density with the full density distance makes every instance a lone tuft.

## Looking at it

`addons/examples/lua/examples/render3d/island_terrain.lua` is the big one: a mountain island, small islands, beaches and ocean over `crysis_jungle` at 2x density and 10x distance (trees out to 4.5 km). `island_view N` goes to its views, `test/harnesses/scatter/island_seq.lua` takes a png of each and `island_perf.lua` measures them (10 to 14 ms from the air and the sea, 12 to 13 ms standing in it, a few seconds of baking after a jump). The harness puts a camera on the ground by going up, tracing down once the terrain has colliders and coming back down (`island_locate.lua`). `scatter.pending_bakes`, `unit_count` and `pool_top` are exported for the harnesses: wait for pending bakes to reach 0 before measuring.

`test/harnesses/scatter/` renders a hills terrain from the views in `views.lua` (`run.sh`, `SET=crysis_forest` picks the set, a view's `ground` puts the camera that many meters above the terrain at its x,z, the height is known in lua), `inspect_models.lua` prints the size of Crysis models. The same terrain as an example: `addons/examples/lua/examples/render3d/scatter_showcase.lua`. The test `test/tests/render3d/scatter.lua` renders red cubes on a plane and checks slope rules. In the tests every test needs its own set name and mesh sizes (meshes with the same vertices are shared and may be on their way out).

## Things that went wrong

- **Job buffer overflow**: every triangle of a surface makes a job per prototype in range, and trees and rocks reach 250-450 m, so a streamed terrain made millions of jobs against `MAX_JOBS` (2^20). Whatever came last was dropped: whole chunks and sides of the screen without trees. A sparse prototype (cell above 2 m) now only gets a job for a triangle that has a cell point in it, found by looking at the few cells a point can come from, and has no level jitter (`scatter_max_level`), which brought 400k jobs to 20k and the gbuffer from 7.8 to 3.9 ms on the meadow benchmark (`glw bench scatter_meadow`, `SCATTER_OFF=1` for the baseline). Past `MAX_JOBS` the jobs are still just dropped, so check the job count if a big scene loses chunks.
- **Culling by the root**: the tile test used the box of the triangle, so a tree whose root was out of view lost its crown that was in it. The box is grown by the instance's radius.
- **Skirts under terrain tiles**: the vertical strips under the tile edges copy the edge vertices with their normals, so by the normals they read as flat ground. The tile pass skips the vertical triangles of terrain surfaces (face normal within 1 degree of horizontal). That did not change the shot of grass hanging past a tile edge, which was the rigid patch problem above, but they are no ground.
- **Cover ray on terrain**: tiles of different levels of detail overlap while they are swapped, a ray straight up would hit the other one and throw the instance away. The cover test is only for surfaces without a layer mask.
- **Streamed tiles never registering** (vegetation gone from a chunk for good): `Visual.Library.GetSceneMaterialUsers()` only refreshed a component's materials on a structure change. A terrain tile whose bounds arrive after its render entries joins the scene without one, so it was missing from the material users, and so from scatter (the old grass too). The scene patch now refreshes a component whose in-scene state changed. If a surface has no scatter, compare `GetSceneMaterialUsers()` with the visuals that have the material before looking at the shaders.
- **Cost moved from placing to drawing**: with the baking, a showcase of 3x density and 10x distance spends about 0.2 ms on the cull pass and the rest on drawing about 400k instances of full models (about 20 ms in the main pass and 24 ms in the shadow maps before the shadow list got its own distance; 29 ms for the whole frame after, against 4.6 without scatter). Before the baking the same frame was 56 ms with 25 ms in the instance pass and 11 in the tile pass. Drawing is vertex bound, so what is left is fewer instances (density and distance per class), billboards earlier, or lods.
- **Pool full of open sea**: a unit's room starts from an estimate that only knows the density rules, not where the land is, so every 1 km cell of sea around the island got a jungle's worth of room and the pool (2^21) was full of empty rooms with 600 bakes pending and no trees past 2 km. Units of the bigger cells with a big estimate (`PROBE_ABOVE`) are first baked into a small room (`PROBE_ROOM`), the gpu counts what there is past the room and the unit is baked again to fit (the machinery of units that outgrow their room). With it the whole island at 2x/10x is 0.4 to 1.2 million instances in the pool.
- The baked pool is `MAX_BAKED` (2^21 instances, 128 MB), and `MAX_UNITS` (16384) units. When it is full the farthest units are dropped (and a log line says so), they bake again when there is room.
- **Units of removed surfaces were drawn**: a unit stays alive 30 frames after nothing wants it (in case it is back), and the cull pass drew every unit that had memory. A removed or swapped terrain tile kept its vegetation for half a second. Only units seen this frame are in the cull tables now.
- **Vegetation vanishes where terrain tiles swap** (found and fixed 2026-10-14, see below): units belong to a surface (a terrain tile's render entry, `surface_states`), a tile that is hidden is not a candidate so its units are not baked, and a tile that is gone takes its units out of the draw. `Terrain` hands a tile over once the replacements are drawn, which says nothing about their vegetation. `test/harnesses/scatter/island_gaps.lua` flies the island's jungle at 60 m/s: the terrain removed 287-312 tiles within 350 m in 30 s, and in every one of them none of the replacements had a unit baked at that moment; the vegetation of the replacements was all there after a median of 0.1-0.17 s, p90 0.5-1.1 s, max 2.3 s, and a tile in front of the camera lacked some in 25-28% of the frames (`scatter.GetSurfaceStatus(entry)` is how many units a surface wanted this frame and how many were baked). The bake order is only the distance of the unit from where the camera is now (no view, no velocity, behind the camera counts like ahead) and the 8 bakes a frame were not the limit (266 bakes a second at 44 fps, 158 units created a second, 33 pending that were new or changed and 159 that were a unit baked for a farther band that the camera came nearer to, 38 for a nearer band that it left).
  Fix: `scatter.Preload(component)` makes a component that is not visible a candidate anyway (a terrain tile hidden behind the one it replaces): its units are baked and kept (`state.preload`), after the new units of what is shown and before a unit that is only baked for a nearer band (`priority + 500`), but they are not drawn and get no scene bvh proxies until the component is visible. `scatter.IsSurfaceReady(entry)` is true when the surface was looked at this frame or the one before and all the units it wants are baked (or scatter is off), and `Terrain:IsTileReplaced` asks it for the replacements that have a scatter set before it hands a tile over (`ScatterWaitFrames`, 120, then it goes anyway), so the replacements stay hidden and the old tile and its vegetation stay until the new vegetation is baked. `island_gaps.lua` at 60 m/s after: at the removal of a near tile all of its replacements were fully baked in 288 of 288 cases (0 of 309 before), the shown near tiles that lacked some vegetation went from 25-28% of the frames to 2% (the rest is units re-baked for a nearer band, not a swap), the old tile waited a median of 13 frames, p90 35, and 1-2 in 300 ran into the limit.
- **Bake order is the view's** (2026-10-14): the units that want baking are ranked by `goluwa/render3d/view_priority.lua` (`scatter.view_priority`), the same ranking the terrain uses for its tiles: the nearer of the camera now and where it will be in 0.5 s, to the box of the unit (the surface's height range), times 1 + `OutsidePenalty` (100) times how far the unit is out of the horizontal view plus 20 degrees, as a share of pi (a unit under the camera has a distance of 0 whichever way it looks, looking mostly down is everything). It is the order inside a class: `priority = class * 1e6 + rank`, class 0 new or changed units (of surfaces that are shown and of the ones that wait hidden to replace a tile, by the same ranking), 1 the same for a surface that is about to go (`scatter.SetRetiring`, which the terrain sets on its retiring tiles: what they would grow is not worth baking before what is wanted), 2 a unit baked for a farther band that the camera came nearer to, 3 a unit baked for a nearer band that it left. `test/harnesses/scatter/island_fill.lua` jumps to six places over the island with a fixed view (`OLD=1` bakes by the distance alone, `BAKE_BUDGET=n`): after the tiles of the view are shown (mean 3.2-3.8 s, that is the terrain's own load rate on this island, about 90 tiles a second, not scatter) the vegetation of them was baked after a mean of 0.45 s with the view ranking and 1.0-1.4 s without; everything within reach took ~5.9 s either way (~1500 bakes at 8 a frame and 45 fps). `BAKE_BUDGET` 16 halves that for a few ms a frame more (21-25 ms a frame while filling at 8, 31-43 at 16, 57 at 32, noisy: the timings in this session were taken while something else was using the gpu).
- **A jump took 3.5 s to show the tiles of the view** (2026-10-14, `island_fill.lua` with `TIMELINE=1`, `island_hitch.lua`): not the bake cost. Baking a tile on the island costs ~1 ms of gpu (heights 0.67 with the readback, the normal map +0.2, the splat +0.17, the color +0.07) and 0.35-0.9 ms of cpu to record, `Terrain:CreateTile` is 0.5 ms (`test/harnesses/terrain/bake_cost.lua`), so a tile is 2-3 ms of cpu, and raising `BuildsPerFrame` did not help. Two things did: (1) the units of the old tiles that were about to go were class 0 and the hidden replacements class 1, so after a jump about 1000 bakes of doomed vegetation went first and the old tiles waited ~2 s for the replacements (the retiring class above); (2) the terrain's BVH admission: a jump makes hundreds of tiles whose `BVHDelay` ends together, and `scene_bvh` rebuilds every unbuilt visual in one frame when it sees that many changes (`AABB_CHANGED_ALL`, the scan mode has no time budget): frames of 300-490 ms. `Terrain.BVHPerFrame` (1) lets one tile a frame in, the nearest first. Tiles of the view shown after a jump: 3.5 s to ~2 s (worst 6.6 s to 3.3 s), everything baked 5.1 s to ~4 s, and with `BUILDS=4` 1.5 s, but the timings of that session are noisy.
