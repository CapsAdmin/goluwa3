# Audio

Physically based by default: sound travels at `audio.speed_of_sound` through a delay line per voice, doppler is the derivative of that delay, and a traced environment drives occlusion, reflections and reverb. World units are meters.

## Mixer (`goluwa/audio/mix.lua`, audio thread)

- Block based. Each spatial voice writes its emitted mono signal (volume and pitch applied, source sample rate honored) into a history ring; the ears, the reflection taps and the reverb sends are fractional reads from that ring at their delays. Nothing else in the mixer knows about geometry.
- The main thread (`spatial.lua`, every Update) writes per path: delay, delay rate (seconds per second) and 3 band gains, stamped with `audio.clock()`. The mixer evaluates `delay + rate * (block_time - stamp)` and ramps across the block, so frame jitter and low update rates do not wobble the pitch. Delays use the retarded (emission time) source position; velocities are estimated from motion, there is no velocity API.
- `doppler_factor` scales the effective speed of sound (0 = instant, no doppler).
- Ears are `audio.ear_distance` apart: interaural time difference falls out of the per ear delays; head shadow is a band gain (`spatial.HeadShadow`).
- Pinna cues (pvar `audio_pinna` is their strength, 0 off, 1 about a measured ear; tunables `spatial.PINNA_NOTCH`, `PINNA_OVERHEAD`, `PINNA_FRONT`): time and level differences only give left and right; up, down, front and back come from the narrow notches the outer ear carves into 5 to 12 kHz, which the 3 bands cannot express (everything above 4 kHz is one band). Each ear's direct path runs through three peaking filters after its band gains: the notch (moving up with the height of the source, fading overhead, shallower behind, deepest in the ear the source faces), a boost around 8 kHz for sources above, and a 4 kHz boost in front that turns into a cut behind. The settings come from the direction in head space; the mixer glides them over a block and recomputes coefficients every 32 samples only while they change.
- Reflection taps reach each ear at its own delay (the image source seen from each ear), so they have their own interaural time difference instead of sitting in the middle of the head. They read a second history per voice that is written already shaped by the taps' band gains, and are blended by their direction (height, front or back) into six groups shared by all voices (below, level and above, each front and back), which get the pinna filters of the group's direction once per ear. A strong unfiltered reflection otherwise fills in the direct sound's cues.
- A voice's first block takes its send and tap gains at once instead of ramping in from silence; a transient that falls inside the ramp loses several dB of reverb.
- Stopping a spatial sound stops emission; what is already in flight still arrives (`slot.draining`). `Sound:Cut()` and `audio.StopAll()` cut hard.
- `Sound:SetSpatial(false)` for UI, music and anything not in the world: plays the buffer directly in stereo. Spatial sounds keep a mid and a side history: the left ear reads mid plus side and the right ear mid minus side (so each ear gets its own channel at the same delay), while reflection taps and reverb sends use the mid only. Summing L and R into one signal comb-filters any stereo source whose channels differ in phase.
- Every start ramps in and every non looping end ramps out over at least 3 ms (`DECLICK` in the mixer), otherwise a sound starting (or ending) on a non zero sample clicks; `Sound:SetFadeIn(seconds)`, `SetFadeInFraction(f)` (a share of the whole sound) and `SetFadeOut(seconds)` lengthen that, measured in source time from the start of each `Play`. Stopping a playing sound still cuts instantly.
- `Sound:SetBandGains(Vec3(low, mid, high))` shapes what a spatial sound emits (direct path and reflections, not the reverb send). The shallow crossovers limit it to gentle tilts, about 10% accurate at the band centers; a steep cut next to a band at 1 bottoms out near -11 dB.
- `Sound:SetNormalized(true)` makes volume 1 play at `audio.reference_loudness` (LUFS at the reference distance): the gain comes from `Sound:GetLoudness()`, the loudest 400 ms of the buffer K-weighted (BS.1770 momentary, `dsp.MeasureLoudness`), measured once when the buffer is ready and capped at +30 dB. Normalize game sounds so their volumes compare: Source files of one kind differ by 20 dB or more. `GetMixVolume()` is what reaches the mixer. Short clicky files have a high crest factor, so normalized peaks can exceed 0 dBFS at volume 1; there is no limiter.
- 32 voices (`audio.SLOT_COUNT`). When all are busy, a new sound takes the voice of the quietest non-looping spatial sound at the listener (slot volume times its mid band ear gain) if it is louder there, otherwise it is dropped; loops are never taken, non-spatial sounds outrank everything.
- Two reverb buses (`dsp.ProcessReverb`, 16 line FDN with per line 3 band absorption, Hadamard feedback, input diffusion). A bus is normalized so a unit send gives an impulse response with energy 1 per channel in the mid band; sends carry the physical energy. Buses sleep when silent.
- A bus has a delay `scale` (line lengths times the scale, so echo spacing follows the space's mean free path: dense in rooms, distinct echoes outdoors) and a `diffusion` (input diffuser amount). The scale snaps to steps with hysteresis and changes by a 50 ms equal power crossfade from reads at the old line lengths to reads at the new ones, never by moving a read position: a moving read pitches the tail, and in the feedback loop it pitches it again on every pass (tripling the size took a 1 kHz tail below 750 Hz). At the switch the lines are rescaled by the ratio of the old to the new output gain, otherwise the energy stored at the old size's level swells by 3 dB. The predelay glides while it moves less than 2% per sample and crossfades within a block when it jumps.
- The bus outputs are coherent between the ears below `dsp.REVERB_COHERENT_BELOW` (a mid/side crossover that keeps the power) and uncorrelated above, like a diffuse field at two ears. Lines summed with orthogonal signs are uncorrelated at every frequency, and uncorrelated lows sound pressured and inside the head on headphones.
- The FDN only reaches its envelope after its mean line delay (about 30 ms at scale 1, more at larger scales), so the diffused input is also emitted directly as a burst holding the energy share `1 - exp(-13.82 * onset / T60)`; without it short rooms lose their early reverb.
- In the feedback loop a small per pass gain error is a large T60 error. Line gains are fitted so the loop response at each band center equals its target (`dsp.FitBandGains`), and only shrunk when the true response peak (`dsp.BandGainPeak`) rings past 1.5 times the longest band. A loose bound instead (summing band differences) shortens long, uneven decays by half.
- Bands: crossovers at 500 Hz and 4 kHz (two cascaded one poles each), centers 250/1500/8000 Hz. Gains are given at the band centers and converted with `dsp.WarpBandGains`, otherwise the shallow crossovers leak neighbouring bands. They cannot make a steep step between bands (12 s mid next to 3 s high comes out around 8 s mid).

## Environment (`goluwa/audio/acoustics.lua`)

A tracer (`acoustics_gpu.lua`: ray queries on the scene TLAS in a PreRenderPass compute pass, results read back one swapchain cycle later; `acoustics_cpu.lua`: `physics.RayCast`, static bodies only) runs the same jobs. Pvar `audio_acoustics` = auto, gpu, cpu or off.

- Listener path tracing (Fibonacci directions; a bounce continues as a mirror reflection with probability `1 - SCATTERING`, cosine distributed otherwise, the split the taps assume) accumulates statistics: hits, escapes, mean free path, log reflectance per band, first hit volume. T60 per band comes from per segment survival (escape counts as absorption, air absorption included), so it matches Eyring in closed rooms and drops outdoors.
- Purely diffuse bounces mix the space too fast: energy that slides out of a corridor's open ends stays, and paths bouncing horizontally between the walls of a roofless room escape upwards. Both were off by 5 to 10 dB against a mostly specular reference.
- Statistics, per source sums and reflectors fade with time (`STATS_MEMORY`) and with how far the listener (or source) moved relative to the mean free path (`STATS_DISTANCE`), so standing still averages and walking through a door switches rooms within half a mean free path. Old stats from a closed room carry about 8 hits per ray against 1 or 2 outdoors, so time alone keeps them dominant for seconds.
- Next event estimation from every bounce to each source gives the reverberant energy at the listener relative to the direct sound at 1 m. Only energy arriving after the split time (`1.5 * mean free path / c`) and after at least two bounces feeds the reverb: first order energy is the taps' job (outdoors it is distinct facade echoes, not tail). Energy past the last traced bounce is completed analytically from the second half of the bounces: their next event yield per throughput, their throughput survival per bounce (escapes included) and their throughput weighted segment length. The paths still alive there are the trapped ones (horizontal under an open sky, along a corridor), while the all bounce statistics describe the early decay. Long spaces mix slowly: in a 20 m corridor the yield per bounce at bounce 4 is twice the mixed value and settles after about 25 bounces, so 8 bounces overestimate its tail by 1 to 2 dB (16 bounces halve that).
- The direct path stops getting louder inside the reference distance, so sends and taps are scaled by `min(distance, reference distance)` as well; otherwise a close sound gets the reverb of one much louder than it is.
- The listener position itself is always traced as one more source (`acoustics.ambient`): the reverb a sound right here would get. A sound that starts new, or far from where its sums were traced, takes a copy of it on Play, so one-shots and reused voices are never dry or stale.
- The listener bus T60 is the ambient trace's energy weighted T60 (from the centroid of arrival times), not the statistical one: with two slopes (open roofs, courtyards) the statistical T60 is the fast early slope and the tail dies far too soon.
- The listener bus predelay is the split time, moved earlier in spaces smaller than the shortest bus scale (`dsp.REVERB_MIN_SCALE`) by the build up its too long lines add, `(minimum scale - wanted scale) * dsp.REVERB_MEAN_DELAY`, down to `acoustics.MIN_PREDELAY`. Without it the reverb of small rooms builds up like a larger room's and they sound larger (their first 10 ms came out 1.5 dB short).
- The listener bus scale is `mean free path / (c * dsp.REVERB_MEAN_DELAY)`; its diffusion falls from 1 at `DIFFUSE_ESCAPING` (10% of segments escaping) to 0 at `SPARSE_ESCAPING` (35%).
- A source whose energy centroid decays much slower than the ambient trace's (a sound at the listener, so both come from arrival times) is in another space and sends to the second bus, which takes the energy weighted T60 of such sources. Comparing against the statistical room T60 instead sends every outdoor sound there, because sparse late echoes make the centroid long.
- Direct occlusion samples 0.6 of the first Fresnel zone per band, so low frequencies bend around edges; fully blocked paths keep `acoustics.TRANSMISSION`.
- First hits are clustered into planes; image sources validated with two rays become reflection taps (early reflections, distinct echoes). The top planes are ranked by hit count (the share of the view they fill) and need `MIN_REFLECTOR_HITS`: ranked by observed area, single far samples (each standing for hundreds of square meters of distant terrain) push out the ground and the facades that actually echo. A plane also keeps the spread of its hits (its observed footprint); on Play every recently seen plane whose mirror point lies within two standard deviations of that footprint becomes a tap at once, because a one-shot ends before a ray validation comes back. Validation still confirms or drops them while the sound lasts.
- New sounds are probed synchronously on Play (CPU physics) so a one-shot behind a wall does not start unoccluded.
- Absorption is the render material param `AcousticAbsorption` (energy coefficients per band). Source materials resolve an acoustic class separately from the render class (`vmt_material.lua`, `ACOUSTIC_*` tables): the vmt `$surfaceprop` walked up its base chain, then keywords in the material's own name and base texture, then the detail texture, then the model's physics surfaceprop, then keywords in the directories. A model's physics surfaceprop covers all of its materials (propper walls say `wood` for plaster and concrete faces, sofas say `ceiling_tile`), so the material's own name beats it. Names that say nothing (`default`, `no_decal`, `player`) fall through. Tool faces that only exist for collision (nodraw, invisible, clips) and fake skies are `open` (fully absorbing): the GPU tracer never sees them, and the CPU tracer must not hear them reflect.
- Listener rays start `LISTENER_CLEARANCE` out (the player's own body); rays toward a source stop `SOURCE_CLEARANCE` short (the emitter's own mesh).

## Impacts, contacts and footsteps (`addons/game/lua/impacts.lua`)

- `impacts.Fire(entity, part, kind, hit, speed)` calls the `Impact` event with `{Entity, Part, Kind, Surface, Material, Other, Position, Normal, Speed}`: `hit` is a physics hit on `Other`, `Speed` the impact speed in m/s. Sounds listen to the event; whatever detects an impact only fires it. Kinds: `footstep`, `physics`.
- `Surface` is the material param `SurfaceType` of `physics.GetHitMaterial(hit)` (the hit polygon's or brush side's material, else the entity's model or first visual, also on a direct child). Source materials take the model's physics surfaceprop, else the vmt `$surfaceprop`, else keyword families; untyped is `default`. BSP collision polygons and brush sides carry their render materials only when rendering.
- Footsteps come from the foot IK ground rays. `source_pose` works in animation space (immune to slopes and render jitter): a foot lifts 4 cm above its stance height, and fires when it stops descending in the lower half of its swing; airborne arms both feet and the body's fall speed is the landing speed. Thresholds against a running minimum alone miss steps, because blends dip the ankle below the stance height. `eye_pose` fires when a step plants.
- Physics impacts come from the core `PhysicsImpact` event (see physics.md). `impacts.FirePhysics` turns one into an `Impact` per side, each with the surface of the other body, so a crate on concrete plays both the crate and the concrete. Static bodies get their surface from a short ray toward them from the contact (materials are per primitive there); dynamic bodies from their entity. A ragdoll part reports its character and limb (`Part` is the ragdoll group) and is flesh; collisions inside one character and character capsules are skipped (characters sound through their feet).
- Sliding and rolling come from the core `PhysicsContact` event. `impacts.FireContact` turns one into a `Contact` per side (`{Entity, Part, Other, Surface, Material, Position, Normal, Slip, Roll, Rolling}`), with the same skipping and surfaces as impacts; surfaces are resolved at most every 0.25 s per pair. `Rolling` is true on the side of the body that rolls, so its `Surface` is the ground it rolls on.
- `Impact` and `Contact` from physics carry `Mass`, the reduced mass of the two bodies (a static side counts as infinite), so a can and a barrel at the same speed differ, and `Size`, the characteristic length (cube root of the bounding box volume) of the dynamic body whose material sounds (the other body's when that one is static; nil if neither is dynamic).
- `lua/sounds.lua` creates every game sound normalized with a 2 m reference distance (`sounds.CreateSource`) and drops a play whose level at the listener (`sounds.GetLevel`: volume over distance past the reference) is under -45 dB; there is no fixed range, loud events carry further.
- `autorun/audio/footsteps.lua` and `autorun/audio/physics_sounds.lua` map surface names to wav sets in hardcoded tables (Source names; an unknown name falls back to its prefix before `_`). Levels are dB relative to the reference loudness: footsteps sit at `LEVEL` (-20) plus a per surface offset, scaled by foot speed; impacts by speed, mass (6 dB per tenfold mass around 20 kg) and a per material offset; scrapes, rolls and grains a fixed amount below impacts. Impacts below `SOFT_SPEED` use the soft set, above it the hard set, and both soften by starting later in the sample (`sounds.Play` `start_fraction`, `Sound:Play(position, "fraction")`: up to half the buffer at the minimum speed, none at `LOUD_SPEED`; the soft set skips half as much), which drops the attack the way a gentle hit lacks one, plus a short fade in at the lowest speeds (`FadeIn`); they are rate limited per pair. Pitch is never random: it is `0.625 / Size` (an oil drum, `models/props_c17/oildrum001.mdl`, plays at 1; twice the length an octave down, clamped to 0.25 to 2) because a resonance falls with the length of the body, not with its mass, and scrape and roll loops multiply their speed pitch by it. Contacts drive looping voices per pair and layer that fade in and out (at most 8, the quietest at the listener is replaced):
  - slip plays the smooth or rough scrape loop of the touched surface;
  - rolling has two layers, the ground's smooth scrape loop pitched and brightened (`BandGains`) with speed on the roller's side, and the roller's own sound on the ground's side: its roll loop where Source has one (canisters, barrels, cans, grenades), else its soft impact samples as grains every 0.3 m rolled, each fading in over its whole length so they swell instead of knocking.

## Voices

- `event.Call("ClientVoiceOrigin", client)` returns where a client speaks from and where it faces: the world `mouth` socket of its player (`addons/game/lua/autorun/audio/voice.lua`), or nothing when it has no character. Chatsounds play spatially from it, following it every frame, with a voice cone (full level within 45 degrees of the face, falling to -6 dB behind, `chatsounds.voice_inner_cone`, `voice_back_gain`); a speaker without an origin (console, no player yet) plays them non-spatial. The addon only fires the event, so it does not depend on `game`.

## Debugging

`perf audio` (or `h`, AUDIO tab, `addons/debug/lua/autorun/render_3d/audio_acoustics.lua`) shows what the system thinks the listener is in: tracer and cost, the listener room (T60 per band, volume, mean free path, escaping rays, predelay), every spatial sound (delay, doppler, direct level and occlusion, reverb send and which bus, reflections), a top down map of first hits, reflecting planes, reflection paths and direct paths, and the decay curves. `audio_debug` draws the same in the world; `audio_stats` prints the rows.

## Measuring

You cannot listen, so render offline and measure: swap `audio.state` for `audio.CreateMixerState()`, create `mix.CreateMixer(state, 44100, 512)`, point `mixer.clock` and `audio.clock` at a virtual time, then alternate `audio.Update()` (frame rate) and `mix.MixOutputBuffer` (block rate). See `test/tests/other/audio_acoustics.lua`, which also has an analytic box tracer for checking the statistics against Eyring.

- Delay: peak index of an impulse. Doppler: zero crossings of a sine. Decay: Schroeder backward integration, T20 range.
- Measure per band decay with a narrow band pass at the band centers; a wide band's late tail is dominated by its slowest frequencies.
- Write a 16 bit wav to hand the user something to listen to.
- Calibrate the environment in rooms you control rather than on a map: axis aligned boxes with per face materials, holes and box furniture as a tracer, against a source ray reference (mostly specular bounces, receiver sphere, no bounce limit). Compare the reverberant energy and the direct window (-1 to 10 ms) separately against the dry direct sound: in narrow spaces the direct window holds the first reflections, so a ratio to it mixes both errors.
- `goluwa/audio/signals.lua` builds deterministic test signals: `Clap` (a hand clap, broad peak around 1.5 kHz, 10 ms) and `Boom` (40 to 90 Hz, about 1 s). In game the `Sound test` weapon (slot 9) plays them, clap on primary and boom on secondary, at -6 and +6 dB on the game's level scale.
